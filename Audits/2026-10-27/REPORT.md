# Winshire Chronicles: full audit of Books 1–3 (27 Oct 2026)

*This audit read every chapter. 19 reviewers each covered a range of about 45,000 words, and one more reviewer looked across the trilogy. A second, independent pass then re-checked every Critical and Major finding against the text, the Bible, the Atlas data and the rulings, and I read the Critical ones myself. Nothing in the manuscripts was changed. The per-range reports, the verification files and the mechanical checks are in this folder.*

## In one paragraph

The trilogy holds together. The arc of protection against control runs from Book 1's unchosen yard to Book 3's remnant. Every settled reveal rule holds in the prose, with one exception: the control relic is called "the seventh" (Book 3, chs 30, 35, 36). The POV order is correct in all three books, and there are no em dashes. The damage sits in two places:

- **Book 3's geography.** Parts One to Four were drafted on a mirror image of the map, with Hearthmere east of the Wend. Parts Five to Seven squeeze the Marches together.
- **A layer of continuity slips across Books 1 and 2.** Day counts, travel times, compass bearings, and people knowing things before they could.

Almost all of it can be fixed sentence by sentence. Only a handful of points need you to decide.

**Totals:** 8 Critical (6 unique), 126 Major, 289 Minor and 107 Craft. Of the 128 Critical and Major findings checked:
- 74 were confirmed as written;
- 46 were confirmed, but with a corrected fix (the reviewer's fix would have broken something else);
- 6 were wrong and are dropped;
- the 8 cross-book findings I confirmed myself.

## 1. Decisions only you can make

1. **"She didn't count them." is used before the last line.** Your last line already appears word for word four times earlier in Book 3: ch 37 lines 320 and 322, ch 50 line 215, and ch 63 line 249. Close variants appear in ch 11 line 151 and ch 47 line 138.
   - As things stand, the ending repeats a refrain rather than landing for the first time.
   - **Suggestion:** rewrite the earlier uses so that Maren counts, or simply doesn't, in other words. Then the last line is the first time the sentence appears. The finding is B3-50-a.
2. **Book 3's mirrored map** (B3-07-a and related; verification §1–3).
   - In Parts One to Four, Hearthmere lies east of the Wend. The Ysse is its border, Hallowmere is evacuated "west toward Greyhold", and from Coldtarn Aurveth looks "east" to the Mere.
   - The Atlas and Books 1–2 put Hearthmere west of the Wend, so the Atlas can't be the thing that moves.
   - **Recommended:** fix the prose in one pass with one rule, *going east from the Wend, you are in Saltreach*. It touches about 12 chapters (4, 7, 10, 11, 23, 41–45, 49–53).
3. **The lower Wend is twelve leagues long** in Book 3 (chs 58, 60, 68 and 76). Canon and Books 1–2 make it about 95–100 leagues: "Wendmouth was a hundred leagues south."
   - The ways to fix it: rescale the barge runs and Calder's river watch to the real distance, or move the market's refuge up the river.
   - Either way, ch 76, where Kit watches the river mouth from the Staithe, needs rethinking. You choose the shape and I'll make it consistent.
4. **Where the battle is fought** (X-B, B3-53-a). From ch 53 on, Book 3 puts the eddy (the hub, the middle of the world) right beside Greyhold and Liss's ferry house. The Atlas and Book 2 ch 82 put it about 3.5 leagues upriver.
   - **Recommended:** keep the battle at the eddy and move Liss. She brings her ferry up to a new landing at the eddy in the autumn.
   - That changes "ferry house" to "boathouse" or "the new landing" through Parts Four to Six, and makes the camp's move six leagues instead of three.
5. **Two Atlas changes that cost nothing.** Neither contradicts Books 1–2.
   - Move Coldtarn to about 4–5 leagues above Varrhold. Book 1 ch 8 and Book 3 chs 67 and 79 already treat it that way.
   - Optionally, stretch the Candle Downs north-east to the Wend.
   - Harrow's End and Cotter's Cross also slide around in Book 3 (chs 54, 56, 59, 67, 77, 78). The prose fixes are listed in verification §5.
6. **Who people are** (X-C, B2-28-a / B2-37-a).
   - **Wynn** is a man until Book 3 ch 10 and a woman from ch 73. Recommended: keep him a man.
   - **Wynn's hound Sorrow** dies at the Hollow Ring (Book 2 ch 20) and then lives for two more books. Recommended: make the dead hound another knight's.
   - **The Warden Brannagh** in Book 2 is a woman in chs 28 and 31 and a man in chs 37 and 40. The verifier recommends keeping him a man, because fewer lines change.
7. **Two judgement calls on settled rules:**
   - **Ch 22's title, "Ulric's Pour",** tells the reader three chapters early that Ulric dies. The plan's own rule says no title names a death. Suggested replacements: "The Black Stone" or "Pour With Me".
   - **Idony's "nothing new in the ground" in ch 52** adds two sentences that explain, ahead of time, where the stone creature in ch 86 comes from. Suggestion: cut back to "For anything new. In the ground. Anything small" and "Nothing comes free."

## 3. The Major findings worth knowing first

The full list, with every verdict, is below in section 6. These are the ones a careful reader would trip on:

- **Calder's account and his literacy.** In Book 3 ch 84 Calder "couldn't write", and his first line is moved to Liss's table, even though Book 2 finished his literacy arc at Bel's table (X-D). The account's opening sentence also says the bonds happened "on a roof", when they happened in the barley stubble (X-E, Book 2 ch 88:305).
- **Idony.** Book 3 makes him a Fourth ring with notches on a staff, and twice gives him "forty years" of work. Canon has him a Second-ring Reader, about 31, whose ring was cut from his hand (X-F: chs 31, 57, 60). In Book 2 ch 76 he is already "grey all through" before his burnout at ch 83 (B2-76-a).
- **Orrun's shadows.** He raises six shadows before the gift (Book 3 ch 25) and holds four for four days (ch 7). Both break Book 2's four-body ceiling and take the force out of ch 77's climax (X-G).
- **The Grey Mother's reveal** in ch 30 calls back lines that Hesper and Tova never said. One plant line each would fix it (X-H).
- **People knowing things too early.** There are many of these. Examples:
  - Maren knows Calder's fleet details in Book 3 ch 27.
  - Maren knows the white crosses before they're painted (Book 2 chs 65 and 67).
  - A trader knows the Moot plan the day after it was made (Book 2 ch 24).
  - Rue knows the three attacks that Maren told only Rowan (Book 1 ch 26).
- **Book 1 timing and position.**
  - Maren watches Harrow's End burn from the island's west shore, which faces away from it (B1-17-a).
  - Calder reaches Wendmouth two days late for the twins' clue (B1-32-a).
  - Ada's verse is sung a night early (B1-46-a).
- **Book 2 geography.**
  - The coast is drawn backwards: Sarovel "beyond Corsa", and Corvin's ship passing Corsa on the way to the Wend (B2 chs 59, 64, 66).
  - The hunters' camp is said to be "a day's flight" from Vael, but people see its lamps and walk to it.
- **The one-life rule.** In Book 3 chs 81 and 84, riders survive their dragons, and a dragon survives its dead rider. That breaks the rule the series uses to make Calder's and Maren's survival special.
- **The Coldtarn arrivals** (Book 3 chs 78–79). Tam's wagons are both last in the column and first over the lip, and the column's leaders reach the hall a day late.

## 4. Craft: what repeats

The reviewers each kept to at most eight craft notes. Across 107 of them, the same few habits come up again and again:

- **"Not X. Y." corrections:**
  - Book 1: 49.
  - Book 2: 143.
  - Book 3: 117.

  This is now the strongest tic in the prose.
- **"the way" similes:**
  - Book 1: 179.
  - Book 2: 77.
  - Book 3: 2.

  Book 1's earlier audit cut these by a third, and Book 3 was written without them. Books 1 and 2 still read differently from Book 3 here.
- **Stock beats:** "twice" and "say it twice" are used so often in Books 2–3 that they blunt Wren's line. "didn't say anything", "for a long time", "didn't know why" and "almost smiled" also recur.
- **The same chapter endings repeated:**
  - lying awake (Book 1, four of the first ten chapters);
  - something "still warm" (four chapters);
  - Maren asleep against the lion's foreleg (three chapters in Book 3 Part Six);
  - two window-at-dusk endings in a row.
- **Summarised where it should be dramatised:**
  - Calder's big speech (Book 1 ch 50);
  - the canon table scene (Book 2 ch 86);
  - Ulric's death, which is reported rather than shown (Book 3 ch 22);
  - ch 66's middle (Book 2).
- **Book-level pacing:**
  - Book 2 is front-loaded. Part One averages 4,914 words a chapter and Part Seven 2,166. The canonical climax, the Titan taking the relic in ch 84, is the shortest chapter in the trilogy, at 1,682 words.
  - Book 3's far shore is its thinnest stretch (X-O, X-P).
- **Arcs** (X-Q to X-V):
  - Calder's Book 3 arc replays and disowns his Book 2 choices.
  - Idony's Book 3 forgets Nessa.
  - Kit's single survival hint is cancelled two chapters later by Idony's "Now you know" (X-T). Decide whether the hint stays open.
