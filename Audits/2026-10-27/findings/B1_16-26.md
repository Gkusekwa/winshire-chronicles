# Findings: Book 1, chapters 16–26
Reviewer summary: This range is in good health. The casting rule holds: Maren and Calder cast nothing, the surgeon and Ada do the charm-work, and running water is respected. The day counts from 5 to 23 line up with the notes and the Bible, and the counts (twelve hunters, three dead, eight and the Captain left) are right. No reveal rule is broken. The real problems are three. First, from the island's west shore Maren watches Harrow's End burn, but the west shore faces Hearthmere. Second, Rook "crosses the water" from the Eelweir, which is already on the farmer's bank. Third, the Saltreach gossip knows details that Maren told only Rowan. There is also one title slip: Sigrun is called "Ulric's champion" when Ulric is the champion.

## Critical   (breaks the story, canon or a settled rule)

None found.

## Major      (a continuity or logic error a careful reader would notice)

### [B1-17-a] Maren watches Harrow's End burn from the west shore, which faces Hearthmere
- Where: Book 1 ch 17, lines 237, 273–283 (and notes line 16); ch 19, lines 27–29, 91, 107 (and notes line 11)
- Quote: "When she lifted her head again, the far bank was on fire." / "Downstream a little, opposite her, where the lights of Harrow's End had been" (17); "She was still sitting on the wet grass of the west shore when the dragons screamed." / "It looked across the river. At the far bank. At the fire on the hill, and the burning mill" (19)
- Problem: The west channel runs between the island and the Hearthmere bank. Harrow's End is on the Saltreach bank, east of the island. From the west shore, the "far bank opposite her" is Hearthmere, and the mill fire would be behind her, across the wooded island. In ch 17 she sees figures running in the firelight. In ch 19 the lion stares across the river at the fire.
- Evidence: Ch 15 line 110: she hides the skiff "round into the reeds of the west channel", out of sight of anyone "coming across from the Saltreach bank". Rulings_41-79.md line 11: Redwatch is "half a league across the west channel". Tools/atlas/marches_items.py: Redwatch at x 174.0, the island at 175–176.5, the Eelweir at 176.6, Harrow mill at 177.7. Decisions_Ch61-70.md line 93: Harrow's End is seen "from the island's east shore".
- Fix: Turn both views east, over the island. In ch 17 line 277: "Not all of it. One place. Over the trees behind her, east, where the lights of Harrow's End had been: a glow on the underside of the sky, orange and growing." Cut the running figures, or let her climb the bluff to see them. In ch 19 line 91: "It looked east, over the trees of its own island, at the glow above the far bank." Line 29 can stay if it says she watched the sky over the trees. Then update both notes blocks.

### [B1-21-a] Rook "crosses the water" from the Eelweir, which is already on the farmer's bank
- Where: Book 1 ch 21, lines 136 and 148 (notes line 14)
- Quote: "The man's gone across the river." / "He heard it at the Eelweir this morning and was off across the water inside the hour"
- Problem: The Eelweir is Harrow's End's own landing, and the dragons are lying in a field above it. The hunters work from the Saltreach bank (ch 17 line 239; ch 21 line 294). A man who heard the news at the Eelweir has no water left to cross to reach the farmer. From the island, Maren's later "went across the river" (23 line 470, 25 line 223) is fine. The Captain's account is not.
- Evidence: Characters.md line 147 (the Eelweir is Harrow's End's fishing landing); ch 21 line 294 ("toward the Saltreach bank"); Characters.md line 187 ("crossed to the farmer once word of the bonds reached the far bank").
- Fix: In line 148: "He had it from the boy at the Eelweir this morning, when we put in for water, and was off up the hill toward that farm inside the hour." In line 136: "The man's gone after the farmer. You heard him."

### [B1-26-a] The ferry gossip knows what Maren said only to Rowan
- Where: Book 1 ch 26, lines 84 and 96
- Quote: "it tried to kill her. Three times. On the shore, and at a stream, and at the old tower all night" / "it'd have been like robbing it, to her way of thinking"
- Problem: Maren told the three attacks, the fire she didn't use and her reason ("it would have been stealing") only to Rowan, alone in the north tower. She told Pell a little and Hale "three goes", and Hale heard it the same evening Rue repeats it. Greyhold's own gossip has only "a monster nobody's seen". Weir's boy can't carry an itemised account and her private reason across the river within five days. The A1 C6 fix changed the words but kept the leak. This is also the echo-retelling pattern that A1 M9 asked to break with a distortion.
- Evidence: ch 23 lines 328 and 356–358; ch 25 lines 53 and 173.
- Fix: Give Rue a rumour that's partly wrong. In line 84: "He says it's a lion, if you can credit that, a lion the size of a hay wain, and it went for her a dozen times, and she never once struck it back. Had a sword, Weir's boy says, and never used it. Came home with nothing to show, so they failed her." In line 96: "Weir's boy says she could've cut a piece off it and wouldn't. Like robbing it, the ferry says." The ch 26 closing echo ("*Like robbing it,* Rue had said") still works.

### [B1-24-a] Sigrun is called "Ulric's champion", but Ulric is the champion
- Where: Book 1 ch 24, lines 44, 48 and 54 (notes line 13)
- Quote: "the champion's chair at Ulric's table at Skarrow, the one Sigrun Holm sat in" / "*Ulric's champion took Dag off your back, child. Sigrun took him.*"
- Problem: At Coldtarn, Ulric claims the pour as champion ("The champion pours"). Sigrun is the Clan's war-leader, and she goes east because "the champion was the Clan's sword". Giving the champion's title to Sigrun muddles the succession quarrel the war turns on.
- Evidence: ch 8 line 73; ch 16 line 202; Story.md line 55.
- Fix: In 44: "That's not a keeper, Fenn. That's a war-leader." In 48: "the war-leader's place at Ulric's table at Skarrow, the one Sigrun Holm sat in". In 54: "*Ulric's war-leader took Dag off your back, child.*" Make the same change in the notes ("the war-leader's place").

## Minor      (small slips: a wrong day, a name typo, a count off by one)

### [B1-16-a] Calder rides 28 leagues in an afternoon and a night (Check)
- Where: Book 1 ch 16, lines 48, 62, 88 and 174
- Quote: "He rode up the Drover's Way before noon" / "rode home down the Drover's Way in the dark"
- Problem: Varrhold is 14 leagues (about 42 miles) by the Way, "one day by horse". He leaves before noon, is there by dusk, and rides the same 14 leagues home that night on a rough Clan mare, then is at home all of day 6. The ride up is tight. The two together strain the Atlas.
- Evidence: Atlas.md lines 8 and 30.
- Fix: Let him sleep at the hold. In line 174: "…rode down the Drover's Way at first light, past the ford…". The split news on day 6 then reaches him on the road or at home that evening.

### [B1-17-b] The patrol boats come "upriver from Greyhold" instead of from the Redwatch boathouse
- Where: Book 1 ch 17, lines 23 and 45
- Quote: "Two patrol boats, long and grey, coming upriver from Greyhold under oars." / "went away downriver in the afternoon"
- Problem: The river watch's boats are kept at Redwatch, opposite the island, and the revision made every island trip start there.
- Evidence: Atlas.md line 174; DECISIONS.md item 3.
- Fix: In line 23: "coming out across the west channel from the Redwatch boathouse". In line 45: "went back across to Redwatch in the afternoon".

### [B1-17-c] The notes give Rook's hands, but the prose keeps them in his pockets
- Where: Book 1 ch 17, notes line 13, against prose lines 97 and 101
- Quote: notes: "soft hands with a pale scar across the knuckles"; prose: "with his hands in the pockets of a good dark coat" / "answered without taking his hands out of his pockets"
- Problem: The notes and the prose disagree. Maren never sees the hands, which is the better choice, since the boots are her only marker.
- Evidence: as quoted.
- Fix: In the notes, change it to "hands kept in his pockets; she sees only the boots".

### [B1-21-b] The Captain says "She" before anyone knows the intruder is a woman
- Where: Book 1 ch 21, line 132
- Quote: "Who cares. She's not here now."
- Problem: The hunters have seen "nothing of her in the night but a cut rope and an empty boat" (line 120). They first learn it's a woman when the guard shouts "it's a girl" (line 228).
- Evidence: ch 21 lines 120 and 228.
- Fix: "Who cares. They're not here now."

### [B1-21-c] The raft goes all the way round the island to reach the other side of the spit
- Where: Book 1 ch 21, line 184 (against lines 92, 104 and 124)
- Quote: "go away up the east side of the island toward the north point. Going round. Going to bring the raft round to this side"
- Problem: The raft is already beached on the far side of the spit, a few lengths from the lion. To reach the cage-loading side, it only has to round the tip. It also came down the Hearthmere side in broad daylight, while the Captain says she fears "eyes on that wall".
- Evidence: ch 21 lines 92, 104 and 128.
- Fix: In line 184: "…push off, with poles and a lantern, out into the current and away across toward the Saltreach bank, for the windlass and the rest of the gear, and come back for it."

### [B1-22-a] Calder is carried in "the evening before", but the pyres burned all night

- Quote: "They'd carried him in at last the evening before, after the fires had burned down"
- Problem: In ch 20 the riders' fires are lit at dusk on day 9 and "burned all night", and he sits watching them with Wren in his arms.
- Evidence: ch 20 lines 378 and 412.
- Fix: "They'd carried him in at last near dawn, after the fires had burned down".

### [B1-22-b] The elders are counted as five, and then Wat goes "with them"

### [B1-22-d] "Any horse he'd ever owned. Bigger than Sorrel": Sorrel is Joss's dray-mare

## Craft      (at most 8)

### [B1-21-d] The overheard plan is an exposition dump
- Where: Book 1 ch 21, lines 120–152
- Quote: "They thought they were alone."
- Problem: The hunters lay out the loading plan, the buyer, the dragons, the bonding and the price in one tidy exchange, while Maren happens to be within earshot. Each line answers a question the reader has. It's the only place in the range where the echo feels staged.
- Fix: Cut the plan lines (122–128) to one order from the Captain, and keep the dragon talk as the real overheard surprise. The raft's movements can show the plan.

- Where: Book 1 ch 20, line 302
- Quote: "They could hear what he said to them, and feel what he felt. They couldn't hear everything he thought."
- Problem: Line 300 has already shown it ("she didn't know… Not unless he gave her them"). The second line reads like a note from the Bible.
- Fix: Cut line 302.

### [B1-25-a] Flat closing lines in 25 and 26
- Where: Book 1 ch 25, line 253; ch 26, line 272
- Quote: "She went in to her supper, and found she wasn't hungry." / "It stayed with him."

- Fix: In 26, end on line 270 (*Like robbing it,* Rue had said.). In 25, end on line 251 ("A farmer, in a burned field, among three dragons.").

### [B1-17-d] The "the way" simile frame is still common
- Where: about 29 in the range. For example: ch 17 lines 61 ("the way you get used to rain"), 129, 171, 225; ch 19 lines 49, 105, 119, 205; ch 18 lines 159, 163.
- Problem: The style rule says use "as" or no comparison. The action chapters (17, 19) carry most of these.
- Fix: Turn them into "as", or cut the comparison, starting with the action passages in 17 and 19.
