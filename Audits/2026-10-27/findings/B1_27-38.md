# Findings: Book 1, chapters 27–38
Reviewer summary: The range is in good shape on the settled rules. Maren and Calder don't cast, nothing gives away a reveal, the earlier audit's fixes are in (Redwatch trips, "two nights ago", eleven days, Tasker, Dun, Hale at twenty-three, "like robbing it"), and the cross-POV days for Ferriby and the Tidesteps (49), Gallows Quay (50), the carter (61) and the homecoming (63) agree. The main problems are timing and logistics. Ch 32's own day count gets Calder to Wendmouth two days late. Tam watches a fire he couldn't have reached in time. In ch 38 Aurveth sees a milestone she has no way to see. And the Callis seal-rider plant in ch 33 doesn't fit the ride it implies. The patrol at Ferriby is also one short of the "eight" that three chapters count.

## Critical
None found.

## Major

### [B1-32-a] Ch 32's internal day count gets Calder to Wendmouth on day 51, not 49
- Where: Book 1 ch 32, lines 26, 28, 58, 144, 184, 200, 288 (against ch 30 lines 35, 341–367)
- Quote: 32:26 "He spent one night at home after the burning."; 32:58 "They found Crow Scar the morning after he went back."; 32:144 "on the third morning"; 32:184 "It was night. The third night."; 32:200 "They went the next night, in the rain."
- Problem: The burning takes all of night 42. Calder wakes "in the middle of the night" (30:35) and walks home at sunrise (30:341, 367). So his one night at home, with supper, the hearth-words and his own bed, is night 43, and he goes back to the Scar on day 44. The prose then gives this sequence: found the morning after (45), parley on the third morning (47), the Chimney found that night (47), the escape the next night (48), and three nights' flying (48–50). That puts him in Wendmouth on day 51. Ch 34 needs day 49, because the Tidesteps falls on the same day as Ferriby, which is the twin clue the book is built on. Ch 35 also needs day 49 (Hale on day 52: Vey's men in "nine days ago", lost him "six nights ago", i.e. 43 and 46). The 32 notes and the decisions table say he went back on day 43 and was found that morning, but the prose doesn't allow that.
- Evidence: ch 34:10 (notes, day 49); ch 35:67; ch 38:263 ("Twelve days back", counted from day 61); Book 1/Notes/Decisions_Ch31-40.md:10–14.
- Fix: Shorten the siege by a day and leave the night at home where it is. 32:58 becomes "They found Crow Scar the morning he went back." 32:144 becomes "on the second morning". 32:184 becomes "It was night. The second night." The sequence is then: back and found day 44, parley and the Chimney day and night 45, escape night 46, flights 46–48, Wendmouth day 49. "In three days, they never once attacked" (32:76) still holds for 44–46. Update the 32 notes and Decisions_Ch31-40.md:10 and 12 to "night 43 at home; back and found day 44". Hale's "nine days ago" (35:67) can stay, since Vey's men were in the wood from day 43.

### [B1-31-a] Tam couldn't have walked from Millbrook in time to see the dragons arrive
- Where: Book 1 ch 31, line 138 (and 150)
- Quote: "A lot of us went up. From Millbrook. When we saw the glow. We went up the River Road in the dark, on foot"; 150: "We watched him do it. From behind the knights."
- Problem: Millbrook is two leagues below Greyhold. The bank opposite Harrow's End is about four leagues above it, so the Millbrook men walk about six leagues, roughly eighteen miles, after the glow goes up. That is five or six hours on foot at night. In ch 30 the dragons arrive within about an hour of the fire starting, and the roof rescue comes early, so Tam would have arrived near dawn. He says he watched "all night", heard the children, and saw "he got the children off the roof".
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:8, 28–31, 84; ch 30:35, 57–59, 127–137, 313.
- Fix: Have the Millbrook men already upriver that evening. At 31:138: "A lot of us were up at Redwatch that night. Odo had the tower's boat-irons to deliver, and he took twenty of us to carry and stay for the ale. When the glow went up we went along the bank with the tower men, to the water opposite Harrow's End. To see." The rest of the speech stands.

### [B1-38-a] Aurveth points out a milestone she can't see
- Where: Book 1 ch 38, lines 75–77
- Quote: "A league off in the forest, lying up in the dark under the oaks, she'd lifted her head. *Stop,* she said. *There. By the road. On your left. The stone.*"
- Problem: Aurveth is a league away under the trees. Under the settled bond scope, sight is not shared unless it is pushed on purpose, and then the bonded partner sees what the giver sees, not the other way round. She has no way to see a mossed stone on Calder's left. Calder can't read the old letters and took the stone for a gatepost, so nothing he feels could tell her about it either.
- Evidence: Bible/World.md:23 ("A live sight can be pushed to a bonded partner the same way, on purpose…; otherwise sight isn't shared").
- Fix: Let her feel the place rather than see it. Lines 75–77 become: "A league off in the forest, lying up in the dark under the oaks, she'd lifted her head. *Stop,* she said. *Here. I know this place. There is something by the road. Look.* He looked, and found it on his left: a stone." Then go on from "He stopped."

### [B1-33-a] The Callis plant doesn't fit the ride it implies, and sits in the wrong place
- Where: Book 1 ch 33, line 59 (against lines 47, 53, 57, 211, 215)
- Quote: 59: "Callis had gone off along the bank to water his hound an hour before dusk, and come back when the lamps were lit."; 211: "Came along the bank at dusk on a grey horse. Stopped at every post."
- Problem: Between Redwatch and Ferriby the patrol rides "inland, away from the river" (47), and Ferriby is a league from the Wend (53), so "along the bank" can't be right. The seal-rider stopped "at every post" from Redwatch to Millbrook (207, 211), which is about five leagues of bank. Callis couldn't do that in an hour and be back at Ferriby a league inland. Canon makes Callis the man who cleared the river (Characters.md:124; ch 71:165), so the plant needs to say the job was shared or limited to Redwatch. The line also breaks into the moment between "Pell nodded and turned his bay to go" and "Bracken lifted his head" with an out-of-sequence flashback.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:249 (Ferriby a league inland from Redwatch); Bible/Characters.md:124; ch 71:165–171.
- Fix: Move the line up into the ride (after 47) and make it fit: "On the road out from Redwatch, Callis had turned back to the tower for a glove he said he'd dropped, and come up with them again at Ferriby as the lamps were lit." At 211, limit the rider to Redwatch and have the rest come from elsewhere: "Came to the tower at dusk on a grey horse, hood up. Showed a paper. Greyhold's seal on it… And word down the bank is the other posts had the same paper an hour before, from a different man." This also sets up ch 71's "the friend with the seal" as a network.

## Minor

### [B1-33-b] The Ferriby patrol is seven, not eight
- Where: Book 1 ch 33, lines 33 and 151 (and ch 38 line 247, ch 39 line 31)
- Quote: 33: "There were eight of them." The list that follows is Pell, Callis, Wynn, Haverly, Ide Darrow, Tobin, Maren.
- Problem: Seven people are named, and no one else appears in the fight or the ride home. The carter (38:247, "Eight of them") and ch 39 ("the same eight as Ferriby") repeat the count.
- Evidence: ch 33:33–35, 89–93, 187–189.
- Fix: Either make it "seven" in 33:33, 33:151, 38:247 and 39:31, or add one unnamed member at 33:33: "and a first-year called Brice with the lanterns, who'd never been further than Millbrook."

### [B1-31-b] "Six weeks" by the stream
- Where: Book 1 ch 31, line 158
- Quote: "she'd been sitting in the mud by a stream for six weeks being very careful"
- Problem: Her first sitting is day 12 (ch 23) and this is day 44, so about four and a half weeks. Ch 29:162 says "three weeks" for the period up to day 34, which agrees with that.
- Fix: "for a month and more".

### [B1-34-a] Dawn and "sun well up" at the same moment
- Where: Book 1 ch 34, lines 29 and 37
- Quote: 29: "He saw it first from the last hill above the coast, with the sun well up."; 37: "people moving about on them in the dawn."
- Problem: It's the same view a few lines apart. The notes put him in the city by late morning after five leagues on foot.
- Fix: At 37, "in the morning light".

### [B1-38-b] Wren went out to Lisska in the morning, not "on the night he left"
- Where: Book 1 ch 38, line 415 (against ch 32 lines 28–42)
- Quote: "the way she'd gone across the yard at home to Lisska on the night he left"
- Problem: In ch 32 Calder wakes at dawn, and Wren comes down the stairs and goes out to Lisska before he leaves that morning.
- Fix: "on the morning he left".

### [B1-37-a] The cat "had known", but it only turns up afterwards
- Where: Book 1 ch 37, lines 55–59 (against line 37)
- Quote: "It had picked. It had known."
- Problem: Grieve's rule (27:113) is that the cat sleeps with the beast whose rider will have a bad day *tomorrow*. Here it shows up in Dun's stall only on the evening after both deaths. The morning scene (37:37) doesn't put it there beforehand, so "It had known" has nothing to rest on.
- Fix: Plant it at 37:37: "Against the old ox's belly, in the straw, lay the warming-stone… and, curled beside it, the cat nobody owned." Then at 55, "was the cat nobody owned, still" keeps the evening image.

### [B1-36-a] Calder puts words in the Crane drinkers' mouths
- Where: Book 1 ch 36, line 194 (against ch 34 line 251)
- Quote: 36: "the barges on the Wend that the beast-sellers in the Crane had grumbled about, going up the river at night with their cargo"; 34: "Over the water on a barge by night and nobody the wiser."
- Problem: In 34 the barge talk is about getting beasts *across* the river out of the Marches, not shipping them north again. His deduction in 36:196 is built on a remark nobody made.
- Fix: At 194, "He thought of barges on the Wend, going up the river at night with their cargo." Then 196 is his own inference.

### [B1-32-b] Check: Sigrun "fought" Vey's men when her own side hired them
- Where: Book 1 ch 32, line 70
- Quote: "Sigrun fought them once, years ago, on the coast road. Ulric hired them for a summer."
- Problem: Sigrun was Ulric's sword (Story.md:60). If Ulric hired the company, "fought them" reads as fighting against her own side's hire. The rest of the line ("fought exactly as hard as they were paid") sounds as if she fought beside them.
- Fix: "Sigrun fought beside them once, years ago, on the coast road, when Ulric hired them for a summer."

### [B1-29-a] Check: poling with an arm in a sling
- Where: Book 1 ch 29, lines 30 and 274 (and ch 35 line 191)
- Quote: 29:30 "when she poled across in Ottery's punt"; 29:274 "He handed her the pole".
- Problem: On days 27–28 her left arm is still in its sling (27:249; 28:181), and it is never said to come off. A day later she is poling a punt, and by 29:274 Ottery hands her the pole out of habit.
- Fix: One clause in 29 near line 30: "The sling had come off at last, and the arm was stiff but it worked."

### [B1-30-a] Check: Lisska "five leagues out over the Reedmarsh"
- Where: Book 1 ch 30, lines 35 and 43
- Quote: "Five leagues, she said afterwards, down over the Reedmarsh"
- Problem: The Atlas puts the Reedmarsh "on the Saltreach bank below Cotter's Cross". Ch 38's walk puts Cotter's Cross about two days, or some twenty leagues, below Harrow's End (38:233, 287). That marsh is much further than five leagues off.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:51, 183.
- Fix: Keep "five leagues" and drop the name: "down over the reed beds below Upper Rush". Or keep the Reedmarsh and make it "Twenty leagues… too far for her voice, too far even to see much but the glow".

### [B1-35-a] Check: how Ottery knew she was coming
- Where: Book 1 ch 35, lines 185–191
- Quote: "Your mother said you'd come," he said. "She said you had a face on you at supper."
- Problem: Maren had supper and went to her dormitory cot at Greyhold. Liss is at the ferry house by Greyhold landing, and Ottery is three leagues upriver at Redwatch. Maren walks there that same night, so there is no time for word to reach him first. It is also not shown that Liss saw her at supper.
- Fix: At 185, "and been to supper at the ferry house"; at 191, "Your mother sent word up with the flour boat. She said you had a face on you."

### [B1-32-c] "Everything he had left" and then four coppers
- Where: Book 1 ch 32, line 204 (against ch 34 line 25)
- Quote: "Everything he had left from the barley money"; 34: "with four coppers in his purse".
- Fix: "Everything but a few coppers from the barley money".

## Craft

### [B1-34-b] "It wasn't X. It was Y." twice
- Where: ch 34 line 33 ("a very big river with no far bank. It wasn't. It was nothing like a river."); ch 36 line 248 ("it wasn't that. It was something wearing its clothes.")
- Fix: 34: "…as a very big river with no far bank. It was nothing like a river." 36: "But whatever was happening in that little stone room… was something wearing that one's clothes."

### [B1-31-c] The same opening move two chapters apart
- Where: ch 29 lines 30–32 ("That was the reason she gave herself. It wasn't all of it."); ch 31 lines 24–28 ("That was the reason… It wasn't all of the reason.")
- Problem: Maren's chapters open twice in three with the stated reason and then its denial.
- Fix: Cut 31:28's first sentence. Start the paragraph at "She hadn't seen Tam since the fair."

### [B1-32-d] The Kharrak quarrel runs on one template
- Where: ch 28 lines 87–145; ch 32 lines 100–140, 318–332
- Problem: Each time it goes the same way. Kharrak wants to burn, Calder refuses out loud, Aurveth backs Calder, and Kharrak ends with "I hate you… For that" / "I hate both of you… I hate him more". Aurveth says "He's right" twice in ch 32 (126, 328), and Lisska even remarks on it ("Why do you always say that?"). The second time, the quarrel reads as a rerun rather than a step.
- Fix: In 32, let Kharrak give way for a new reason, or say nothing at all (he does that well in 38), and cut his "I hate both of you" exchange (134–140). Keep one of Aurveth's two "He's right" lines.

### [B1-35-b] The lion's lip goes back at every visit
- Where: ch 27:217, 219; ch 29:42, 150, 294–298; ch 35:207–213; ch 37:251
- Problem: Each visit replays the same three beats: its lip goes back, she doesn't move, the lip comes down. The progress across the visits is real (fifteen paces, then lying down, then the arch), but the identical choreography blurs it.
- Fix: Drop the lip in 35 and 37. In 35 have it look only at her empty hands. In 37 let the beat be just "It saw her. Its head turned." (37:247), then "It came down."
