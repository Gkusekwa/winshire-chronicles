# Findings: Book 1, chapters 39–52
Reviewer summary: This range is in good shape. Geography matches the Atlas and Rulings 7 and 18 (Redwatch, Upper Rush, the Ewe Beck camp, Greyhold's distances), the day counts agree across the POV switches, and none of the settled rules is broken (no casting by Maren or Calder, no reveal leaks, no "Book N" in the prose). The biggest problem is the Restoration's paper. Ch 45 says outright that the dragon farmer is not on it, and chs 46–49 all quote him from it. There is also a one-day slip in when Ada sings the verse, and Maren's "first lie" comes two days after she has already lied to Ottery. Everything else is small.

## Critical
None found.

## Major

### [B1-45-a] Is the farmer on the paper or not?
- Where: Book 1 ch 45, lines 57 and 153 (and ch 46 lines 49–51 and 199; ch 47 line 202; ch 48 line 51; ch 49 line 200; ch 50 line 374)
- Quote: ch 45:153 "There's more," he said. "Not on the paper. Something I've seen."; ch 45:57 "At the bottom, two words. *One again.*"
- Problem: In ch 45 the paper ends with "One again", and Reede adds the farmer and the island beast as his own spoken words. Every later chapter treats the farmer as printed on the paper. Calder reads it there in 46 ("at the bottom, under the two words… a smaller part, that Fisk had said had him in it"). Ada, Liss, Calder and Hale all say "they've put his name on their paper". Ch 45's own notes (line 18) list the farmer among the paper's claims.
- Evidence: ch 46:49–51; ch 47:202 "They've put his name on their paper."; ch 48:51; ch 49:200 "the Restoration's printed him at the bottom of their paper"; ch 45:18 (notes).
- Fix: Make ch 45 agree with the other five chapters and its own notes. At line 57, change it to: "At the bottom, a smaller part, and under it two words. *One again.*" Give Maren a line on the smaller part after line 55, for example "*The old powers are waking. In the Marches, a farmer, one of the unchosen, has been chosen by three dragons in one night.*" At line 153, change it to: "There's more," he said. "At the bottom. Some of you will have heard of it." Also note that the paper says "a farmer", not his name. So Ada's "put your name" (46:199) and Liss's "put his name" (47:202) can stay as people talking loosely, or become "put you on it".

### [B1-46-a] Ada sings the verse on night 89, but the lights go out on night 90
- Where: Book 1 ch 46, lines 31, 57, 65, 85 (and ch 47 line 24)
- Quote: ch 46:31 "Old Fisk brought the paper up the river lane on the eighty-ninth day"; 46:57 "By dark, all of Harrow's End had heard it."; 46:65 "'Calder,' said Bel, at supper."
- Problem: As written, the paper, the supper and the verse all fall on day 89. The lights at Greyhold go out "just when she stopped singing" (46:231). Maren's chapter puts that blackout "an hour before midnight on the ninetieth night, two nights after the midwinter oath" (47:24). Ch 46's own notes, ch 44's notes ("two nights before they fail"), ch 41's notes ("goes cold after night 90") and A2's "magic fails from night 90" all agree on night 90.
- Evidence: ch 46:12 (notes: "Ada sings it… on the night of day 90"); ch 47:24; ch 47:9 (notes); ch 44:18 (notes).
- Fix: Move the singing to the next night in ch 46. At line 57, change it to: "By the next dark, all of Harrow's End had heard it." The supper at line 65 then falls on night 90.

### [B1-43-a] Maren's "first lie" comes after she has already lied to Ottery
- Where: Book 1 ch 43, line 214 (against ch 41 lines 209 and 255)
- Quote: ch 43:214 "She'd never lied to him. Not once in two years. She'd never lied to the orders in her life."
- Problem: Two days earlier, in ch 41, she tells Ottery, the river-watch sergeant, "The current threw us up", and she tells the Haddow boy "It was the current". Ottery knows it is a lie, and ch 51:69 brings it back ("You'll not tell me it was the current"). A careful reader will catch "never lied to the orders in her life" straight after that. The plan's beat ("her first lie to the orders") only works if the earlier lie is acknowledged.
- Evidence: ch 41:209, 41:255; ch 51:69; Book 1/Chapter_Plan.md row 43.
- Fix: Change 43:214 to: "She'd never lied to him. Not once in two years. A lie to Ottery on a frozen shore was one thing. This was the orders."

## Minor

### [B1-40-a] Check: Lisska's view of the island
- Where: Book 1 ch 40, lines 44 and 64 (against ch 40 lines 298 and 312, ch 42 line 144, ch 39 lines 159–197)
- Quote: ch 40:44 "Can we go over the island? On the way? Just to see? Just once?"; ch 42:144 "She'd watched it, all those nights in the autumn, flying high over the island at dusk."
- Problem: In ch 40 Lisska begs to see the island "just once", and only then remembers Imra's landing. But she has flown over it all autumn (24, 28, 42:144). Later in the same chapter she also describes the lion's first crossing, which happened on night 64 (ch 39). That was before the "just once" trip, and line 298 says she watched only "high over the Saltreach bank". She can't both rarely go near the island and have seen the crossing.
- Evidence: ch 39:159–197 (the crossing on night 64); ch 40:298, 40:312.
- Fix: At 40:44, change it to: "Can we go down low over the island? On the way? Low, just once?" At 40:64, change it to: "I went low. Lower than I've ever been. And I remembered something." Low flight explains the memory coming back, and her earlier high passes cover the night-64 sighting.

### [B1-40-b] Aurveth's age
- Where: Book 1 ch 40, line 220
- Quote: "not even Aurveth, who was three hundred years old"
- Problem: Canon gives her age as "more than three hundred" (Ruling 3), and ch 42:230 uses that wording. Here the flat "three hundred" sits too close to the chains' "three hundred years" (45:47, 49:124), and Ruling 3 keeps the two numbers apart.
- Evidence: Bible/Characters.md:96; Book 1/Notes/Rulings_41-79.md:7 (Ruling 3); ch 42:230.
- Fix: Change it to "who was more than three hundred years old".

### [B1-45-b] Check: the crown's answer arrives too fast
- Where: Book 1 ch 45, lines 225 and 239
- Quote: 45:225 "From Ashford, eighteen leagues off, on the third lathered horse he'd had since morning"; 45:239 "the crown had its answer written by noon."
- Problem: The paper went up in Ashford before dawn. The herald has been riding "since morning", yet Maren concludes the answer was "written by noon", and it is issued "in council with the orders". The council sits at Hallowmere, about 27 leagues beyond Ashford (Atlas: Greyhold–Hallowmere 46, Greyhold–Ashford 19). Either the answer was drafted before the paper appeared, which is a sinister hint nobody picks up, or the timing doesn't work.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md lines 32–33.
- Fix: Make the hint deliberate. At 239, change it to: "So the paper had been on doors in Ashford too, before dawn, and the crown's answer had been on the road by morning. As if somebody had had it written already." Or drop "by noon".

### [B1-45-c] Check: a spoken charm works on the ice after two chapters say it can't
- Where: Book 1 ch 45, line 215
- Quote: "Wynn knelt on the ice and said the setting-word over it… and the bone took it."
- Problem: Chs 42:66 (Fisk: "Nothing said holds on it") and 43:24 (Ottery: the dusk-word "slid off" on water) set up river lore that words don't hold on the ice. Running water breaking a spoken charm is canon. Millbrook's back channel is "still behind the weir", so this may be allowed, but the reader has just been taught the opposite.
- Evidence: Bible/Magic.md:250; ch 42:66; ch 43:24.
- Fix: Change it to: "The knights got Callis up the bank. His collarbone was broken… Wynn knelt by him on the frozen grass and said the setting-word over it". This also keeps the notes' magic touch.

### [B1-46-b] Fisk's paper is in two places at once
- Where: Book 1 ch 46, lines 37, 59 and 157 (and ch 48 line 25)
- Quote: 46:59 "Fisk, who'd gone back down to the Eelweir with his paper and read it out"; 46:157 "said the paper on the table, in Fisk's careful reading"
- Problem: Fisk hands his copy to Calder (46:37 "Calder took it"), and it is still on Bel's dresser in 48:25. Yet he takes "his paper" back to the Eelweir. Also, Calder read it himself ("Fisk… didn't help", 46:39), so nobody gave him "Fisk's careful reading".
- Evidence: ch 46:37–39; ch 48:25 "with Fisk's paper still lying on the dresser".
- Fix: At 59, change it to "who'd gone back down to the Eelweir with another copy". At 157, change it to "in his own slow reading".

### [B1-46-c] Calder knows what the knights face at noon
- Where: Book 1 ch 46, line 163
- Quote: "Every day. At noon. Facing east, toward the river, toward the old wall."
- Problem: Calder only knows that the knights swear "facing the river" (46:153). The "old wall" (the inner wall the knights face) is something Maren learns in 45 and 49. Calder has no way to know it.
- Evidence: ch 46:153; ch 45:77; ch 49:28, 49:116.
- Fix: Cut "toward the old wall." The sentence then reads: "Facing east, toward the river. Where the oath is kept."

## Craft

### [B1-43-c] Four chapters end on something "still warm"
- Where: ch 43:228 (the scale, "like something that wasn't going to go out"); ch 45:281 ("still warm"); ch 48:315 ("It was still warm."); ch 51:273 ("in the morning it was still warm.")
- Problem: Within nine chapters, four of them close on the same quiet beat: a warm object, held or remembered. The fourth one, the most important, is weakened by the first three.
- Fix: Keep 51. End 45 on Kesh and the upriver dark (cut line 281). End 43 at "She went in and picked up the fork." (cut lines 227–228). End 48 on line 317.

### [B1-39-c] Rowan is "small and neat" every time
- Where: ch 39:259, 265, 271; ch 43:206; ch 45:65, 67; ch 47:128
- Problem: She is tagged this way seven times in four chapters, three of them in ch 39 alone. It has become a tic rather than a description.
- Fix: Keep one use per chapter at most, and none in 39:265 and 39:271 (use "her hands" and "her little book").

### [B1-47-c] The humming ground is always a "struck bowl"
- Where: ch 45:243 "like a struck bowl"; ch 47:44 "A bowl of bronze, struck once"; ch 51:99 "Like a bowl that was struck long ago" (also used in ch 37)
- Problem: The same image comes back in every Maren chapter for the same sensation, so it has stopped telling the reader anything new.
- Fix: Keep 47:44, the first full use in this range. In 45:243 and 51:99, describe the sensation plainly ("the ground humming under her") or give it a fresh, physical detail.

### [B1-50-c] Calder's big moment of speaking is summarised
- Where: ch 50, lines 258–268 ("He told her about the camps… He told her about the island…")
- Problem: The chapter's beat is "the first thing he has ever chosen to say", yet the scene runs entirely as reported speech. The turn of his whole arc reads as a summary.
- Fix: Keep the summary, but give one passage as direct speech, the part he has avoided longest. For example: "'They've nobody in their eyes, Bel. I've seen it. Old Grey won't have known him.'" Then go back to "It took a long time."

### [B1-46-d] Ch 46 spells out what the reader has already worked out
- Where: ch 46, lines 141–165
- Problem: The reader has heard all three "do not ask"s within the last two chapters. Calder lists them again, then explains the conclusion twice (163, 165). The verse already made the point.
- Fix: Cut 145–149 (the three recaps) and 163. Keep 141, 151 and 165.
