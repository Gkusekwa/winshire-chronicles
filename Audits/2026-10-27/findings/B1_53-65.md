# Findings: Book 1, chapters 53–65
Reviewer summary: The range is in good shape on the settled rules. Neither Maren nor Calder casts, no title gives anything away, no forbidden names appear, and the moon phases (dark 102, full 117, about six past full on 123) and most cross-POV hand-offs line up. The real problems are a few continuity breaks between the two POVs: when Maren touches the lion (55 against 56), how far the Restoration has advanced by day 126 (58 against 62 and 65), a kill tally that doesn't add up (54), and one bond-rule slip, Wren getting Lisska's reports directly (56). Most of the rest are small day and distance slips.

## Critical   (breaks the story, canon or a settled rule)

None found.

## Major      (a continuity or logic error a careful reader would notice)

### [B1-56-a] Lisska reports the touch at dawn as if it were new, but Maren touched the lion in the night
- Where: Book 1 ch 56, lines 249–257 (against ch 55, lines 196–226 and 234)
- Quote: 56: "*She's put her hand on it.* He was awake." / "She put her hand out and it let her."; 55: "She'd slept with it there."
- Problem: In 55 the touch happens during the night, by lantern light, before Maren falls asleep. She wakes at dawn with her hand still in the mane and takes it away. In 56, Lisska watches all night (she reports the lantern, five paces, and the two lions) but says nothing about the touch until dawn, and then describes it as happening right then. She would have seen it hours earlier. The 56 notes block (line 13) also puts the touch during the night.
- Evidence: ch 55:220 ("the lantern went out" comes after the touch); ch 55:234; ch 56 notes line 13.
- Fix: in 56, move the reveal into the night section after "*I can see its breath.*" (line 149), e.g. "*She's put her hand out. On its mane. It's letting her.*" At dawn, line 253 becomes: "*Calder, look. She's still got her hand on it. All night. And it's still letting her.*"

### [B1-56-b] Wren is promised Lisska's reports directly, but only bonded humans hear a dragon
- Where: Book 1 ch 56, lines 373–385
- Quote: "\"And she'll tell me about the lion-woman?\" ... *Yes,* said Lisska. *Every night. Everything I see.* A pause. *She'll make me say it twice.*"
- Problem: Calder is moving up to the Scar for good, so he can't pass anything on, yet the exchange has Lisska talking to Wren herself. Canon allows mind-speech only with the bonded partner (or with kin). Ch 64 gets it right: "he tells me what they say".
- Evidence: Bible/Bestiary.md:44 ("They speak mind to mind with a bonded partner and to no one else"); Rulings_41-79.md 5 and 13 ("Humans hear only their own bonded"); ch 64:112. The notes block (line 18) has Lisska tell *him*, not Wren.
- Fix: "\"And she'll watch the lion-woman for me?\" ... *Yes,* said Lisska. *Every night. And I'll tell you, and you'll tell her.* A pause. *She'll make you say it twice.*" Then line 385: "\"Yes,\" he said to Wren. \"Everything. I'll send it down with Lisska's eyes and bring it myself when I can. Twice.\"" Or drop the promise and keep only "she'd see Lisska on the crag every night."

### [B1-58-a] On day 126 the tents are already "over the hills behind Harrow's End", but the march only starts on day 133
- Where: Book 1 ch 58, line 266
- Quote: "You could see them strung down the Way, dark on the brown, through the Fellwood's edge and over the hills behind Harrow's End. Much nearer the river than before."
- Problem: In 62, on day 133, the Ram's Head tents only "began to come down". The first company is "Far. The other side of the moor", and on night 134 it is camped "at the foot of" the moor. In 65 the campfires first appear on the hill behind Harrow's End at dusk on day 139. If tents were already over the hills behind Harrow's End on day 126, the whole build-up in 62 (and Harrow's End packing at leisure) makes no sense.
- Evidence: ch 62:153–161, 221; ch 63:296 (column still on the Way on day 136); ch 65:291–293; ch 66:25.
- Fix: "The tents had come further. A new camp below the Ram's Head, astride the Way, and fires at the Fellwood's edge. Nearer the river than before."

### [B1-54-a] "Eleven" dead, but the tally given lists eight
- Where: Book 1 ch 54, lines 94–98 (repeated at 380; ch 56:241 "the eleven men in the snow")
- Quote: "Eleven of them lay on the hillside below Bel's farm." then: the dog-man; "The horsemen, five of them"; "Two crossbowmen"
- Problem: 1 + 5 + 2 = 8 men. The dogs are counted separately ("Eight of them"). The number comes back as "eleven men" twice, so a reader who adds it up will notice.
- Evidence: ch 54:96, 98, 380; ch 56:241; notes line 11 ("Eleven dead").
- Fix: add the missing three at line 96: "Three spearmen who'd come through the gap behind the dogs and met Kharrak there." (Keeps "eleven" everywhere.)

### [B1-64-a] Calder has "never stood this close" to Greyhold, from a jetty he's stood on a hundred times
- Where: Book 1 ch 64, lines 68 and 74
- Quote: "He'd been there a hundred times. Every autumn of his life, with the barley" / "He'd never stood this close to it. It was right there, across a river a long bowshot wide."
- Problem: Tolly's Staithe is the Saltreach end of the Greyhold ferry, directly across from the school. Six lines apart, the chapter says he has been there all his life and that he has never been this close.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:177 (ferry between Greyhold landing and the Staithe); Rulings 18.
- Fix: line 74: "He'd stood here every autumn of his life and never looked at it. He looked now. It was right there, across a river a long bowshot wide."

### [B1-59-a] Rowan has "known since the night of the lamps" that Maren knows, but Hale only told her six nights later
- Where: Book 1 ch 59, line 80
- Quote: "So I know. And I know you know. I've known since the night of the lamps."
- Problem: The lamps failed on night 90 (ch 47). Hale told Maren what is below on night 96 (ch 49). On the night of the lamps Maren didn't know yet. Rowan's own next clause, "for a month", points to day 96 or later.
- Evidence: ch 47:20 ("the ninetieth night"); ch 49 notes line 11 (night 96, "He tells her").
- Fix: "I've known since the night the lion roared through the stone." (night 102, ch 51), or "since a week after the lamps."

## Minor      (small slips: a wrong day, a name typo, a count off by one)

### [B1-53-a] Rowan's room "for the first time since the trial", but Maren reported there in ch 23
- Where: Book 1 ch 53, line 55
- Quote: "It was the first time since the trial."
- Problem: Maren went up to Rowan's room in ch 23 to report the lion ("It's a lion, Captain").
- Evidence: ch 23:260, 286.
- Fix: "It was the first time since the autumn, since she'd stood there and said *It's a lion, Captain.*"

### [B1-53-b] The roar was three nights before, not two
- Where: Book 1 ch 53, line 331
- Quote: "Where three dragons had answered a roar across the ice two nights ago."
- Problem: The roar was night 102 and this is night 105. (The Ewe Beck fire at line 335, night 103, is correctly "two nights ago".)
- Evidence: ch 52:28; ch 51 notes line 16; ch 53:301.
- Fix: "three nights ago".

### [B1-53-c] Lion prints "in the snow at midwinter" were found about a week before midwinter
- Where: Book 1 ch 53, line 115
- Quote: "In the mud by the stream in the autumn, and in the snow at midwinter."
- Problem: The snow prints are from ch 43 (days 81–83). Midwinter is about day 88. The 41–60 pass fixed the same loose cue in 55 ("prints at midwinter… wrong time cue").
- Evidence: ch 43 notes line 10; APPLIED_41-60.md:102; Rulings 37.
- Fix: "and in the snow at the turn of the year."

### [B1-53-d] Notes vs prose: where the Ewe Beck fire is
- Where: Book 1 ch 53, notes line 11 against prose line 37
- Quote: notes: "straight across the river on the Saltreach bank, three leagues off"; prose: "East … upriver of Tolly's Staithe, below the long bend at Harrow's End. Two leagues, perhaps."
- Problem: The prose was corrected (A2 m13) and the notes weren't. The Staithe is directly across from Greyhold, so a fire upriver of it isn't "straight across".
- Evidence: APPLIED_41-60.md:26.
- Fix: update the notes to match the prose.

### [B1-54-b] The Ram's Head is "half a day's walk" from Harrow's End; the Atlas makes it a full day
- Where: Book 1 ch 54, lines 228 and 238
- Quote: "Half a day's walk from Harrow's End." / "toward the Ram's Head, a morning's walk off"
- Problem: The Ram's Head is seven leagues from Harrow's End (about 21 miles). The Atlas gives 14 leagues as 2 days on foot, so 7 is a day. Ch 54 itself remembers Calder overnighting there with the grain cart. Hallam's "half a day's ride" (252) is fine.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:254, 30.
- Fix: "A day's walk from Harrow's End." / "a long day's walk off".

### [B1-54-c] Notes vs prose: when the Skarrow steading was burned
- Where: Book 1 ch 54, notes line 15 against prose line 252
- Quote: notes: "two nights ago"; prose: "three nights ago"
- Fix: align the notes to "three nights ago".

### [B1-55-a] Maren "known the name since the turn of the winter", from Liss; she heard it on the ferry on day 44
- Where: Book 1 ch 55, line 348
- Quote: "She'd known the name since the turn of the winter. Liss had told her, at supper."
- Problem: Maren first hears "Calder Fenn" on Liss's ferry in ch 31 (day 44, autumn), from a refugee. Liss's supper talk in ch 43 was about the price, not the name.
- Evidence: ch 31:256–258; ch 43:144.
- Fix: "She'd known the name since the autumn, from a man on her mother's ferry."

### [B1-56-c] Lisska sees the lion's breath across two leagues and more, at night, in rain
- Where: Book 1 ch 56, line 149 (and 141–147)
- Quote: "It's five paces off now. Lying across the front of the arch. I can see its breath."
- Problem: The chapter's own rule is that from the crag she "can't see faces from there in the dark, but she can see shapes" (notes line 11; prose 157). Breath at night in the rain is finer detail than a face.
- Fix: cut "I can see its breath." or make it "*I can see the lantern on it.*"

### [B1-58-b] One line of dialogue holds two speakers
- Where: Book 1 ch 58, line 38
- Quote: "\"I'd go myself. Morrow won't let me. Says I'm too old to be a carter.\" \"She's right.\""
- Problem: The second quote sits inside Hallam's paragraph with no tag, so it reads as Hallam answering himself, or as a missing Calder line.
- Fix: break it into its own paragraph: "\"She's right,\" said Calder." Or cut it.

### [B1-59-b] Thorne went to the island "while you were here drawing stores", but Maren only reached Greyhold that evening
- Where: Book 1 ch 59, lines 58 and 90
- Quote: "She'd been at Greyhold since the evening before" / "In the morning, while you were here drawing stores. At first light."
- Problem: At first light on day 126 Maren was still at Redwatch or on the road.
- Fix: line 90: "In the morning, while you were on the road down here."

### [B1-59-c] Check: a pre-dawn crossing on day 124, straight after the hedge-meeting on night 123
- Where: Book 1 ch 59, line 32 (against ch 57:72–82, 162)
- Quote: "He took her twice. On the hundred-and-twenty-fourth day and the hundred-and-twenty-fifth, in the grey hour before dawn"
- Problem: The meeting begins at moonrise six nights past full (late evening) and runs an hour. It is a league inland of Millbrook. On foot, that is about three leagues back to Greyhold and over the wall, then three more to Redwatch, before first light. That isn't possible.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:84 (Millbrook 2 leagues below Greyhold); Rulings 7/23 (Redwatch 3 leagues above).
- Fix: shifting both crossings a day later would clash with Thorne's crossing on day 126, so make it one crossing: "He took her once, on the hundred-and-twenty-fifth, in the grey hour before dawn," and cut "twice". Line 42 ("On the hundred-and-twenty-fifth morning") still works.

### [B1-60-a] Check: Ulric arrives "from the south" and leaves "up the Way"
- Where: Book 1 ch 60, lines 113 and 251
- Quote: "Down the near slope, the long slant of the Way from the south" / "Ulric's people were going too, up the Way, at a run"
- Problem: The near bank is the south bank, toward the Ram's Head and Harrow's End. "Up the Way" from there means north across the ford, toward Arn. The notes say each side goes back up "their own sides of the Way".
- Fix: line 251: "were going too, back down the Way the way they'd come".

### [B1-65-a] Tam's "Before noon yesterday" is a day late
- Where: Book 1 ch 65, line 163
- Quote: "The whole town heard. Before noon yesterday."
- Problem: Maren was cast out on the morning of day 136, and Tam is speaking on day 138 (notes line 14). Millbrook is two leagues off, so the town heard on 136.
- Fix: "Before noon the same day."

### [B1-65-b] Check, hand-off to 66: when the campfires first appear behind Harrow's End
- Where: Book 1 ch 65, lines 291–293 (against ch 66:25)
- Quote: 65 (dusk, day 139): "Dozens of them, then more, coming out one after another in the dusk all over the hill behind the village."; 66: "He'd thought about it all night on the Scar, with the campfires coming out on the hill behind Harrow's End one after another like stars" (night 138)
- Problem: 66 has the fires coming out on night 138. 65 presents them as new at dusk on 139, and Maren poled past on night 138 without seeing any.
- Fix: in 66, "with the fires of the column at the foot of the moor, and nearer every hour," or in 65, "more than last night, coming out one after another".

## Craft      (at most 8)

### [B1-53-e] Six of thirteen chapters still open with a folk-saying paragraph
- Where: ch 53:25, 54:24, 55:26, 57:26, 59:26, 60:27 (55 and 57 both use the present-tense "Liss says" frame)
- Problem: The 1–40 pass cut these to about one in three. In this range the device returns nearly every other chapter and becomes a tic. 54 (roads) and 59 (the hand that writes) carry the least weight, and 59's line is spoken again later in the scene anyway.
- Fix: cut the opener in 54 and 59 (59's saying survives in Rowan's mouth at 174). Keep one of the two "Liss says" openers.

### [B1-53-f] "It couldn't understand. She said it anyway." is repeated across five chapters
- Where: ch 53:261; 55:162; 63:84, 192; 65:265, 275
- Problem: The same beat appears six times with near-identical words, and it weakens the bond scene in 73.
- Fix: keep 53 (the first) and 63:192 (the pointing, where it matters). Cut the tag in 55:162 and both in 65 ("Out loud, though it couldn't understand" / "It couldn't understand. There was no thread…").

### [B1-65-c] Wren's dragon speech is told four times in two chapters
- Where: ch 64:112 and 126 (Wren, twice); ch 65:63–67 (Liss recaps it nearly word for word); 65:265–273 (Maren to the lion, twice)
- Problem: The echo rule (DECISIONS, style pass, A1 M9) says each echo adds a fact or is cut to a line. Liss's recap adds nothing the reader didn't get a page earlier.
- Fix: 65:63–67: "She told me her father had three dragons. All of them, by name and colour. And that he talks to them, and they answer, and she makes him say it twice." Keep the scene with the lion.

### [B1-56-d] Calder's realisation is spelled out three times
- Where: ch 56:239–245
- Quote: "She'd be safer anywhere he wasn't." / "He'd known it since the hillside…" / "knew it all the way through, for the first time, the way you know a bone's broken."
- Problem: The scene has already landed it with the sack ("You bring it for something you can carry"), and the plan beat is then restated three times.
- Fix: keep "He'd led them." and "Over the water." Cut the "He'd known it since the hillside" paragraph and the bone simile.

### [B1-53-g] "It wasn't X. It was Y." in the pit chapter
- Where: ch 53:175–177 ("It wasn't a hollow any more. / It was a pit."); 53:213/223 ("It wasn't guarding anything. … It was trying to get down.")
- Fix: 175–177: "The hollow was a pit now." The second can stay if the line between is kept, or open with "It had never been guarding anything."

### [B1-59-d] "The fire ticked." three times in one scene
- Where: ch 59:112, 170, 246 (also 54:320, 65:65)
- Fix: keep one in 59 (170). Use Thorne's silence or the stair bell for the others.
