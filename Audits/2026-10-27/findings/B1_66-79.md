# Findings: Book 1, chapters 66–79
Reviewer summary: The run from the Ruin to sunrise holds together well. Day and hour counts across 70–77 interlock (fourth hour at the spit and on the knowe, under the hour to Greyhold, the candle counts), the Atlas distances are respected, and I found no reveal-rule breaches, em dashes or book references in the prose. The real problems are three. The bond-range canon breaks on day 143 (Lisska sits within word range of Calder and he feels "nothing"). Corvin gets off a falling disc from its centre. Millbrook's barricade ward works the length of a street. The rest are small slips: seasons, hand-offs, and remembered details that don't match their source.

## Critical
None found.

## Major

### [B1-72-a] Lisska at Hollin Knowe is inside word range, but Calder feels "nothing"
- Where: Book 1 ch 72, line 37 (also ch 70 line 338; ch 73 line 285; ch 76 lines 281–287)
- Quote: "Not a fire through a wall now. Nothing. Lisska was too far."
- Problem: On day 143 Lisska is hidden at the Brackenwood edge on Hollin Knowe, two leagues west of the river (ch 67 line 23: "two leagues west of the river"; ch 67 line 207: the island "two leagues off"). At dusk Calder is on the island. That is inside the three-to-four leagues words carry, and feeling "carries much further". In ch 70 he still had "the feeling, small and bright and frightened" at the same distance. In ch 76 he feels her from Greyhold, about 3.5 leagues off and through rock. So "Nothing" breaks canon and the book's own pattern. It also opens a plot hole. Lisska watches the bond-forcers take the lion from noon (73:285) and can see the dragon-fire over the island at dusk (kin-speech works "within sight", Ruling 5). She could have told Calder or Aurveth, and nobody uses it.
- Evidence: Bible/World.md:24 ("words carry three or four leagues; beyond that only feeling, which carries much further"); Book 1/Notes/Rulings_41-79.md:9 (Ruling 5); Bible/Bestiary.md:44; ch 78 notes line 13 (voice returns "within three leagues").
- Fix: In 72, replace "Nothing. Lisska was too far." with the feeling plus a scrap of voice, and Calder's order to stay hidden. This also gives a reason for Ruling 16's hiding: "Not words, or almost none. She was out at the edge of them, west, on the hill by the wood: a thin bright fear, and once his name. *Stay hidden,* he told her. *Stay till she comes.* He felt the space round her, cold, where she always was beside him, chattering." In 73:285 Orrun can then add: "*He told her to stay hidden. She stayed.*"

### [B1-76-a] Corvin escapes from the centre of a dropping disc
- Where: Book 1 ch 76, line 241 (against lines 45, 111, 123; ch 74 line 150)
- Quote: "when the floor went he'd been a step from the edge of it, and the stair."
- Problem: Corvin turns the key kneeling "on the iron boss in the middle of the stone disc" (45, 111), with Calder's hand on his wrist. The disc fills most of the chamber (74:150, "as wide as a threshing floor"). When it drops "whole, like a trap-door" (123), Calder falls with it, but moments later Corvin is climbing a stair "cut into the far wall of the cavern" (239). Nothing shows how he got from the boss to the rim. Line 241 asserts he was "a step from the edge", which contradicts 45 and 111. A careful reader will ask how the man holding Calder's wrist didn't fall too.
- Evidence: ch 76 lines 45, 111, 123, 239; ch 74 line 150.
- Fix: Give Corvin one beat between the grinding stopping and the drop. After "And he smiled." (line 113), add: "Then he let go of the key, and Calder's hand came away with nothing in it, and Corvin was up and walking across the lit stone to the rim as if he'd paced it out beforehand." At line 241 change "he'd been a step from the edge of it" to "he'd already been at the edge of it".

### [B1-71-a] Check: the barricade ward works across a street, against touch range
- Where: Book 1 ch 71, line 47 (with lines 29 and 33)
- Quote: "His word wasn't landing. Something the hundred were saying was turning it aside."
- Problem: The ring-men chanting the ward stand "at the barricades" at the top of the town. The knight whose stand-word fails is "at the bottom of the street", where the column checked (line 33). A charm, even a many-voiced one, works "on what the speaker can touch or see close by". This one acts the length of a street. That breaches settled rule 3 (live ground and touch range). The chapter's geography is also muddled: line 29 has the knights "coming down at" a barricade at the top, while line 33 puts the column at the bottom.
- Evidence: Bible/Magic.md:48 ("They don't carry"); Magic.md:279 (rule 4: "Range is touch or a few paces"); BRIEF settled rule 3.
- Fix: Bring the knight up to the barricade. In line 47 change "she saw the bottom of the street: a knight on a rearing horse" to "she saw the foot of the near barricade: a knight on a rearing horse with its forefeet almost on the carts, a few paces from the chanting men". In line 33 change "checking at the bottom of the street" to "checking in the street below the barricades".

## Minor

### [B1-73-a] The lion's chaining and the stream are put "in the summer", but they happened in autumn
- Where: Book 1 ch 73, lines 39 and 51; ch 75 line 30; ch 78 line 114
- Quote: "The island hunters had burned it in the summer, on the spit" / "lying on the spit in the summer" / "She'd seen him run on the island, in the summer" / "at the stream in the summer"
- Problem: These events fall on days 8–12. By day 4 there is already "frost in the hollows" and geese "going south" (ch 14 line 40), and 68:173 calls the day-4 ford "in the autumn". In ch 73 itself Orrun puts the same chaining "In the autumn" (line 149). Calder could also only have had the stream from Lisska after she bonded on day 8.
- Evidence: Bible/World.md:239 (day 0 is the end of summer); ch 14 line 40; ch 73 line 149.
- Fix: Change "in the summer" to "in the autumn" in all four places (78:114 can be "at the stream in the first autumn days").

### [B1-70-a] Calder found the first chain at night, not "the morning after the raid"
- Where: Book 1 ch 70, line 258; ch 78, line 144
- Quote: "From the ruts at Upper Rush, the morning after the raid"
- Problem: In ch 2 Calder picks the chain up by the ruts "in the light of the burning barn" and walks home "in the dark". The plan calls it "the cut beast chain he pockets on the first night".
- Evidence: ch 2 lines 217, 223, 263; Book 1/Chapter_Plan.md, Threads ("on the first night").
- Fix: In both places, "From the ruts at Upper Rush, the night of the raid".

### [B1-67-a] Maren reaches the knowe too early
- Where: Book 1 ch 67, line 27 (against ch 65 lines near the end and ch 66 lines 165–167)
- Quote: "She came up the knowe in the first grey of the hundred-and-fortieth day"
- Problem: She leaves the island "before first light" (ch 65). Calder finds her keel-mark "in the first grey", with the water "just beginning to seep" into it (66:165–167), so she left only minutes before. She then crosses two leagues of wet plough and flooded ditches in the dark. She cannot also be on top of the knowe "in the first grey".
- Evidence: ch 65 ("She left before first light on the hundred-and-fortieth day"); ch 66 lines 165, 167; ch 67 line 29 ("two leagues across the fields in the dark").
- Fix: "She came up the knowe as the sun came up on the hundred-and-fortieth day". Line 37's "in the dawn" and line 53's "before the sun was up" still work if line 53 becomes "She knew that before the sun was clear of the hills."

### [B1-68-a] The twins' plan for the gate doesn't match how it falls
- Where: Book 1 ch 68, lines 111 and 231; ch 71 line 171
- Quote: "Tomorrow Millbrook rises and you take the gate." / "He opens the river gate tomorrow night. In the third hour."
- Problem: Corvin plans for Cassian to take Greyhold's gate on day 142, with the drawer "for the gate". Yet the friend inside is already set to open the river gate on night 143 (Tobin, 71; Callis does it, 75). If Callis can open it, the 142 assault reads as either redundant or a plan that failed and was replaced, and the text never says which.
- Evidence: ch 68:111, 231; ch 71:171; ch 75 notes line 13.
- Fix: Make 142 a feint. At 68:231: "Tomorrow Millbrook rises and you knock on the gate, loud, so they look at it. The night after, our friend opens it, and you go down."

### [B1-68-b] Check: Rook's night before the ford doesn't match ch 16
- Where: Book 1 ch 68, line 185
- Quote: "I went down the night before ... Lay up in the gorse above the ford all night."
- Problem: Ch 16 has Rook drawing his wages the night before and leaving "before light", and ch 17's notes have him leave Varrhold "before light on the morning of the ford". The ford is six leagues below Varrhold, so he cannot have lain in the gorse all night.
- Evidence: ch 16 line 158; ch 17 notes line 9; Bible/Atlas.md:255.
- Fix: "Took a horse before light. Rode it hard. Lay up in the gorse above the ford till he came."

### [B1-77-a] The chain links change size between the two ledges
- Where: Book 1 ch 77, line 54 (against ch 74 line 118; ch 78 line 252; ch 79 line 167)
- Quote: "One link of it taller than Orrun's shoulder."
- Problem: Orrun stands about ten feet at the shoulder (Bestiary). Calder's link is "taller than he was by a head", and elsewhere links are "taller than a man". The same six chains can't be both.
- Evidence: Bible/Bestiary.md:29; ch 74:118; ch 78:252; ch 79:167.
- Fix: "One link of it taller than a man, as high as Orrun's chest."

### [B1-78-a] Maren says Wren gave her the "hello", but Wren never did
- Where: Book 1 ch 78, line 164
- Quote: "She said it to my mother on the jetty and to me on the road. *A girl called Wren says hello.* Twice."
- Problem: In ch 64 it was Calder who gave Liss the message (64:224), and Liss passed it on (65:111). At the gate in ch 69 Wren says "Did you say hello? To the lion? From me?" (69:194). She never says hello to Maren.
- Evidence: ch 64 line 224; ch 65 line 111; ch 69 line 194.
- Fix: "She sent it by my mother. *A girl called Wren says hello.* And on the road she asked if I'd passed it to the lion." Keep "Twice. So I wouldn't forget." only if it attaches to Liss's retelling.

### [B1-70-b] Notes and prose disagree in ch 70
- Where: Book 1 ch 70 notes lines 11 and 24; prose lines 43 and 334
- Quote: notes "Rook leaves the step at dusk"; prose "Rook stayed on the step, whittling, until the afternoon." Notes "west, low, toward the Brackenwood"; prose "high, so high she was nothing".
- Problem: Two small notes/prose mismatches.
- Evidence: as cited.
- Fix: Update the notes: "Rook leaves the step in the afternoon"; "Lisska goes ... west, high".

### [B1-71-b] Check: Odo carried two and a half leagues on a door
- Where: Book 1 ch 71, lines 179–181
- Quote: "when she saw them coming along the River Road out of the smoke, three of them and a door"
- Problem: Millbrook is 2.5 leagues by road from Greyhold's landing. Maren and Tam would be carrying a huge man on a door for about seven miles at night, Maren with fresh-burned palms. The text skips straight to the arrival. The back channel full of boats was right beside them.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:31 and 84; ch 71 lines 41 and 83.
- Fix: At the back channel, put Odo in a drawn-up boat: "They got him into one of the eel-punts on the back channel, door and all, and Tam poled them up under the bank in the dark." In line 181, change "coming along the River Road" to "coming up the river under the bank".

## Craft

### [B1-75-a] POV slip: Maren knows where Rowan went before anyone tells her
- Where: Book 1 ch 75, line 70
- Quote: "She'd taken every rider who could sit a horse back up the River Road at dusk, to Redwatch, where the bank was going."
- Problem: Maren has just ridden in through the gate. She can see that Rowan and the horses are gone, but not when or why.
- Fix: Keep "Rowan wasn't in the yard. There wasn't a greyhart or a horse anywhere in it." Move the reason into Haverly's dying speech: "Rowan took every rider up to Redwatch at dusk. The bank was going."

### [B1-73-b] Orrun's relay runs out of order
- Where: Book 1 ch 73, lines 283–291
- Quote: "*What's she saying?*"
- Problem: Orrun has already relayed Lisska's apology (285) when Maren asks what she's saying (287). The scene then restarts with Orrun listening.
- Fix: Move the "*She's been in the trees since noon …*" paragraph after "*She says he said tell her …*" (line 299), or cut Maren's question.

### [B1-67-b] The day-140 sections are told out of order
- Where: Book 1 ch 67, lines 73–85
- Quote: "It hunted that night, or tried to." … "She watched all of the hundred-and-fortieth day."
- Problem: The night of 140–141 is told before the daylight watch of day 140, so the count of boats, rafts and ladders reads as if it comes after the hunt.
- Fix: Move the hunt section (lines 73–81) to just before "On the hundred-and-forty-first day, at dawn".

### [B1-79-a] Repeated images for the cost of Orrun's duplicates
- Where: Book 1 ch 73 lines 195 and 311; ch 79 line 71 (also "Pell had taught her a thousand times", 69:296, 73:71, 77:122)
- Quote: "like blood out of a cut" / "cost Orrun like blood" / "like blood from a cut you can't reach to stop"
- Problem: The same simile carries all three duplicate costs, and the Pell drill phrase repeats in all three of Maren's fights. Both blunt the beats they mark.
- Fix: Keep the blood image in 73:195. In 79:71 the same line already has "the way a horse shakes under a load too heavy for it", so cut "like blood from a cut you can't reach to stop". In 77:122 drop "a thousand times".

### [B1-79-b] Calder calls his own mother "Wren's grandmother"
- Where: Book 1 ch 79, line 45
- Quote: "*Wren's grandmother says she was the best in the barn at her letters,*"
- Problem: Calder is talking to Aurveth, and he would say "Ma". The phrasing is there to orient Maren, and it shows. It also leaves "she" ambiguous.
- Fix: "*Ma says she was the best in the barn at her letters,* the farmer was saying, to the old dragon … *At Varrhold. Sixty years ago.*" Maren can infer the rest from 78.
