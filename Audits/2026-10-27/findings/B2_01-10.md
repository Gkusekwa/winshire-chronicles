# Findings: Book 2, chapters 1–10
Reviewer summary: The opening run holds together well. The hand-off from Book 1 ch 79 is clean, the apart rule is kept (the two part at dawn on day 6 and never share a road), and the magic rules (dead ground, touch range, bond gifts working on dead ground, the lock burning because it is old magic) are applied consistently. The real problems are three. The Restoration's mark gains a seventh dot in ch 2, against the Bible, against ch 10, and close to the "seventh relic" guard. Idony's day-25 reading has the walker on the Scarfell when Maren is already well east of it. The Drover's Way's "last climb out of the trees" in ch 10 doesn't fit the Way ch 10 itself describes. The rest are day-count slips of one or two, and a few craft tics that build up across chapters.

## Critical
None found.

## Major

### [B2-02-a] The ring of six gains a seventh dot
- Where: Book 2 ch 2, lines 219 and 221 (against ch 10 line 335)
- Quote: "a ring of six dots round a seventh" / "stamped with the same six round the seventh"
- Problem: The Restoration's mark is a ring of six dots, and nothing sits in the middle of it. A central seventh dot goes against the Bible and against ch 10, which describes the same knife as "the ring of six dots". The mark was also copied from the control relic, "a stone ring with six hollows", so "round a seventh" draws the reader's eye toward a seventh thing at the centre, close to the guarded "seventh relic".
- Evidence: Bible/World.md:202 ("a small **ring of six dots**"); Bible/Magic.md:204; Book 1 ch 72 lines 173, 275 ("a ring of six"); Book 2 ch 10:335.
- Fix: line 219: "and on the pommel, cut small, a ring of six dots." Line 221: "stamped with the same ring of six."

### [B2-08-a] The walker is still on the Scarfell glasses on day 25
- Where: Book 2 ch 8, lines 346–354 (and the Part 1 ledger, ch 8 line)
- Quote: "It was on the middle glasses. *The high moor. The black water. The edge.*"
- Problem: Idony's glasses put the walker (Orrun) on the Scarfell high country on day 25, "from the river toward the hills". By then Maren and Orrun left the edge before dawn on day 17. They came down the far side of the Scarfell and were out on the Hallow Plains by about day 24, so the reading is about a week out of date.
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 12 notes line 12 (day 17, Lisska sees the lion go east); ch 13 notes line 12 ("out onto the Hallow Plains on about day 24") and ch 13 line 28 ("came down off the far side of the Scarfell... for eleven days after that").
- Fix: Move the walker onto the two recut glasses at the end of the line, the ones now turned east. For example: "It was on the last glasses, the two he'd turned east. A small draw, there for a count and gone..." Keep "Going north-east... As if it were following." Update the ledger line to match.

### [B2-10-a] Where the Way "comes out of the trees under the moor"
- Where: Book 2 ch 10, lines 455 and 499 (and ch 11 lines 25–27, 79)
- Quote: "At the moor's edge, where the Way comes out of the trees." / "Coming up the Way, the last bit, where it comes out of the trees under the moor."
- Problem: In ch 10 Calder has just walked the Way above Teo's ford, up the glen of Hollow Beck past the falls, through bracken and heather with no wood (lines 69–77). So he can't name a spot where "the Way comes out of the trees". Ch 11 then puts the Fenns on "the Drover's Way, at the foot of its last climb", three leagues south-east of Varrhold, and from that same edge shows the falls and "the Way going down past them" far off to the west. That makes two Ways.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:255 (the Way crosses Hollow Beck at Teo's ford; the falls are about 3 leagues below the hold); Book 2/Notes/Part1_Ledger.md line 7; ch 10:71–77.
- Fix: Make the climb a different road from the Way in both chapters, such as "the old drove track" that comes up out of the Fellwood onto the edge. Ch 10:455: "At the moor's edge, where the old drove track comes up out of the Fellwood." Ch 10:499: "Coming up the old track, the last bit, where it comes out of the trees under the moor." Ch 11 needs the matching change (the ch 11 reviewer should be told).

## Minor

- Evidence: Book 1 ch 67 notes lines 15–16.
- Fix: "Three days ago, from a hedge below Hollin Knowe, she'd watched..."

### [B2-04-a] Bond and lock-night counts are a day too long
- Where: Book 2 ch 4, lines 49, 173, 191, 209
- Quote: "She'd learned in four days that he always said things slowly" / "She'd stood on it once before, four nights ago."
- Problem: The bond was made on night 143 (ch 1:38, "It wasn't a night old"), so on day 1 it is under two days old. The cavern visit is on the evening of day 2, three nights after the lock, not four.
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 73 notes (night 143); Book 1 ch 77 (midnight, night 143); Book 2 ch 1:38.
- Fix: Line 49: "She'd learned in two days". Lines 173, 191 and 209: "three nights ago".

### [B2-04-b] "The night of the gate" used for night-143 events
- Where: Book 2 ch 4, line 361; ch 6, line 174
- Quote: "Hale went to the black door at dusk, the night of the gate. Liss got to him at the water-gate." / "whether anything could have got in or out through it in the night of the gate"
- Problem: The gate fell on day 142 (ch 2:63, "It rang the day the gate fell"). Liss reached Hale at dusk on day 143. Also, the inner wall only split when the Titan rose at dawn on day 0 (ch 6:148), so nothing could have come through it "in the night of the gate".
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 69 notes (day 142); Book 1 ch 75 lines 176, 232; Book 2 ch 5:171 already uses "the night of the locks".
- Fix: In both lines, "the night of the locks". In ch 6:174 also: "...or out through it since the morning it split."

### [B2-06-a] Lisska's report puts Calder past the Ram's Head too early
- Where: Book 2 ch 6, line 370 (and the Part 1 ledger, ch 6 line)
- Quote: "*He is past the inn with the ram on its sign.*"
- Problem: Lisska left Calder on day 9 at the Skell Water, which is below the Ram's Head. He then lay up at the inn and was still there at dusk on day 10. She can't report him past it.
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 7 lines 332, 348–358, 370, 374; ch 10:27 (leaves the Ram's Head on day 11).
- Fix: "*He is near the inn with the ram on its sign.*" Change the ledger to "Calder below the Ram's Head".

### [B2-06-b] Two rest days go unmentioned
- Where: Book 2 ch 6, lines 304 and 352–354; ch 7, lines 370–374

## Craft

### [B2-craft-a] "Say it twice" is used so often it loses its force
- Where: ch 6:388, ch 7:414–418 and ch 10:507–511 all end on a "twice" beat. It also appears at ch 1:76, 2:153, 5:257/283, 6:264/342, 9:182 and 10:477.
- Problem: Wren's tic is spreading to Lisska, Gunna, Ada, Calder and Maren, and it closes three of the five chapters from 6 to 10. It is starting to read as a device rather than a child.
- Fix: Keep it for Wren alone, and end at least ch 7 and ch 10 on something else. For example, end ch 10 on "He said it again." without Lisska's prompt, and end ch 7 on "Maybe not bad," with no request to repeat it.

### [B2-craft-b] "The way" similes
- Where: e.g. ch 1:44 ("the way a man talks to frightened stock"), 1:84; ch 9:64 ("the way hail presses barley"); ch 10 has about a dozen.
- Problem: This is a style-guide breach and is very dense in chs 1 and 10.
- Fix: Use "as" ("as hail presses barley"), or cut the simile.

### [B2-craft-c] "It wasn't X. It was Y."
- Where: ch 3:46 ("It wasn't low. It was a hole."); ch 3:144 ("It wasn't a draw..."); ch 10:265 ("He'd thought the knife was the worst thing... It wasn't.")
- Problem: These are the balanced reversals the style guide asks writers to avoid.
- Fix: For example, ch 3:46: "Not low. A hole. A draw, as if something under that one kingdom..."

### [B2-craft-d] "Her legs decided it"
- Where: ch 2:133 ("His legs had decided it"), 2:171 ("as if her legs had decided it too"), 4:135 ("her legs decided it. They'd been deciding things for four days"). Also ch 1:290 reuses Book 1 ch 79's "something had been decided that he hadn't decided".
- Problem: The same body-overrules-mind phrasing comes back in two POVs within a few pages, and it reads as a writer's tic.
- Fix: Keep one (ch 4's works best) and recast the others in plain action.

### [B2-craft-e] Rook's account told a third time
- Where: ch 10:325–337 (after Book 1 ch 68 and Book 2 ch 2:263–275)
- Problem: The reader hears the gorse, the reins and "went in the water like a stone" again almost word for word. What is new in the scene is Arn's reaction and the cold west hearth.
- Fix: Summarise the account in two sentences and keep only one quoted line ("He never drew"). The room this frees can go to Arn not touching the knife.
