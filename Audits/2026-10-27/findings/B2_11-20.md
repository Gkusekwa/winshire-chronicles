# Findings: Book 2, chapters 11–20
Reviewer summary: The range is strong. The day counts inside it mostly hold, and the apart rule, the reveal rules and the magic rules are all kept: Maren never goes the last three leagues, nothing names a destroyer or an elemental, and every working has its toll. The real problems are continuity. A few people know things they couldn't yet know: Gunna knows about the road (17), the fur-trader knows Maren sat with the Titan (19), and Lisska is on the edge when ch 10 sent her home (12). Two details from Book 1 have slipped: Calder "never" having been to Skarrow, and the mill being placed before Teo's ford. And in ch 15 Rowan's mount and her chain contradict ch 1. A handful of geography slips trace back to the Atlas map data, not the prose.

## Critical

None found. I checked the apart rule (ch 11), the reveal rules (no Council, no "seventh relic", no elementals, no book-number talk), the magic rules (live ground, touch range, running water, tolls) and the titles of chs 11–20; none is breached.

## Major

### [B2-12-a] Lisska is on the edge before dawn on day 17, but ch 10 sent her home and ch 11 shows only three dragons
- Where: Book 2 ch 12, line 38 (and ch 11 lines 61, 99, 335)
- Quote: "I couldn't sleep on the edge, it's too windy and Leif's brown one snores. I was up on the rocks above the fire."
- Problem: In ch 10, Lisska flies down with Gunna, finds the Fenns and comes back to Calder at dusk on day 15. In ch 11, Maren sees her circle at noon on day 15 and go "away north again", and from then on the camp has exactly three dragons ("Three.", "The three dragons lifted their heads"). Nothing puts her back on the edge on the night of day 16, yet in ch 12 she says she watched Maren leave from the rocks above the fire.
- Evidence: ch 10 lines 493–495 ("He'd sent her down again with Gunna, to go on ahead and find them and come back"); ch 11 lines 61, 99, 335.
- Fix: Add one line to ch 12 before her report: "He'd sent her back down on the sixteenth, to sit on the edge and watch." Optionally give Maren a glimpse of her in ch 11 on day 16 ("a green shape on the rocks above the peat-hag, keeping out of the way").

### [B2-15-a] Rowan rides a greyhart, but ch 1 says she never does
- Where: Book 2 ch 15, line 251
- Quote: "on a big grey greyhart with a white blaze, a small neat woman with iron-grey hair cut short."
- Problem: Ch 1 makes a point of it: Rowan rides a horse, never a greyhart, because greyharts are "for people who wanted to be looked at."
- Evidence: ch 1 lines 126–128 ("At the head of the column rode a small woman on a grey horse. Not a greyhart. Rowan had never ridden a greyhart that Maren had seen").
- Fix: "At the head of the column, on a plain grey horse among the greyharts, a small neat woman with iron-grey hair cut short."

### [B2-15-b] The chain is said to be the same one Maren saw in the reeds, but its size and load have grown many times over
- Where: Book 2 ch 15, lines 245 and 249 (also ch 16 line 260, ch 19 line 236, ch 20 line 57)
- Quote: "The great new chain they'd forged at Greyhold before the gate fell, that she'd seen on its wain in the reeds below Redwatch"
- Problem: In ch 1 the chain is the trap's beast-chain, "forty fathoms of ordinary iron" on one wain behind two dray-oxen. In ch 15 it rides "six great wains drawn by oxen, eight to a wain", with "links as long as a man's body". A reader who remembers ch 1 can't reconcile the two, and ch 15 explicitly says they are the same chain.
- Evidence: ch 1 line 14 (notes: "the crown's beast-chain from the trap... on its wain behind two dray-oxen") and line 190 ("forty fathoms of ordinary iron").
- Fix: Make the Thornwood chain bigger on purpose, in ch 15 line 249: "The chain from the reeds, and more: the crown had had it lengthened and its links recast at Greyhold's forge since the spring, every link now as long as a man's body". (Or name it a new chain sent up from Hallowmere.)

### [B2-17-a] Gunna knows about Calder's road before anyone could have told her
- Where: Book 2 ch 17, line 54
- Quote: "Gunna sent word down from the caves this morning, before you were even back."
- Problem: Calder made the bargain on day 33 in the back room of the Plough and flew home on day 34 with Lisska beside him. Delling's first party doesn't leave until day 35. Nobody could have carried the news to the hatching caves by the morning of day 34.
- Evidence: ch 14 lines 318–358 and 390–410; ch 17 line 26 ("the night he came back").
- Fix: Have Lisska go ahead. In ch 17: "Your green one was at the caves before you'd got off his neck. Gunna sent word down an hour since." Or have Arn say Gunna *will* say it: "Gunna'll say the old law. The bonded keep a bonded man's word."

### [B2-17-b] The ledger puts the mill before Teo's ford, and it's read out of order
- Where: Book 2 ch 17, line 342 (also lines 136–144)
- Quote: "every night he waited for the autumn page that wasn't there, the one from before the ford, before Teo. The harvest. The night of the mill."
- Problem: In Book 1 the mill comes after the first ford (ch 16 The Ford, ch 18 The Mill). There's a second problem too. The book is said to be "in order, day by day", yet in the first nights Ada reads Hob's sale of "the farmer's road and hour". That was Book 1 ch 24, after both the ford and the mill, and she reads it before she reaches the autumn horse on the fifth night.
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 18 notes, line 9 (the mill is the same night as ch 17, after ch 16's ford); Bible/Characters.md line 144 ("sells Calder's road and hour to the coin man (24)").
- Fix: Line 342: "the autumn page that wasn't there, the one from after the ford. The harvest. The night of the mill." Move the miller entry (line 140) after the horse, or replace it in the early nights with an entry from before the ford.

### [B2-19-a] Calder "never" went to Skarrow, but in Book 1 he did
- Where: Book 2 ch 19, lines 56–58 (and line 88)
- Quote: "It was bigger than he'd thought. / He'd never been. Nobody from Harrow's End went to Skarrow."
- Problem: In Book 1 ch 48 (day 96) Calder flies to Skarrow on Aurveth in the snow and talks with Ulric at his stockade gate, and that passage already says "Bigger than Calder had thought." Ch 19 line 88 also reaches back to "the face he remembered from the funeral feast", skipping that later meeting.
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 48 line 233 ("They went on the ninety-sixth day. East, over the Scarfell... to Ulric's steading in its long valley... Bigger than Calder had thought") and line 237.
- Fix: Lines 56–58: "It was bigger than he remembered from the winter. Then it had been a stockade in the snow and a man in the gate who wouldn't let him past it. He'd never been inside." Line 88: "the face he remembered from the stockade gate in the snow".

### [B2-19-b] On day 58 the fur-trader reports that Maren has already sat down with the Titan
- Where: Book 2 ch 19, lines 246–250
- Quote: "Into the grey, where nobody goes." / "He says she sat down with it." / "With the great one. In the ring of stones."
- Problem: On day 58 Maren is still at the Speaking Stones. She leaves on day 60, enters the grey on about day 70 and lies down at ten paces on day 74. A trader who left the Plains before day 58 can't know any of that. And she didn't go in "ahead of" the knights either: Rowan went in on day 53 and Maren followed on day 60.
- Evidence: ch 15 lines 277, 303, 395–397; ch 16 lines 312–334; ch 18 line 28.
- Fix: Tie the name to the Stones, where Hesper coined it (ch 15 line 95). Lines 246–250: "He says there was a lion... And a woman with it. A lowlander. At the Speaking Stones, in front of the old ones." / "He says the old ones asked what she was to it, and the Keeper said: she sits with him. That's what the clans call her now." Drop "ahead of them" and "Into the grey".

## Minor

### [B2-13-c] The upper Wend is on the wrong side of the Scarfell
- Where: Book 2 ch 13, line 30
- Quote: "Becks ran down out of it north-east, toward the upper Wend"
- Problem: On the atlas the upper Wend runs north–south *west* of the Scarfell, past the Old Ruin. East of the Scarfell the water drains toward the Ysse.
- Evidence: Tools/atlas/world.py lines 50–53 (the Wend's course, x≈176–179) and line 59 (the Ysse); Bible/Atlas.md line 48.
- Fix: "Becks ran down out of it south-east, toward the Ysse's far feeders".

### [B2-13-a] The Thornwood lies east of the Plains in the prose but north of them on the map (Check)
- Where: Book 2 ch 13, line 110
- Quote: "across the whole of the eastern sky, under the white of the mountains, a dark line... the line between them ran north and south"
- Problem: In the atlas data the Hallow Plains (y 92–146) lie south of the Thornwood (y 18–80), and they meet along a roughly east–west line, with the Ring at (262,54). Walking east from the Scarfell, the Thornwood would be on Maren's left (north), not a wall across the east. The Speaking Stones ("a day west of the Thornwood", ch 15 line 27) and Lake Ysse being south of the Ring point the same way.
- Evidence: Tools/atlas/world.py lines 81 and 96.
- Fix: Decide which is canon. The lighter fix is to the map: move the Thornwood's south-west lobe so its western edge borders the Plains' eastern half. If the prose has to change instead: "a dark line across the north-east... the line between them ran away east-south-east as far as she could see".

### [B2-13-b] The horse clans couldn't see a dragon over the Marches from the Plains' edge
- Where: Book 2 ch 13, line 362
- Quote: "Over the Marches. Far off, at the Plains' edge, from the rises."
- Problem: The Plains' western edge (x≈232) is about 50 leagues from the Drover's Way (x≈180). That's too far to see a dragon, let alone the man on its back. The text says the word came "passed rider to rider", but this line places the sighting itself at the Plains' edge.
- Evidence: Tools/atlas/world.py lines 96, 165 and 173.
- Fix: "A horse-clan family trading wool on the Scarfell's far side saw it, and the word came back rider to rider."

### [B2-12-b] The atlas data puts Coldtarn and the Ashlings far from where the ledgers and prose put them (Check: an Atlas fix)
- Where: Book 2 ch 12, line 68 ("past Coldtarn... four leagues"); ch 14 line 38 (the Ashlings "Two leagues of it between the gully and the town")
- Quote: "So he walked up past Coldtarn in the morning, four leagues"
- Problem: In world.py, Coldtarn is at (196,74), about 24 leagues from Varrhold (184.4,94.8). The Ashlings polygon (200–218, 116–140) begins about 20 leagues east of Cotter's Cross (180.5,113). Both the Part One/Two ledgers and the prose use 4 leagues and 2 leagues.
- Evidence: Tools/atlas/world.py lines 67, 85, 165 and 173; Book 2/Notes/Part1_Ledger.md line 23 ("4 leagues from the hold").
- Fix: Move Coldtarn to about (189,90) and the Ashlings to just north-east of the Cross in world.py, then regenerate the Atlas. No change to the prose.

### [B2-14-a] The flight to the Cross takes a whole day for about 19 leagues
- Where: Book 2 ch 14, line 30 (and ch 12 line 416)
- Quote: "Half a day on the red dragon's neck"
- Problem: They leave at dawn (ch 12) and land at dusk (ch 14), which is a full day, though the prose says "half a day". Varrhold to the Cross is about 19 leagues, roughly 2½ hours by dragon on Atlas pace.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md line 20 (dragon, 80 leagues a day); world.py lines 165 and 173.
- Fix: "Most of a day, because he made Kharrak come down twice to let the tide go out of them both, and they lay up in a fold till the light was going."

### [B2-15-c] "A day from the grey" at the Speaking Stones, and again at the grove
- Where: Book 2 ch 15, lines 225 and 339
- Quote: "Out here, on the Plains, a day from the grey, it held." / "Forty trees of it. A day from the grey."
- Problem: The grey is a league round the Ring (ch 16 line 128), and the Ring is deep in the Thornwood, about seven days' walk from the Stones (Stones to edge 1 day, edge to Barrows 2, Barrows to Ring 4). The Stones are a day from the *forest*, not from the grey.
- Evidence: ch 15 line 27; ch 13 lines 184 and 286; ch 16 line 128.
- Fix: Line 225: "a day from the forest and a week from the grey". Line 339: "Within reach of the grey."

### [B2-15-d] Maren knows about a setting-song the farmer never told her about (Check)

### [B2-15-e] Greyharts are grey in the prose but white in the Bestiary

### [B2-15-f] The smoke "hadn't reached him", but he can smell it

- Problem: He can only smell it if the smoke, or at least its edge, has reached him. As written, the two sentences contradict each other.

### [B2-17-c] "Three days ago" is wrong on the seventh night

### [B2-17-d] The parties come up out of the Ashlings in sight of a crag by the hold (Check)
- Where: Book 2 ch 17, line 70
- Quote: "They came up out of the Ashlings and over the lip of the moor"

### [B2-18-a] Five days to cross one league of grey, and "four days from Hesper's oak" doesn't fill the fortnight (Check)

- Fix: Account for the time, for instance: "the whole eight days from Hesper's oak... and the last of them slow, in the grey, where Hush would only go a few hundred paces before she lay down and had to be talked up again." Align ch 18's notes to day 69.

### [B2-20-d] Hand-off to ch 21: two dates there clash with this range (for the 21–30 reviewer)

## Craft

### [B2-12-c] Verbal tics: "a long time" and "didn't say anything"
- Where: across chs 11–20; for example ch 12 lines 52, 144 and 200; ch 17 lines 34, 214 and 356; ch 18 lines 82, 128 and 152
- Quote: "He sat with that a long time." / "He didn't say anything."
- Problem: "a long time" appears about 63 times across the ten chapters, and "didn't say anything" (or "Nobody said anything") about 34 times. That's roughly six and three per chapter, and it flattens the silences that matter (Wren's stone, the mill page, Rowan's eyes closing).
- Fix: Cut about half. Keep them where the silence is the beat, and elsewhere let the gesture carry it (for example ch 12 line 144: "...and the warm water going by, and turned the stone over, and over").

### [B2-17-e] The "Not X. Y." correction is overused, and the banned pattern appears once
- Where: about 41 lines across the range; the plain breach is ch 17 line 344; "It wasn't a question." appears twice (ch 17 line 114, ch 18 line 214)
- Quote: "It wasn't in the autumn. It was at the back."
- Problem: Lines like "Not anger. The other thing." (ch 12 line 392), "Not grief... It was something emptier" (ch 13 line 204) and "Not dimmed. Out." (ch 20 line 215) are the same move again and again. The style guide asks for the "It wasn't X. It was Y." form to be avoided.
- Fix: For ch 17 line 344: "It was at the back." Thin the others to the strongest few, and replace one of the "It wasn't a question" lines.

### [B2-16-b] Maren and Idony share the same images
- Where: ch 16 line 152 and ch 18 line 70 (owls "as tall as children"); ch 16 line 146 and ch 20 line 81 ("ice under water"); ch 13 line 298, ch 16 line 76 and ch 20 line 75 (frost on a pane)
- Quote: "Great pale owls as tall as children"
- Problem: Idony's chapters should sound like a measurer. Here the woman of the river and the reader reach for the same similes, so the R voice blurs into the S voice.
- Fix: Give Idony numbers and instruments in place of the simile ("owls, he guessed, a yard and a half standing"; "pale as quicksilver at rest"), and keep the frost image for Hesper and Maren.

### [B2-12-d] "The way" similes (the style rule)
- Where: for example ch 12 line 348 ("the way a dog lies across a door"), ch 13 line 70 ("the way cattle part"), ch 16 lines 144 and 202, ch 18 lines 94 and 288, ch 19 line 126
- Quote: "the way a hill lies across a valley"
- Problem: The style guide wants "as". There are about 36 in the range, and they're checked mechanically elsewhere.
- Fix: "as a dog lies across a door", "as cattle part", and so on.

### [B2-20-e] "On the seventh day" reads as day 7
- Where: Book 2 ch 20, line 59
- Quote: "Rowan had looked at her once, across the ring, on the seventh day"
- Problem: The book counts days absolutely everywhere else, so the reader takes this as day 7.
- Fix: "on the seventh of those days".
