# Findings: Book 2, chapters 21–30
Reviewer summary: The range holds together well on its big beats. The Roosts raid, the pour, the waking at the Moot, the drain, the Gallows Quay raid and the arrival at Vael all agree with the plan, the ledgers and the magic rules, and I found no breach of the settled reveal or magic rules. The problems are clock and travel problems. News and travellers move faster than the Atlas allows (chs 24, 26, 27, 28), and two lines in ch 24 date earlier events wrongly. There is also one cross-range character slip: Brannagh is a woman in chs 28 and 31 and a man in chs 37 and 40. The rest are small day-count and season slips, plus a few tics that repeat across chapters.

## Critical   (breaks the story, canon or a settled rule)

None found.

## Major      (a continuity or logic error a careful reader would notice)

### [B2-24-a] The pour-day speeches date the Brack steading and the raid wrongly
- Where: Book 2 ch 24, line 249 (and line 289)
- Quote: "He burned the Brack steading three nights ago" / "He came for our dragons with smoke two nights ago"; Ulric: "And at the choosing," ... "Two nights ago."
- Problem: The pour is on the morning of day 102. Midsummer night (the raid) is day 98, so that was four nights ago, not two. The Brack steading was found burned on day 64, about five weeks earlier, so "three nights ago" is far out. Every reader of ch 19 will catch this.
- Evidence: ch 24 line 197 ("They poured on the hundred-and-second day"); ch 21 Days: 98; ch 19 line 350 ("They found the steadings on the sixty-fourth day").
- Fix: Line 249: "He burned the Brack steading at the turn of the summer and left our grey on its door so I'd ride on my uncle. He came for our dragons with smoke on midsummer night and killed Hallam's grandson on the Choosing Ledge." Line 289: "And at the choosing," said Ulric. "On midsummer night."

### [B2-24-b] The trader on the Scarfell road knows news he can't have
- Where: Book 2 ch 24, lines 341–357
- Quote: "A horse-train came over the rise toward them going east." ... "She's going to the Moot," said Orm. "With a Keeper. And the lion. And a lowlander with a box."
- Problem: It is the afternoon of day 102. Hesper only decided on the Moot in the night of day 101, and the party set out at dawn on day 102, at the Ring, nearly a hundred leagues east. A trader heading east, so coming from the west, can't have heard that "on the road", and he can't know Idony has joined them either. The Keepers' hollow owls can't carry it: they left the Ring on midsummer night and won't fly over dead ground, and the Ring was a league of grey.
- Evidence: ch 22 line 321 (Hesper decides in the night; they set off at dawn on day 102); Part2_Ledger line 13 (Varrhold to the Thornwood is days 17–31 on foot); Bestiary line 155 (hollow owls won't fly over dead ground).
- Fix: Keep what he could plausibly have: the great one flew west on midsummer night, and the lion-woman sits at the stones with it. Make the Moot a guess: "He says the clans are all at the lake for the last week, as they do. If she's anywhere now, he says, she'll be with them." Drop "a lowlander with a box". The plan's echo ("the clans are going to the Moot") still holds.

### [B2-27-a] Idony crosses the Hallow Plains in about a day
- Where: Book 2 ch 27, lines 217–223
- Quote: "The Speaking Stones, on the hundred-and-eleventh day, at noon." ... "On the hundred-and-twelfth day, at the Plains' western edge"
- Problem: In ch 13 Maren walks from the Plains' western edge (about day 24) to the Thornwood's edge (day 31), roughly fifty-six leagues at eight a day. The Stones are one day west of the Thornwood, so there are about six days of walking (some 48 leagues) from the Stones to the western edge. Idony rides it between noon one day and the next, more than three times the Atlas's steady horse pace of 14 leagues a day, on a borrowed pony shared with a boy.
- Evidence: Part2_Ledger line 6 (onto the Plains about day 24; the Thornwood on day 31; 8 leagues a day); ch 15 line 27 ("a day west of the Thornwood"); Atlas line 12ff (horse, steady: 14).
- Fix: Move the lift to day 114: "On the hundred-and-fourteenth day, at the Plains' western edge...". Write the sentence that evening, and let him see the north-west turn the same night. Line 271 ("He was at the foot of the hills by then") then follows on directly. The ledger line for ch 27 needs the same change.

### [B2-28-a] Brannagh changes sex between chapters
- Where: Book 2 ch 28, line 361 (and ch 31 line 117; ch 37 line 188; ch 40 line 311)
- Quote: ch 28: "One of the Wardens, a big woman with short hair"; ch 31: "Old Brannagh was on the gate, the big one, and she looked at the lion"; ch 37: "The short-haired one who'd let her through ... He looked at the old cow"; ch 40: "the big Warden stood with his lynx. Brannagh"
- Problem: The same gate Warden is a woman in chs 28 and 31 and a man in chs 37 and 40 (and in ch 40's notes, line 21).
- Evidence: the lines above.
- Fix: Pick one. The fewest edits keep the woman: in ch 37 change "He looked" / "He didn't ask" / "He took" to "She", and in ch 40 change "his lynx" to "her lynx" and fix its notes. Ch 37's dusk lines for the big Warden ("Five days I've been pulling people out...") need checking for pronouns too.

## Minor      (small slips: a wrong day, a name typo, a count off by one)

### [B2-21-a] "A week after" the board went under the chair
- Where: Book 2 ch 21, line 39
- Quote: "So on the eighty-fifth day, a week after he'd taken the board with the boot-print into the hall and put it under the chair"
- Problem: Gorm reports the board already under the chair on day 68 (Arn rode out on day 66), so day 85 is two and a half weeks later.
- Evidence: ch 19 lines 440–446.
- Fix: "So on the eighty-fifth day, more than a fortnight after he'd taken the board..."

### [B2-21-b] The heather "in the summer, through Lisska's eyes"
- Where: Book 2 ch 21, line 407
- Quote: "He'd felt it once before, in the summer, through Lisska's eyes: the heather on the Scarfell moving behind the lion"
- Problem: Lisska saw that on day 17, in spring, and Calder only heard about it from her. He didn't feel or see it himself.
- Evidence: ch 12 lines 12, 46–50.
- Fix: "He'd heard of it once before, in the spring, from Lisska: the heather on the Scarfell moving behind the lion where there wasn't any wind."

### [B2-22-a] Three small day-count slips in the surge chapter
- Where: Book 2 ch 22, lines 189, 201, 155
- Quote: "two days after she'd taken him off the other one into the beck"; "the way it had been every day for a hundred days"; "a Keeper's moss that had sat in a bag on the dead grey for twenty days"
- Problem: The beck was the night of day 98 and this is the afternoon of day 99, less than a day later. Idony hasn't known Pask for a hundred days, because the expedition left Vael about twenty days after the breaking (line 269). Hesper entered the grey on day 70, so by day 99 her moss had been there for about thirty days.
- Evidence: ch 20 Days: 98; ch 22 line 269; Part2_Ledger line 8 ("entered the grey day 70").
- Fix: "the day after she'd taken him off the other one"; "every day since Vael"; "on the dead grey for a month".

### [B2-22-b] The Warden count doesn't add up across chs 22 and 27
- Where: Book 2 ch 22, lines 95, 171, 265; ch 27, lines 27, 39, 47, 55
- Quote: ch 22: "five of them who could walk" ... "At the five Wardens who could walk. At Pask"; Dane on day 101: "Five Wardens fit." ch 27: "Five of us kneeling by them" ... "Two didn't get up" ... Ysolde arrives "with two Wardens behind her"; Dane "gone west ... Three Wardens and the Scribes".
- Problem: If Pask is one of the five who could walk, only four are fit after he burns out on day 100. Even taking Dane's five, those five are all at the lamps: two dead, and three (Ysolde and two more) at the Moot. That leaves no three Wardens to go west with Dane, unless they are Pask and the badly hurt.
- Evidence: the lines above.
- Fix: In ch 27 line 55, "Pask and the Scribes and the mules". Or in ch 22 line 265 make it "Four Wardens fit, and me", with ch 27 line 39 "Four of us and the Magister" and the dead and survivor counts adjusted to match.

### [B2-26-a] The Roosts' stone hums a day late for the Antlered
- Where: Book 2 ch 26, lines 43–53 (notes line 13)
- Quote: "On the hundred-and-sixth day the Roosts' stone hummed again." ... "Something has stood up"
- Problem: The Antlered stood in the full morning of day 105 (the notes say so). In ch 21 the Ring's opening reached the Roosts the same night, so a one-day lag here is inconsistent.
- Evidence: ch 25 line 164; ch 21 lines 401–419.
- Fix: "On the hundred-and-fifth day the Roosts' stone hummed again", with Calder's hand on the rock at mid-morning instead of first light. Also change "106" to "105" in the ledger.

### [B2-26-b] Orm arrives alone on day 108, then has "come with Ulric" on day 109
- Where: Book 2 ch 26, lines 65 and 93
- Quote: "Orm Garrick, on his grey hill-pony, lathered, come over the Scarfell from Skarrow in a day and a night" ... "Ulric had come over the moor with Orm, behind the flood, with forty men"
- Problem: Orm reached Varrhold at noon on day 108 and didn't get down. Skarrow is a day and a night away, so he couldn't have gone back for Ulric and returned by dusk on day 109.
- Evidence: the lines above.
- Fix: Line 93: "Ulric had come over the moor behind Orm, behind the flood, with forty men, to see."

### [B2-26-c] Check: the horse-clan family's ride from the Moot
- Where: Book 2 ch 26, line 89
- Quote: "The first Wyldmarch people came over the moor on the hundred-and-ninth day." ... "They'd come away from the Moot."
- Problem: The man watched the waking from a boat on day 105, and the boats came in at dusk. He reaches Varrhold at dusk on day 109. Varrhold to the Thornwood's edge alone is about 110 leagues (Maren, days 17–31). That is over 27 leagues a day for four days, with six riders on three elk and all their goods, above the Atlas's one-day hard-riding figure of 25.
- Evidence: Part2_Ledger line 6; Atlas, leagues-a-day table.
- Fix: Have them leave the Plains, not the Moot ("they'd been on the Plains, and heard it from the riders coming out of the forest"), or move their arrival to day 112 and Orm's telling to match.

### [B2-27-b] Check: Idony sees the walker "on the Plains" the day Maren reaches live ground on his own road
- Where: Book 2 ch 27, lines 311–317; ch 28, lines 55–61
- Quote: ch 27: "*Day 118. The walker: west, on the Plains.*" ... "on the dead ground of the Plains"; ch 28: "On the hundred-and-eighteenth day the ground came alive again under her feet." ... "The tide-reader's road."
- Problem: On day 118 Maren is on live ground at the foot of the road Idony took. That same night Idony sees her far behind, still on the dead Plains. One of the two has the wrong day.
- Evidence: the lines above.
- Fix: In ch 27, date the echo "Day 116" (as its notes already do: "day 116 at the Plains' edge").

### [B2-28-b] "It is the lanes": Orrun's grey band dated to the wrong event
- Where: Book 2 ch 28, line 45 (and ch 31 line 47)
- Quote: "*It will not go,* he said ... *It is the lanes.*"; ch 31: "the band of pale at the roots of his mane that hadn't been there at the Ring"
- Problem: The ash-pale band first appears after the stakes at the Ring (day 99), before the lanes. Idony himself sees it then, so ch 31's "hadn't been there at the Ring" contradicts what he saw.
- Evidence: ch 22 line 321 ("woken thin and hungry and grey at the roots of his mane"); ch 23 line 68.
- Fix: ch 28: "*It is the stakes. And the lanes.*" ch 31: "the band of pale at the roots of his mane, wider than it had been at the Ring".

### [B2-28-c] Check: the salt-merchant gets from the coast to Vael in a fortnight
- Where: Book 2 ch 28, lines 381–389
- Quote: "who'd come all the way up from the coast and over the pass" ... "A fortnight ago," said the merchant. "On Gallows Quay."
- Problem: Wendmouth to Vael is about 270 leagues by the Atlas roads (Greyhold, Hallowmere, the pass), roughly a month with mules. The date (day 121, fourteen days before) is right. The traveller can't have brought the news in person.
- Evidence: Atlas lines 35–36 (Greyhold–Wendmouth 122.5 by road; Hallowmere–Vael 94); ch 30 line 184 onward (the raid on night 120, the burning at dawn on day 121).
- Fix: Make it hearsay: "It was all the talk at the pass-toll. Came up by bird, a fortnight ago it was. On Gallows Quay."

### [B2-29-a] Idony's walk and the pass-watch report are each a day or two off
- Where: Book 2 ch 29, lines 29, 413 and 299
- Quote: "He'd walked four days from the foot of the High Crown"; "On their summer road, west, two days out from the pass-stone."; "He would know, in a fortnight."
- Problem: (1) In ch 27 he leaves Tamm on the morning after the night of day 118, so on day 119, and arrives at dusk on day 121: three days. (2) The report is heard on day 133, but the herd reached the pass-stone on day 133 (ch 28 line 291), so it can't be two days out. (3) The reading of the contract (ch 31) is on day 138, eleven days after day 127.
- Evidence: ch 27 line 325; ch 28 line 291; Part3_Ledger line 21 (night 138).
- Fix: "three days"; "a day out from the pass-stone" (or move the report to day 131); "in a dozen days".

### [B2-30-a] Book 1's Wendmouth visit is "autumn" and "winter" in the same chapter
- Where: Book 2 ch 30, lines 50, 76, 82 (autumn) against lines 58, 188, 392, 396, 404 (winter)
- Quote: "into Wendmouth in the autumn" / "on these same quays in the winter, on the cage bolts, the night he took Mabbin home"
- Problem: The Tidesteps and Gallows Quay were Book 1 days 49–50, which is autumn (Book 1 days 0–40 are late summer into autumn, and ch 39 is "First Frost"). Ch 24 line 45 also says "In the autumn". The Halloran woman at Greyhold's gate (line 352) can stay "winter".
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 34 notes line 10 (day 49); Book 1 ch 36 notes line 11 (day 50); Bible/Characters.md line 225.
- Fix: Change "winter" to "autumn" at lines 58, 188, 392, 396 and 404.

### [B2-30-b] "Since the summer. Since the thing flew."
- Where: Book 2 ch 30, line 330
- Quote: "Cartloads. Every month since the summer. Since the thing flew."
- Problem: The Titan flew on day 0, in early spring. Midsummer is day 98.
- Evidence: Chapter_Plan line 25.
- Fix: "Every month since the spring. Since the thing flew."

## Craft      (at most 8)

### [B2-22-c] The "three together" echo is used four times in four chapters
- Where: ch 20 (end), ch 22 lines 377–391, ch 23 lines 336–348, ch 25 lines 142–148
- Problem: The green-glass reading of the three dragons is the echo in four chapters in a row, each time in nearly the same words ("Three. Together."). It stops landing by the fourth. Ch 23's version is the strongest, because Maren silently understands it.
- Fix: In ch 25, cut the glass to a gesture: he turns it south-west, nods once, and puts it away, and Maren doesn't ask. Or move the beat to Maren's thought.

### [B2-21-c] "He didn't know why." as a tic
- Where: 12 times across chs 21–29 (e.g. ch 21 line 403, ch 22 lines 197, 381, 389, ch 24 line 77, ch 25 lines 148, 232, ch 27 lines 31, 305, ch 29 line 317)
- Problem: It is the default way into a scene for all three POVs. It flattens the voices and starts to read as filler.
- Fix: Cut it in at least half of these places. The action carries the motive without it.

### [B2-24-c] Overheard news as the echo device, five chapters in ten
- Where: ch 24 (trader), ch 26 (horse-clan family), ch 28 (salt-merchant), ch 29 (report through Dane's door), ch 30 (clerk)
- Problem: Five of ten echoes are a stranger reporting the other strand's news. Together with the travel problems above, it makes the device feel convenient.
- Fix: Turn one or two into the POV character's own sensing, as ch 21's humming stone and ch 27's walker do. For example, in ch 24 Calder could feel the stone's eastward hum fade.

### [B2-21-d] "The way" similes, which are against the style rule
- Where: e.g. ch 21 lines 183, 189, 273, 315; ch 22 line 39; ch 23 line 214; ch 26 lines 251, 431; ch 29 lines 39, 43, 171; ch 30 lines 348, 418
- Problem: The Style Guide asks for "as" instead.
- Fix: For example, ch 29 line 39: "as a log lies on a pond"; ch 30 line 348: "as a dog gets off a chair".
