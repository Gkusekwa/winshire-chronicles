# Findings: Book 2, chapters 31–41
Reviewer summary: The Vael sequence holds up. The settled reveal rules are kept (the control relic is shown as the six-hollow ring and never named, "the Unbound" stays the seventh ring's name, nothing is called the seventh relic), the Maren/Calder meeting stays inside the planned single night, and the magic tolls are paid. The real problems are physical continuity. The hunters' camp is "a day's flight" (80 leagues) from Vael, yet it is six days' walk away and close enough to see the city's lights. The big Warden Brannagh is a woman in chs 28 and 31 and a man in chs 37 and 40. The white-lamp line on the hill is in the wrong place on night 149. There is also a scatter of compass bearings and day references that don't agree. I found nothing Critical.

## Major

### [B2-33-a] The camp is "a day's flight" from Vael, which can't be squared with everything else
- Where: Book 2 ch 33, line 93 (also lines 379, 387, 397)
- Quote: "a day's flight north-east of the lake"; "so bright that from a day off he could see the shape of the hill"
- Problem: A day's flight is about 80 leagues (240 miles). The hunters walk it in six days, which is 48 leagues at most, and less over a cornice pass. The bound dragons fly it between midnight and first grey. From the camp Calder sees the hill's lamps, and Lisska is seen at the camp's valley from the Library window as a small green shape. None of these work at 80 leagues.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:19 (dragon 80 leagues a day), Atlas.md:14 (on foot 8); ch 38:73 ("It's six days on foot"); ch 38:91–113 (midnight to dawn); ch 32:358–368 (Lisska seen from Vael on day 140, the same dusk ch 33:79 has her drop into the camp's valley).
- Fix: line 93: "a few hours' flight north-east of the lake, six days by the cornice pass on foot". Lines 379, 387 and 397: "a day off" becomes "a long way off", or "six days off on foot".

### [B2-33-b] On night 149 the white-lamp line is at the wrong height on the hill
- Where: Book 2 ch 33, lines 387–389
- Quote: "from the top of the hill to a line a third of the way down"
- Problem: On day 121 the line already stood a third of the way *up* the hill, and it came down from there. Ch 36 has it past Candle Row by day 131 and at Lamp Street by day 145. On night 149 the white should therefore cover most of the hill, with only a dark band along the shore. As written, the white has *shrunk* to the top third.
- Evidence: ch 29:63 ("About a third of the way up the hill"); ch 36:32 ("Candle Row on the hundred-and-thirty-first day; Lamp Street on the hundred-and-forty-fifth; the fish-market on the hundred-and-fifty-ninth"). Related, outside the range: ch 29:309 puts the line at Rope Street on day 124 and Candle Row on day 131. But chs 31:149, 32:64–70, 36:136 and 37:162 all make Candle Row the street *above* Rope Street, so ch 29's line moves uphill. Separately, chs 31:135 and 32:68–74 keep the line at Candle Row on days 138 and 140, which fits ch 36's slower pace but not ch 29's "a street a week".
- Fix: ch 33 line 387: "from the top of the hill nearly to the water: white, cold, bright". Line 389: "And along the shore, at the very foot of the hill: almost nothing." In ch 29:309, swap the two street names (Candle Row on day 124, Rope Street later), or cut the dates.

### [B2-37-a] Brannagh changes sex
- Where: Book 2 ch 37, lines 188 and 430 ("He looked at the old cow", "She saw him go down four times"), lines 558–574 ("his shape", "his lynx"); ch 40, line 311 ("with his dead sleeve-ward on his arm")
- Quote: "The big one came out of the toll-house. The short-haired one who'd let her through, on the first day... He looked at the old cow."
- Problem: The short-haired Warden who passed Maren at the lake gate is a woman when introduced, and ch 31 names her as "Old Brannagh... she". Chs 37 and 40 make the same person a man. The ch 40 notes and the Part 3 ledger also say "his", so the clash has spread into the canon notes.
- Evidence: ch 28:361 ("a big woman with short hair"), ch 28:369–375 ("Then she looked at the clerk... 'Pass,' said the Warden"); ch 31:117 ("Old Brannagh was on the gate, the big one, and she looked at the lion").
- Fix: make Brannagh a woman throughout chs 37 and 40 ("She looked at the old cow"; "She saw her go down four times"; "her shape against the frost... with her lynx"; "with her dead sleeve-ward"). Correct the ch 40 notes line 21 and the Part3_Ledger ch 40 line to match.

## Minor

### [B2-31-b] 304 years against 306 for the College's measuring
- Where: Book 2 ch 31, line 223
- Quote: "A Reader of the College of Tides, three hundred and four years ago, had measured the draw"
- Problem: Ch 34 dates the quarry discovery to 306 years ago, and says the archivist's letter was also written 306 years ago, after the forging and the waking. The College's measuring came between those two, so it was about 306 years ago. "Three hundred and four years" is how long the bird has *kept* the case (31:185, 39:82), which is a different figure. Ch 34 also leaves no gap between a spring discovery, a winter of forging and the letter, though "306" is fine as a round figure.
- Evidence: ch 34:51 ("three hundred and six years ago, after he'd cut it out of his book"), ch 34:59, ch 34:63 and ch 34:191.
- Fix: ch 31 line 223: "A Reader of the College of Tides, three hundred years and more ago, had measured the draw". Keep 304 for the keeping only.

### [B2-33-e] Compass bearings among Vael, the camp and Starwatch don't agree (Check)
- Where: Book 2 ch 33, lines 93, 341, 379 and 409; ch 35, lines 48, 412 and 430; ch 38, line 127; ch 32, line 352
- Quote: "north-east of the lake" (33:93) against "pointed, north-west, down the long valley" (33:341); "Starwatch looked north-east" (38:127); "North-east. Toward the mountains. Toward him" (35:430)
- Problem: Each of these contradicts another one:
  - If the camp is north-east of Vael, Vael lies south-west of it. Ch 38 agrees: the owl and the night-172 glow come from the south-west (38:49, 38:53). But ch 33 twice has Calder and Tasso look north-west to Vael.
  - From Starwatch, Calder looks north-west to see Vael (35:48, 35:412), so Starwatch is south-east of Vael. That means:
    - Maren looking north-east isn't looking "toward him".
    - Starwatch doesn't "look north-east" (38:127).
    - Starwatch can't be simply "west" of a camp that lies north-east of Vael (33:409).
    - The hunters' "nobody watches the lake's foot" fails against a tower that sees "the whole lake. Everything" (33:409).
  - Vael is on the *north* shore of Glasswater, so a north-east window doesn't look "across the lake" (32:352).
- Evidence: Atlas.md:143 (Vael on the north shore of Glasswater); Atlas.md:47 (the Fells run south-east from the High Crown).
- Fix: settle the triangle once: camp north-east of Vael, Starwatch south-east of Vael, Starwatch south-west of the camp. Then:
  - 33:341 and 33:379: "south-west".
  - 33:409: "a peak south-west of here".
  - 35:430: drop "Toward him, though she couldn't know".
  - 38:127: "Starwatch watched the sky."
  - 32:352: "across the city... to the mountains". Drop "across the lake with the island lying on it".

## Craft

### [B2-31-c] The narrator jumps ahead of the POV character
- Where: Book 2 ch 31, line 241; ch 38, lines 275 and 351
- Quote: "The words were older; he'd learn whose."; "He didn't know the word yet. He thought it later."; "He understood that afterwards."
- Problem: Each line steps outside the close POV to promise or explain things the POV character doesn't know yet. In ch 38 this is how Calder, who "doesn't know the word", ends up explaining the surge and why the binding slipped. It reads as the author leaning in.
- Fix: cut the clause at 31:241 (ch 34 delivers the payoff anyway). In ch 38, keep the images and drop the hindsight: "The stone was too full, and the man had drawn on it as hard as he would on ordinary stone." Line 351: "Everything in Vael was too full that afternoon. The binding held as a wet knot holds when the rope swells."

### [B2-34-a] Two window-at-dusk endings in a row
- Where: Book 2 ch 32, lines 350–376; ch 34, lines 419–443
- Quote: "The Iron Fells... White, in the last of the light. Going pink." / "It was dusk. The Fells were going pink along their tops."
- Problem: Both of Maren's Library chapters end the same way: the hill-end window at dusk, the Fells going pink, a dragon sighting, Idony at her side asking, a one-line answer. The second time the beat feels replayed, and it weakens the stronger line, "A barley farmer."
- Fix: move ch 34's echo somewhere else, such as the shingle that night with Orrun, where Idony brings the glass down to her. Or end ch 32 on the bird's "Not even me" and let ch 34 keep the window.

### [B2-35-b] Two one-eyed sages in back-to-back chapters
- Where: Book 2 chs 32 and 34 (the bird's "good eye", 15 times), ch 35 (Esk's "good eye", 7 times)
- Quote: "He turned his head sideways and looked at Calder with his good eye." (35:104)
- Problem: The Old Reader and Master Esk are both old, dry, one-eyed keepers of records who look at the hero "with its/his good eye". Esk ends up reading as an echo of the bird, and the phrase becomes a tic.
- Fix: keep the good eye for the bird. Give Esk a different physical marker, such as a squint he looks past, or his habit of tucking his beard into his belt, and cut "good eye" from ch 35 entirely.

### [B2-36-c] The "Not X. Y." correction pattern clusters in chs 36–37
- Where: Book 2 ch 36, lines 36, 80, 202, 230 and 284; ch 37, lines 222, 254, 260, 440 and 466; ch 41:205
- Quote: "It wasn't drinking the hill to fill a cup. It was drinking the hill to reach what was under it." (36:80); "Not slowed. Stopped."; "Not faded. Gone."; "Not cracked. Moved."
- Problem: Ch 36:80 is the banned "It wasn't X. It was Y." shape word for word, and 37:466 nearly is ("It wasn't fewer. It was five."). The rest are the same move in short form. Ten of them in two climactic chapters flatten the climax into a rhythm.
- Fix: 36:80 becomes "It was drinking the hill to reach what was under it." Cut at least half of the remaining "Not X." openers, keeping the strongest ("Not faded. Gone.").

### [B2-35-c] "For a long time" and "didn't know why" are used too often
- Where: Book 2 chs 31–41 (counted in the prose: "for a long time" 31 times, at least once in all but one chapter and six times in ch 35; "didn't know why" 12 times)
- Quote: "He looked at it for a long time." (ch 35 and passim)
- Problem: These are the range's most repeated filler phrases. Slow looking is a strength of the voice, but stated this often it stops doing anything.
- Fix: cut about two-thirds. Where the pause matters, show what fills it, as ch 31:233 does ("with his palm flat on the vellum and didn't move").
