# Findings: Book 2, chapters 42–54

Reviewer summary: Part Four mostly holds together. The magic rules are kept throughout: Calder's charm fails on dead ground and running water (43), Maren's stand-word is touch-range on surge-live ground (48), and the hearth-word is dead on the moor (51) but live on Clan ground (52). No reveal rule is broken. The serious problems are cross-chapter continuity. Calder's letter is a different object in each of chs 51–54. The Scald basin's size keeps changing. Ch 54 sends the companies "west" to Cotter's Cross. Ada claims nobody in her family knew why the song is sung fast, which ignores the forbidden verse her grandmother taught her in Book 1 ch 46. There are also a dozen small slips in dates, counts and directions. No Critical findings.

## Critical   (breaks the story, canon or a settled rule)

None found.

## Major      (a continuity or logic error a careful reader would notice)

### [B2-51-a] Calder's letter changes between chapters: line count, whose hand, spelling
- Where: Book 2 ch 51, lines 305 and 329; ch 52, lines 65 and 69–79; ch 53, lines 273–275; ch 54, lines 73 and 107
- Quote: ch 51 "Nine lines." … "*Ada says send you this: till the six are one agen?*"; ch 52 "Nine lines." then Ada "wrote something at the bottom. Not in his letter. Under it. Small. In her own upright hand."; ch 53 "Nine lines going uphill. And at the bottom, under the farmer's ninth line, in a small upright hand that wasn't his … *till the six are one again?*"
- Problem: In ch 51 the song line is line 9 of nine, in the same charcoal hand as the rest, spelled "agen", and Maren sees no second hand. In ch 52 Calder's draft is already nine lines before Ada adds hers, which makes ten, and Calder changes "again" to "agen". In ch 53 Maren suddenly sees Ada's different hand under the ninth line, and the spelling is "again". In ch 54 it's "His nine lines" again. A reader holding the letter up "word for word" against the dome (53) is the turn of the book, so the letter has to be the same object every time.
- Evidence: the four places above. The ledger (Part4_Ledger.md, ch 51 entry) has nine lines with Ada's as line 9.
- Fix: Settle on eight lines of Calder's plus Ada's line. Ch 52 line 65: "Eight lines." Ch 51 line 305: "Eight lines. And under them, smaller, upright, in a hand that wasn't his, one more, with its last word crossed out and spelled again his way." Ch 53 line 273: "under the farmer's eighth line", and quote it as "*till the six are one agen?*". Ch 54 lines 73 and 107: "eight lines".

### [B2-52-a] Ada says nobody kept the reason, but Book 1 gave her the hidden verse
- Where: Book 2 ch 52, lines 344–354; ch 54, line 137
- Quote: ch 52 "My mother did … And hers. I never asked why … we kept the faster and lost the why."; ch 54 "They sang the last verse fast because they knew what it meant. And they didn't tell. And then they forgot."
- Problem: In Book 1 ch 46 Ada's grandmother taught her a third, secret verse ("The last one sleeps where the oath is kept … When two hands open what one hand shut, / it wakes") and made her swear on the hearthstone never to sing it. Ada sang it to Calder in Bel's kitchen. Her family did know, and did tell, and Ada is the one who kept it. Her "I don't know" and "they didn't tell" contradict that. They also waste the best setup the turn has.
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 46, line 99 (the grandmother's oath), lines 113–135 (the verse sung, Calder present), and the notes at lines 12–23.
- Fix: Make Ada remember her grandmother. In ch 52, after "I don't know what *one again* is": "My grandmother knew something. She gave me a verse for it and made me swear. It said where the last one sleeps. It never said what *one* was." In ch 54 line 137: "My grandmother knew. She gave me the verse that says where it sleeps, and kept back what it was for. Or she never had that part either."

### [B2-48-a] The size of the Scald basin and cage keeps changing
- Where: Book 2 ch 42, line 396; ch 45, lines 54 and 234; ch 46, line 377; ch 48, lines 182 and 332; ch 49, line 421
- Quote: ch 42 "Wider than Glasswater had been."; ch 45 "A league out across the glass to the middle, and a league back"; ch 48 "inside a ring a league across" and, 150 lines later, "a league off on every side"; ch 46 and ch 49 "A league across."
- Problem: Is the field a league across, or a league in radius (two or more across)? Ch 48 says both. "Wider than Glasswater" is a third answer: the atlas draws Glasswater at roughly 12 by 7 leagues (Check: Tools/atlas/world.py:66 gives `((150, 44), 6, 3.5)`, read as semi-axes). The Scald notes in ch 42 say "a league and more across".
- Evidence: the lines cited above; Tools/atlas/world.py:66.
- Fix: Use two leagues across, a league to the middle. Ch 48 line 182: "a ring two leagues across". Ch 46 line 377 and ch 49 line 421: "Two leagues across." Ch 42 line 396: "Wide. As wide as Glasswater's lower shore."

### [B2-54-a] The companies go "west" from Ashford to Cotter's Cross
- Where: Book 2 ch 54, line 31 (and notes line 12)
- Quote: "Back over the river by the fords above Ashford. West. For Cotter's Cross."
- Problem: Hearthmere lies west of the Wend and Cotter's Cross is on the Saltreach (east) bank. Recrossing from Ashford to the Cross is a move east. As a secondary point, Ashford is 18 leagues west of the river, so "the fords above Ashford" (also ch 43, lines 422 and 432; ch 54, line 313) reads oddly for crossings of the Wend.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:70 ("the Wend on its eastern border"); Atlas.md:80 (Ashford "eighteen leagues west of the border"); world.py:141 (Ashford x=155) and world.py:173 (Cotter's Cross x=180.5).
- Fix: Line 31: "Back over the river by the fords below Greyhold. East. For Cotter's Cross." Notes line 12: "gone back east over the Wend". Optionally say "the Ashford fords" or "the fords on the Ashford road" wherever the crossings appear.

### [B2-54-b] Ch 54 adds "to his face" to the chair's condition, and Brand Halloran was named to his face in ch 49
- Where: Book 2 ch 54, line 341
- Quote: "The man who'd paid hadn't been named to his face. He'd been named in a Council room in Corsa"
- Problem: Arn's vow in ch 24 was "Till the men who paid for him are named. Every one", with nothing about "to his face". In ch 49 the line "The Varr boy at the ford … at the instruction of the Council's member" is read aloud with Brand Halloran at the table, and Marrow turns to him: "Brand … Is there anything you'd like to say?" So he was named to his face, in that room. As written, the reason the chair stays empty doesn't hold up.
- Evidence: ch 24, line 271; ch 49, lines 203 and 241–249.
- Fix: Point the condition at the people not yet named. Line 341: "The men who'd paid had been named. Not every one. Halloran had been named in a Council room in Corsa, and had said nothing. The man who'd asked for the boy at the ford, and sent the silver up the Wend, was somewhere south with a box." Ch 24's vow ("Every one") supports this.

### [B2-45-a] Check: how Tobin knew to go to the Scald, and what he did for months
- Where: Book 2 ch 45, lines 418 and 426
- Quote: "Walked … From Millbrook. In the summer. Down the Downs, and over the dead country, and here." … "I just went where the stories went."
- Problem: He threw his ring away "when the companies came over", which ch 43 line 422 puts in early summer. He says he followed the stories, including "the wall at Vael" (day 177), yet he came straight over Hearthmere to the Scald, about 71 leagues or nine days on foot, and arrived on day 233. Maren decided on Kessmoor only on day 178, and the stories ran "down the canals with the salt-barges" (ch 42, line 172), not through Hearthmere. That leaves months unaccounted for, and he knows where she is without any way of knowing.
- Evidence: ch 42, line 172; ch 43, line 422; Ashford to the Scald from world.py:141 and world.py:212.
- Fix: One line after "Every inn. Every barge.": "I went to Vael first. Got there after. A ferrywoman said the lion went over into Kessmoor, so I came round by the dead country, because the Pass was shut."

### [B2-46-c] Check: when and how the crown's envoy reached the Downs

### [B2-47-a] "He'd been wrong" about four days, but four was right, and the gap isn't "fewer" than Vael's

## Craft      (at most 8)

### [B2-42-c] "The way" similes and stock pauses (style, noted in passing)
- Where: e.g. ch 42, lines 33 and 338; ch 43, line 410; ch 44, line 191; ch 45, line 392; ch 46, line 315; ch 47, line 199; ch 50, line 238; ch 51, line 81; ch 52, lines 125 and 261; ch 53, lines 125 and 335
- Problem: The Style Guide asks for "as" in place of "the way" similes. Separately, "was quiet" and "didn't say anything" appear about 59 times across these 13 chapters, which is a tic.
- Fix: Convert the similes to "as". Cut about half the pauses, keeping the ones that carry weight.

### [B2-49-b] Lisska's arrival is the same scene every time
- Where: ch 45, lines 176–178; ch 49, lines 95–103; ch 51, lines 279–287; ch 54, lines 57–67
- Problem: Each time she comes in low, "labouring", thin, ribs showing, says "I'm back", hears "I know", and holds out the oilcloth. By the fourth arrival it reads as a template.
- Fix: Vary one or two. Ch 54, for instance, could open with Calder unwrapping the letter and only then show Lisska asleep against his leg.

### [B2-54-d] The "let him go, regretted later" beat is used twice
- Where: ch 49, lines 295–297 (Rusk: "He was wrong about that, he'd think later"); ch 54, line 299 (Reede: "He'd think afterwards that he should have meant to")
- Problem: Five chapters apart, a villain escapes because Calder doesn't act, followed by the same forward-looking regret. The second weakens the first.
- Fix: Keep the regret line for Rusk. For Reede, end on what Calder sees: "When he looked back, the cart was gone into the scrub, and the press with it."

### [B2-46-d] Chs 46 and 49 have the same shape
- Where: ch 46 (Hallowmere) and ch 49 (Corsa)
- Problem: Both chapters run: books held up to a council, someone else reads them aloud, a half-win, an offer of pardon plus a dragon contract, "No." Twice in three F chapters, it flattens the Corsa climax.
- Fix: Cut or sharply shorten the ch 49 contract offer on the balcony (lines 301–341). Rusk's flight and Vey's scene already carry the chapter.

### [B2-52-b] Ch 52 delivers the turn before ch 53 does
- Where: ch 52, lines 310–336 ("It's not about kingdoms … The six are them. The sleepers.")
- Problem: Ada and Calder reach "six sleepers, not kingdoms" on midwinter night, so Idony's big speech in the temple (ch 53, lines 287–295) repeats it. The canon turn should land in ch 53.
- Fix: Leave Ada at the question. "The song doesn't say kingdoms." / "No." / "Then what's the six?" / "I don't know. That's why I sent it." Let ch 53 supply "sleepers".

### [B2-47-c] Ch 47 explains instead of showing
- Where: ch 47, lines 109–187 ("That was the first thing … He took the second thing slowly … That was the third thing")
- Problem: A numbered lecture to himself on the tower, then the same content told again to Maren (line 199). It's the book's key mechanism, and it sags.
- Fix: Cut the numbering. Fold the third thing (the margin-argument) into the moment his hand goes flat on the rail. Since line 199 already sums up the telling, the reader needn't hear it twice.
