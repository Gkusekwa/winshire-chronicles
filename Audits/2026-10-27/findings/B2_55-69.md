# Findings: Book 2, chapters 55–69
Reviewer summary: Chapters 55–69 keep the settled rules. Maren and Calder don't meet, the control relic is never called "the seventh relic", nothing names the Council of Immortals or an elemental, the prose never mentions a book number, and there are no em dashes. The castings follow the plan too: Calder's deep sight at 59, and Maren's stand-word failing at 63. The main problems are geography and knowledge. The coast is drawn the wrong way round in three chapters (59, 64, 66). Sorrel Isle, the Elderwood and the Highcrest–Solenne leg don't fit the Atlas. Several characters know things they can't yet know: Maren knows about the white crosses, Idony about Blackwater and about Maren's boat. There is also a run of season slips, where autumn events are called "spring". Two names drift: the ledges' dragon Harl becomes a man, and Tamsin turns dun-gold.

## Critical
None found.

## Major

### [B2-59-a] The Saltreach coast is drawn backwards: Sarovel "beyond Corsa", and Corvin's ship passing Gullhaven
- Where: Book 2 ch 59, line 376; ch 64, line 259; ch 66, lines 137 and 159
- Quote: "where it went away beyond Corsa" (59:376); "where it went away past Corsa into a dark he'd never flown" (64:259); "Along this coast. Past Corsa. Past the Saltings. Past here, perhaps, in the night" (66:137); "go north and east... To Greyhold" (66:159)
- Problem: On the map the coast runs west to east as Solenne, then the Wend's mouth and Wendmouth, then Gullhaven, the Saltings, Corsa and Blackwater. Corsa is 46 leagues east of Gullhaven. So Sarovel, to the south-west, lies away from Corsa, not beyond it. A ship going from Solenne to the Wend's mouth never comes near Gullhaven, the Saltings or Corsa. Greyhold is north-north-west of Gullhaven, not north-east. Ch 59 notes:12 ("along the coast west from Blackwater") and ch 70 notes (Lisska comes "from the west... via the Wend's mouth") have it right, so these lines contradict their neighbours.
- Evidence: Tools/atlas/world.py:255–262 (Wendmouth 183,204; Gullhaven 216,198; Corsa 262,193; Blackwater 295,178), 220 (Solenne 48,172), 151 (Greyhold 174,107); Bible/Atlas.md:62 (the Coast Road).
- Fix: 59:376 "Toward the far end of the long shore, west, past Wendmouth and the river's mouth and beyond everything he knew". 64:259 "where it went away west past the Wend's mouth into a dark he'd never flown". 66:137 "Along the coast. West of here. To the Wend's mouth, and up the river. Home." (cut "Past Corsa... Past here"). 66:159 "go north. Over the Ashlings, over the Marches."

## Craft

### [B2-craft-a] "Twice" has become a tic
- Where: 13 of the 15 chapters (for example 55:288–296, 57:49, 58:434, 60:42 and 60:453, 61:114 and 61:416, 62:196, 63:449, 64:163, 66:235 and 66:267, 68:76)
- Quote: "Say it twice." / "She knew he'd said it twice." / "She read it twice."
- Problem: The farmer's-family habit is a good motif, but here it lands in nearly every chapter, often as the closing beat, and it stops meaning anything. Idony's "Twice. So he'd believe it" (61:114) and his refusal to say "no" twice (58:438) are the two that matter.
- Fix: Keep 58:434–438, 61:114 and 63:449. Cut the rest: 60:453 → "She knew his voice."; 66:267 → "He read it. Once."; 64:163 → "She read it. She put it down."

### [B2-craft-b] Two chapters end on the same beat, and the head-turn echo is used three times
- Where: ch 59:388–392 and ch 62:304–306; head-turns at 59:370–388, 62:254–290, 64:253–277
- Quote: "*The next one is south. Sarovel. The Sun Stair. Within the month.* / It had been a month." (both chapters)
- Problem: Chapters 59 and 62 close on the same quoted line and the same three-word sentence. Aurveth or the caves turning their heads south-west in silence happens three times in four Calder chapters. The third use (64) weakens the second.
- Fix: End 59 on Aurveth's stillness and cut the quotation ("She lay all night with it lifted... and he sat against her and didn't sleep."). Keep 62's version, which has the payoff.

### [B2-craft-c] Repeated similes across chapters
- Where: "wanted it so much his chest hurt" (58:282, 66:159); "like a river through a broken sluice" or "a flood through a sluice" (58:342, 60:293, 61:154); "as a cat lifts a kitten" (60:409, 66:39)
- Problem: Each is good once. Repeated within a few chapters, they read as stock phrases.
- Fix: 66:159 "and wanted it so badly he couldn't sit still". 61:154 "and the Grace came up through them and didn't stop". 66:39 "as gently as a ewe noses up a lamb".

### [B2-craft-d] Letters quoted in full again and again
- Where: Maren's answer in full at 65:355–363 and again at 66:115–119; Calder's Corsa lines at 68:306–316 and again at 69:389–399
- Problem: The reader has just read each text. Quoting it verbatim again slows chs 66 and 69 at their hinge moments.
- Fix: 66:115–119 → "*Going home. Keep your coast.* He read the rest, and then that line again." 69:389–399 → "The second was short. Six lines. The Council, the oysters, Vey's harpoons, the Clan over Corsa. *Keep the door.*"

### [B2-craft-e] "The way" similes (style rule)
- Where: about 19 in the range, for example 59:388 ("the way a dog lies"), 62:110, 62:152 ("the way a horse takes an apple"), 60:335, 60:357, 57:143, 57:225, 56:291
- Problem: Style_Guide bars "the way" similes; use "as".
- Fix: For example 62:152 "as a horse takes an apple"; 59:388 "as a dog lies when it hears something far off".

### [B2-craft-f] "It wasn't X. It was Y."
- Where: 58:134–138 ("It wasn't *hold*. / It wasn't *turn back* or *within*. / It was a shaping."); 59:162 ("This wasn't a door."); 59:216–218; 63:235–237
- Problem: This is the banned pattern, three times in two chapters.
- Fix: 58:134–138 → "Not *hold*, nor *turn back*, nor *within*. A shaping."; 59:218 → "Not his."

### [B2-craft-g] Ch 66 summarises its middle
- Where: ch 66:225–253
- Problem: The chapter's turn ("I'm staying") is dramatised well, but then a week, a page and the departure prep pass in summary. Since 68 re-covers some of the same ground, 66 ends flat on a reading of the letter.
- Fix: End 66 on Arn's "Tell her that." (66:221), or on the dusk shingle (66:257–269), and cut the week's summary to two lines, moving the page-writing into 68's Anchor-and-Net scene.
