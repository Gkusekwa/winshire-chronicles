# Findings: Book 2, chapters 70–88

Reviewer summary: The run from Gullhaven to the account is in good shape overall. Day counts inside each chapter add up, the remnant of beasts, relics and deaths matches the plan, and the hand-off into Book 3 days 0–3 mostly lines up. The serious problems are in the magic and in who knows what. Corvin's chain-words work from across a flooded cavern without his hand on the iron, and the second cage's spoken working is carried across the Wend. Idony is grey and shaking before the burnout that is supposed to make him so. Maren twice knows things she can't have heard. A few geography and travel-time slips need fixing, as does one dusk scene (362) that two chapters stage differently.

## Critical

### [B2-83-a] Corvin's chain-words work at range and across running water
- Where: Book 2 ch 83, lines 75–99
- Quote: "In the arch. He had the roll open on a binder's bent back ... and Corvin's palm flat on it." / "The chain-ends answered anyway."
- Problem: Corvin reads from the arch at the cavern's far corner. His palm is on the roll, not on the chains, and the chains are on the Titan's legs at the shut place, out in the floodwater the Wend is pouring into. The chain-ends still bite. This breaks the settled touch-range rule. It also breaks the book's own rule for chain-words: Corvin had to put his palm on the collar's iron (ch 71 line 165), and ch 47 says the words work only "with the hands on the iron". On top of that, the words cross running water.
- Evidence: Bible/Magic.md:279 (rule 4, touch range), :280 and :250 (running water; "A word said across a river doesn't land"); Book 2 ch 47 line 153; ch 71 line 165; ch 81 line 248 (the flood "Running. Brown now, cold, fast").
- Fix: Bring Corvin to the iron. Ch 83 line 77: "On the ledge's edge. The roll open on a binder's bent back beside him, and Corvin's palm flat on the nearest chain-end where it hung over the stone, on the runes inside the curve." Keep the arch for ch 84–85, which he goes back to. Idony can then take a different chain-end (line 245, "The nearest chain-end" becomes "Another chain-end").

## Major

### [B2-76-a] Idony is grey and shaking before his burnout
- Where: Book 2 ch 76, lines 282–284; ch 81, lines 74 and 104; ch 82, line 277
- Quote: "Grey all through now, not just at the temples." / "a grey-haired man on a piece of spar" / "His hand was shaking." / "A grey-haired man at her shoulder. Thin. Shaking."
- Problem: The burnout in ch 83 is canon. It turns him grey "in a breath" (line 379), and his hand starts shaking and doesn't stop (lines 383–385). If he's already grey all through and shaking in 76, 81 and 82, that moment loses its force and reads as a contradiction.
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 83 lines 377–385; Bible/Magic.md:111 and :321; BRIEF rule 3 ("hands shake ... from Book 2 ch 83 onward").
- Fix: ch 76 line 282: "Thinner. Greyer at the temples than he'd been at the Sun Stair." Cut "Shaking" at line 284 or make it "Cold." Ch 81 line 74: "a thin man on a piece of spar". Ch 81 line 104: "The lantern light shook on the stone" (from the shaking cavern, not his hand). Ch 82 line 277: "A thin man at her shoulder. Holding a lantern he didn't need."

### [B2-77-a] Maren remembers words Corvin said after she'd gone
- Where: Book 2 ch 77, line 373 (and notes line 21)
- Quote: "*Come down after us,* he'd said. On the shingle. *I'd rather she was there too.*"
- Problem: Corvin says both lines to Idony after Maren has walked off down the bar and is "gone ... into the dark, downriver". She can't have heard either of them, so her whole thought here is built on knowledge she doesn't have.
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 76 lines 334–346.
- Fix: Build the thought on what he did say to her (ch 76 line 270): "*So you'd stand aside,* he'd said. On the shingle. *I wanted you to know.*" At line 375: "She didn't know what he'd wanted. Her lost down here in the dark, out of his way, while the big one came. Or her at the vault when he got there."

### [B2-79-a] Maren tells Mother Ketch about the Gullhaven wave before any news of it could reach her
- Where: Book 2 ch 79, line 135
- Quote: "She told her about Gullhaven, as much as Lisska had given her, and the reef, and the wave."
- Problem: Lisska's last visit to Maren left at dawn on day 342 (ch 69 line 455). The reef is day 348 and the wave day 349. The only coastal news Maren gets afterwards is the boatman's report that the Titan went over the Saltings (ch 72 lines 288–292). Calder lands at the Staithe at dusk on 354 (ch 78 line 346), after this noon scene.
- Evidence: ch 69 line 455; ch 75 (day 349); ch 72 lines 288–292; ch 78 line 346.
- Fix: "She told her about the farmer's spit, and the white crosses, as much as Lisska had given her. She told her about the Scald, and the Sun Stair, and Vael."

### [B2-72-a] Saltings news reaches Greyhold in three days
- Where: Book 2 ch 72, lines 288–292 (and line 298)
- Quote: "He had it off a Saltings barge at the river's mouth." / "It went over the Saltings. Three days ago."
- Problem: The Titan crossed the Saltings at noon on day 345, and this is the afternoon of 348. The news has to go by sea from the Saltings to Wendmouth and then about 98 leagues up the Wend against the current. Barges make about 20 leagues a day downstream. "Up from Wendmouth on the tide" can't cover that distance in three days. Line 298 also calls Gullhaven "A day's flying", but Lisska needed two days for it (ch 70 line 224).
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:35 (Greyhold–Wendmouth 98 leagues), :12–20 (speeds); ch 71 line 377; ch 70 line 224.
- Fix: Give the news wings. Line 288: "A Clan rider. Down on the Staithe to water his grey, with Arn's word for Varrhold. He had it off a Saltings boat at Gullhaven." Line 298: "Two days' flying."

### [B2-80-a] The second cage's spoken working is carried across the Wend
- Where: Book 2 ch 80, lines 150–162 (and ch 79 line 67)
- Quote: "Standing along the wall's top above the iron eyes with their hands lifted." / "From the iron eyes in Greyhold's rock to the anchors on the Saltreach bank, the inside of every curve going white"
- Problem: All twelve binders speak from the Hearthmere side without touching the chains, and the working lights the links all the way over the river to the Saltreach bank. The magic rules say a word said across a river doesn't land, and that this is exactly why the first oath's runes were under the river and not across it. Touch range is broken too.
- Evidence: Bible/Magic.md:250, :279, :280.
- Fix: Split the binders and put their hands on the iron. Ch 79 line 67: "Twelve of them, in the Blue College's blue. Six along the inner wall above the eyes, six on the far bank by the anchors." Ch 80 line 152: "Six on the wall with their hands on the chains where they came out of the rock, six on his bank with their hands on the anchor-links, so no word of theirs had to cross the river."

### [B2-81-a] Haverly's edge: dead ground in 81, live ground with a muddled reason in 84
- Where: Book 2 ch 81, line 176; ch 84, line 95
- Quote: "Haverly's edge was on her hip. Dark. Dead ground." / "The hill was live now, live as it had ever been ... And the letters on the blade were still dark. Old magic. Not hers."
- Problem: Ch 74 and 77 make the old stone under Greyhold "full and drawn through", and Maren's hearth-word takes on it (ch 77 line 343), so "Dead ground" in 81 is wrong. In 84 the sword is called "Old magic", but Haverly's edge is a rune-sword that wakes when fed (plan line 335; Magic rule 6), not old magic.
- Evidence: ch 74 line 221; ch 77 lines 229, 343; Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md:335; Bible/Magic.md:280–281.
- Fix: Use one reason in both places: the stone is full of the held breath, which is old magic, and runes can't feed on it. Ch 81 line 176: "Dark. Whatever ran in this stone, the sword couldn't eat it." Ch 84 line 95: "And the letters on the blade were still dark. What filled the hill was the held breath, and the held breath was old. Haverly's runes couldn't feed on it."

### [B2-86-a] The wrong college sold the first chains
- Where: Book 2 ch 86, line 238
- Quote: "Three hundred years ago, the College of the Blue had sold Hearthmere's first knights the craft for the chains."
- Problem: It was the College of Tides, Idony's own college. The lord of the hill wrote to it and its Master sold him the chain forms, and that is the point of Idony's "My college ... I was one of its students" (lines 244–248). Corvin also says it to Idony: "Your college sold them" (ch 71 line 271).
- Evidence: ch 31 line 241 ("The charm was his college's"); ch 34 notes line 14 (item 3, College of Tides); Bible/Magic.md:302 (Idony, College of Tides).
- Fix: "Three hundred years ago, his own college, the College of Tides, had sold Hearthmere's first knights the craft for the chains." This year's sales can stay with Dane and the Blue.

### [B2-87-a] Dusk on 362 is staged two ways: Idony alone, Maren on the jetty
- Where: Book 2 ch 87, lines 40–42 and 278–308; ch 88, lines 221 and 233
- Quote: ch 87: "said the last reading aloud, to the empty kitchen, so somebody would have heard it." / ch 88: "*On the ferry-house jetty. At dusk. She was standing on the end of it watching the east*"
- Problem: In ch 87 Maren and Liss have gone down the River Road, and Idony stands in the ferry-house door at dusk on 362 with nobody to hear him. In ch 88 Maren is at the end of that jetty, a few paces from the door, watching the same six go, and Liss is home writing down his readings. Book 3 ch 1 (notes line 12) follows ch 88.
- Evidence: ch 87 lines 40–42, 306–308; ch 88 lines 221–233; Book 3 ch 1 notes line 12.
- Fix: Put her in ch 87. After line 306: "Down at the jetty's end a small figure stood with her back to him and her hand in a lion's mane, looking the same way. He didn't call to her." Line 308: "... said the last reading aloud. Down at the jetty she didn't turn round, but she'd have heard it."

### [B2-88-a] The island-sized Bell walks past the ferry house and nobody there sees it
- Where: Book 2 ch 88, line 125
- Quote: "Out of the sea. Up the Wend's valley, he heard later, through the Marches, past the fallen hill."
- Problem: Near midnight on 361 a leviathan "the size of Gullhaven" would walk up the river right past the ferry house. That is the hour Idony is at its window reading the glasses (ch 87 lines 252–274), and he only sees "dips" in quicksilver. The returning Marches folk on both banks would see it too. Nobody does.
- Evidence: ch 75 lines 132–134 (its size); ch 87 lines 252–274.
- Fix: Route it away from the river. "Out of the sea. Up over the Saltreach downs from the coast, he heard later, east of the river and well clear of it. Over the hills above Harrow's End." Or let Idony feel the ground shake at midnight and keep his eyes on the glasses.

## Minor

## Craft

### [B2-C1] "Twice" is used so often it dulls the last echo
- Where: 24 uses across 70–88, for example Rowan (74 line 215), Tam (77 line 129), Liss (79 line 227), Tobin (86 line 332), Idony (87 line 188), and six in 88.
- Problem: It's Wren's word, but Rowan, Tam, Liss, Tobin, Idony and Maren all pick it up, so Maren's "Twice" in ch 88 has no room to land.
- Fix: Keep it for Wren, Calder and Maren's last message. Cut Rowan's (74) and Tam's (77), and either Liss's (79) or her note in 88.

### [B2-C2] Corvin "never raised his voice", said about six times
- Where: ch 71 lines 97, 191, 275; ch 76 lines 136, 240.
- Fix: Keep one in each chapter at most. The quiet already shows in his lines.

### [B2-C3] The narrator keeps saying "He knew it later"
- Where: ch 80 line 274; ch 82 lines 101 and 221.
- Problem: Each one steps outside Calder's POV, and three in two chapters make it a tic.
