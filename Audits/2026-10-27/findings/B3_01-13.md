# Findings: Book 3, chapters 1–13
Reviewer summary: Part One reads cleanly and the voice holds. The hand-off from Book 2 ch 88 works, apart from one small timing slip. The biggest problem is geography. Chapters 4, 7, 10 and 11 make the Ysse Hearthmere's eastern border and put a "hundred leagues" of Hearthmere downs between it and the Greenwold, but in the Atlas Hearthmere's eastern border is the Wend, and Saltreach (the Marches and the Clan's moor) lies between Hearthmere and the Wyldmarch. Most of the other errors are travel times that can't be done in the days given (the horse-clan rider, Crale, Moss's news), a few contradictions inside the range (the glasses' six breaths, how long the ford toll lasts, Arn's reading), and two people placed where canon can't have them (Dickon as Liss's grandson, Wren at Ironhythe).

## Critical

### [B3-07-a] Hearthmere's border is put on the Ysse, and the route east never crosses the Wend
- Where: Book 3 ch 7, lines 28–32 and 196 (also ch 4 lines 290, 334, 346; ch 10 line 311; ch 11 lines 25, 375)
- Quote: "Here, at the eastern edge of everything, it was the border. Hearthmere on this side." (ch 7:32); "It came down out of Isvaren in the north, out of the lake" (ch 7:32); "between the Ysse and the oaks, a hundred leagues of downs" (ch 11:25)
- Problem: In the Atlas, Hearthmere's eastern border is the Wend. The Ysse rises in the Worldsend, fills Lake Ysse and runs south through the Wyldmarch and Saltreach to Corsa. It is not Isvaren's river and not Hearthmere's border. Saltreach's Marches and Scarfell, and then the Wend, lie between the Plains and Hearthmere. The Greenwold is north of Hallowmere, west of the Wend, and its eastern edge is only about 9 leagues from the river (world.py: Greenwold x 138–168; Wend x 176–179 at that latitude). So "Hearthmere's eastern farms" east of Greyhold, a "Hearthmere bank" of the Ysse, crown knights holding Ysse fords, and a hundred-league Hearthmere "eastern march" can't exist on the map. The prose doesn't hold together on its own terms either. In ch 4 Maren walks "east... away from the river" from Greyhold (on the Hearthmere bank) without crossing the Wend, yet at dusk she looks back at "The Saltreach bank beyond" (4:346). In ch 11, Dickon passes "the Wend crossing" (11:375) on his way from the ferry house to the Greenwold, a trip that doesn't cross the Wend at all.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:48 (the Wend marks the Hearthmere–Saltreach border; the Ysse "comes out of the Worldsend, fills Lake Ysse, and reaches the sea at Corsa"); Atlas.md:50 (Greenwold "north of Hallowmere"); Atlas.md:70 ("the Wend on its eastern border"); Atlas.md:164 (*Established*: a river marks the border); Tools/atlas/world.py:42 (the Saltreach/Wyldmarch border), :83 (the Greenwold polygon).
- Fix: The author has to choose. (a) Change the Atlas: give Hearthmere a march east of the Wend reaching to the Ysse, which also moves the Marches and the Clan's lands. (b) Keep the Atlas and change the prose. The Ysse fords become the Wyldmarch's own way off the burning Plains, so cut "Hearthmere on this side" / "into Hearthmere" / "the Hearthmere bank" in ch 7 and take the crown's knights off the Ysse. Ch 11's march becomes the empty scrub and moor on the Saltreach side. The shut border is the upper Wend at the Greenwold road's ford, where Rowan's post stands (ch 10). In ch 4, Liss takes Maren and Orrun over on the ferry before she goes east. (b) also fixes B3-10-a.

## Major

### [B3-02-a] Calder "from the river on the night the six went up": he was on the Scarfell, and the lights hadn't begun
- Where: Book 3 ch 2, line 313 (and ch 1 line 332)
- Quote: "He'd seen them from the river on the night the six went up."
- Problem: On the night the six went up, Calder was on the cave rock above Coldtarn with Wren, not at the river. Ch 1 also has the lights first appear on night 4 ("The lights came that night"), when Maren had been watching from the jetty since day 0. So he can't have seen them on night 0 from anywhere.
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 88:155 ("He was on the cave rock with Wren"); B3 ch 1:332, 360; ch 1 notes line 20 and ch 2 notes line 19 (lights nights 4 and 5).
- Fix: "He'd seen them first the night before, from the cave mouth: a glow. Tonight they were more than a glow."

### [B3-02-b] Lisska's picture shows the lintel being held, a day after it fell
- Where: Book 3 ch 2, lines 323–337 (against ch 1 lines 162–198, 310–328)
- Quote: "And in the doorway of something, under a great cracked stone, the lion-woman. Small. With one hand flat on the stone above her head."
- Problem: Calder sends Lisska on the morning of day 4. In ch 1 she arrives at dusk on day 4 and stays "for a breath", while Orrun is lying in the road. But the lintel was held and dropped on the afternoon of day 3. She can't have seen Maren holding it, and she can't have seen "three lions... lifting" either. Her picture is told as something she saw herself ("The fallen hill. From the air, at dusk").
- Evidence: ch 1:162 ("The lintel went on the third afternoon"), 198, 310–316 ("Orrun lifted his head from the road").
- Fix: Make the second half the lion's picture, passed on: "*The lion gave me this,* said Lisska. *From yesterday.*" Also change "on the heap, three lions... lifting" to the rows of laid stone and the lion lying in the road.

### [B3-04-a] The horse-clan rider's "two days ago" is impossible
- Where: Book 3 ch 4, line 164 (and lines 136–148, 188–192)
- Quote: "They came down two days ago," he said.
- Problem: Ch 4's own glasses show the descent at dawn on day 9 (the lights were still up on night 7 and gone on night 8). Ch 5 has the pens fall silent "Since dawn" on day 9, with the eastern watch's news at noon. Ch 7's notes have Tova walking the clans west "for nine days" by day 18, so they started on day 9. Even taking "two days ago" at face value, the rider would have covered roughly 95 straight-line leagues from the Plains to Greyhold in two days on one mare (the Atlas allows 25 a day for one day of hard riding). And in that same short window, riders were turned back at the fords and a council letter closing the border was written in Hallowmere and delivered to the river that morning.
- Evidence: ch 4:124–148; ch 5:131, 139; ch 7 notes line 13; Atlas.md:17 and the table at :26–43 (Greyhold–Hallowmere 57.5 by road).
- Fix: Have the rider arrive several days later (for example on day 14, with Maren leaving on day 15), or reword it so he was sent ahead "when the lights first went low over the Plains", and have the council's letter answer the tribunes' earlier question rather than the clans' flight. The ch 7 days would then need to follow.

### [B3-06-a] Wendmouth to Harrow's End isn't "thirty leagues"
- Where: Book 3 ch 6, line 295
- Quote: "Thirty leagues," said Calder. "More. By the road."
- Problem: Greyhold to Wendmouth is 98 leagues in a straight line and 122.5 by road, and Harrow's End is next to Greyhold. Calder knows the Way: his father drove it every autumn (6:27).
- Evidence: Atlas.md:35; the Book 1 ch 34 map item "to Wendmouth, 100 leagues" (Atlas.md:212).
- Fix: "A hundred leagues," said Calder. "More. By the road."

### [B3-07-b] The ford toll lasts four days, but everyone calls it "a day"
- Where: Book 3 ch 7, lines 276, 354, 370–374, 420
- Quote: "My legs," said Maren. "For a day." / "Only a day?"
- Problem: She loses her legs on day 22. She's still carried on day 24, when she says this, and feeling returns on day 26. That's four days, and two have already passed when she speaks. The notes and the plan say one day.
- Evidence: ch 7:276 ("on the twenty-second day"), 356 ("the twenty-fourth day"), 420 ("On the twenty-sixth day she could feel her feet"); ch 7 Beats line 8 ("for a day").
- Fix: Have the ridge come up on day 25 (or just say "the last day of the crossing") and feeling come back on day 26. Or keep the days and change her line to "For a few days. It's coming back," and Tova's to "Only that?"

### [B3-08-a] Moss knows of Maren's eastward journey "before I came south"
- Where: Book 3 ch 8, lines 130 and 224
- Quote: "The semaphore had it, before I came south. The clans are running west off the Plains, and she's gone east to bring them over"
- Problem: Moss arrives on day 20 with iron-wheeled carts, about 128 leagues from Ironhythe at a cart's 7 a day, so he left around day 2. The six came down on day 9 and Maren went east on day 10. In the same scene he says "There are six of them now, in the mountains. When they come down..." (8:130), so he doesn't even know they've come down.
- Evidence: ch 4 (Maren leaves day 10); Atlas.md:18 (cart 7 a day); ch 8 notes line 15 ("news by Kessmoor semaphore and the Sun Road").
- Fix: "A stage-rider had it on the Sun Road, three days back." Change 8:130 to "There are six of them. They've come down onto the Plains, the stages say."

### [B3-10-a] Crale's ride from the Ysse to Hallowmere can't be done in the time
- Where: Book 3 ch 10, lines 25–27, 137, 315 (with ch 7 lines 378–390)
- Quote: "told the council what he'd seen at dusk on the twenty-fourth day"; "Back from Hallowmere two days ahead of her, on a faster horse."
- Problem: Crale sees the clans at the Ysse at dusk on day 24. The council's summons, sent on his news, reaches the river on day 29 "on a crown horse that had been ridden too far", so Crale must have reached Hallowmere by about day 27 (the notes say day 27). By the ch 7/11 geography that's more than 100 leagues in under three days. Crale also stands at two different posts: at the Ysse ford with twenty riders (ch 7), and at the barricade at the Greenwold's edge with a dozen knights (ch 10), which ch 10 puts "eight days off, ten" from the clans.
- Evidence: ch 10 notes line 12 ("reached Hallowmere on day 27"); ch 10:329; ch 11:25; Atlas.md:17.
- Fix: Mostly solved by B3-07-a (b). Otherwise move his report to the post and send it by falcon, with Crale following later: "His report had gone by falcon. He'd come after it."

### [B3-11-a] The glasses' "six breaths" split twice
- Where: Book 3 ch 11, lines 271–273 (against ch 4 line 144)
- Quote: "He says the glasses went still for a long breath and then started again and they weren't breathing together any more. Six breaths."
- Problem: Ch 4 already has the one breath break into six, out of time with each other, at dawn on day 9. Ch 11 has the Bell's breath first separate on day 25 and the other five stop breathing together only on day 41.
- Evidence: ch 4:144 ("Six. Six breaths. Out of time with each other."); ch 4 notes line 13.
- Fix: In ch 11, make the day 41 change one of direction, not rhythm: "the six leans went still for a long breath, and then each of them leaned a different way." Change the day 25 line to "He saw one of the leans swing off south on its own."

### [B3-11-b] Dickon can't be "Liss's grandson"
- Where: Book 3 ch 11, line 251
- Quote: "Liss's grandson. Twelve."
- Problem: Liss's children are Maren (27 in Book 1, childless) and Tam (19 in Book 1). A twelve-year-old grandson would have to be Maren's nephew from a third child nobody has mentioned, and Maren meets him like a stranger.
- Evidence: Bible/Characters.md:13, 22–24, 129–130.
- Fix: "Old Tolly's grandson, from the Staithe. Twelve." Keep the ferry pony.

### [B3-12-a] Arn "couldn't" read
- Where: Book 3 ch 12, line 262
- Quote: "Arn signed without reading it, because he couldn't"
- Problem: Arn reads in Book 2 ("Arn could read. Better than Calder.") and in this range (ch 5:93–95, where he reads Gorm's ledger entry for Imra Sallow).
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 66:195; Book 2 ch 30:258; B3 ch 5:93–95.
- Fix: "Arn signed without reading it again." (This keeps the point that he trusted the clerk.)

### [B3-12-b] Wren at Ironhythe behind Arn's chair, and Calder doesn't react
- Where: Book 3 ch 12, line 132
- Quote: "Arn, for the Clan. Wren behind his chair with her hands folded."
- Problem: Calder's ten-year-old daughter is 150-odd leagues from the caves, standing behind another man's chair, and he never speaks to her, looks at her or wonders why she's there. Nothing explains it. Later in Part Two (the ch 26 ledger) she writes to him from home.
- Evidence: ch 12:132; ch 5 (Wren at the caves with Ada and Bel); ledger Part 2 ch 26.
- Fix: Cut Wren from the line ("Arn, for the Clan, with Gorm Askell behind his chair and his ledger"), or give Calder one beat with her and a reason she came.

## Minor

### [B3-01-a] Notes against prose: Mother Ketch's day and Orrun's sleep
- Where: Book 3 ch 1, notes lines 13, 17; prose lines 240, 364–374
- Quote: notes "Mother Ketch comes up from Millbrook on day 3"; prose "Mother Ketch came up on the fourth day."
- Problem: The notes put Ketch on day 3 and Orrun's day-long sleep on day 4. The prose (and the ledger) put Ketch on day 4 and the sleep after the dig stops on day 5.
- Evidence: ledger Part 1 ch 1 ("D.4 Mother Ketch").
- Fix: Correct the notes to day 4 and "after day 5".

### [B3-03-a] Maren's fingertips are still grey four days after the lintel
- Where: Book 3 ch 3, lines 284, 302 (against ch 1 line 232)
- Quote: "The fingertips grey... from the nails to the first knuckle" / "It comes back by morning. Less, every time."
- Problem: Ch 1 says that by the next morning "it was her hand again. A little stiff. A little pale." Night 7 is four days later. "Every time" implies further uses that aren't shown anywhere between day 3 and day 7.
- Evidence: ch 1:228–232.
- Fix: In ch 1, "In the morning it was her hand again, all but the tips," or add one more small use on day 4 or 5 of the dig.

### [B3-02-c] The hand-off: the second line is written at once in Book 2, after half a day in Book 3
- Where: Book 3 ch 2, line 29
- Quote: "He'd sat looking at the first one for half a day before he wrote it."
- Problem: Book 2 ch 88 ends with Wren saying "Read it again", and then "he dipped the pen, and wrote the next line."
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 88:319–323.
- Fix: "The second after noon. He'd written it straight after the first, crossed it out, and sat half a day before he wrote it again."

### [B3-05-a] Arn and Ulric "on their two dragons" and Ulric "on his grey"
- Where: Book 3 ch 2, line 209; ch 5, line 353
- Quote: "On their two dragons, red and grey"; "Ulric at the front on his grey"
- Problem: Ch 18 makes Arn never chosen before Ildra, and ch 15 makes Ulric never chosen ("He rode behind Sigge Brack on Sigge's grey"). Ch 15 explains the coast's "Ulric's grey", but ch 5 is told from Arn's point of view, and Arn would know whose dragon it is.
- Evidence: ch 15:234; ch 18 notes line 12 and prose 76–78.
- Fix: ch 2: "On two of the Clan's dragons, red and grey, behind their riders." ch 5: "Ulric at the front behind Sigge Brack on Sigge's grey."

### [B3-05-b] Check: Teo "with a green dragon nobody else could handle"
- Where: Book 3 ch 5, line 321
- Quote: "Teo had been like that at sixteen with a green dragon nobody else could handle."
- Problem: Ch 18 makes Teo never chosen and never bonded. Readers may take this line as a bond. Book 1 ch 48 has only Teo throwing apples at Lisska.
- Evidence: ch 18 notes line 12; Book 1 ch 48:101.
- Fix: "...at sixteen, throwing apples at a green dragon nobody else dared go near."

### [B3-04-b] Davin's letter has been on the table three days, not two
- Where: Book 3 ch 4, line 222
- Quote: "It had been on the table two days."
- Problem: Davin arrived on day 6 (ch 3) and this is the night of day 9.
- Fix: "three days".

### [B3-06-b] When Gullhaven's people were put off the land
- Where: Book 3 ch 6, line 41 (with ch 2 lines 271–279)
- Quote: "Two weeks since," said Ulric. "...They put them off on the last day of the year."
- Problem: The last day of the year is day 3. That's nine days before day 12, not two weeks. Ulric's own news on day 4 (ch 2) already had them on the road, brought by a rider who came up the Coast Road and over the Ashlings, which takes days.
- Fix: "They put them off before the year was out. Ten days before."

### [B3-06-c] Style: a "the way" simile, and a repeated comparison
- Where: Book 3 ch 6, line 405 (and line 27)
- Quote: "the way people look at a country they've never been to"
- Problem: This breaks the "as" rule, and it repeats the line 27 simile ("as you know things about a country you've never been to").
- Fix: "as people look at a field they've been given."

### [B3-09-a] Check: Lisska "asked [Aurveth] as I came over"
- Where: Book 3 ch 9, line 377
- Quote: "It turned away from them three days ago, Aurveth says. I asked her as I came over."
- Problem: Aurveth is at Harrow's End, in the Marches. Lisska was coming south from the Plains to Corsa, more than 80 leagues east of the Marches. Harrow's End isn't on her way, and kin-speech needs sight.
- Fix: "I went round by the farm and asked her."

### [B3-10-b] Greyhold to Hallowmere is west, not "north and east"
- Where: Book 3 ch 10, line 43
- Quote: "Two days by the Marches road. North and east, over the downs."
- Problem: Hallowmere is 46 leagues due west (57.5 by the King's Road). Two days is also faster than a hard rider's 25 leagues a day, unless greyharts are faster than horses.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:33, 56.
- Fix: "Three days by the King's Road. West, over the downs."

### [B3-11-c] The Furnace goes "south and west" toward Kessmoor
- Where: Book 3 ch 11, line 281
- Quote: "The hot one, the one with the chain on it, is going south and west. Toward Kessmoor."
- Problem: Kessmoor and the Scald lie north-west of the Plains' western edge. The ledger has "south-west" too.
- Evidence: world.py: scald (94, 68); kess_temple (94, 70.5).
- Fix: "is going west and north. Toward Kessmoor."

### [B3-12-c] Check: Calder's flight route, and Ironhythe "at the Fells' foot"
- Where: Book 3 ch 12, lines 40–42, 50; ch 8, lines 310, 318
- Quote: "West along the coast, and then up the Coldrun where it came down out of the Fells"; "a valley at the foot of the Iron Fells"
- Problem: From Corsa, "west along the coast and up the Coldrun" means going right round Sarovel to Gearmouth, more than twice the 250-league straight line. And in the Atlas, Ironhythe is on the Coldrun about 40 leagues west of the Iron Fells.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:47, 115; world.py: ironhythe (62, 45); Iron Fells polyline x 100–134.
- Fix: ch 12: "North-west, over Hearthmere, and then down the Coldrun's valley." ch 8 and 12: "a valley on the Coldrun, under the moors" for "at the Fells' foot".

### [B3-12-d] "the old realm's chart"
- Where: Book 3 ch 12, line 290
- Quote: "The tide-reader's chart: the old realm's chart"
- Problem: Maren knows it's the College of Tides' founding chart, about 200 years old, not something from the old realm (ch 3:188; ch 4:42).
- Fix: "the College's oldest chart".

### [B3-12-e] When the Blackwater raid happened
- Where: Book 3 ch 12, line 382; ch 13, line 95
- Quote: "I was there before the winter." / "Last winter. By dragons."
- Problem: Book 2 ch 56 is days 291–300, in winter. Ch 13 (and its notes, "before the winter") disagree with each other, and ch 12 has it wrong.
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 56 notes line 6.

### [B3-13-a] Gannet misremembers Millbrook
- Where: Book 3 ch 13, line 333
- Quote: "in a grey coat, with a practice sword... The week before the harvest fair."
- Problem: Book 1 ch 7 is "the day before the fair", and Maren was in "her Greyhold blue".
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 7, notes line 9 and prose lines 17, 111.

### [B3-13-b] Check: "the hundredth day of the year"
- Where: Book 3 ch 13, line 177
- Quote: "falls due on the hundredth day of the year"
- Problem: Book 3 counts its days from dusk on day 0, but "the last day of the year" (day 3) has already passed in this book, so the 100th day "of the year" would fall about four days later than Fosse's "Thirty-eight days" (day 62 + 38 = 100). Chapters 14 and 17 depend on it.
- Fix: Drop "of the year": "falls due on the hundredth day."

## Craft

### [B3-01-b] "I know you know" twice in one chapter
- Where: Book 3 ch 1, lines 110 and 256
- Quote: "I know you know," said Thurl. "I'm writing it down." / "I know you know," said Mother Ketch. "I'm telling you anyway."
- Problem: Two minor characters use the same exchange, with the same reply pattern, 150 lines apart. The second makes the first look like a formula.
- Fix: Keep Ketch's. Give Thurl: "Then you'll not mind me writing it down."

### [B3-02-e] "Read it twice" dilutes Wren's "twice"
- Where: ch 2:293, ch 4:204, ch 8:260, ch 10:31, ch 11:305 (plus Davin, ch 3:324–330)
- Quote: "She read it twice."
- Problem: Calder, Maren, Tobin and Rowan all "read it twice" in five chapters. That wears down the Wren motif the range is carefully building (ch 1:324, 2:403, 2:443, 3:330).
- Fix: Keep it for Wren's line of descent (Calder, Davin) and vary the rest ("She read it to the end, and then the seal").

### [B3-04-c] Ch 4 opens by restaging ch 3's margin scene
- Where: Book 3 ch 4, lines 24–50
- Quote: "I said to Davin it's like a lamp. A lamp left burning in an empty house."
- Problem: Ch 3 has just shown the silk, the six *errors* and the lamp image at length. Ch 4 goes through them again before reaching the new beat (Orrun's memory).
- Fix: Open ch 4 on Orrun's head in the doorway and "Light," and give the silk only two lines.

### [B3-all-a] Stock beats: "didn't say anything" and "for a long time"
- Where: across the range: "didn't say anything" 14 times (ch 1 ×4, ch 7 ×4), "for a long time" 16 times
- Quote: "He looked at the lion for a long time."
- Problem: These pauses do the same job in almost every scene, and they start to read as filler in the lean style.
- Fix: Cut about half. Where the pause carries weight, name what fills it (what the person looks at, or does with their hands).
