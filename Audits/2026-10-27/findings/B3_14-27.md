# Findings: Book 3, chapters 14–27
Reviewer summary: Part Two reads well and mostly holds together: the salt contract, the copper contract, Corsa's fall and the crossing all pay off cleanly, and the reveal and magic rules hold throughout. The serious problems are hand-offs between POVs. In ch 27 Maren sees a fleet that ch 28 has already reduced, and she knows figures she couldn't know. Ch 23's evacuation runs the wrong way on the Atlas, and its news from Corsa arrives impossibly fast. Ch 16 makes Idony's 200-year-old chart older than the split. Kit's own POV chapters say she doesn't know the reason for the rule, which chs 26–29 then contradict.

## Critical   (breaks the story, canon or a settled rule)

### [B3-22-a] Chapter title names a death (Check)
- Where: Book 3 ch 22, line 1
- Quote: "# Chapter 22: Ulric's Pour"
- Problem: Rule 4 says titles must not give away twists, and the plan adds that "None of these titles names a death". A reader looking at the contents page learns three chapters early that Ulric dies, which is the turn of ch 19. The plan uses the same working title, so this may have been accepted. Flagged as Check against the rule as written.
- Evidence: `Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md`:570; BRIEF rule 4; Ulric dies in ch 19 (`Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md`:58).
- Fix: Retitle with something that doesn't name the dead, such as "The Black Stone" or "Pour With Me". Don't use "Who Pours", which ch 85 already uses.

## Major      (a continuity or logic error a careful reader would notice)

### [B3-27-a] Maren sees fourteen ships that ch 28 has already reduced to ten
- Where: Book 3 ch 27, lines 384 and 394
- Quote: "Eleven. Then three more, behind, close together." / "They were ten days off. More."
- Problem: This is after midnight on d.126. By then, in ch 28, Rennick's three black ships have turned back (d.121) and the *White Heron* has sunk (d.123), so ten ships remain. There is also a visibility problem: lights "ten days off" could not be seen from a hilltop. The fleet reaches pier twelve on d.127, which is still days of sailing from the shore.
- Evidence: ch 28 lines 64–66 (Rennick turns back d.121) and line 90 (*White Heron* lost d.123); ch 28 lines 126–128 (the Bell rises near pier twelve, d.127).
- Fix: "Ten. Strung out, low, and no more behind them." Replace "They were ten days off. More." with "They were days off yet."

### [B3-27-b] Maren knows the fleet's figures and pilots, which she couldn't know
- Where: Book 3 ch 27, line 392
- Quote: "Five hundred and forty people, at six hundred gold a head." / "with two pilots reading paper"
- Problem: Maren left Blackwater on night 94, when the board said five hundred and four hundred berths were sold. The 540 passengers, the 600-gold price and the two pilots reading Hal's charts come from Calder's ch 26 (d.112–114), after she had sailed.
- Evidence: ch 20 line 165 (four hundred sold, board at five hundred by d.93); ch 26 lines 102 and 158–162.
- Fix: "Four hundred people at least, at five hundred gold a head, coming to a land without Titans... Sailing at night, without a Merrin."

### [B3-25-a] In her own POV, Kit says she doesn't know why the rule exists, though she does
- Where: Book 3 ch 25, line 292 (also ch 14 line 271)
- Quote: "I don't know why. My father didn't. My great-grandfather made it and never said."
- Problem: Before sailing (night 93), Kit left Calder a message explaining exactly why: Abram's brother slept ashore and never woke. Ch 29 says she heard it from her father. In ch 25, Kit's own POV, she tells the crew she doesn't know, and her narration gives no sign that this is a lie. In ch 14 she also tells Maren "he never told me why", but that is in Maren's POV, so a lie there is fair. The ch 25 line reads as a POV cheat.
- Evidence: ch 26 line 90 (the message); ch 27 lines 226–230 ("I didn't tell you. I'm telling you now."); ch 29 line 103 ("She'd heard it once, from her father, at the tiller").
- Fix: In ch 25, after the speech, add a line in Kit's head: "That wasn't all of it. She'd heard the rest once, from Hal, at the tiller. She'd not say it with the knight listening."

### [B3-23-a] Hallowmere's evacuation runs the wrong way on the Atlas
- Where: Book 3 ch 23, lines 62, 74, 76, 122, 334 and 448
- Quote: "Out by the King's Road west toward Greyhold and the Marches."
- Problem: The King's Road runs Stillhaven–Hallowmere–Greyhold, and the Wend is Hearthmere's eastern border, so Greyhold and the Marches lie *east* of Hallowmere (Atlas data: Hallowmere x128, Greyhold x174). In the prose the columns go "west toward Greyhold" but leave by the *east* gate, toward the oncoming Titan, and pass behind Beacon Ridge, "the last chalk ridge east of the city". Yet the old man walks out of the *west* gate (line 76), and the last cart goes "over the far side of the valley, west" (line 334). The Atlas also puts the Candle Downs south of the Mere, not east of the city.
- Evidence: `Bible/Atlas.md`:56 (King's Road), :66 (Wend on Hearthmere's eastern border), :78 (Hallowmere on the Mere's north shore), :82 (Downs south of the Mere); `Tools/atlas/world.py`:134, 151, 97.
- Fix: Send the columns out of the west gate "by the King's Road south toward Stillhaven, and the Sun Road" (both lead away from an eastern approach), and drop "toward Greyhold and the Marches" in lines 62 and 448. Make the last column "still coming out of the west gate" (lines 122, 246) and the first cart leave by the west gate too (line 74).

### [B3-23-b] News of Corsa's fall reaches Hallowmere in one day by horse
- Where: Book 3 ch 23, lines 432–438
- Quote: "The courier came on the eighty-ninth." / "A crown courier from the Wend crossing, on a lathered horse, before the Downs."
- Problem: Corsa falls at mid-morning on d.88 (ch 19). Corsa to Hallowmere is well over 150 leagues (Corsa–Wendmouth 80 straight, Wendmouth–Greyhold 98, Greyhold–Hallowmere 46). That is more than a week by hard riding (25 leagues a day) and two days even by dragon (80 a day).
- Evidence: ch 19 lines 83–93; `Bible/Atlas.md`:15–19 and :33–39.
- Fix: Move the courier after the battle ("The courier came on the ninety-ninth") and cut "She'd had it before the battle, and put it away, and taken it out again afterwards." The current lines 440–442 then work as written.

### [B3-16-a] The founding chart is made older than the split, and its six readers span six hundred years
- Where: Book 3 ch 16, lines 270 and 282
- Quote: "because the chart was older than the split" / "six scholars across six hundred years"
- Problem: In chs 3–4 the founding chart is "two hundred and some years old", with *error* written "across two hundred years". It shows the six kingdoms, which is the post-split world. A 200-year-old chart can't show the old realm's coast or "land" where the Shatters are now, and the six hands can't span six centuries. Line 282's "The edge of the world, as the old realm had known it" has the same problem.
- Evidence: ch 3 lines 188 and 206; ch 4 line 42. Ch 12 line 290 also calls it "the old realm's chart" (outside my range, same fix).
- Fix: Line 270: "Blackwater's inlet, a notch under the mountains' end. The edge of the Shatters, and past it, nothing but sea and the readers' marks." Line 282: "six readers across two hundred years". Line 274: cut "across what had been land when the chart was drawn".

### [B3-24-a] "It slept on this road" contradicts the Bell going home to the Saltings, and the settled reef
- Where: Book 3 ch 24, lines 224–228
- Quote: "It slept on this road," / "Its temple was on it,"
- Problem: In ch 17 and ch 19 the Bell goes "home. To where it slept" by turning in off the Saltings. Canon settles its reef as two leagues off Gullhaven, which is far west of Blackwater. The drowned road runs south-west from Blackwater, so the temple can't lie on it. The ledger notes the clash and Q29 settled the distance, but this line still contradicts it.
- Evidence: ch 17 line 345; ch 19 line 119; BRIEF settled fact 5 ("two leagues off Gullhaven"); `Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md`:678 (Q29); Atlas data, Bell at x217 and Blackwater at x295.
- Fix: "'It's going the same way,' she said at last. / 'Toward the light,' said Idony. 'Like us.'" Cut the two lines about sleeping on the road and the temple.

### [B3-22-b] Calder is in two places when Lisska leaves, and her flight to Blackwater takes too long
- Where: Book 3 ch 22, lines 45 and 61–63 (and line 31)
- Quote: "He'd flown north on the ninetieth" / "He'd lain on the *Merry Agnes* and felt her go out of reach along the coast"
- Problem: Lisska leaves on d.92, but Calder left the *Merry Agnes* on d.90 and is at Skarrow from the third night of the pyre. If she left from Corsa, Blackwater is about 36 leagues away (half a day by dragon), so leaving on d.92 she would have arrived before the *Kittiwake* sailed on night 94. She couldn't be "too late" at dawn 95.
- Evidence: ch 22 lines 29–31 and 45; Atlas data (Corsa x262/y193 to Blackwater x295/y178); `Bible/Atlas.md`:21 (dragon 80 leagues a day).
- Fix: Make it "Lisska had gone east on the ninety-fourth. On her own, from Skarrow. He'd been too sick to stop her. He'd lain by the pyre and felt her go out of reach over the moor..." Skarrow to Blackwater (about 140 leagues) then fits arriving at dawn on d.95.

### [B3-26-a] Lisska is sent to Varrhold and over the Shatters on the same day
- Where: Book 3 ch 26, lines 186–196 and 286 (and line 214)
- Quote: "Lisska flew south-west on the hundred-and-fifteenth." / "Calder had sent Lisska to Varrhold with a letter on the hundred-and-fifteenth"
- Problem: She can't spend d.115 flying out over the sea and back by dusk and also carry a letter about 140 leagues to Varrhold, then be back "on a rock above" the headland on night 116. Her report, "I couldn't tell one way from another after the first day", also contradicts a flight that came "back at dusk" the same day.
- Evidence: ch 26 lines 186, 190, 196, 214 and 286; Atlas data (Varrhold x184/y95, Blackwater x295/y178).
- Fix: Send the letter on the hundred-and-twelfth, when Calder arrives, by one of Arn's twenty or by Kharrak. Change Lisska's line to "I couldn't tell one way from another after the first few stones."

### [B3-14-a] Gannet's looks and Maren's coat don't match Book 1
- Where: Book 3 ch 14, lines 159 and 171
- Quote: "Thick. Fifty, perhaps. A Wendmouth face." / "A student in a grey coat."
- Problem: In Book 1 Gannet is "long and thin and brown, with a sharp nose and quick black eyes" and a silver ring in each ear (Characters: "Long, thin, silver earrings"). At Millbrook Maren is "in Greyhold blue". Grey is the inner circle's colour. Ch 13 repeats the grey coat and says "the week before the harvest fair", but Book 1 ch 7 is the day before the fair (outside my range, same fix).
- Evidence: `Book 1/Chapters/Chapter 07 - The Caged Beast.md`:9, 33, 111, 115; `Bible/Characters.md`:198.
- Fix: "Long. Thin. Fifty, perhaps, and brown as a boot. A silver ring in each ear." Line 171: "A student in Greyhold blue."

## Minor      (small slips: a wrong day, a name typo, a count off by one)

### [B3-18-a] Harrow's End to Corsa is not sixty leagues
- Where: Book 3 ch 18, line 380
- Quote: "The same hour. Sixty leagues apart."
- Problem: By the Atlas data, Harrow's End (x177, y103) to Corsa (x262, y193) is about 124 leagues in a straight line.
- Evidence: `Tools/atlas/world.py`:161, 259; `Bible/Atlas.md`:39 (Wendmouth–Corsa alone is 80).
- Fix: "A hundred and twenty leagues apart."

### [B3-16-b] Abram's descendants are counted as four generations but listed as five
- Where: Book 3 ch 16, line 132
- Quote: "and his son had, and his son's daughter, and the man who'd drowned, and the woman holding the book. Four generations"
- Problem: Abram is Kit's great-grandfather and Wenna her grandmother (ch 14 line 271; ch 26 line 90), so the line runs Abram → Wenna → Hal → Kit. The text adds a son and makes Wenna his daughter, which gives five generations and makes Abram Kit's great-great-grandfather.
- Evidence: ch 14 line 271; ch 26 line 90; ch 27 line 226.
- Fix: "and his daughter had, and her son, who'd drowned, and the woman holding the book. Four generations".

### [B3-20-a] "Blackwater's first chain" after three earlier chains
- Where: Book 3 ch 20, lines 205 and 355 (against line 41)
- Quote: "Three chains." / "Blackwater's first chain."
- Problem: Ch 13 and ch 20 line 41 have the Council hanging three chains at Blackwater in Hal's lifetime: one stolen, one sold back, one with a drowned harbour-master.
- Evidence: ch 13 line 87; ch 20 line 41.
- Fix: "Blackwater's first chain that's stayed up a night." Line 355: "Blackwater's fourth chain."

### [B3-24-b] Pier count outruns the stated pace
- Where: Book 3 ch 24, lines 26, 50 and 244
- Quote: "Every half-day, or every day, a column out of the sea ahead."
- Problem: Pier forty-one comes on d.97 and pier nine on d.110, which is thirty-two piers in thirteen days. At no more than one pier every half-day, with no night sailing, the most they could pass is about twenty-six. From pier nine to pier two (d.110–114) the pace is right.
- Evidence: ch 24 lines 26 and 244; ch 24 line 98 (no sailing at night).
- Fix: "Two in a day, sometimes three, the stones closer together on this stretch," or move the ninth pier to about d.112 and adjust the dates after it.

### [B3-24-c] Sailing out of the reef-maze at night, at an impossible moonrise (Check)
- Where: Book 3 ch 24, line 28
- Quote: "the last tooth of rock gone astern at moonrise"
- Problem: The Merrins don't sail at night (ch 24 line 98; ch 26 line 168), yet here they clear the reefs in the night. Ch 21 also puts the moon a day past new on d.94, and a moon only two or three days old rises in the morning, not in the night.
- Evidence: ch 21 line 29; ch 24 line 98.
- Fix: "They'd left the Shatters' reefs behind them the evening before: the last tooth of rock gone astern at sunset."

### [B3-16-c] Two spring tides twenty-five days apart (Check)
- Where: Book 3 ch 16, line 356 (and ch 13 line 29)
- Quote: "The eighty-fifth," said Kit. "The bottom of the spring tide."
- Problem: Ch 13 puts the bottom of a spring tide on d.60. Spring tides come about fifteen days apart, so the next ones fall around d.75 and d.90. D.85 would be near neap tide.
- Evidence: ch 13 line 29.
- Fix: Make it "The bottom of the tide. The lowest this month," or move ch 13's look at the stone to d.70.

### [B3-14-b] Small counts in ch 14
- Where: Book 3 ch 14, lines 35 and 83–85
- Quote: "In twelve words, at Ironhythe" / "Ten days."
- Problem: Calder's tip in ch 12 runs to more than forty words. Leaving Ironhythe on d.65 and reaching Blackwater at dusk on d.76 is eleven days.
- Evidence: ch 12 line 382; ch 14 line 27.
- Fix: "In a handful of words" and "Eleven days."

### [B3-14-c] The "salvage hold" is in two places, and the ketch's glass reading is told two ways
- Where: Book 3 ch 14, lines 345 and 355; ch 16 lines 38 and 194
- Quote: "The salvage hold. Under the deck, aft, among the ballast." / "It's never climbed on water in its life."
- Problem: Ch 16 puts "the Merrins' salvage hold" under the house, between the stilts. In ch 14 the ketch's hold carries that name. Ch 14 also has Idony thrilled that the glass climbs on water, but ch 16 says he "thought it might be the sea" and dismissed it.
- Evidence: ch 16 notes line 12, prose lines 28, 38 and 194.
- Fix: Ch 14 line 345: "The *Kittiwake*'s hold." Ch 16 line 194: "A finger's width. He'd hardly dared believe it."

### [B3-14-d] Orrun says what Lisska is looking for without hearing her
- Where: Book 3 ch 14, lines 389–393
- Quote: "Too far. She can't hear me. I can't hear her." ... "She's looking for the bell one. In the sea."
- Problem: Out of kin-range, Orrun can't know her errand.
- Fix: "*No,* said Orrun. *He's with her. She's looking at the sea. Something in it.*"

### [B3-15-a] Calder's return from Ironhythe: notes against prose, and the travel time
- Where: Book 3 ch 15, line 36 (notes line 12)
- Quote: "On the sixty-seventh, the day after he got back from Ironhythe."
- Problem: The notes and ledger say he got back on d.67, but the prose puts it on d.66. He left Ironhythe at dawn on d.65 after a two-day outbound flight (ch 12 notes), so getting back on d.66 is also fast. The distance is about 250 leagues in a straight line.
- Evidence: ch 12 notes line 12; `Book 3/Notes/Part2_Ledger.md`:13.
- Fix: "On the sixty-eighth, the day after he got back from Ironhythe", and shift "the sixty-seventh" at line 86 to the 68th to match. Or leave d.67 and change the notes.

### [B3-19-a] The Bell's homecoming is credited to Aurveth, who wasn't there (Check)
- Where: Book 3 ch 19, line 119
- Quote: "Aurveth had said it would go home and lie down where it had slept."
- Problem: Aurveth is at Harrow's End. In ch 17 it is Lisska, at Corsa, who says "it's going home. To where it slept", and Idony's reading (ch 2) is the source.
- Evidence: ch 17 line 345.
- Fix: "Lisska had said it would go home..."

### [B3-23-c] The named dead don't add up to 412 (Check)
- Where: Book 3 ch 23, line 394
- Quote: "Pryne. Crale's dozen. Ninety of the crown men. A hundred and sixty of the militia... Six knights of Greyhold."
- Problem: Those listed come to about 269, plus a wagon crew and the thirty lost on d.92. That is well short of "Four hundred and twelve."
- Fix: Raise the counts, for example "Two hundred of the crown men. A hundred and sixty of the militia..."

### [B3-23-d] The falcon bells may be reversed (Check)
- Where: Book 3 ch 23, lines 192, 212 and 224
- Quote: "When it turned left... its bell rang once, long. Right, twice." / "*one, one, one*. The head came round to the north"
- Problem: The Titan is walking west, so turning north is turning to its right, which should be two rings, and turning south toward the engines should be one ring. The bells are only correct if "left" means the riders' left as they face east. The text doesn't say so.
- Fix: Line 192: "When its head swung north, the falcon over it swung with it, and its bell rang once, long. South, twice." Then line 224 should read "*One, one*" instead of "*Two, two*", or swap 212 and 224.

### [B3-26-b] Notes against prose: when Kit gave Brask the message
- Where: Book 3 ch 26, notes line 15
- Quote: "Kit came to him on the night of 93"
- Problem: Ch 20 has Kit alone in the stern cabin all night on d.93. The ch 26 prose only says "Before she went", which is fine.
- Evidence: ch 20 lines 269–271.
- Fix: Change the notes (and the ledger) to "the night of 92" or "dusk 93".

### [B3-17-a] "The last pier" names both ends of the road
- Where: Book 3 ch 17, line 133 (and ch 13's reef-stone notes)
- Quote: "stood it on the last pier!"
- Problem: In the Merrin book, the pier near Blackwater would come before forty-one, and from ch 25 on "the last pier" means pier one, at the shore ("The last of them", ch 25 line 38). Readers may not know which end is meant.
- Fix: "stood it on the road's first pier!" or "on the pier at this end!"

## Craft      (at most 8)

### [B3-craft-a] The "Not X. Y." tic
- Where: ch 14:51 "Not running. Walking backwards."; ch 19:233 "Not fire. What fire came from."; ch 23:114 "Not burning. Dying."; ch 24:280 "Not wrong. Missing."; ch 25:30 "Not fell. Sat."; ch 25:304 "Not lamps. Not fires."
- Problem: This is the near cousin of the banned "It wasn't X. It was Y." pattern, and it recurs in almost every chapter of the range.
- Fix: Keep two of them, for example ch 19's and ch 23's, and make the rest plain statements: "Walking backwards. Fast." / "He sat."

### [B3-craft-b] Similes reused word for word
- Where: "like a cat's purr through a floor" (ch 16:64 and ch 27:274); "as mist comes up off a field" (ch 25:304 and ch 27:258); shadows rising "as if the rock were water" (ch 17:89, ch 21:169, ch 25:160).
- Problem: These read as stock phrases rather than echoes.
- Fix: Keep the first use of each. For the shadows, try "as a swimmer comes up out of a pool" at 21:169 and "as if it had been waiting under the slab all along" at 25:160.

### [B3-craft-c] Verbal fillers pile up
- Where: "didn't say anything" occurs 21 times across chs 14–27 and "a long time" 33 times (e.g., ch 16:190, 236; ch 17:205; ch 26:298, 216).
- Problem: They flatten the beats they're meant to weight.
- Fix: Cut about half. A silence usually lands on its own as a one-line paragraph.

### [B3-craft-d] Characters think in the ledger's cross-book day numbers
- Where: ch 19:87 "On the three hundred and forty-ninth day of last year, at noon."; ch 23:342 "at noon on the three hundred and fifty-fifth day".
- Problem: Book 2's count (day 0 = sunrise of Book 1 ch 79) is the author's bookkeeping, and "of last year" makes it worse. In a character's head it reads as meta talk.
- Fix: "At Gullhaven. At the end of the winter, at noon." / "on Greyhold's north tower at noon, the day the hill came down."

### [B3-craft-e] Gannet's betrayal is over-signposted
- Where: ch 16:332–334 (reads upside down, easily); ch 17:319 (smiles at Maren); ch 21:63–69 ("knew... a great many things... she'd said nothing"); ch 27:150–154 (the heavy bag); the smile 11 times in the range.
- Problem: Each plant is good on its own. Stacked in one part, with Maren noting in so many words that he knows too much, they give away the chs 29/44/60 payoff early.
- Fix: Drop Maren's explicit thought in ch 21 (lines 63–69). Let the white-spar knowledge stand without comment.

### [B3-craft-f] Ulric's death is reported, not shown
- Where: ch 19, lines 303–313
- Problem: Ulric's death is the chapter's turn, and it arrives secondhand as "Sigge told it", in summary, while Calder is unconscious. The choice is defensible, but the moment is thin compared with Marrow's and Cobb's.
- Fix: Give Calder one real sensory glimpse before he blacks out, such as the boy going up into Sigge's arm and Ulric's hand opening. Then let Sigge's account fill in the rest.
