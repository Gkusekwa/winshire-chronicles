# Findings: Book 3, chapters 57–71

Reviewer summary: The run from midwinter through the betrayal holds together well inside itself. The hints are planted and paid off, Maren's injuries carry through from chapter to chapter, the counts mostly agree (olifants 60 to 56, bolt-throwers 60 + 40, Davin's lists), and none of the settled reveal rules is broken. The serious trouble is geography against the Atlas and earlier books. The lower Wend has shrunk from about 95 leagues to 12, and Cotter's Cross has moved north of the hub. Two direction errors also undercut the battle's layout: the Horned Sun is called south-west while it stands on the east bank, and the Last Titan walks "north" along a ward that runs east to west. There is also one object history that contradicts Books 1 and 2: the five links.

## Critical   (breaks the story, canon or a settled rule)

### [B3-58-a] The lower Wend is twelve leagues long; canon makes it about ninety-five
- Where: Book 3 ch 58, lines 28, 36, 218, 424, 474 (and ch 60 lines 25, 35; ch 68 line 153)
- Quote: "Twelve leagues of the lower Wend between the hub and the sea" (58:28); "Down the Wend in the morning, low, over the grey water, to the mouth and back. Twelve leagues." (58:36); "It was twelve leagues off, at the Wend's mouth." (68:153)
- Problem: The hub is the eddy where the island (the Old Ruin) was, 3.5 leagues from Greyhold. Wendmouth is about 98 leagues from Greyhold in a straight line. Several things in this range only work over twelve leagues: Calder's daily round trip to the mouth, the market towed to the Staithe and counted "two days later", the 8-league barge run in ch 60, Aurveth's view "from the ridge twelve leagues off", and the shadow's run in ch 68.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:8 (Greyhold to the Old Ruin 3.5) and :35 (Greyhold to Wendmouth 98 straight, 122.5 by road); Book 1 ch 32:298 "Wendmouth was a hundred leagues south"; Book 1 ch 50:58 "A hundred and twenty leagues on the drove road"; Book 2 ch 72:298 "to Wendmouth... A day's flying". A dragon flies 80 leagues a day (Atlas:20), so a daily round trip of about 190 leagues is impossible.
- Fix: This needs the author's ruling, because Book 1's prose fixes Wendmouth at a hundred leagues and the Atlas can't simply be changed. The smallest consistent version keeps the Book 3 events and rescales them:
  - ch 58:28 and :36 become "ninety leagues of the lower Wend"; Calder is posted at the mouth and doesn't fly a daily round trip ("So from the two-hundred-and-seventy-eighth he lay at the mouth with Lisska, and flew the river up and down it in stages").
  - The Ostrey barge is stopped a few leagues below the hub, which keeps "two days at a pole's pace".
  - Aurveth's sight comes "from the ridge, ninety leagues off".
  - The market is towed only to a refuge near the mouth. Its people come up by the Drover's Way, and the barges carry only the old, the sick and the stores. That means ch 60's "eight leagues", "fifteen days" and the round-trip arithmetic must be reworked.
  - ch 68:153 becomes "ninety leagues off". A shadow that runs "a league in a breath" can still get there in a morning.
  - Ch 56 (Eskby "twelve leagues above the hub") is fine and needs no change.

## Major      (a continuity or logic error a careful reader would notice)

### [B3-61-a] The five links: who left them on Liss's table, and when, contradicts Books 1 and 2
- Where: Book 3 ch 61, lines 256, 264, 276–284
- Quote: "Since the night the six went up and everyone left Liss's table and he'd gone back in for his coat and found them there... Where she'd put them down"; "You left them on Liss's table," he said. "At the end."; "From the ruts at Upper Rush, the morning after the raid"
- Problem: Maren gave the bundle back to Calder at the end of Book 1. In Book 2 ch 2 it was Calder who emptied the links out of his own coat onto Liss's table. On the night the six went up (Book 2 day 362), Calder was at the Scarfell, not at the ferry house. He also found the links at night, by the burning barn, not "the morning after".
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 79:125 (he holds "The bundle she'd given him"); Book 2 ch 2:215, :225 ("he emptied his coat onto Liss's table... Five links"); Book 2 ch 88 notes :12 ("Day 359 Calder flies up from the ferry house to the Scarfell") and :16 ("Day 362 dusk: the six go up... off the moor"); Book 1 ch 2:217–223 (the ruts "plain in the light of the burning barn", the same night).
- Fix: Keep "she left them", but set it at the envoys' table and give her the links in between. 61:256 becomes: "He'd been carrying them since the spring. Since the night of the envoys' table, when everyone left Liss's kitchen and he'd gone back in for his coat and found them there, among the cups, where she'd put them down." 61:276 becomes "At the end. After the table." 61:264 becomes "From the ruts at Upper Rush, the night of the raid". If no Book 2 scene has Maren taking the links off Liss's table after Book 2 ch 2, add one line there or here, for example: "She'd picked them up off Liss's table the first night of the second spring, and carried them a year."

### [B3-63-a] The Horned Sun is called "south-west", but it stands on the Saltreach (east) side
- Where: Book 3 ch 63, lines 127–139; ch 64, lines 79, 169–181, 239; ch 61, line 196
- Quote: "South-west. Over the hills on the Saltreach side." (63:131); "beyond the Saltreach wall's eastern end... a league south of the wall's end" (63:135, 139); "Five for the south-west." (64:79); "The frost went. Off the whole south-east of the Vale." (64:239); "Kharrak goes south-west if the Sun comes up the Sun Road side." (61:196)
- Problem: The Saltreach bank is east of the Wend, and the Saltreach wall runs east from the river to the scrub. Hills south of that wall's eastern end lie south-east of the hub, not south-west, and ch 64 itself says the frost goes off the south-east. The Sun Road and the Sunward Hills are on the west side. The stormkites' code and Calder's plan therefore point the line the wrong way.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:48 (the Wend marks the Hearthmere–Saltreach border) and :70 (Hearthmere has "the Wend on its eastern border"); Atlas.md:59 (the Sun Road runs Solenne to Hallowmere); Part4_Ledger.md:9 (the Saltreach rampart runs "from the river east to the scrub"); ch 60:375 ("Going away from the river into the dusk, east").
- Fix: Change "south-west" to "south-east" at 63:131, 63:153 ("West, north, south, north-west, south-east") and 64:169. The code at 64:79 becomes "Five for the south-east". 61:196 becomes "Kharrak goes south-east if the Sun comes round the scrub side." Update the ch 63 and 64 notes and the plan row 64 ("from the south-west") to match. 63:135 already says it "had come round", so the story still works.

### [B3-68-a] The Bell jumps from a league below the line to the river mouth overnight
- Where: Book 3 ch 64, line 139; ch 68, lines 153–165
- Quote: "It came up to a league below the line and lay there. Waiting." (64:139); "It was twelve leagues off, at the Wend's mouth... when the Bell's long back came up out of the white water toward the mouth, it put its shoulder into it." (68:153, 163)
- Problem: At dusk on day one the Bell is lying a league below the line. On day two it is back at the sea coming in, and the shadow has to run to the mouth to meet it. No chapter shows it going back, and ch 66–67 never mention it.
- Evidence: ch 63:105 ("It stopped two leagues below the line"); ch 64:139; ch 68:153–165; ch 70:311 and :323 (the shadow at the mouth "all morning").
- Fix: In ch 68 at line 153, add: "It had gone back down the river in the night. Back to the white water off the mouth, as it had after Kharrak's fire, and at sunrise it came on again." The simpler alternative is to have the shadow hold the Bell where it lay, a league below the line. That would also ease B3-58-a.

### [B3-59-a] Cotter's Cross is on the road north from the hub; canon puts it south
- Where: Book 3 ch 59, line 38; ch 67, line 380 (same error in ch 56 and ch 77, outside this range)
- Quote: "the ice burned off the fords where the farmer's fire had been: black ground at Cotter's Cross, black ground at the beck... Through Harrow's End, empty." (59:38); "A wyvern's sling, parting, over Cotter's Cross." (67:380, on the flight north to Varrhold)
- Problem: The Drover's Way runs Varrhold, then Harrow's End, then Cotter's Cross, then Wendmouth. Harrow's End is 1.5 leagues from the Ruin (the hub), so Cotter's Cross is downriver. Nobody going north from the hub to Coldtarn or Varrhold passes it, and Maren can't reach it before Harrow's End.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:61 (the Drover's Way, in that order) and :8 (Harrow's End 1.5 leagues from the Ruin); Book 1 ch 16:216 ("up the Way from the south, from Cotter's Cross"); Book 2 ch 72:298 ("toward the sea... Past Millbrook, past Cotter's Cross, past the Reedmarsh, to Wendmouth").
- Fix: Use the established ford north of Harrow's End, Teo's ford, where the Drover's Way crosses Hollow Beck eight leagues up (Atlas.md:255). 59:38 becomes: "Through Harrow's End, empty. Up the Drover's Way, rutted and frozen, and the ice burned off the fords where the farmer's fire had been: black ground at Teo's ford, black ground at the beck below the Ram's Head, the snow round it melted back in a ring." 67:380 becomes "over Teo's ford". Tell whoever reviews ch 56 and ch 77 to change those to match.

### [B3-57-a] Check: Idony's "old room" overlooks the eddy, but the Book 2 ferry house stood at Greyhold
- Where: Book 3 ch 57, lines 38–40 (the setting starts in ch 53:377 and ch 55)
- Quote: "He had his old room... with the shelf and the cot and the window onto the eddy, where he'd lived in the last weeks before the six went up."
- Problem: In Book 2, Liss's ferry house is at the foot of Greyhold, where Idony lived at the end (Book 2 chs 86–87). Greyhold is 3.5 leagues from the Ruin, and Book 3 itself says the camp "moved three leagues up the Wend to the eddy" (ch 55). The same house can't be at Greyhold and look down on the eddy. Labelled Check because the setting was decided upstream in ch 53 ("as it always had").
- Evidence: Atlas.md:177 (Ashdown Ferry runs "between Greyhold landing and the Saltreach jetty at Tolly's Staithe") and :8; Book 2 ch 86 notes :12 ("the ferry house at its foot: cracked"); Book 3 Part5_Ledger.md:18 ("The camp moved three leagues up the Wend to the eddy").
- Fix: Settle it once, in ch 53, either way. If Liss moved upriver, 57:38–40 becomes: "He had his old room. Or its twin: Liss had brought the shelf and the cot up the river from the cracked house under Greyhold and put them in the back room of the new one, with the window onto the eddy." If the house never moved, Atlas.md:8 and ch 55's "three leagues" must change instead.

## Minor      (small slips: a wrong day, a name typo, a count off by one)

### [B3-57-b] Pike dates the Scald fifty years back; canon says forty
- Where: Book 3 ch 57, line 302
- Quote: "We make the best hands in Winshire," said Pike. "We have for fifty years. Since the Scald."
- Problem: The Scald happened forty years before Book 2, so it is about forty-two years ago now.
- Evidence: Bible/Magic.md:226 ("forty years ago"); Book 2 ch 42:182 ("Every hour for forty years"); Book 3 ch 12 notes :14 ("forty years since the Scald").
- Fix: "We have for forty years. Since the Scald."

### [B3-60-a] Fifty to a barge, then five hundred on Kit's barge
- Where: Book 3 ch 60, lines 113, 159, 261
- Quote: "Eight leagues up with fifty or sixty aboard each" (113); "the five hundred people on her barge, behind her" (159); "with five hundred asleep in the hold under canvas" (261)
- Problem: Within the same chapter one barge carries fifty or sixty, then five hundred. The 15-day sum only works at about fifty a barge.
- Evidence: 60:49 and :113.
- Fix: 159 becomes "the fifty people on her barge"; 261 becomes "with fifty asleep in the hold". 99 ("five hundred people on the barges") is fine as written.

### [B3-60-b] Five thousand at the Staithe is fewer than the 5,200 lifted out of Wendmouth alone
- Where: Book 3 ch 60, line 31 (ch 58, line 476)
- Quote: "Five thousand of them. Wendmouth's. And the coast's last villages" (60:31); "Five thousand two hundred, off the water and out of Wendmouth." (58:476)
- Problem: Wendmouth's people plus the villages should come to more than 5,200, not 5,000. The ch 60 notes say "Five thousand two hundred... and the coast's last villages".
- Evidence: 58:472–478; ch 60 notes :12.
- Fix: 60:31 becomes "Six thousand of them. Wendmouth's five thousand two hundred, and the coast's last villages". Line 49 becomes "Six thousand people".

### [B3-65-a] At noon Rowan is in two places
- Where: Book 3 ch 65, lines 79–83 and 165–167
- Quote: "The Last Titan came along the ward to them at noon. / She was there. She'd ridden ahead." (the Sarovel sector, "Halfway along"); "At noon she'd seen the beast-flood... from the wall's top at the river end."
- Problem: The Sarovel sector is halfway along the wall, and the river end is a league or so away. She can't watch both at the same noon.
- Evidence: 65:77 ("Halfway along").
- Fix: 165 becomes "Before noon she'd seen the beast-flood." 167 becomes "from the wall's top, riding back from the bend".

### [B3-64-a] The Last Titan walks "north" along a ward that runs west to east
- Where: Book 3 ch 64, line 107; ch 65, lines 47 and 113
- Quote: "And walked. Along the ward. North, toward the river." (64:107); "Moving north. Toward the river." (65:47)
- Problem: The Hearthmere rampart and its ward run from the downs' foot east to the river. Walking along it toward the river means walking east.
- Evidence: Part4_Ledger.md:9 ("from the river's edge below the ferry house west to the downs' foot"); ch 60:375.
- Fix: Change "North" to "East" at 64:107, 65:47 and 65:113. Line 113 becomes "Past them. East. Toward the river."

### [B3-66-a] When the shadows left their sectors
- Where: Book 3 ch 66, lines 511–515; ch 68, line 141; ch 70, line 345
- Quote: "They stayed like that all night... The three shadows in their sectors." (66); "It had gone in the night up the Hearthmere bank to the channel" (68:141); "She'd taken the shadows out of the sectors at dawn" (70:345)
- Problem: Three different times are given for the same move.
- Evidence: as quoted.
- Fix: 68:141 becomes "It had gone at first light up the Hearthmere bank to the channel".

### [B3-67-a] Calder "asleep since the Sun went down", but awake in the night in ch 66
- Where: Book 3 ch 67, line 336
- Quote: "Calder was asleep. On the ridge. He'd been asleep since the Sun went down."
- Problem: In ch 66 Calder is on the ridge between Kharrak's forelegs in the night, seeing the coal through Aurveth and Lisska. By dawn he is asleep between Aurveth's forelegs, so the switch is fine, but "since the Sun went down" is not.
- Evidence: ch 66:467 ("the farmer on the ridge between Kharrak's forelegs... seeing through all of them").
- Fix: "Calder was asleep. On the ridge. He'd gone to sleep at last, some time after the coal."

### [B3-71-a] "North... toward the pontoon", but the pontoon is downstream
- Where: Book 3 ch 71, lines 276, 320–322
- Quote: "North. Toward the pontoon. The chained barges below the eddy, to the Saltreach bank."
- Problem: The pontoon is below (south of) the eddy, so the column goes south to cross it and then north up the Drover's Way.
- Evidence: ch 57:266 and ch 70:359 ("the pontoon of chained barges below the eddy").
- Fix: 320 becomes "South first. To the pontoon. The chained barges below the eddy, to the Saltreach bank. And then north, up the Drover's Way."

### [B3-69-a] Check: "a year and a half" in chairs
- Where: Book 3 ch 69, line 262
- Quote: "He'd been sitting in chairs for a year and a half."
- Problem: Idony burned out at Book 2 ch 83 (day 355), about 326 days ago. Labelled Check in case he used chairs earlier for his age.
- Evidence: Book 2 Part7_Ledger.md:18 (ch 83, day 355).
- Fix: "He'd been sitting in chairs for most of a year."

### [B3-70-a] The notes and the prose disagree on the bolt-throwers
- Where: Book 3 ch 70, notes line 14 against prose line 117
- Quote: notes: "the hundred bolt-throwers on both banks swing from the line to the eddy"; prose: "All of them but the hub battery's forty on their rail... Sixty."
- Problem: The notes block contradicts the chapter and ch 71's premise, which is that the forty are still facing west. The prose is right.
- Evidence: ch 71:46–48; Part6_Ledger.md:7.
- Fix: Notes line 14 becomes "the sixty line bolt-throwers on both banks (all but the hub battery's forty)".

### [B3-60-c] Check: "the Staithe" is easily confused with canon's Tolly's Staithe
- Where: Book 3 ch 60, line 25 (and ch 58:474, ch 63:99)
- Quote: "A ford and a landing and a dozen houses, eight leagues below the hub on the Hearthmere bank"
- Problem: The Atlas already has Tolly's Staithe, the Saltreach-bank village at Liss's old ferry. A new "Staithe" on the other bank, further down, will make readers think of the old one.
- Evidence: Atlas.md:177, :179.
- Fix: Give it a distinct name, for example "Shepcote Staithe" on first use (60:23, "Shepcote Staithe had been a ford"), and "the Staithe" after that.

### [B3-71-b] Check: Tobin says "In our words" about Sarovel's olifant-speech
- Where: Book 3 ch 71, line 272
- Quote: "Talk to them. In our words."
- Problem: Tobin is a Hearthmere boy from Greyhold, and the low-city words are Ines's. This may be deliberate, as a sign of how far he has taken the hands as his own; if not, it is a slip.
- Evidence: Bible/Characters.md:120; ch 71:290 and :302 ("the low-city words... that he'd heard her use").
- Fix: If unintended: "Talk to them. In your words."

## Craft      (at most 8)

### [B3-craft-a] A recurring tic: "She didn't know how she knew" / "He didn't know why"
- Where: ch 59:238; ch 60:253 and :403; ch 63:163; ch 66:347; and "didn't know why" at ch 62:310, ch 62:410, ch 65:277, ch 67:314
- Problem: Nine times in fifteen chapters a character acts or knows "without knowing how". It reads as the narrator covering for intuition, and it blunts the moments where not knowing matters, such as Tobin's silence at 62:410.
- Fix: Keep it at 62:410 and 66:347. Elsewhere, cut the line or give the reason. For example, 63:163 becomes "Orrun knew. She knew it from him."

### [B3-craft-b] The same tableau closes three chapters: Maren asleep against the lion's foreleg, watched, with a question
- Where: ch 60:381–405 ("Is she dead?" / "She's asleep"); ch 62:428–444 ("Is it asleep?" / "I don't think so"); ch 67:400–420 (Calder asleep in Aurveth's foreleg); also ch 59:60
- Problem: The plan asks for these echoes, but ch 60 and ch 62 repeat the same picture and the same exchange two chapters apart, and the second loses its force.
- Fix: Change ch 60's ending to something Kit can do that Tobin can't. For example: Kit gets the last barge in under the wall, Maren on the lion's head lifts her bandaged hand to the barges, and Kit counts the tally aloud to her across the water. Keep the sleeping tableau for Tobin (62) and Arn (67).

### [B3-craft-c] Kessmoor's men are all "pleasant", "polite" and "mild-faced"
- Where: ch 62:32 and :264; ch 69:308 and :392; ch 71:28, :74 and :102
- Problem: Wardle, the magazine guard and two different crew-masters get the same few words. The menace is meant to be quiet, but the repetition blurs them into one man, and in ch 71 the crew-master Tobin hits needs a face of his own.
- Fix: Keep "pleasant" for Wardle. Give the ch 71 crew-master one physical detail and a line of his own ("He was crying. He held up his watch anyway."). Cut "with a mild face" at 69:392.

### [B3-craft-d] Ch 69: the explanation slows the race
- Where: ch 69, lines 160–232, between "*An engine breathes too*" and Idony sending the falcon knight
- Problem: Idony reasons it through in about seventy lines (the gloss, heartstone breathing, the six hollows, the reader, the old realm's fall) before he acts. The chapter is meant to feel too slow because his body is too slow, not because his thinking is.
- Fix: Move "You. Go to the Marshal" up to just after 156. Let the understanding come to him in pieces while he runs and falls (lines 178–186 and 218–220 are the parts to keep).

### [B3-craft-e] Small repeats
- Where: "the most beautiful thing he'd seen" (57:34; 64:73; and "it was beautiful", 62:136); the "It wasn't X. It was Y." pattern at 66:31–33 ("She'd thought it was the five, coming. / It was the knot.")
- Problem: The beauty line lands three times in eight chapters and loses its force. The ch 66 lines fall into the pattern the style guide lists.
- Fix: Keep 64:73, the one most earned, and cut the other two. For 66:31–33: "She'd thought it was the five, coming. It was the five, and what came with them: the knot, filling."
