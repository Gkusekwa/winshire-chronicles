# Findings: Book 3, chapters 72–86

Reviewer summary: The closing run is strong and nearly every settled rule holds. The remnant counts (473, 533, 553, 555), the twenty dragons, the thirty pairs under the slip, the destroyers' ends, Kit's single ambiguous sighting and the unnamed stone creature all match canon. The real problems are geographical. Harrow's End sits far from the hub, against the Atlas, and the lip-to-Coldtarn leg is walked in a single night. Ch 79 also has Tam's wagons arriving first when ch 78 makes them last. Three further breaches: several riders and dragons outlive their partners (against the one-life rule), the creatures' positions at the moment of the relic's call in ch 74 are wrong, and ch 82 gives two different directions for the hall's door.

## Critical

None found.

## Major

### [B3-79-a] Tam's wagons "came over the lip first", though ch 78 makes them the column's tail
- Where: Book 3 ch 79, lines 139 and 263 (also lines 89–107)
- Quote: "because Tam's wagons had come over the lip first." / "The wagons came first."
- Problem: In ch 78 the heads of the columns cross the lip during the night of 320–321, and Tam's forty wagons come last ("Tam on the last box") at about the third hour after noon on 321. So in ch 79 thousands should already be at Coldtarn before Tam. Instead the shore holds only the wagons and the Clan families, nobody is at the door, and "the rest" come over the rim behind Kit. Tam also changes boxes: the first wagon in ch 77 (line 242), the last in ch 78 (line 84), the first again in ch 79 (line 95).
- Evidence: ch 78:46–50 ("The heads of the columns going over the lip in the dark... Tam's wagons last."); ch 78:84, 308–322.
- Fix: At 79:263, change to "because the wagons had the old and the hurt in them, and the knight had said those went in first." At 139, change to "The wagons go first." At 103–105, change to "Thousands. On the shore since the night, in the snow, not going in. Nobody had told them they could."

### [B3-79-b] Harrow's End to Coldtarn walked or driven in one night (Atlas: 14+ leagues; two days by cart)
- Where: Book 3 ch 79, lines 27 and 55 (and line 95); ch 85, line 324
- Quote: "They came over the lip in the second hour of the night." / "Coldtarn at dawn." / "on a top field four leagues off"
- Problem: Kit, Gannet and a lame dog go from the lip at Harrow's End to Coldtarn between the second hour of the night and dawn. Tam's loaded wagons cross the lip mid-afternoon on 321 and are already on Coldtarn's shore by dawn on 322. The Atlas puts Varrhold 14 leagues up the Way from Harrow's End, two days by cart, and Coldtarn is above Varrhold; ch 79 itself makes Varrhold half a day below Coldtarn. Ch 85's "four leagues" contradicts the Atlas as well. Ch 80's "twenty leagues" from the Vale is the only figure that fits.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:30 (Harrow's End–Varrhold, 14 leagues by road, 2 days by cart); Atlas.md:166, 170 (Coldtarn above Varrhold; Hollow Beck runs from Coldtarn past Varrhold and Harrow's End); ch 79:309 ("toward Varrhold, half a day"); ch 80:277.
- Fix: Check with the author which figure is meant. If the Atlas stands, Kit should cross the lip in the night of 320–321, which ch 76 rules out, or Coldtarn needs a short moor path from the lip that skips Varrhold, written into the Atlas. Either way, change ch 85:324 to "on a top field a long day's walk off". Give Tam's wagons a night and a day on the moor in ch 79's opening, or have his old ones flown up by the Clan.

### [B3-78-a] Harrow's End is placed a night and a day's march from the hub; the Atlas puts it 1.5 leagues from the island
- Where: Book 3 ch 77, line 80; ch 78, lines 38–40, 64 and 478–484 (also ch 54:192, ch 59:38 and ch 67:194, outside this range)
- Quote: "to the Scarfell's lip a day off" / "up through Harrow's End and over the Scarfell's lip" / "Two leagues off. Very small."
- Problem: The hub is "where the island was", and the Atlas puts Harrow's End a league and a half across the water from it, at Hollow Beck's mouth. Book 3 instead has the column march all night to "the Scarfell's foot" and then a day up the moor before it reaches Harrow's End. Ch 78 even keeps the village on the river ("the Eelweir below, where the beck went into the river"), which puts it on the Vale floor, not a day above it. From the lip, the lion at the Scarfell's foot is "two leagues" west, although ch 77 puts the foot a day's walk below the lip. Cotter's Cross is also out of order: the Atlas puts it south of Harrow's End on the Way to Wendmouth, so it should lie between the village and the sea, not up on the moor.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:8 ("Harrow's End is 1.5 leagues from the Ruin across the water"); Atlas.md:61 (the Drover's Way runs Varrhold–Harrow's End–Cotter's Cross–Wendmouth); Atlas.md:96, 281 (the hub is the island).
- Fix: Check. This runs through the whole book, so the decision belongs to the author: either move the battle's hub south in the Atlas, or move the Scarfell's lip above Harrow's End. If the prose stays, the smallest change in this range is to drop "two leagues" at 78:478 (use "Far off. Very small.") and to cut the river from 78:64 ("where the beck went down toward the Vale, a ditch of mud").

### [B3-74-a] At the relic's call, the Sun and the Coil are put back where they were at noon
- Where: Book 3 ch 74, line 437
- Quote: "South, in the shallows and beyond: the Sun, getting up, white. North, in the channel: the Coil, turning in the water."
- Problem: By this hour the Horned Sun has been up since noon (ch 70). It has come out of the shallows past the eddy onto the Saltreach bank, where Kharrak's fire has held it all afternoon. The Coil left the channel too: it is in the Hearthmere battery gap beside the Flat, fighting Rowan's line, for "an hour. More." after Rowan sees Corvin climbing.
- Evidence: ch 72:202–210 (the Sun "Out. Onto the Saltreach bank. At the Wyldmarch's river end"); ch 75:178–180 ("Where Kharrak's fire had held it all afternoon"); ch 73:298–414 and 482 (the Coil in the gap, then going back toward the eddy).
- Fix: "East, on the Saltreach bank, behind the farmer's fire: the Sun, flaring white. Near, in the river by the Flat: the Coil, lifting its head."

### [B3-81-a] Riders outlive their dragons, and a dragon outlives its rider (the one-life rule)
- Where: Book 3 ch 81, lines 30 and 364; ch 84, line 149
- Quote: "A few of Arn's riders on foot, whose dragons had died on the Vale." / "A rider whose dragon had died at the hub" / "that was her dead husband's, and that had followed her in."
- Problem: A bond shares one life between two, and "a dragon almost never survives one." Three or more casual survivals in two chapters wear the rule down just before the book makes it the point of Calder's and Maren's survival (81:410, 83:458). Ch 84's dragon also clashes with ch 83's count of twenty riders for twenty pairs.
- Evidence: Bible/Bestiary.md:19; Book 2 ch 34:333 ("A dragon almost never survives one."); ch 83:44 ("She counted the riders. 'And twenty.'").
- Fix: ch 81:30 to "A few of Arn's riders on foot, whose dragons were too hurt to fly"; 81:364 to "A rider whose dragon lay burned at Varrhold"; ch 84:149 to "A riders' wife with a small brown dragon's head in her lap, that wasn't hers, that was her husband's, and her husband lying hurt in the niche behind her."

### [B3-82-a] The hall's door faces east, then "south"
- Where: Book 3 ch 82, lines 37, 97, 211 and 391–393 (and the notes, line 12)
- Quote: "One toward the door, which faced east over the tarn." / "South. / Toward the door."
- Problem: The glasses' directions drive the chapter. Early on the door glass is the east glass and reads the Horned Sun going out east. Later the Lady's far-south breath falls on "the door glass" first, and the last breath leans "South. Toward the door." The notes block makes the door glass south. Chs 79 and 84 put the door in the western crag, facing east toward first light.
- Evidence: ch 79:73 ("on the western side of the bowl, under the crag, a door"); ch 84:221–229 (dawn through the door-stone); ch 82 notes line 12 ("one toward the door, south").
- Fix: Keep the door east. At 211, change to "South. Far. The back glass and the western glass together, slowly." At 391–393, change to "East. / Toward the door." Change the notes to match.

- Quote: "The spring before last." / "two springs ago" / "The spring before last."

## Craft

### [B3-78-b] Tic: "didn't know why"
- Where: twelve times in this range, four of them in ch 78 alone (lines 172, 276, 458, 474); also 73:438, 76:269, 82:323, 83:90, 84:71, 85:534
- Quote: "He didn't know why. He couldn't look at the field any more."
- Problem: A deliberate beat has become a reflex, and in ch 78 it blunts Kharrak's chapter.
- Fix: Keep 78:172 ("He didn't know why. He'd never once not come.") and cut the other three in ch 78. Thin the rest to one per chapter.

### [B3-84-a] Repeated tag: "eyes that looked at things too far away"
- Where: Book 3 ch 82:157; 83:254; 84:101, 367; 86:469
- Quote: "His eyes were strange... They looked at things too far away."
- Problem: Five uses in five chapters. The line should belong to Wren, at 84:367, and it is spent before she says it.
- Fix: Keep it at 84:367 (Wren) and 86:469. Elsewhere use plain description ("his eyes on something past the door").

### [B3-84-b] "Twice", then three "Once"s
- Where: Book 3 ch 84, lines 197–201
- Quote: "He'd lost them twice. / Once on the top field. Once on the black water. Once on the beck's mouth, white."
- Problem: Read quickly, three "Once"s straight after "twice" look like a counting error at the most exact moment of the book.
- Fix: "He'd lost them twice. The first time three times over: on the top field, on the black water, on the beck's mouth, white."

### [B3-85-a] POV slip: "his mother" in Arn's chapter
- Where: Book 3 ch 85, line 288
- Quote: "where the farmer's family had lived, where his mother had stood in the mouth with a lantern"
- Problem: In Arn's POV, "his mother" reads as Arn's own mother, not Ada.
- Fix: "where the farmer's mother had stood in the mouth with a lantern".
