# Findings: Cross-book audit, Books 1–3 (whole trilogy)
Reviewer summary: The trilogy holds together well at the level of spine and theme. Every canon beat in Series_Outline lands on the page, and the reveal rules are clean (no "Council of Immortals", no "elemental", no "Book N" anywhere in the prose). The serious problems are cross-book facts that Book 3 restates wrongly. Book 3 moves the eddy/hub next to Greyhold and Liss's ferry house. It calls the control relic "the seventh". It brings back a hound that died in Book 2 and changes the knight Wynn from a man to a woman. It takes Calder's hard-won literacy away again. Structurally, Book 2 is front-loaded and its climax is rushed, Book 3's far-shore part (the elders, the gifts) is its thinnest, and a few arcs replay or drop beats they had already earned.

## Critical   (breaks the story, canon or a settled rule)

### [X-A] The control relic is called "the seventh"
- Where: Book 3 ch 35, line 271
- Quote: "The control relic," said the old man. Under his breath. "The seventh. The one from Hearthmere's vault."
- Problem: This breaks the settled naming rule. It also contradicts the canon count: six relics remain, and the control relic is "the sixth relic".
- Evidence: Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md:475 ("never 'seventh'"); Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md:691; Bible/Series_Outline.md:131; Bible/World.md:87–89.
- Fix: "The control relic," said the old man. Under his breath. "The ring. The one from Hearthmere's vault. The coal in its back."

### [X-B] Book 3 moves the hub/eddy to Greyhold's hill and Liss's ferry house
- Where: Book 3 ch 53, lines 371 and 375; ch 41 line 12 (notes) and 27; ch 53 line 419; ch 55 lines 12 and 46; ch 57 line 40. This carries on through the battle chapters (the Ferry Flat, "Liss's slope", the ferry house window on the eddy).
- Quote: "Where the river bent below Greyhold's fallen hill, the Wend spread out and went round and round on itself in a great grey eddy" (53:371); "Liss's ferry house stood on the Hearthmere bank above the eddy, as it always had." (53:375); "the window onto the eddy, where he'd lived in the last weeks before the six went up" (57:40)
- Problem: The eddy is where the island was, about 3.5 leagues upriver of Greyhold, opposite Redwatch. The ferry house is at Greyhold's own landing under the river wall. Book 3 puts the camp "three leagues below Greyhold" (41), then moves it "three leagues up the Wend, to the eddy" (53, 55). That only works if the eddy is at Greyhold. So the middle of the world, the settled site of the climax, ends up beside Greyhold's ruin, and Idony's room in the Greyhold ferry house (Book 2 ch 87) looks out on water three leagues away.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:8 and 175 (the Ruin about 3.5 leagues upriver of Greyhold), 177 (the ferry runs Greyhold landing to Tolly's Staithe); Book 1/Notes/Rulings_41-79.md:11 (Ruling 7) and 26 (Ruling 18: the ferry house is under Greyhold's wall); Book 2 ch 82 lines 121–127 ("Away from Greyhold… Three leagues… past Redwatch… To the eddy where the island had been"); Book 2 ch 86 line 52.
- Fix: Keep the battle at the eddy and move Liss. In 53:371 write "three leagues above Greyhold's fallen hill, below cracked Redwatch". Replace 53:375 with "Liss had brought her ferry up to the eddy in the autumn: a new landing on the Hearthmere bank, the old Redwatch boathouse for a kitchen, a hay-barn above it." Make the camp's move in 53:419 and 55:26 "six leagues", or put the camp "three leagues below the eddy" in 41. Change "ferry house" to "the boathouse" wherever Parts Four to Six mean the eddy landing, and cut "where he'd lived… before the six went up" from 57:40.

## Major      (a continuity or logic error a careful reader would notice)

### [X-C] Wynn's hound Sorrow dies in Book 2 and comes back; Wynn changes sex
- Where: Book 2 ch 20, line 409 (and notes line 25); then Book 2 ch 72 line 26, ch 77 line 115, and Book 3 chs 10, 23, 73, 80, 84, 86. For Wynn's sex: Book 2 ch 15 line 255, ch 72 line 26, Book 3 ch 10 line 105, against Book 3 ch 73 line 424, ch 84 line 121, ch 86 line 125.
- Quote: "a hound was howling, Wynn's dark one, Ash, standing over something pale that lay still in the grey grass." (B2 20:409; the notes say "Wynn's pale hound Sorrow, crushed"); later "his two hounds running at the horse's heels, Ash and Sorrow" (B2 72:26); "Wynn and her hound." (B3 86:125)
- Problem: Sorrow is killed at the Hollow Ring and then appears alive for two books, up to the remnant's last morning. Wynn is "he" from Book 1 through Book 3 ch 10 ("Wynn got down on his belly") and "she" from ch 73 on.
- Evidence: the lines above; Bible/Characters.md:124 and Bible/Bestiary.md:59 (Wynn's hounds Ash and Sorrow).
- Fix: Make the hound that dies in B2 20 someone else's. In 20:409 write "a hound was howling, a gate knight's brindled one, over its packmate"; in note 25 write "a knight's hound, crushed". Keep Wynn male: in B3 73, 80, 84 and 86 change "her" to "his" and "she" to "he" (e.g. "Wynn beside her. With his torn arm.").

### [X-D] Book 3 takes Calder's literacy back and misplaces where the account began
- Where: Book 3 ch 84, lines 255, 271 and 275; ch 86 line 389
- Quote: "He'd written its first line himself at Liss's table with a burnt stick… Since the night the six went up." (84:255); "He couldn't write." / "Not enough for a page." (84:271, 275); "The only page of his own account he'd written all through." (86:389)
- Problem: Book 2 finishes Calder's literacy arc. He reads Maren's hand "without his finger" and writes a whole page to the Tide Council, both sides, unaided. He writes the account's first line in ink at Bel's table in the hatching caves on day 365, and Book 3 ch 2 has him write its second line there. Book 3 ch 84 undoes all of this and moves the first line to the ferry house on a night he was at Coldtarn.
- Evidence: Book 2 ch 66 line 113; Book 2 ch 78 line 196 ("One page. Both sides. His own hand."); Book 2 ch 88 lines 281, 305 and 315; Book 3 ch 2 lines 26–31.
- Fix: 84:255 becomes "He'd written its first line himself at Bel's table, the last morning of the year, with Ada pushing the ink-pot nearer." Lines 271–275 become: "He could write. Slowly. Badly. Enough for a page, if somebody held the light." Keep "Spell it" as Davin checking his spelling. In 86:389 write "the last page of his account, in his own hand, as the first line had been."

### [X-E] The account's opening sentence says the bonds happened "on a roof"
- Where: Book 2 ch 88, line 305; Book 2 ch 86, line 214
- Quote: "*The dragons came to me on a roof, and I had never asked them for anything.*"; "A roof. A sky full of stars. Three dragons coming down out of the dark"
- Problem: The bonds took in the stubble of the Fenn barley field, with Calder lying hurt. That event founds the series, and the account Calder writes misstates it. (Wren's "on a roof" in Book 2 ch 62 is the Vael roof and is fine.)
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 18, line 185 ("He was lying in the stubble with his hand on the dragon's jaw"); Bible/Story.md:75.
- Fix: "*The dragons came to me in a barley field, and I had never asked them for anything.*" In B2 86:214 write "A field. A sky full of stars."

### [X-F] Idony's ring, and his age
- Where: Book 3 ch 31, lines 227 and 353; ch 57 line 290; ch 60 line 345
- Quote: "Fourth, a tide-reader like himself, once." / "A worker's rings are notches cut on his staff… The fourth was mine, once."; "I've mended Kessmoor glasses for forty years."; "The College sold my tide-charts to Kessmoor for forty years"
- Problem: Idony was a Second-ring Reader. His ring was an iron ring on his hand with two notches, which the proctor cut off his hand on the Library steps. A ring is not a notch on a staff, and "fourth" is the Fellow's ring. He is in his early thirties, and his charts go back eleven years, so neither "forty years" works.
- Evidence: Bible/Magic.md:150 (the ring is worn "on the hand of their working"), 164 ("Idony Vael wears a Second ring"), 302 (he is 31), 304 (eleven years of charts); Book 2 ch 8 (the chapter title "Second Ring"); Book 2 ch 40 line 301 ("He took the ring off Idony's hand").
- Fix: 31:227 becomes "Second, a Reader like himself, once." 31:353 becomes "A worker's ring has notches cut in it… The second was mine, once." 57:290 becomes "I've mended Kessmoor glasses since I was a boy on the ferry." 60:345 becomes "The College sold my tide-charts to Kessmoor for eleven years."

### [X-G] Orrun's shadows go past the Book 2 ceiling before the gift, which deflates "Six Roads"
- Where: Book 3 ch 25, lines 186–188; ch 7, lines 242 and 264; ch 77, lines 178–180
- Quote: "Six grey lions standing in the sea on the drowned road" (25:188); "She'd never seen six. … The six on the drowned road, at the last milestone, hay-cart-sized" (77:178–180)
- Problem: Book 2 ends with four bodies as the hard limit: Maren stops breathing for a count of ten. Before the Grey Mother's gift, Book 3 has him hold four bodies for four days at the fords (ch 7), with only her feet going numb, and raise six at the Needle (ch 25), at the cost of grey on her neck. Canon also says every duplicate is his own size and strength, so "hay-cart-sized" shadows break the rule. Ch 77 then says she'd "never seen six" and lists the six she saw in the same breath, and its seven-body climax, the one that stops her heart, reads as a repeat.
- Evidence: Bible/Bestiary.md:33; Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md:296 ("four is possible once, not a habit"); Book 2 ch 83, lines 307–329; Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md:403.
- Fix: In ch 25 use two shadows ("Two grey lions… passing her from one to the other"). In ch 7 have one ford held by Orrun and one shadow at a time, moving between fords. In 77:178–180 write "She'd never seen six. Three, in the line. Four, under Greyhold, once. Never six."

### [X-H] Book 3 reveals the Grey Mother through callbacks that were never planted
- Where: Book 3 ch 30, line 86
- Quote: "Hesper had said it. At the Hollow Ring… Tova had said it, at the Ysse fords"
- Problem: Neither Hesper (Book 2 chs 13–25) nor Tova (Book 3 ch 7) ever says "Grey Mother" or anything close. The only real plant is Book 2 ch 67's "hearth-mother… the grey one". The Bible also promises a second plant that the prose never delivers: Greyhold's grey cat leaving before the hill falls and turning up at Liss's, and its look seen again in the Grey Mother's eyes. The cat is set up in Book 1 (chs 9, 27, 37) and then disappears.
- Evidence: grep of Books 2–3 for "Grey Mother" (nothing before Book 3 ch 30); Book 2 ch 67, line 160; Bible/Bestiary.md:82.
- Fix: Plant one line in each place. In Book 2 ch 15 or 23, Hesper: "The Grey Mother keeps the stones. That's what we say when the ground shakes." In Book 3 ch 7, Tova under her breath at the ridge: "Grey Mother." For the cat, either add two lines (Book 2 ch 79: the cat gone from the Long Barn; Book 3 ch 30: Maren knows the Mother's look from it) or cut the claim from Bestiary.md:82.

## Minor      (small slips: a wrong day, a name typo, a count off by one)

### [X-I] Two different "Staithes"
- Where: Book 3 ch 60, line 23; ch 76, line 123 (also chs 58, 77, 79)
- Quote: "Not the Staithe's bank. The Staithe was on the Hearthmere side."
- Problem: Through all three books "the Staithe" means Tolly's Staithe: the Saltreach end of Liss's ferry, opposite Greyhold. In Part Five Book 3 brings in a different "Staithe", on the Hearthmere bank eight leagues below the hub, while ch 41 line 45 still uses the old one.
- Evidence: Bible/Atlas.md:177 and 179; Book 3 ch 41, line 45.
- Fix: Give the downstream village its own name (for example "Sheepford", since it was a drovers' sheep-ford) in chs 58, 60, 76, 77 and 79.

### [X-J] The Restoration's mark gains a centre dot
- Where: Book 2 ch 2, lines 219 and 221
- Quote: "a ring of six dots round a seventh."
- Problem: The mark is a ring of six dots, copied from a relic with six hollows. A seventh, central dot is new, and in a series where "seventh" is a word to avoid, it invites a misreading.
- Evidence: Book 1 ch 10, line 168; Bible/World.md:202; Bible/Magic.md:204.
- Fix: "a ring of six dots." in both lines.

### [X-K] Jory's age
- Where: Book 3 ch 76, line 209
- Quote: "He went in front with a lantern, sixteen, his voice gone down"
- Problem: Jory is nineteen (eight when Hal drowned eleven years ago).
- Evidence: Book 3 ch 13, line 37; ch 26, line 146.
- Fix: "nineteen".

### [X-L] The Bible says other Titans live beyond the sea
- Where: Bible/World.md:248
- Quote: "Other Titans exist beyond the six destroyers and the elders, in the land beyond the sea"
- Problem: This contradicts the Book 3 canon: only four elders answer, the rest "have gone out", and none remain by ch 82. It also reads as a survival hint, and the rules allow none beyond Kit's.
- Evidence: Book 3 ch 34 ("Others did not answer. They have gone out."); Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md:346 and 427; Bible/Series_Outline.md:239.
- Fix: "The old ones were many; by Book 3 only four answer on the Lit Shore, and the rest have gone out."

### [X-M] Setups that never pay off
- Where: Book 3 ch 62, lines 78 and 146 (the cracked runner on the seventh engine); Book 2 ch 49 (Rusk escapes, and the notes say "Rusk for Book 3"); Book 2 chs 54 and 67 (Reede and his press)
- Quote: "The left sledge-runner. Under the flat. It's cracked." / "The seventh runner held."
- Problem: Odo's warning is planted and then dropped, and ch 71, the fight at the seventh engine's winch, never uses it. Rusk's escape turns out to be nothing (Book 3 ch 58, line 340 shows only his empty office). Reede, the Restoration's voice, never appears in Book 3.
- Evidence: Book 3 ch 71 (the brake scene); Book 3 ch 58, line 340; a grep of Book 3 finds no "Reede".
- Fix: In ch 71 let the cracked runner give under the drum's pull, so the engine slews and Tobin's wedge holds. For Rusk and Reede, either cut the forward plants or give each one line in Book 3 (Reede's last broadsheet at the muster in ch 46).

### [X-N] Idony's hands steady on the far shore, and nothing comes of it (Check)
- Where: Book 3 ch 31, line 319; ch 42, line 151 (also ch 16 and ch 27)
- Quote: "Not still. Steady. An old man's hands."; "His hands were steady."
- Problem: The settled state after the burnout is "his hands shake". Book 3 steadies them among the far-stone more than once, then lets the thread drop (he refuses the Mother's mending in ch 36, and is back to "shaking hands" in ch 57). It reads like a promise.
- Evidence: Bible/Magic.md:321; BRIEF rule 3.
- Fix: Either one line in ch 36 tying the refusal to it ("They'd steadied on the stone. They'd shake again at home. Let them."), or cut 42:151's "His hands were steady."

## Craft      (at most 8)

### [X-O] Book 2 is front-loaded and its climax rushed
- Where: Book 2. Part One (chs 1–12) has 59,000 words for days 0–25; Part Seven (chs 79–88) has 21,700. Ch 84, "The Held Breath", is 1,682 words.
- Problem: Chapter length falls by more than half across the book: the part averages are 4,914, 4,670, 4,704, 3,713, 3,304, 2,802 and 2,166 words. The longest stretches are road logistics (ch 7: 5,135; ch 10: 6,880). The canon turn, the Titan taking the control relic, plus Corvin's hand and the Titan's one line to Orrun, sits in the shortest chapter in the trilogy. Ch 85 (the five answer, Kessmoor's cage breaks, Greyhold falls) gets 2,042.
- Fix: Cut about a quarter of chs 7, 9, 10 and 11 (the walks to Varrhold and the hand-over at the moor's edge). Spend those words on chs 81–85, especially the vault in 84 and the call in 85.

### [X-P] Book 3's far shore is its thinnest part
- Where: Book 3 Part Three (chs 28–42, average 2,549 words); ch 31: 2,223; ch 35: 2,178; ch 36: 2,177; ch 37: 2,341
- Problem: The trilogy's spiritual centre (the gods were Titans, the plan, the lion's gift, the dragons' gift) gets Book 3's shortest chapters. Book 3 as a whole (240,000 words) is 60,000–100,000 words shorter than Books 1 and 2 and carries seven POVs, so each gift happens almost as fast as it is explained.
- Fix: Give ch 37 (Consent) and ch 39 (the dragons' gift) a scene each before the gift, showing what is being given up (the Mother's last day; the Wayfarer's three faces turning from the roads).

### [X-Q] Calder's arc is replayed in Book 3, and his earlier choices are disowned
- Where: Book 3 ch 26, lines 242–244
- Quote: "He'd let things happen to him all his life." / "He'd never once stood on a road with two ways off it and picked one because he wanted to."
- Problem: Book 1 ends this arc (he chooses to speak, to tell Bel, to let Kharrak burn, to name Rook at the ford). Book 2 adds more choices: refusing the Council's contract, and staying for the Bell, "It's my coast". Ch 26 reads as a regression with no cause, and it shrinks a real turn into a re-run.
- Evidence: Bible/Characters.md:56; Bible/Story.md:71; Book 2 ch 49, line 331; Book 2 ch 66, line 213.
- Fix: Reframe the turn as choosing for himself instead of for duty: "He'd chosen before. For the village. For the coast. For whoever was drowning. He'd never once chosen a thing because he wanted it."

### [X-R] Theme: where it's strongest, and the one hole in it
- Where: Strongest at Book 1 ch 73 (Maren gives herself into the binding); Book 3 ch 37, lines 120–122 ("It didn't ask… This asked."); Book 3 ch 18, line 360 ("Any dragon that flies with the Clan flies by choice."); Book 3 ch 74, line 185 ("Give it. Don't take it. Give."); Book 3 ch 63 (the oath said without "below" and "do not ask"). The weak point is Book 3 chs 35–36.
- Problem: Idony's indictment of Corvin, "you never once asked it… Nobody has" (Book 3 ch 57, line 204), applies just as well to the elders' own plan. That plan compels all six into the light through the control relic, a safeguard built for command (35:281). Nobody in Book 3 notices. Book 2's middle (Parts Three and Four) also lets the protagonists' bonds recede into transport while the plot runs on ledgers and lore, though Book 2 ch 61 ("My knowledge has a body count now") brings the theme back sharply.
- Fix: Add one exchange in ch 36. Maren: "Does it ask them?" Lamp script: *It calls. They can still choose to stop. The one in the chains cannot hear calling. That is why it must be carried.* That keeps the gift versus taking line intact.

### [X-S] Idony's Book 3 arc forgets Nessa
- Where: Book 3, all eight Idony chapters (3, 16, 31, 36, 52, 57, 69, 82); ch 56 (Quell arrives from Vael under the ice); ch 82, line 325 ("Glasswater. The Library hill.")
- Problem: In Book 2 Nessa is his anchor (chs 8, 29, 36–37, 40). In Book 3 he never once thinks of her, even when Vael's last people come down the ice or when he names Glasswater on the surge line.
- Fix: Add one beat in ch 82 after "Glasswater": "*The ferry. Nessa's ferry, on the ice.* He didn't say it. Davin didn't write it." Add one line in ch 56 or 60: Quell has no word of the ferry-folk.

### [X-T] Kit's single survival seed is cancelled two chapters later
- Where: Book 3 ch 82, line 269
- Quote: "Now you know," said Idony. Quietly. Across the hall. To her.
- Problem: Series_Outline keeps exactly one doubt for the next series, Kit's "I don't know" in ch 76 (line 385). Idony's line settles it for her on the page, so the seed is spent.
- Evidence: Bible/Series_Outline.md:239.
- Fix: Cut the line. Kit looks at the flat glass and says nothing, and Idony doesn't say anything to her.

### [X-U] Voice drift: stand-alone simile fragments multiply in Book 3
- Where: Book 3 throughout (for example ch 66 "As a dog puts its head round a door."; ch 62 "As a man bends his head in a chapel.")
- Problem: Paragraphs that open with a bare "As a…"/"Like a…" fragment rise from 2 in Book 1 to 9 in Book 2 to 29 in Book 3. "As water" for stone moving appears 21 times in Book 3 against 1 in Book 1. This looks like the "as, not the way" rule applied mechanically. It flattens the lean voice into a tic, most of all in Parts Five to Seven.
- Fix: In Book 3, fold about two-thirds of the fragment similes back into the sentence before them, or cut them. Keep "as water" for the Mother's gift (ch 37) and the fallen door (ch 86), and find other verbs elsewhere (gave, slid, opened).

### [X-V] Arcs across the trilogy: where they hold and where they gap
- Where: As listed.
- Problem: Most arcs run from start to end on the page. Maren: Book 1 chs 1, 23, 63, 73 → Book 2 chs 65, 83 → Book 3 chs 37, 74, 83, 86. Rowan: Book 1 ch 59 → Book 2 chs 1, 18, 82 → Book 3 chs 10, 23, 41, 73. Arn: Book 2 chs 10, 24 → Book 3 chs 18, 38, 85. Kit: Book 3 chs 13, 20, 60, 79. Liss: Book 1 ch 64 → Book 2 chs 43, 79 → Book 3 ch 80 (the pin). Wren: Book 1 chs 22, 70 → Book 2 ch 62 → Book 3 ch 86. Corvin: Book 2 chs 76, 84 → Book 3 chs 57, 74. The gaps: Calder (X-Q, X-D); Idony (X-S); Tobin's turn from the Restoration (Book 1 ch 71 at the barricade → Book 2 ch 45 ringless) happens off the page and is reported in a few lines, though he becomes a Book 3 POV.
- Fix: For Tobin, give ch 45 one dramatised beat inside his telling (the farm on the Downs, what "our side" did) rather than a summary, so his Book 3 refusal at the brake (ch 71) has a scene behind it.
