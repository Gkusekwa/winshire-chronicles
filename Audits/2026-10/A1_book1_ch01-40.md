# Audit A1: Winshire Chronicles, Book 1, Chapters 01–40

*Scope: the prose below the `---` line in Chapters 01–40, checked against the Bible (World, Story, Characters, Bestiary, Atlas, Magic, Series_Outline), Chapter_Plan.md, Notes (Decisions_*, Rulings_41-79, Voice_Samples) and Style_Guide.md. I skimmed Chapters 41–79 only to check setups. I read the whole of every chapter from 1 to 40. I edited nothing under the repo. "Check" marks a finding I'm not sure is an error.*

---

## Summary: how healthy is 1–40

The first forty chapters are in good shape. The voice is close to the Style Guide's B+C target: lean in the scene, mythic at the chapter openings, built on specific nouns and concrete bodies. It almost never uses the banned stock phrases. I found zero uses of "testament", "something shifted", "in that moment" as a cliché, "a flicker of", or metaphorical "echoed" or "whispered". The big threads are tracked with care:

- Aurveth's lost eye and her raked back.
- Kharrak's torn wing and Calder's ribs and left-leg gash.
- The lion's foot, collar and cut chain.
- Wren's blue ribbon, taken off in 32 and back on in 38.
- The five-link chain.
- The day counts from the fair (day 0) through day 72.

The reveal rules hold:

- The leader is "one man", Vale. The two faces are kept separate and the reader can tell them apart.
- Nobody says "Orrun", and the lion never multiplies.
- Titans appear only as legend ("the Last Titan" once, in 37, as Hale's slip).
- No cannon.
- The Restoration's hand in Teo's death stays at suspicion.

The highest-value problems are:

1. **Maren casts magic three times** (5, 19, 21), against the settled rule.
2. **The river geography between Greyhold, Redwatch and the island** changes from chapter to chapter: half a league, three leagues, "down" the river, "across the channel".
3. **Maren's river-watch years don't add up** against her age and canon.
4. **The "seventeen words" become "eleven"** (20, 24).
5. **Two POV knowledge slips**: Calder quotes words Maren said only to Rowan (34, 36).
6. **Haverly's trial sends a Greyhold student across the river**, and 59 says no knight ever crosses.
7. **Structural repetition**: about 26 of 40 chapters open with a folk-saying prologue. The "echo" rumours retell events the reader has just seen, often with the same details. The simile frame "the way you/a…" appears about 160 times.

Pacing is strongest in Part Two (13–23). Part Three sags slightly where a chapter retells (31, 33, 35) instead of moving.

## Findings, ranked by severity

### Critical (contradictions or canon breaks a reader would notice)

**C1. Ch 5: Maren casts a charm.** *"Maren leaned down and said the settling-word in his ear, the one every child on the river learns for a horse, and he walked on, which might have been the word…"*
This breaks the settled rule that Maren and Calder cast no magic in Book 1 (Magic.md §10a). The chapter's own notes ask for it ("Maren's settling-word… half works"). It is also an unlicensed word in Hearthmere, which is a fineable offence (Magic.md §6).
**Fix:** *"Maren leaned down and blew in his ear, the way Ottery did with the boat ponies, and he walked on, which might have been the breath and might have been that he'd finished thinking."* Also update the chapter's Magic touches note.

**C2. Ch 19: Maren casts again.** *"She said the stanching-word over it too, the one every child learns for a cut knee, though she knew it was no use out here…"*
She tries a charm, which is still casting even though it fails.
**Fix:** *"She thought of the stanching-word the surgeon said over cut knees, and didn't bother. She had no licence for it, and out here, with the river going by on both sides, it would only have gone downriver."*

**C3. Ch 21: Maren casts, and it may work.** *"She said the stanching-word over it again, on dry ground this time, and the arm stopped seeping through the linen, or she wanted it to."*
**Fix:** *"She pressed the heel of her good hand over the worst of it and counted to three hundred, the way Ottery taught, and the arm stopped seeping through the linen, or she wanted it to."*

**C4. Ch 20 and 24 against Ch 16: the number of unsaid words.** Ch 16 counts them: *"Seventeen words. He'd had seventeen words in his pocket…"*, and the quoted sentence really is 17 words. Ch 20 has *"the eleven words he hadn't said"*, and Ch 24 has *"About the eleven words he'd carried in his pocket for more than a week."*
**Fix:** change "eleven" to "seventeen" in both places.

**C5. Maren's service timeline (Ch 1, 3, 5, 9, 11, 13, 23, 35; also 67).** The prose says, consistently, *"four winters on the river watch"* (1, 3, 9; 67 again), *"I've known it's the law since I was seventeen"* (3), *"She'd been on the river watch since she was seventeen"* (35), *"exactly as she'd left him four years ago"* (5), *"You haven't been [on the river watch] for four years"* (11), and *"She'd said it every day for two years"* at Greyhold (13). Canon (Characters) says: age 27, joined at 17, two years at Greyhold. Seventeen plus four winters plus two years makes 23, not 27, and "left four years ago" clashes with "two years at Greyhold".
**Fix, option B (keeps canon, preferred):** change "four winters" to "eight winters" in 1, 3, 9 and 67. In 13, change "for four years" and "the habit of four years" to "eight". In 5 and 11, change "four years" to "two years".
**Fix, option A (keeps the "four winters" refrain):** she joined at 21. Change "seventeen" to "twenty-one" in 3, 23 and 35. In 15, change "eight years ago" to "five years ago". In 5 and 11, change "four years" to "two years". Then update Characters.md.

**C6. Ch 34 and 36: Calder knows something only Maren and Rowan heard.** Ch 34: *"*It would have been stealing,* she'd said."* Ch 36: *"a girl… with a knife in her hand and a lock of mane within reach, putting the knife away. *It would have been stealing.*"* Maren says that line only to Rowan (Ch 23). Calder got the story secondhand from Rue: *"like robbing it."* This is a POV knowledge break.
**Fix:** In 34, *"*Like robbing it,* Rue had said."* In 36, *"a girl… who could have taken a hair or a claw home and wouldn't. *Like robbing it,* Rue had said."*

**C7. How far the island is from where Maren sets out (Ch 11, 29, 35, 37, 39).** Ruling 7 puts Redwatch opposite the island, half a league across the west channel, with Greyhold about 3 leagues downriver.
- Ch 11: *"She rowed upriver on the Hearthmere side… Past the water meadows below Redwatch"*. She rows upriver from Redwatch yet passes ground below it, and with Redwatch opposite the island there's no need to row upriver at all.
- Ch 29: Ottery takes them *"out of the west channel into the main river… downstream… for a league. Then, with the lights of Redwatch showing ahead"*.
- Ch 35: from Greyhold's landing, *"She poled them across. Half a league of black water"*.
- Ch 37: *"three leagues down the black river"*. The island is upriver of Greyhold.
- Ch 39: from the landing, *"poled it across the channel alone"*, and then the same chapter says *"Upstream, three leagues off"*.

**Fix:** start every island trip at the Redwatch boathouse, as 25 and 27 already do.
- 35: *"…gone out the postern and up the River Road to Redwatch. Ottery was there with the punt."* Keep "half a league".
- 37: change "three leagues down the black river" to *"across the half league of black water from Redwatch"*.
- 39: *"took Turnip up the River Road to Redwatch in the dark, and Ottery's punt from the boathouse steps, and poled it across the west channel alone"*.
- 29: *"He kept quiet the whole way across the west channel. Then, with the lights of Redwatch showing ahead…"*.
- 11: drop "upriver… with the current against her" and "below Redwatch". She crosses the west channel.

*(Context, check: Atlas.md's own coordinates put Redwatch at (174.3, 101.9), about 2 leagues north of the Ruin (175.6, 103.5), and 4.7 leagues from Greyhold. That disagrees with Ruling 7. Settle the Atlas data first.)*

### Major

**M1. Ch 11 and 23: Haverly's trial crosses the river.** Rowan: *"Lucan Haverly will ride the Scarfell road on the Saltreach side."* Haverly: *"Three days on the ridge above the Drover's Way, below the hold… rode down the Way… By first light I was on the hill above it."* In Ch 59 Thorne says *"the orders said no knight crosses the river into the Marches, not for anything."* "They never cross" is the book's spine, and a Greyhold student riding through the Clan's war goes unremarked.
**Fix:** make it a known exception. In 11, add after Rowan's line: *"Out of a drover's coat, without the pin, and you were never there. No knight crosses. A student on trial isn't a knight yet."* Or keep him on the Hearthmere heights, with Kesh doing the looking. Then 23's "hill above it" becomes "the bluff opposite".

**M2. Ch 3 and 4: Tolly in two places.** In Ch 3, just after sunrise, he walks into the smokehouse at Tolly's Staithe *"with the look of someone who has been walking around a burnt place all morning."* Greta says he's *"Up at Upper Rush with half the staithe,"* and he says he was *"out checking the traps"* at the Staithe at midnight. Upper Rush is about 6 leagues away (Ruling 18). In Ch 4, the same morning with *"the sun… well up"*, *"Old Tolly from the Staithe was poking along the edge of the ash."*
**Fix:** in 3, Greta says *"Out on the weirs since before light"*, Tolly enters *"with the look of a man who hasn't slept"*, and his cart detail comes secondhand: *"Penner's lad came down from Upper Rush in the night. They had a cart…"*. That lets 4 keep Tolly going up by boat after dawn.

**M3. Ch 37 against Ch 49: Hale's age when sworn in.** 37: *"Forty years ago… I was twenty-four."* 49 and canon: *"I was twenty-three."*
**Fix:** in 37, "twenty-four" becomes "twenty-three".

**M4. Ch 7: the Wend doesn't run to Stillhaven.** *"cheaper to float flour down to Stillhaven than to cart it"*, and the river runs *"down toward the weir and the barges and Stillhaven and the sea."* Stillhaven is at the mouth of the Meryl. The Wend reaches the sea at Wendmouth, a Saltreach port 42 leagues east of Stillhaven.
**Fix:** *"cheaper to float flour down the Wend and round by sea to Stillhaven than to cart it"*, and *"…toward the weir and the barges and Wendmouth and the sea."*

**M5. Ch 31: Tam's boast ignores their father.** *"he used to say he'd be the first knight in the family who wasn't a girl."* Edric, their father, was a knight, and when Tam was small Maren wasn't one.
**Fix:** *"he used to say he'd be a knight like Da, and a better one."*

**M6. Ch 33 against canon: Callis as the seal-carrier (check).** Canon (71, 75) says Callis has carried the seal since before Upper Rush and is "the one who cleared the river for the barges". In Ch 33 Callis rides with Pell's patrol all day, while *"A rider… came along the bank at dusk on a grey horse… Hood up."* That could be a deliberate alibi with an accomplice riding. If so, plant it.
**Fix:** a half-line in 33: *"Callis had gone off along the bank to water his hound an hour before dusk, and come back when the lamps were lit."* Or have Tobin in 71 say "Callis's man rode it."

**M7. Over-explaining the emotion after showing it (Ch 27, 29, 30, 31, 39).** Five examples:
- 39, after Ide's death: *"*That's what one life between two means. Not together. One, and then the other. And the one who's left goes on, a little…*"* The death scene has already shown this.
- 30: *"He'd saved the village… And they were afraid of him."* and *"Because they'd seen what he could do…"* both pre-empt the good line *"Because we could have done the other thing."*
- 31: *"Because he was right. Not about the chain. About the rest. And she didn't know what to do with that."*
- 29: *"…And I said the wrong one. And it nearly killed me for it. And it was right to."*
- 27, Hale: *"It's a very old trick… And then you let them feel proud of it."*

**Fix:** In 39, cut the second italic paragraph and keep *"That's not together."* In 30, cut the two paragraphs before Lisska's question. In 31, cut to *"Because he was right."* In 29, keep *"It read me"* and cut the closing triple. In 27, keep Hale's line but cut Maren's *"felt something go cold in her"*.

**M8. Every chapter opens the same way (structural tic).** About 26 of 40 chapters open with a proverb, custom or folk-saying paragraph, then a section break (1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, 24, 26, 28, 29, 30, 32, 34, 36, 37, 39, 40). "There's a thing/word/saying…" starts 7 of them. "Teo had told him (once)…" is the frame in 16, 24, 32 and 40.
**Fix:** keep the device for about one chapter in three: the Part openers, Coldtarn, the ford, the mill, proof, the Widow's Frost. In the rest, open in scene, the way 12, 15, 17, 22, 31, 33 and 38 already do so well.

**M9. The "echo" rumours retell what the reader has already seen.**
- The Harrow's End rescue (30) is told again by Tam (31), at length by a refugee on the ferry (31), by the Redwatch scout (33), and by Maren to Hale (35).
- Maren at the stream is retold by Lisska (24, 28, 40), Rue (26), the beast-seller (34) and the pedlars (38), often with the same details: *"twenty paces"* (14 uses), *"three times… never once struck it"*.

**Fix:** make each echo add one new fact or one distortion. For example, the refugee gets the dragon's colour wrong, or the scout saw the man beating someone in the lane. Cut 31's refugee monologue by about half, and give 33's scout a single line.

**M10. The "the way you / the way a…" simile frame.** It appears about 161 times in roughly 168,000 words; "the way you" alone appears 87 times. It's the most noticeable mechanical tic in the book.
**Fix:** a pass to cut about half of them, and convert some to plain "like" or to no comparison at all.

**M11. Polysyndeton at the end of paragraphs ("…, and X, and Y, and Z").** About 128 chains of three or more "and"s, plus 32 "X. And Y. And Z." sequences. Chapter endings 9, 11, 12, 13, 20 and 31 all close on the same cadence. It works as Maren's counting rhythm, but it's now everyone's rhythm.
**Fix:** vary the chapter-closing sentences, and keep the chain for Maren under pressure.

**M12. Repeated signature images (see the tics table).** Calder thinks about the girl *"twenty paces from something that had roared like the end of the world"* almost word for word in 24, 26 and 34 (four uses of "roared like the end of the world"). Lisska's *"I remember everything"* appears 7 times, and *"Imra said those were mostly the same"* twice. The "honey-smoke / like honey burned in a pan, and under it something sharper, like crushed leaves" description is copied whole from 17 into 36.
**Fix:** keep one use of each as the anchor and paraphrase or cut the rest. In 36, have Calder simply fail to name the smell: *"Sweet. Burned. He knew it, and couldn't think from where."*

**M13. Ch 9 against Ch 1 and Characters: how Pell lost his fingers.** Ch 1: *"lost two fingers on the Saltreach road."* Ch 9: the coast road below Stillhaven, which is in Hearthmere, and the wyvern was bonded. Characters.md: *"a wild wyvern on the Saltreach road."*
**Fix:** in 1, *"lost two fingers on the coast road below Stillhaven"*. Update Characters to "bonded wyvern" to match 9's notes.

**M14. Ch 31 and 34: the narrator's wording is copied between Tam and Vale.** Both have *"He said the word as if it tasted bad"* about "unchosen", and nearly the same "There wasn't (even) a word for it… it didn't mean anything." Tam repeating Reede's words is fine. The narrator's tag shouldn't repeat.
**Fix:** in 34, *"He let the word sit on the steps a moment, like something spat out."*

**M15. Ch 3 against Ch 7, and Ch 9: Tam's fair days.** Ch 3: *"He's coming home for the harvest fair. Five days."* Ch 7 (which the notes put at Ch 3 + 5): *"the day before the fair."* Ch 9: he is home *"for three days"*, yet *"By the fourth night… their mother had started laughing."*
**Fix:** in 3, "Six days". In 9, "By the third night".

### Minor

**m1. Ch 11 and 13: Rowan's room.** *"Pell was waiting at the bottom of the gatehouse stair"* (11) and *"Rowan in her bare room at the top of the gatehouse"* (13). These are leftovers from before Ruling 12 moved her to the north tower; 23 and 35 already have it right.
**Fix:** "north tower stair" and "the top of the north tower".

**m2. Ch 19 against Ch 11: where Pell gave her the sword.** 19: *"the one he'd given her at the door of the barracks."* 11: he gave it under the armoury carving.
**Fix:** "at the armoury door".

**m3. Ch 37 against Ch 39: the first frost.** 37 (day 56): *"the warming-stone Grieve had begged off the infirmary at the first frost."* 39 is "First Frost", the first hard frost, on night 62. Frost already appears on day 4 (14) and days 29–38 (29), which is fine because 39 says "hard".
**Fix:** in 37, "at the first cold nights".

**m4. Ch 2 against Ch 38: house count.** 2 has *"thirty-one houses"* and 38 has *"Nine. There'd been thirty."*
**Fix:** "There'd been thirty-one."

**m5. Ch 22: Kharrak's legs.** *"moving on three good legs."* No leg wound is established, only the torn wing.
**Fix:** "on his belly and four legs, with the wing dragging".

**m6. Ch 21: three days of patrol boats?** The Captain: *"They've been all round this island three days."* The boats came on days 6 and 7 only.
**Fix:** "two days".

**m7. Ch 21: muddled line.** *"Whoever cut loose that boat of theirs. One of their own, I'd guess."* The hunters cut it loose themselves.
**Fix:** *"Whoever came over in that boat we cut loose. One of theirs, I'd guess."*

**m8. Ch 31: one night out.** *"The night Harrow's End burned. The second time. Three nights ago."* The burning was night 42 and the chapter is day 44.
**Fix:** "Two nights ago."

**m9. Ch 33: days since her last visit.** *"She hadn't been in eight days."* Her last visit was day 38 and Ferriby is day 49.
**Fix:** "eleven days".

**m10. Ch 26: visit rhythm.** *"He went back every two days,"* but the visits shown fall on the third and fourth days.
**Fix:** "After that he went back every two days."

**m11. Ch 23: the punt's approach.** *"out on the dark water, upstream, coming down on the current,"* then *"A punt came round the south point."* Coming from Greyhold it arrives from downstream.
**Fix:** "downstream, coming up against the current".

**m12. Ch 33 against Ch 5 and 27: Ide never speaking.** *"The first word Maren had ever heard her say out loud,"* after two years in the same year, and after Ide has stood beside her at drill (27). Ch 27 also has Maren having *"never really noticed"* Ide, against 5's close attention.
**Fix:** "The first word Maren had heard her say all day." In 27, cut "whom Maren had never really noticed".

**m13. Ch 35 and 31: "he crossed".** *"Twenty of our knights sat on the bank and watched, and he crossed"* (35), *"The farmer crossed"* (31), and Tam's *"you know who crossed?"*. Calder never crossed the river; he flew in from his own bank.
**Fix:** "and he came".

**m14. Ch 15: what Maren hears on night 5.** *"This was what a dragon sounded like when it was fighting."* On night 5 the dragons are calling over Teo's pyre (16). Nobody fights until day 8. It's her misreading, which is allowable.
**Check:** fine if intended. Otherwise: *"…when it was grieving, or fighting. She couldn't tell which."*

**m15. Ch 15: breathing arithmetic.** *"A horse breathed that many times in a minute. A man, in half a minute."* A resting man takes about 12–16 breaths a minute, so 11 breaths takes nearly a minute, not half.
**Fix:** "A man, in less than a minute."

**m16. Ch 3 against Ch 61: Haverly's bonding age (setup).** 3: *"a falcon found him when he was fourteen"*; 27: *"six years"*. 61: *"chosen at fifteen."*
**Fix:** in 61, "fourteen".

**m17. Ch 16: the other bonded pairs (setup).** *"The rest of the bonded riders, nine or ten of them."* Later canon (48, 54) says "the eleven bonded pairs on the ledges".
**Fix:** "ten or eleven of them". Or make 48 say "nine".

**m18. Ch 37: the ox named Holm.** Merrow's ox shares the surname of Sigrun and Ragnald Holm, which are both prominent names.
**Fix:** rename the ox (for example "Dun").

**m19. Ch 33: the sergeant called Brask.** *"a heavy man called Brask"*. Later there is a sellsword, Coll Brask (54), and the cast already has Brack, Brann Holt and Brannoch.
**Fix:** rename the sergeant (for example "Tasker").

**m20. Ch 24: the coin man looks like Corvin (check).** *"grey eyes and a way of standing very still"* are Corvin's signature traits, and canon says "Not Rook", with no twin intended. Readers who are tracking the twins will take him for one of them.
**Fix:** give him a different marker (brown eyes, a restless thumb), unless the echo is meant.

**m21. Ch 31: a missed beat.** The refugee names *"Calder Fenn"*. Maren pictured that very farmer in Ch 3 (*"A big man, she thought, slow, purple in the face…"*) and doesn't connect the two.
**Fix (optional payoff):** one line: *"Calder Fenn. Greta's farmer, who ran two leagues to his sister's fire."*

**m22. Spelling.** "stanching-word" (19, 21) against "staunching-word" (33).
**Fix:** pick one.

**m23. Ch 13: sound carrying over distance (check).** A fiddle and a thin voice heard 1.5–2 leagues (4.5–6 miles) across water (11, 13), and a horn heard from the ford about 10 leagues off. The text hedges ("so faint it was more like remembering"), so this is acceptable. Consider cutting the horn.

**m24. Ch 1 and 9: the wall bench.** 9: *"every autumn, the instructors sit the first-years down"*, but this class is second-years.
**Fix:** "sit the year down".

**m25. Ch 14: Fisk's eyesight.** He sees the skiff *"Pulled up on the spit under a log"* from the Eelweir, 1.5–2 leagues away, at night.
**Fix:** "My boys saw it from the punt."

**m26. Ch 8 and 9 notes against the prose (notes only).** Ch 8's notes say "new moon about twelve days after Ch 2". The prose (*"eleven nights since the pyre was lit"*) gives about 9. Ch 9's notes say "two weeks after Ch 1". The prose works; correct the notes.

**m27. Decisions_Ch21-30 against Story and the prose (doc only).** It says "Ada's seventh verse". The prose (22, 40) and Story say the third.
**Fix:** correct the Decisions note.

**m28. Chapter_Plan row 55 (doc only).** "The river breaks up with a noise like cannon." There are no cannon in this world, and Ch 55's own notes already avoid it.
**Fix:** reword the plan row.

**m29. Ch 6 against Ch 4: Haldor's years.** Teo (4): *"My father never once in forty years hid his face."* Arn (6): *"chief of this Clan for thirty-one years."*
**Fix:** "in thirty years". It's harmless if he means his father's adult life.

**m30. Ch 16: dragon sizes.** Hob: *"Sigrun's dragon's twice the size of the other two."* The canon sizes are 20, 16 and 12 ft. It reads as the miller's exaggeration; keep it, or change to "half again the size".

## Strongest chapters

1. **Ch 18, The Mill.** The threefold bond lived from inside three dying riders. It's the book's centre, and it's earned.
2. **Ch 39, First Frost.** Ide's three-day withering laid over Edric's death, and the lion's first crossing of the stream. It's restrained and devastating (apart from M7's closing explanation).
3. **Ch 28, Aurveth.** Ragnald's bees, Kharrak's "What would Dag have done?", and the white-bridge memory over the island. The chapter widens the world without stalling it.
4. **Ch 14, The Younger Son.** A witness who looks. The armband, the sheathed knife and the chain in his fist make a perfect guilt engine.
5. **Ch 32, Sellswords.** Vey is a fresh antagonist. The Chimney escape ("Old girl. Come on. Push.") is the book's best set-piece of tension without combat.

## Weakest chapters

1. **Ch 25, The Midden.** Two Hale Q&A scenes that mostly recap what Maren already narrated. The "too carefully" beat lands three times.
2. **Ch 35, The Mentor.** It re-reports Ferriby twice (Pell, then Rowan) before the real beat, Hale's slip, and then a third report scene with Hale on the bench.
3. **Ch 31, Tam.** The quarrel is strong, but the second half is a long refugee retelling of Ch 30 that the reader has just lived through.
4. **Ch 24, Hunted.** Four callers in a row at the gate make a list structure. The Hob betrayal and the flight into the Fellwood are excellent; compress the callers to two scenes.
5. **Ch 15, Signs.** Atmospheric, but the north-end silence, the birds stopping and the "held breath" beats repeat Ch 13 almost image for image before the breathing finally arrives.
