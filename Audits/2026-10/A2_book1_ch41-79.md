# Audit A2: Winshire Chronicles, Book 1, Chapters 41–79

Scope: the prose of Ch 41–79 (about 142,700 words). I checked it against the Bible (World, Story, Characters, Bestiary, Atlas, Magic, Series_Outline), Chapter_Plan, Notes (Decisions 41–79, Rulings_41-79, Voice_Samples), Style_Guide, Book 1 Ch 1–40 (to check setups) and Book 2 Outline Ch 1–12. I read every chapter in full and checked each quoted line against the file. Findings I'm unsure of are marked **(check)**.

## Health summary

The back half is in good shape. Every major beat in the plan arrives where it should: the freeze (41), the paper (45), the verse (46), the lamps failing (47), Hale's confession (49), the door (51), the pit (53), the collar (55), the oath broken (63), the twins (68), Pell (69), Ennis (70), Rook (72), the bond and Orrun's name (73), Haverly and Hale (75), the two locks (76–77), the meeting (78) and the flight (79). All of the settled Book 1 resolutions are on the page: Imra's shooter (68), Ennis (70), the twins' father (77), Davin (69), Old Grey's first step (69). The meeting comes only after midnight (78). The magic rules hold. Magic fails from night 90 and surges on 142–143 (69, 70, 71, 72, 75). The final battle has its extra workers: Halloran's or Delling's drawer (69), the ring-broken Fellow (74, 76), Haverly's quickened sword (75) and the runes burning in the chains (74, 77, 78). Neither Maren nor Calder casts anything. Orrun's multiplying follows the rule: glimpses at 53, 56 and 63, then one duplicate at 73, 75 and 79. Day counts, moon phases and most cross-POV timings match.

The real problems are few:
1. **The calendar.** Midwinter is day 88 and the book ends on day 144, about eight weeks later, in thin early spring. That contradicts the settled rule ("through to the next summer") and Book 2's "late spring" opening.
2. **The reveal rule.** Two lines in 77 and 79 hint at other Titans beyond the single permitted hint (Ruling 15).
3. **Some battle logistics.** Where Rowan is during the breach, whose keys open the locks, and how a "ten breaths" countdown survives long dialogue.
4. **Voice tics.** "That was the first thing", "for the rest of her life", "ten paces", the "the way you…" simile (496 uses in 41–79) and a repeated deathbed formula.

The 41–79 drafts have no banned words and no em dashes, which is a good sign.

## Findings

### Critical

**C1. Ch 44–79: the calendar can't reach "the next summer". (Settled rule, Plan, Book 2.)**
- Quotes: 44 "On the seventh night, midwinter night"; 57 "in the thin spring sun"; 67 "the long bend toward the Scarfell, white still on the far tops"; 68 "The Clan won't stand together again this side of the summer."
- Problem: midwinter is day 88 (Story timeline) and the Titan flies at sunrise on day 144, about 56 days later, so the climax falls in late winter or the first days of spring. The plan's Part Six heading, "Spring into summer", the plan's "across one year … to the next summer's war", and Book 2 Part One's "Late spring, days 0–25" all disagree with the text.
- Fix, option (a), keeping the prose: change the plan line to "late summer to early spring", the Part Six heading to "Early spring", and Book 2 Part One to "Early spring, days 0–25 (the thaw's end)". Then let Book 2 reach summer by its own Part Two ("Midsummer" already fits).
- Fix, option (b), keeping the rule: add roughly 60–90 days between the collar (day 120) and the twins (day 141). The bridge could be the river staying high and the Restoration waiting for low water. That would need every later day number moved, so (a) is far cheaper.

**C2. Ch 77: a second hint at other Titans, breaking Ruling 15.**
- Quote: "*There were destroyers once. Before. The old ones saw to them.*"
- Problem: Ruling 15 says the pull "toward somewhere a long way off" is the only Book 1 hint of the sleepers. This line says outright that other destroyers existed and that "the old ones" dealt with them, which is Book 2's reveal at ch 23.
- Fix: cut the line and keep the two after it: "*This one was never that. / They made it one.*"

**C3. Ch 79: a third hint at other Titans.**
- Quote: "*It wants them awake. It wants everything down.*"
- Problem: "them" can only mean other sleepers. It's the same breach of Ruling 15, and it's spoken aloud to Maren with Calder there.
- Fix: "*It wants everything down.*"

**C4. Ch 69 vs 71: two different stories of how Millbrook burns.**
- Quotes: 69, at dawn: "Black, thick, going up in a great slanting column from the roofs of the town". 69, in the afternoon: "A great black wall of it now… Not one column. The whole town." 71: "Millbrook burned because of the horses… The rising hadn't meant to burn the town… At dusk on the hundred-and-forty-second day… It fired the thatch."
- Problem: 69 has the town burning from dawn and burning whole by mid-afternoon, before Rowan's knights ever ride down at dusk. 71's central irony depends on the town not burning until the horses come.
- Fix in 69, at dawn: "Black, thick, going up from the water-meadows below the town and leaning north on the wind." In the afternoon: "The smoke over the meadows had spread to the jetty end. A few roofs. Not the town. Not yet."

**C5. Ch 75 (with 69, 71 and Book 2 ch 1): Rowan disappears during the breach, and Book 2 places her somewhere Book 1 doesn't.**
- Quote, 75 (Hale): "Rowan didn't believe it. Callis was on the gate with her that minute."
- Problem: that puts Rowan inside Greyhold at dusk on day 143. Three hours later the gate falls, Thorne dies in it and the yard is full of fighting, and the Captain isn't mentioned at all. Book 2 ch 1 then has "Rowan comes down from cracked Redwatch with the knights left, the great new chain on its wain". Book 1 left the chain with the half of the knights pinned at Redwatch (69), but last placed Rowan at Greyhold.
- Fix: one line in 75's yard paragraph: "Rowan wasn't in the yard. She'd taken every rider who could sit a horse back up the River Road at dusk, to Redwatch, where the bank was going." That empties Greyhold, explains the breach and matches Book 2.

**C6. Ch 74, 75, 77: which key opens which lock is never accounted for.**
- Quotes: 74 "He had a long black key in his hand. Iron, as long as his forearm"; 77 "Cassian kneeling on the boss with a long black key in it"; 75 "Callis had Thorne's key off her at the gate."
- Problem: Ruling 14 allows exactly two keys, both to the black door: Hale's and Thorne's. Corvin's key for the Ruin's lock comes from nowhere. It's also unclear whether Cassian turns the knight's lock with Thorne's door key or a third key. A reader will ask how a Restoration man owns a Watch key.
- Fix: settle it in a line. In 74, Calder sees the bow: "Its bow was cut with the same hooked marks as the boss. Old. Older than the iron round it." (That suggests the Restoration found or bought it.) In 77, Maren notes "Thorne's key, the dead woman's copy, in the boss to the bow." Then log the origin of Corvin's key as a Book 2 thread (see Open Setups).

### Major

**M1. Ch 45: Maren couldn't have said the noon oath "that very morning".**
- Quote: "She'd said it at the noon bell on midwinter morning, that very morning, facing east toward the old grey stones of the inner wall".
- Problem: the detail left Greyhold at the ninth hour, and Reede reads at noon in Millbrook, two leagues away. She was holding horses at Millbrook when the noon bell rang at Greyhold.
- Fix: "She'd said it at the noon bell yesterday, facing east toward the old grey stones of the inner wall, and every day before that."

**M2. Ch 51: the wrong owner for the hound Ash.**
- Quote: "Nothing like the thing Haverly had with Kesh, or Callis with Ash".
- Problem: Ash is Wynn's hound (Characters; 45 "Wynn… with his hound Ash"; 63 "Wynn's two, Ash and Sorrow, and Callis's own, Grip").
- Fix: "or Wynn with Ash".

**M3. Ch 62 vs 42: Aurveth's memory of the lion.**
- Quotes: 62 "*I have known since the field. Since it roared. I knew its voice.*" 42 "*I know that smell… I don't know how. I have never been near it.*"
- Problem: in 42 she's bewildered. In 62 she claims she knew all along that the lion was the cat from the bridge.
- Fix in 62: "*I have known since your coat. The smell on it, in the snow. And then its voice, when it roared through the hill. I didn't want to know.*"

**M4. Ch 44 vs 46: Calder's count of "do not ask".**
- Quote, 44: "He'd heard that before. He didn't know where."
- Problem: 46 counts Aurveth's "Do not ask me" on the ice as the first time ("For the third time. Twice on the ice at midwinter, and now here").
- Fix: cut the two sentences and keep "It went into him and stuck, like a burr in wool."

**M5. Ch 77: the countdown can't stay in step across the dialogue.**
- Quotes: Orrun says "*A few breaths. The candle's nearly at its mark.*" Cassian says "Four", then makes a long speech about his father, then says "Three".
- Problem: the whole plot rests on "the same breath". In 76 Corvin counts breath by breath. Between "four" and "three" Cassian and Maren trade about 150 words, which is many breaths, and before that Maren fights Callis. Cassian would be well out of step with his brother.
- Fix: count to the candle, not to breaths. In 74 and 76, Corvin: "Ten marks on the glass. A mark a breath." In 77, Cassian is watching the candle, not counting aloud, and says "four" only when the flame touches the mark. Or move the father speech to before the count starts, with Callis still on the floor, and keep "four…three…two…one" to clipped lines.

**M6. Ch 65 and 71: the order date for Odo's collars contradicts the summer chains.**
- Quotes: 65 Tam: "A hundred collars. In the autumn." 71 Odo: "With the order. In the autumn. A hundred collars."
- Problem: Odo's crossed hammers are on the Upper Rush chain from day 0, at the end of summer (2, 50), and on the lion's collar from the island hunters (17–21). Both are older than an autumn order.
- Fix: "A hundred collars. In the spring, before the summer. And chain by the hundredweight." Make the same change in both chapters.

**M7. Ch 58, 68 and 69: the drawer is "Delling's woman", not Halloran's, and an early setup goes unused.**
- Quotes: 58 "a woman in a plain grey coat… A house drawer"; 68 "And Delling's woman?" "Goes with you. For the gate."
- Problem: your settled rule names "Halloran's drawer". Ch 34 set up exactly this woman ("Grey-haired, plain, in a plain coat… 'Halloran's drawer'"). Calder sees her again at the Ram's Head and doesn't recognise her.
- Fix in 58: "He'd seen her before. On Gallows Quay, coming out of the Halloran door, and two beast-sellers crossing the street to keep clear of her." In 68: "And Halloran's woman?" "Lent to Delling. Goes with you." This also sets up Book 2's Halloran-money thread (ch 30).

**M8. Ch 68: a slip outside Calder's point of view.**
- Quote: "They didn't need to. They both knew."
- Problem: Calder can't know what both twins knew.
- Fix: "Neither of them needed to, by the sound of it."

**M9. Ch 62: the days run out of order.**
- Quotes: "Bel decided on the hundred-and-thirty-fourth." then "He sat on the top of the Scar that night", then "On the morning of the hundred-and-thirty-fourth day there was smoke over Greyhold… Three days to the ferry."
- Problem: the morning of 134 comes after the night of 134.
- Fix: move the smoke section before "Bel decided". Or change it to "On the morning of the hundred-and-thirty-fifth… Two days to the ferry. He counted them."

**M10. Ch 70 vs 46 and 62: the distance to Greyhold.**
- Quotes: 70, from the hill above the Fenn farm: "three leagues downriver"; 46, from Upper Rush: "six leagues and more"; 62, from Crow Scar: "seven leagues downriver".
- Problem: Atlas gives the hill to the Ruin as 1.5 leagues and the Ruin to Greyhold as 3.5, so the hill is about 4.5–5 leagues from Greyhold.
- Fix in 70: "four leagues and more downriver". In 62: "six leagues downriver".

**M11. Ch 52–56: where the dragons' hill is, relative to Upper Rush.**
- Quotes: 54 "On the hill above the Fenn farm, a league off, he felt the other two"; 52 "He went up the hill… The dragons were on the hillside where they always lay, above the Fenn farm"; 50 "the ridge behind Bel's".
- Problem: Upper Rush is 2 leagues upriver of Harrow's End (Atlas), so the hill above the Fenn farm is about 2 leagues away, not a short walk "up the hill".
- Fix: in 52 and 54, use "the ridge behind Bel's" throughout the Upper Rush winter.

**M12. Ch 50 vs 52: four leagues or five to the Ewe Beck camp.**
- Quotes: 50 "I'm going four." 52 "It was five leagues… five leagues up the river lane".
- Fix in 52: "It was four leagues by the lane, and Aurveth did it in less than an hour…"

**M13. Ch 50: the memory-push runs the wrong way.**
- Quote: "She pushed it at him, the way she'd pushed the arch."
- Problem: in 42, Calder pushed the arch to Lisska.
- Fix: "She pushed it at him, the way she pushed her memories."

**M14. Ch 76: Calder feels Lisska coming too early.**
- Quote: after the fall, "A small bright fire. Green. Frightened. Coming toward him."
- Problem: in 77 Lisska is still at the top of the archive stair. She comes down only after Cassian dies, the seal breaks and Orrun grieves.
- Fix: "A small bright fire. Green. Frightened. Far off, under the other bank, and still." Make it start moving in 78.

**M15. Ch 47 vs 44: "the turn of the year" means two different nights.**
- Quotes: 47 "Round the turn of the year. A week before midwinter, just after the river locked." 44 "at midnight, on the turn of the year".
- Fix in 47: "Round midwinter. A week before. Just after the river locked."

**M16. Ch 57 vs 68: are the twins the same height? (check)**
- Quotes: 57 "He was taller than she'd remembered from the roadblock." 68 "The same height, the same narrow shoulders".
- Problem: if the twins are identical, 57 plants a false clue. If the height difference is a deliberate tell, 68 cancels it.
- Fix in 57: "He seemed taller than she'd remembered from the roadblock. People do, she thought, with seven hundred faces turned up to them."

**M17. Ch 75: Haverly quotes Pell's words but probably wasn't at the gate. (check)**
- Quote: "'Everybody says that.' He almost smiled. Pell's words, in his mouth. He must have been there, she thought. In the gateway."
- Problem: nothing puts Haverly at the gate on day 142, and he isn't in 67 or 69. The echo reads as a device.
- Fix: cut "Everybody says that" from Haverly. Give him something of his own: "'You always say that,' he said, which wasn't true. She'd never said it to him."

**M18. Ch 60, 69 and 75: the same deathbed scene three times.**
- Quotes: 60 "'Don't talk.' 'I'll talk if I like.'"; 69 "'Don't talk.' 'Everybody says that.'"; 75 "'Don't talk.' 'Everybody says that.'"; 75 Hale: "'Don't,' said Hale, before she could say it."
- Problem: four deaths, one routine: a hand held, a wise last line, "almost smiled" (10 uses in 41–79).
- Fix: keep the routine for Pell only. Hallam's reply could be "Shut up, farmer." Haverly can die mid-sentence about the falcon. Hale is right as written.

**M19. Ch 53: emotion named after it's already been shown.**
- Quotes: "What she felt now was shame. Ashamed in front of it, the way you'd be ashamed in front of a person." "She didn't feel sorry. That was the strange part. She felt something, but it wasn't sorry."
- Problem: the style guide says don't name the emotion. Her "We did it" speech already carries the shame.
- Fix: cut both passages. Replace the first with "She couldn't look at its feet. She made herself."

**M20. Ch 79: the drain paragraph tells and sums up.**
- Quotes: "She didn't need to see Hallowmere. She knew." "Three hundred years of light, gone out between one breath and the next."
- Problem: it's a lecture paragraph closed with a summary line (an AI tell). The Redwatch lamps and Orrun's sense of the draining already carry it.
- Fix: keep the Redwatch lamps going out and the land draining through Orrun. Cut the Hallowmere list and the summary line. End on "Going away, north-east, after a speck in the sunrise."

**M21. Ch 46: an emotion beat that explains itself.**
- Quote: "Not tired. Old. As if she'd been carrying something for fifty-one years, and had just put it down, and found that it had been holding her up."
- Problem: the "Not X. Y." pattern (74 uses in 41–79) plus an explaining simile.
- Fix: "She looked older than he'd ever seen her. Her hands lay on the table like something she'd set down."

**M22. Ch 45: the box simile explains what the scene already shows.**
- Quote: "As if, all her life, she'd been carrying a box, and singing about it, and never once thought about what was in the box".
- Fix: cut it. "Heavier." is enough. Or use her own river: "Heavier, like a rope when the thing on the end of it wakes."

**M23. Ch 44: a stacked triplet and the same image three times.**
- Quotes: "Vast." … "Not asleep." … "And bound." … "And under all of that, something worse." The millwheel appears three times, and doors three times ("Like a door. Like a hand closing…", "as if a door had shut").
- Fix: keep "Vast" and "bound". Fold "Not asleep" into a clause: "Something vast, and awake, and bound." Keep one millwheel and one door.

**M24. Ch 54 and 59: two different sellswords both say "like a pair".**
- Quotes: 54 Brask: "Two hundred for her and two hundred for you. Like you were a pair." 59, Rowan quoting another sellsword: "Like a pair, he said."
- Problem: the echo is too neat for two unrelated witnesses, and the phrase then returns in 59 and 64.
- Fix in 59: "Same price, he said. Same buyer."

**M25. Ch 49: which way is the inner wall? (check)**
- Quotes: 49 "East, from the yard of Greyhold, there's nothing but the inner wall, and the archive under the north tower". 47 "toward the north end of the barn. Toward the north wall… the inner wall beyond it… under the north tower".
- Problem: the oath faces east to the inner wall, but the beasts face north to it.
- Fix: in 49, "the inner wall at the north-east corner, and the archive under the north tower, and the river beyond". Since the catacombs run north-east toward the Ruin, both directions then make sense.

**M26. Ch 72 and 79: Calder's keepsakes are inconsistent.**
- Quote, 72: "He put it inside his coat. With the two chains, and the iron ring from the ford."
- Problem: in 70 he gave the five-link chain to Lisska, so at 72 he has only one chain. Maren hands it back in 78, which is correct.
- Fix in 72: "With the island chain, and the iron ring from the ford."

**M27. Ch 68: Rook says "a byre", but the bonds took in a field.**
- Quote: "The dragons went to a farmer in a byre."
- Fix: "The dragons went to a farmer in a barley field."

**M28. Ch 67: a flat chapter.**
- Quotes: "She didn't go down." "She sat there all day." "She didn't know which way to go."
- Problem: the whole chapter is watching, counting and not deciding, much of it summarised ("She counted. She couldn't stop herself."). The lion's broken question at the end is the only turn.
- Fix: give Maren one act. For example, she goes halfway down to Rowan's column, a knight's greyhart scents Orrun, and she turns back. That makes the outcast's choice cost something.

## Book 1 setups still open at the end of Book 1 (Book 2 must pay these off)

1. **The truth about Teo.** Calder alone heard Rook's account under the arch (68). Rook is dead (72). The ledges' witness of the boots (62) and Gunna's reading (Arn threw her out; Ulric listened and said nothing) are still unresolved.
2. **Imra's shooter's name.** "Gone" with Rook (72). Book 2 ch 17 needs Rook's ledger or Delling's chest. Plant one line in Book 1 72 or 74 that the paymaster's ledger is still in Delling's keeping.
3. **Corvin** went up the older stair with two men (76). Where does that stair come out?
4. **Corvin's long black key** (74): where it came from, and which key Cassian turned (77) (see C6).
5. **Callis**: alive in the knight's lock chamber with both arms broken (77). Thorne's key is with him. His grievance (passed over for the inner circle) is still open.
6. **The lit sentence on the knight's lock** that "didn't fade" (77), plus the runes still burning on the chain-ends (79). Check Book 2 ch 4, which has them "still burn[ing] white" after the drain. That needs a reason under Magic §6 (dead ground starves marks).

## Strongest chapters
- **50 Bel**: the cart, the hoof-print and Bel scrubbing the chains with ash. The plot's central object pays off through character, not exposition.
- **60 The Ford Again**: a clear, fair ambush that turns the no-kin law into a weapon. Hallam's fall, and the boats watching the dead come down, make a perfect echo.
- **69 The Gate**: Pell's death, Wren's "You're her", Old Grey's step and the noon lamps flaring. Several threads land without anything feeling crowded.
- **55 Thaw**: the river going out, earned trust, the collar coming off, and Odo's mark under her thumb.
- **73 Hollin Knowe**: the bond written as giving rather than taking, the first duplicate, and the kin-speech carrying Lisska's message cleanly.

## Weakest chapters
- **67 Muster**: passive watching and counting. Maren decides nothing (M28).
- **62 Before the Storm**: the days run out of order (M9), Aurveth's contradiction (M3), and the cat-on-the-steps recap over again.
- **44 The General's Silence**: mood without movement. The images repeat (millwheel ×3, doors ×3) and the triplet beats stack up (M23).
- **54 The Gathering**: Brask's exposition dump and a village meeting where "Nobody decided".
- **78 Underground**: the meeting is right, but the second half is recap-by-dialogue ("She told him. Fast. In pieces.") over news the reader already has.
