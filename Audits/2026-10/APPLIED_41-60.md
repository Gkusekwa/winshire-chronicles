## Style pass (DECISIONS "Style pass")

Every chapter was read whole before editing. Cuts were preferred to rewrites. No new plot or facts, no em dashes, no banned phrases introduced. Per chapter (details in the session log):
- **"the way" simile frame:** cut or converted about a third or more in each chapter, mostly in action and in the "the way you'd..." asides that repeated an earlier image. Examples: the three bull-in-a-field similes (42/43/58) are down to one (42); the wave/sea similes (50, 52) are gone; "the way it heard the wind" is gone from 51 and 53. The literal uses ("the way he did", "the way she'd held Joss's knife out") were left alone. 56 and 59 had few real similes, so only one or two came out of each.
- **Caps (range keeps at most one of each; book-wide caps checked against the current 61–79 files):**
  - "That was the first thing": 5 -> 0
  - "for the rest of her/his life": 2 -> 0 (reserved for 77)
  - "ten paces": now only in 42 (5) and 55 (3), the two chapters allowed in this range; all others paraphrased
  - "almost smiled": 4 -> 1 (Hale, 49)
  - "That was the worst of it": 3 -> 0 (so 61–79 may keep its one)
  - "Two hammers, crossed." as a standalone line: 4 -> 1 (50)
  - "twenty paces" / "like the end of the world" (A1 M12 echoes): 4/1 -> 0/0
- **"Not X. Y." fragments:** down by about a third (narrative ones only; dialogue and mind-speech mostly kept).
- **Over-explained emotion:** A2 M19, M21, M22, M23 as above. Elsewhere, sentences that named or re-explained a feeling already shown were cut. Examples: "She couldn't have said why that felt different... It did." (43); "She couldn't have said how she knew it. She knew." (45); "That was the thing he'd remember." (50); "which was the worst thing he'd ever heard" and "It was the hardest thing he'd said that night" (52); "She didn't know why that mattered. It mattered." and "She knew that. She didn't know how she knew." (55); "He didn't know what to say." (46).
- **Banned-phrase near-misses fixed (44):** "Something shift in her" (a form of "something shifted") rewritten; "with the weight of whatever she'd touched" -> "under whatever she'd touched".
- **Door similes (A2 tics, M23):** 5 -> 2 (one in 41, one in 44).
