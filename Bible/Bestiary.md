# Winshire — Bestiary

*The creatures of Winshire, kingdom by kingdom. Everything here is a **working proposal** unless the bible marks it established. Established: beasts, bonds, the one-life rule, ancient beasts, dragons, the lion, the speaking archive beast, and the Titans. Creatures first set while drafting Book 1 are marked with their chapter numbers.*

## How creatures are sorted

People in Winshire sort creatures by what they can do, not by what they look like.

| Kind | What it means | Can it bond? | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Animals** | Ordinary creatures. No bond, no mind to share. | No | Cows, sheep, herons, eels, ordinary horses |
| **Beasts** | Creatures with enough mind to bond. Most are no cleverer than a good dog until they bond; a few are clever before. | Yes, rarely, and only by the beast's choice | Bond-hounds, falcons, great cats, duskcats, sabrecats, stonehorns, dray-beasts, wyverns |
| **Great beasts** | Beasts of great size or power, often long-lived and sentient. Bonds with them are rare and change kingdoms. | Yes, very rarely (unicorns almost never; the phoenix never) | Dragons, unicorns, the phoenix, tuskers (mammoths), olifants (elephants) |
| **Ancient beasts** | Very old, sentient, and powerful. They regard bonding as a taint and bond only through respect and sacrifice. | Almost never | The lion (Orrun), the speaking raven of Isvaren |
| **Titans** | Mountain-sized. Known to ordinary people only as legend in Book 1. (The Last Titan, as first seen in Ch 79, is hill-sized, still diminished by its chains.) | No new bonds in Books 1–3. A bond made **before** a beast grows into a Titan lasts through the change: Maren and Orrun, Calder and the three dragons. | The Last Titan |

**Sizes** *(settled by the author)*: most beasts are 8 ft and more, apart from small personal ones such as falcons, hounds, martens and the cat (see `World.md`).

**The rules every creature follows** *(established)*: a bond lets a person and a beast understand each other; without one they can't. A bond shares one life between two: when either dies, the other usually dies too. Exceptions are extremely rare.

**Bonds and bindings** *(settled)*: a true bond forms **only by the beast's choice**. What bond-forcers do, and what Sarovel's royal line does to its unicorns, is a **binding**: coercion by the forbidden discipline of binding (honey-smoke, words, will and collar), chains and pain, imitating a bond from the outside. A bound beast obeys, but there is no understanding, no mind-speech and no shared life. If the binder dies, the beast is simply free; if the binder flees, alive, it goes slack and follows any hand (`World.md`; `Magic.md` §6). If the beast dies, the binder lives. A binding can never become a bond by force. It can be the place where a bond happens, if someone offers themselves into it and the beast chooses them (Maren and Orrun, chapter 73).

**Bound beasts** *(from the drafts)*: in six-dot collars, empty-eyed, silent, feeling no pain, driven by one handler's will. One looks up at Kharrak passing overhead, and for a moment something of its own comes back.

## The lion *(established; settled by the author)*

- **In Book 1:** about 10 ft at the shoulder, growing toward dragon size over the series. Earth-and-stone power, faint for now (`World.md`). Mane iron-grey, almost white at the roots; pale ice eyes; forefoot as broad as a round shield. It sleeps in a dug-and-refilled hollow in the island's north end.
- **41–60:** it breaks the ice from above to drag Maren out (41). It lies in the arch; then from day 104 digs at the north-end pit, scraping at dressed stone. It roars on night 102 when Maren's hand is on the door; something far below pulls its chains taut, and three dragons answer. It lets Maren touch its mane (night 119), then lays its neck down for the collar to be struck off (day 120). Afterwards it lets her come to five paces and lay a hand on its foreleg. When it shakes, the island shakes; the ground loosens ahead of its claws when it digs. Still no bond and no words.
- **The lion's collar:** hinged, the pin peened over; the ring of six on the outside and Odo Marl's crossed hammers inside.
- **Orrun, after the bond (73):** speaks in Maren's head slowly and deeply, "like stones turning over in a riverbed"; the ground moves for him (stakes pulled, beacon-stones rolled, stone opening); can speak to dragons as far kin.
- **Orrun's multiplication** *(the author's decision; it advances through the series)*: Orrun can raise a second lion out of the ground he stands on, earth, turf, flagstone or living rock, in his own shape and size. **Each duplicate is as powerful as Orrun himself.** **Book 1:** before the bond it happens without his knowing, only for a breath, and nobody who sees it is sure: a lion-shape of fill beside him in the pit (53); Lisska sees two lions at the arch, then one (56); Callis's hounds stare at something lion-shaped standing up out of the oaks behind him (63). After the bond it's real but limited: **one duplicate at a time, lasting minutes, and costly**. Maren feels it go out of him like blood, and afterwards he shakes, slows or has to stand. Hollin Knowe, fighting the bond-forcers (73); Greyhold's yard-stone, holding the Long Barn while he goes to the stair (75); the hill's rock, holding the passage roof off Aurveth on the way up (79). When a duplicate is done it goes back into the ground it came from. **Book 2:** it grows to three or four at once, each still as strong as he is. The rumour that the Restoration's leader has "an old beast's gift" for being in two places is the wrong guess that this power invites (Maren, 73).

## The Last Titan, as seen (79)

Lion-kin, the size of a hill; grey as old stone; a mane like a ridge of broken rock; plates along its back like the courses of a wall; wings of something like stone and something like leather, wider than the river; broken chain-ends on its legs; pale eyes. The ground heaves when it moves.

## Across the Six Kingdoms

### Dragons *(established)*
Great beasts, sentient, long-lived, and the Dragon Clan's power. The Clan breeds and keeps hundreds; only a few are bonded, to its warriors: **fourteen bonded pairs before the mill, eleven after (48)**. Four-limbed and winged: two wings *and* four legs. They speak mind to mind with a bonded partner and to no one else, except as kin to other dragons within sight (Ruling 5). Aurveth, Kharrak and Lisska are Clan-bred: Lisska about 12 ft at the shoulder and 40 ft long; Kharrak about 16 ft; Aurveth about 20 ft.
- **The Roosts, winter of Book 1:** a dozen dragons on the ledges, the eleven bonded pairs who won't fight kin; the unbonded hunt in the high glens.
- **Morrow:** an old black dragon of the ledges, greying at the edges of her scales, bonded to Hallam Brecc. Lies down beside him at the ford (60).
- **Book 1:** the funeral flight (2), the Roosts above Varrhold (6), the threefold bond (18).

### Wyverns
Two-legged, winged, smaller than dragons: the size of a large horse (smaller than Lisska), black-green, with a barbed tail and no forelimbs. **Not sentient** in the way dragons are: as clever as a hawk, and as easy to frighten. They nest on sea cliffs and high crags.
- **Saltreach** pirates and sellsword companies tame them, without bonds, using hoods and hooks and hunger. A tamed wyvern is a scout and a weapon, and it will kill its handler the first time he's careless.
- A wyvern **can** bond, very rarely. People who see one bonded never forget it, because a bonded wyvern stops being frightened.
- **Dragon or wyvern?** Clan folk find it insulting to confuse the two. Count the legs: dragons have four and wings; wyverns stand on two and walk on their wings.
- **Pell's wyvern** *(Chapter 9)*: thirty-one years ago on the coast road below Stillhaven, one of a Saltreach sellsword company's two tamed wyverns got loose and took Pell's fingers. His friend Aldo Venn killed it. It was the rare bonded one: its partner, a pirate girl of fifteen, dropped dead on the shingle a hundred yards away. ("A wild wyvern doesn't bond.")
- **Book 1:** Captain Vey's sellswords have two hooded wyverns, **Sable** and **Hask**, at the siege of Crow Scar (32), and Kharrak despises them. A wyvern cage sits at the Gallows Quay beast market (36); a wild-caught young one freed there flew out to sea.

### Bond-hounds
Rough-coated, heavy-jawed, built for long patrols. They are the most common bonded beast in Hearthmere's knightly orders, and the most common reason a knight dies of grief.
- **Book 1:** Maren's father's dog (1); **Bracken**, Ide Darrow's hound, brindled and waist-high, killed at Redwatch, whose death kills her (39); Wynn's hounds Ash and Sorrow and Callis's Grip (63).

### Falcons and hawk-beasts
Fast, proud, far-sighted. Prized by scouts and archers.
- **Book 1:** Kesh, Lucan Haverly's falcon (1, 9, 75).

### Great cats
Grey, calf-sized, apparently bored. They choose people rarely and change their minds never.
- **What they are** *(proposal, Oct 2026)*: the last small line of **the old lions' kind**, which is why they're grey (Orrun's mane is iron-grey). They're calf-sized where a lion is a hill, but they keep something of the old lions' sense: a great cat feels what's coming to the beasts around it, grief especially, and goes and lies beside the one that will need it.
- **Book 1:** the grey cat at Greyhold that "nobody owns" (1, 27). It sleeps each night beside the beast whose rider will have a bad day tomorrow (Merrow's Dun, Ide's bed).

### Dray-beasts and ox-beasts
Working beasts of the farms: huge, slow, gentle, and stronger than any horse; 8 ft and up. Some farmers swear their greys understand them without a bond. They might be right.
- **Book 1:** Joss's greys and Hollin's ox-beast, stolen from Upper Rush (2); one grey found at Gallows Quay (36); **Old Grey**, bound at the Ewe Beck camp (50). **Dun**, Merrow's ox-beast, died of age with him (37). A **white hump-ox** freed on Gallows Quay; Aurveth remembered one walking free on the bridge.

## The big cats

### Duskcats *(panther-like)*
Long, low, black or smoke-grey, with eyes like lamps and a coat that drinks light. They hunt the deep forests at night: the Thornwood, the Elderwood, and the far end of the Brackenwood. Beasts, not great beasts, but clever before any bond, and patient in a way that frightens hunters.
- **Bonds:** rare, and almost always with someone who has been alone a long time. Wyldmarch Keepers bond them more than anyone; a Keeper with a duskcat is the clans' night watch.
- **In Sarovel** a bonded duskcat is a mark of a spy, an assassin, or a noble who wants to be thought one.
- **The grey cat at Greyhold is not a duskcat** *(proposal, Oct 2026)*: duskcats are black and panther-like, and it is a great cat (see Great cats). There have been grey cats at Greyhold since before it was a school, since there was a door to keep under it. Nobody owns this one because it's on duty. It sat down beside Maren at Pell's lesson (9) before anyone knew why. In Book 2 it leaves Greyhold the night before the hill falls and turns up at Liss's ferry house, and in Book 3 Maren sees its look again in the Grey Mother's eyes (30).
- **Book 1:** a smoke-grey duskcat cub poached from the far end of the Brackenwood, caged on Millbrook jetty and freed by Maren (Chapter 7). Tam named it Shadow.

### Sabrecats *(sabre-toothed)*
Heavier than a bear, short-tailed, with two fangs as long as a man's forearm. They live in the cold: the High Crown, the Iron Fells, the snowfields above Isvaren. They follow the tusker herds and take the old and the young.
- **Bonds:** very rare. A bonded sabrecat is a soldier's dream and a farmer's nightmare.
- **Kessmoor** hunts them for their fangs, which carve better than ivory, and for their pelts.
- **Isvaren** College Wardens on the high passes sometimes ride in pairs with one bonded sabrecat between them. Bandits leave those pairs alone.

## The great herds

### Tuskers *(mammoth-like)*
Great beasts of the cold north: shaggy, red-brown, high-shouldered, with tusks that curl back on themselves like old roots. They walk the snowfields under the High Crown in herds led by the oldest cow, and they **remember**: routes, water, and the places their dead are buried. They mourn.
- **Language** *(proposal, Oct 2026)*: yes. Tuskers speak in **the rumble**, a call too low to hear properly, felt in the feet and the chest more than the ears. It carries for leagues through frozen ground, so two herds in different valleys can talk. Oldest cows speak most and calves hardly at all. No person understands it without a bond, but Isvaren's drovers read its moods the way a ferryman reads weather, and the Vael Pass goes quiet when the herds are talking.
- **Memory of the old realm** *(proposal)*: yes, but as a map, not a story. Each herd walks routes its oldest cows learned from theirs, and those routes are the old realm's: the Oneroad, the fords where the bridges stood, the Deep's tide-lines. Herds still walk round lakes that were valleys when the routes were made. They won't cross dead ground. They know the old lions' kind as far kin (Book 2: the old cow and Orrun).
- **The pass agreement** is older than the colleges: the old realm made it with the herds, and every winter the eldest cow renews it by laying her trunk on the pass-stone above Vael. Isvaren thinks it hires the herds. The herds think they're keeping a promise.
- **Bonds:** very rare, and almost always with a child. A bonded tusker outlives three generations of a family and treats them as one person.
- **Isvaren** hires tusker herds (by agreement with the herd, not by force) to haul stone and supplies over the Vael Pass in winter. The tusker road is the slowest and safest in the north.

### Olifants *(elephant-like)*
Great beasts of the warm south-west, grey and nearly hairless, with great fanned ears, long trunks and straight tusks. They live in herds on the Sunward Hills and the Gilt valley. Like tuskers, they remember and mourn, and people who have watched an olifant herd stand over its dead stop calling them animals.
- **Sarovel** makes them a royal beast second only to the unicorn. Bonded olifants walk in the Bond-King's processions, painted, and in war they are the walls the Sarovel army hides behind.
- **Language and memory** *(proposal, Oct 2026)*: olifants speak in rumble too, with trumpets for alarm and grief. The **wild herds** of the Sunward Hills remember the old realm the way tuskers do, as roads and water. The **royal olifants** have kept the language and lost the map: nine generations in Sarovel's stables, bred from cows taken young, and no old cow to teach them. They're a herd with no memory of where it's from, which is Sarovel's story in one animal.
- **Book 2 hook** *(proposal)*: when the Grace fails at Solenne, a wild cow comes down out of the Sunward Hills to the city walls, and the royal olifants break their pickets and follow her out in a line along a road nobody knew they remembered. Maren and Tobin take the low city's people out behind them, because the olifants know the safe way down.
- **Ivory** is forbidden in Sarovel and sold anyway in Saltreach. An olifant tusk at Gallows Quay is a hanging matter in Solenne and a good day's trade in Wendmouth.

### Stonehorns *(rhinoceros-like)*
Massive, grey, plated, with one great horn and a smaller one behind it, short-sighted and short-tempered. They graze the Hallow Plains and the scrubland of Saltreach. Beasts, not great beasts: stubborn rather than clever.
- **Bonds:** rare. A bonded stonehorn is said to be the only beast that will walk into fire for its partner without being asked twice.
- **Saltreach** sellswords and the Restoration use tamed stonehorns, unbonded and blinkered, to haul siege carts and break gates.
- **The Wyldmarch** horse clans count stonehorn herds the way farmers count weather.

### The rest of the grazing kind
- **Great elk** *(Wyldmarch)*: taller than a man at the shoulder; the horse clans' beasts.
- **Greyharts** *(Hearthmere)*: white, elk-like, nine feet at the shoulder; bred by the crown as knights' war-mounts. Wild herds in the Brackenwood are royal game.
- **Aurochs** *(Hearthmere and the Candle Downs)*: the wild black cattle the farm ox-beasts were bred from, still loose in the Greenwold. Their bulls kill more people every year than all the wyverns in Saltreach.
- **Dray-beasts and ox-beasts** *(everywhere)*: the farms' working beasts.

## The sky: flying creatures

*Everything here is a working proposal unless marked established. New in October 2026; the Book 1 creatures are marked with their chapters.*

**The sky is shared** *(author's direction: these are beast books, not dragon books)*. Every kingdom has its own beasts of the air, and none of them owns the sky. The Clan's dragons are the biggest flyers in the Marches, but the Isvaren colleges have their stormkites, the Wyldmarch clans have the rocs of the Worldsend, Saltreach's companies have their wyverns, and every tower in Hearthmere has its falcons. Over each kingdom the sky has its own order, and a traveller can tell where they are by what's flying. Hill people read it the way river people read the water: when every bird in a valley goes to ground at noon, something big is crossing it, and an empty sky at night means something worse.

**Who can carry a rider** *(proposal)*: four kinds, each in its own country, each paid for in its own way.
- **Dragons** (the Scarfell): the strongest and the furthest, and only by the dragon's choice. The Clan's power rests on them.
- **Worldsend rocs** (the Wyldmarch): they can carry a grown rider for a day, but they bond only with a clan's chosen, perhaps a dozen in a generation.
- **Stormkites** (Isvaren): small, light riders only, ledge to ledge and pass to pass, on a good wind.
- **Tamed wyverns** (Saltreach): a light scout for a short hop, hooded and hungry. They never carry anyone twice gladly.

Kessmoor, Hearthmere and Sarovel have no riders of the air, and each says it doesn't want any. In the war that matters: the Accord's sky in Book 3 is four kingdoms' beasts flying together for the first time.

**Messages** *(proposal)*: each kingdom sends its news on its own wings. Hearthmere's knights fly **falcons** between towers. Saltreach's pirates use **gull-hawks**. Isvaren's colleges use **lantern owls** by night. The Wyldmarch's Keepers use **hollow owls**. Sarovel's court uses **sun-hawks**, mostly to be seen doing it. Kessmoor uses semaphore towers and is proud of it. A messenger bird flies to a *place* it knows, never to a person, so a message chases a moving army only as far as its last roost. Lisska is the exception: she flies to Maren because Orrun is kin, which is why she's worth more than any bird.

**Flyers and the Deep** *(proposal; follows `Magic.md` §1)*: flying things feel the Deep's movements before people do. On dead ground birds don't nest, and the sky over Hearthmere after Book 1 is noticeably empty. In a **stir** they wheel over the temple. In a **surge** they fly into walls and lamps. Before a **waking** every bird leaves the country for a day, and the silence is the warning *(Book 1 ch 15: the birds stop on the island before the breathing)*.

### Great beasts of the air

- **Dragons** *(established; see Dragons above)*: four legs and two wings; the Dragon Clan's beasts. The Clan breeds them; a few bond. Calder's three become Titan-class in Book 3.
- **The phoenix** *(established that there are a few; see the Wyldmarch)*: fire that dies and returns. It never bonds. It flies at the Worldsend's peaks, and fire on the peaks is the clans' sign of it.
- **Stormkites** *(Isvaren and the High Crown; proposal)*: great grey fork-tailed kites with a wingspan of forty feet, so light-boned that a grown one weighs less than a man. They ride the storm fronts off the High Crown and are seen over Vael before bad weather, sliding down the wind in pairs. They're beasts. They bond rarely, and only with small, light people, so in Isvaren a stormkite bond goes to a girl of fifteen or a slight old woman, never to a Warden. The college **Pass Wardens** keep a roost above the Vael Pass, where the bonded kite-riders carry warnings and medicine across the pass in hours instead of days. Stormkites fly the Deep's tide-lines the way ships use a current, and the College of Tides has charted their flights for two hundred years. One of Idony's oldest charts is a stormkite chart.
- **Worldsend rocs** *(the Wyldmarch and the Worldsend; proposal)*: the largest birds in Winshire, rust-brown and white, with a wingspan wider than a barn is long. They nest on the Worldsend's eastern faces and hunt elk on the Hallow Plains, lifting a yearling whole. They're great beasts: long-lived and wary, and they remember. Each clan of the horse folk has one or two bonded **roc-riders**, chosen by the birds, who carry news across the plains faster than any horse and are buried, when they die, on the high ledges beside their birds. Rocs and the phoenix share the Worldsend's peaks and leave each other alone. Rocs and dragons dislike each other the way two proud old houses do.
- **Crag-eagles** *(the Scarfell and the Worldsend; proposal)*: bone-breakers. Dun and black, with a twenty-five-foot wingspan and a beard of stiff feathers under the beak. They carry sheep bones up and drop them on the scree to break them for the marrow. They're the only thing in the Scarfell that will fight a young dragon for a kill. They take lambs, and once in a generation a child left in a field, and the Clan pays a silver a head for them. Lisska hates them on principle. A Clan rider bonded to a crag-eagle instead of a dragon is a family scandal, though it has happened, and the riders of the ledges still tell the story.

### Beasts of the air

- **Wyverns** *(established; see Wyverns above)*: two legs and two wings, horse-sized, clever as a hawk and as easy to frighten. Wild rookeries sit on the sea cliffs from Gullhaven to Blackwater, and every spring the Saltreach companies send boys down ropes to steal the eggs. A tamed wyvern is hooded, hooked and kept hungry. Book 1: Sable and Hask (32), the Gallows Quay cage (36), the Ram's Head lines (54). Book 2: Corvin's bound wyvern pair in the cavern under the river.
- **Falcons and hawk-beasts** *(established; see above)*: the knights' scouts. A bonded falcon shares what it sees in flashes, not pictures: Haverly said "she shows me where to look, not what's there". Hearthmere's towers keep unbonded falcons for messages: Northwatch to Redwatch to Greyhold, each bird knowing its two roosts. Book 1: Kesh (1, 9, 75).
- **Gull-hawks** *(Saltreach; Book 1, 34–36)*: half gull, half hawk, all appetite. Pirates' messengers. A gull-hawk will carry a message to a ship it knows, which is the one trick no other bird can do. A captain who loses his gull-hawks is a captain who can't be found.
- **Sun-hawks** *(Sarovel)*: golden hawks kept by the nobility, mostly for show. Each noble house flies its own on feast days from the Sun Stair, and the order they fly in is the order of precedence at court. The unchosen who do the court's work (Book 2 ch 58) are the ones who actually train them.
- **Lantern owls** *(Isvaren)*: owls with faintly glowing eyes that nest in the observatory towers. Students think they bring luck in exams. The glow is the Deep: a lantern owl's eyes go dark on dead ground and blaze in a surge, so lantern owls are the poor man's tide-glass, and Vael's ferry-folk keep one in a wicker cage on every boat. Idony grew up with one.
- **Hollow owls** *(the Wyldmarch; proposal)*: pale owls as tall as a child, which nest in the hollows of the Hollow Ring's stones and the Thornwood's dead oaks. The Keepers' messengers by night. They won't fly over dead ground, so when the Thornwood dies behind the first waking (Book 2), the Keepers lose their messengers along with their charms.
- **Pit bats** *(Kessmoor; proposal)*: leathery bats the size of a cat that roost in the mine shafts of the Iron Fells. They shriek when the Deep rises through the rock, and since spoken magic is banned in Kessmoor, a miner's caged bat is the only warning of an upwelling the law allows. Kessmoor calls them "Bleed-bats" and pretends it isn't the same thing as a tide-glass. They're animals, not beasts: they've never bonded, and Kessmoor is proud of that too.
- **Glass moths** *(the Scald; established in the Atlas)*: pale moths that live only on the Scald's fused glass, the only living thing there. Their wings ring faintly in a wind. Nobody knows what they eat; Idony thinks it's whatever the Deep has left.
- **The speaking raven** *(Isvaren; established)*: the Long Library's keeper, an ancient beast that learned human speech. Killed in Book 2 by Corvin's hunters. It hasn't flown in living memory.

### Animals of the air (no bond)

- **Lady's petrels** *(Saltreach; proposal)*: small black storm-petrels that run on the water ahead of bad weather. Sailors call them the Drowned Lady's birds and won't kill one. "By the Lady's petrels" is a captain's oath on the Corsa quays. (This plants the Lady under the Water, Book 3.)
- **Sun-storks** *(Sarovel; proposal)*: white storks that nest on the palace roofs above the Sun Stair. The year the storks don't come back, the Grace fails. They don't come back in Book 2's last spring. (This plants the Lamp's cult, Book 3.)
- **Wend geese** *(the Marches)*: grey geese that fly north up the river in early spring and south at harvest. River people tell the season by them, and Liss counts the skeins.
- **Rooks of Crow Scar, ravens of the Gallows Oak** *(Saltreach bank; Hearthmere)*: ordinary birds with long memories in their stories. The rooks of Crow Scar went silent the day the dragons came (32).

### The sky in the Titan War *(proposal)*

- **The Last Titan** is the only Titan with wings *(Book 1 ch 79)*. When it flies the sky empties beneath it for leagues, and that's how people far off know where it's going before they see it. The five it wakes walk, swim or burrow.
- **The air war in Book 3** is four kingdoms' beasts together: the Clan's dragons (Calder's three Titan-class after the gift), the Wyldmarch's roc-riders, a few dozen Isvaren kite-riders scouting for the Accord, and Saltreach wyvern companies hired by contract. Kessmoor has no wings and builds bolt-throwers to fight from below. Messages run on falcons, gull-hawks and owls, and a lost bird can lose a battle.
- **On the Lit Shore** *(Book 3)*: flocks that have never seen a person settle on Kit's rigging and on Orrun's back. The **Wayfarer**, the elder who gives to the dragons, is wind and sky: dragon-kind, three-headed.
- **After the quiet** *(Book 3's end; canon: bonds go quiet, beasts don't die)*: every bonded falcon, kite and wyvern flies off with its own mind. In the remnant's first spring, the Wend geese still come north.

## Kingdom by kingdom

### Hearthmere
- **Bond-hounds, falcons, great cats, martens:** the knights' beasts.
- **Greyharts:** the crown's white war-mounts, bred for the orders (Ferriby's breeding pair was taken in the raid, 33); wild ones in the Brackenwood are royal game, and poaching one is a hanging offence (the Gallows Oak remembers).
- **Captain Thorne's boar:** black, the size of a hay cart.
- **River otters of the Wend:** large, clever and thieving; the eel-fishers' oldest enemies.
- **Boar of the Brackenwood:** big enough that patrols go round (5). Fellwood boars, across the river, are as big as dray-beasts, and Fellwood bears are taller than a house's eaves.
- **Aurochs** in the Greenwold; **duskcats** at the far end of the Brackenwood.

### Saltreach
- **Wyverns:** sea cliffs from Gullhaven to Blackwater.
- **Reefwyrms:** long, eel-like sea beasts that follow ships for scraps and sometimes sink them. The Drowned Bell is thick with them.
- **Gull-hawks:** half gull, half hawk, all appetite. Pirates' messengers.
- **Lady's petrels**, the sailors' birds; wyvern rookeries on the sea cliffs (see The sky).
- **Stonehorns** in the inland scrub; tamed ones haul siege carts for sellswords.
- **Beast markets:** Wendmouth's Gallows Quay sells anything with a pulse: legal beasts, stolen beasts, and beasts from kingdoms that would hang the seller.

### The Dragon Clan (the Scarfell)
- **Dragons:** bred, kept, and sometimes bonded.
- **Scarfell sheep:** black-faced, stupid, and the reason the dragons are fed.
- **Crag-eagles**, the dragons' rivals for the high glens (see The sky).

### Sarovel
- **Unicorns:** the bond-monarchy's crowned beast.
  - Not white horses with a horn. Unicorns are lean, deer-boned, grey to silver-black, with a single spiral horn of living bone, cloven hooves, and a temper.
  - They are great beasts: sentient, long-lived, and **they remember the old realm**, as the lion does *(settled)*. Like the ancient beasts, they see bonding as a taint, and **they almost never bond with anyone** *(settled)*.
  - In Sarovel, a unicorn bond is the highest rank there is, and the Bond-King's line claims to have held one for nine generations.
  - Wild unicorns live in the Elderwood and the Sunward Hills, and they kill hunters.
  - **The Sarovel secret** *(settled: the royal "bonds" are bindings; revealed in Book 2)*: since unicorns almost never bond, the royal line's "unicorn bond" is a lie. Its "bonds" are bindings, a quiet atrocity at the heart of the bond-kingdom, which is why Sarovel later looks for a way to bind a Titan. A unicorn that remembers the old realm, held captive by a crown, is a witness waiting to be heard.
  - **Book 1:** a unicorn colt smuggled out of Sarovel and caged on Gallows Quay: silver-black, deer-boned, stub of spiral horn, cloven hooves, bites. Calder freed it, and it looked back at him "as if remembering him" (36).
- **Bond-steeds:** horses bred to bond, the knights' mounts. Bonding is required for knighthood here.
- **Sun-hawks:** golden hawks kept by the nobility, mostly for show. **Sun-storks** nest on the palace roofs (see The sky).
- **Olifants:** the royal herd beasts, painted for processions and walls in war. **Duskcats** in the Elderwood.

### Kessmoor
- **Ironbacks:** armoured, boar-like beasts of the Iron Fells, once also in the south (Aurveth's memory, Ch 74). Plated, the size of a barn, grey-black, plates like doors, a snout horn as long as a man; fire doesn't burn them. Their plates are harvested for the foundries, which is the root of Kessmoor's quarrel with the Wyldmarch. **Book 1:** one, bound, comes out of the Ram's Head lines onto the island (74) and falls into the cavern (76).
- **Sabrecats** in the Iron Fells, hunted for fangs and pelts.
- **Glass moths:** pale moths that live only on the Scald, the only living thing there. Their wings ring faintly in a wind.
- **Pit bats** ("Bleed-bats") in the mine shafts, the only warning of an upwelling Kessmoor's law allows (see The sky).
- **Pit ponies and canal-mules:** animals, not beasts, and Kessmoor is proud of that.
- **Bonded people** are registered or exiled, so Kessmoor's beasts are mostly wild or harvested.

### Isvaren
- **The speaking raven** *(established: an ancient beast that learned human speech; keeper of the Long Library; killed in Book 2)*: owl- or raven-like, ancient and dry-witted.
- **Snow lynx:** the college Wardens' beasts, white and silent.
- **Tuskers:** hired, by agreement with the herd, to haul over the Vael Pass in winter. **Sabrecats** follow them; Wardens ride with bonded ones.
- **Lantern owls:** owls with faintly glowing eyes that nest in the observatory towers. Students think they bring luck in exams. Their eyes are a poor man's tide-glass (see The sky).
- **Stormkites** and the Pass Wardens' kite-riders above the Vael Pass (see The sky).

### The Wyldmarch
- **The phoenix:** the rarest creature in Winshire. **There are a few, not one** *(settled)*, but they are so rarely seen that people believe there is only one. The series reveals the others slowly. *Working proposal:*
  - A great beast of fire, gold and red, the size of an eagle on a cold day and a burning hayrick on a hot one. When it dies it burns, and from the ash it comes back.
  - **That is why no one bonds a phoenix.** A bond shares one life between two. When the phoenix burns, its partner dies with it, and only the phoenix returns. The Wyldmarch clans tell of the three people who tried.
  - Phoenixes nest in the Worldsend, where the clans see fire on the peaks once in a lifetime, and always assume it is the same bird.
  - It doesn't appear in Book 1 except in story and as a single feather that never cools, red-gold, under a glass case that is warm to the touch, in Rusk's window on Gallows Quay (36). It isn't for sale, and Calder leaves it.
  - *For the later books:* the phoenix is fire that dies and returns, which makes it a natural bridge to the Age of Elementals. Keep that link quiet in Books 1–3.
- **Great elk:** taller than a man at the shoulder; the horse clans' beasts.
- **Worldsend rocs** and their clan roc-riders; **hollow owls**, the Keepers' night messengers (see The sky).
- **Stonehorn herds** on the Hallow Plains; **duskcats** in the Thornwood, bonded to Keepers.
- **Wolves and bears:** the Keepers' companions; bonds with them are sacred.
- **Ancient lions** *(established for the one)*: the lion's kind came from the Wyldmarch long ago. Orrun may be the last.

## Settled
- **Phoenixes:** a few exist, revealed slowly across the series because they are so rarely seen.
- **Unicorns** remember the old realm and almost never bond with anyone.
- **Pell's fingers** were taken by a Saltreach company's tamed wyvern, the rare bonded one, loose on the coast road below Stillhaven (Ch 9).
- **Greyharts** are the crown's white war-mounts (Ch 27, 30, 33).
