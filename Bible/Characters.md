# Winshire — Character Profiles

*Profiles for the two protagonists and their beasts. Ages are established (the student is between 26 and 30; the farmer is between 28 and 36). Everything else is a **proposal**: a recommended version, with alternatives, to accept or change.*

---

## The student

**Recommended name:** **Maren Ashdown** · *Alternatives:* Ilsa Vane, Kesia Thorne, or for a man: Aldric Vane, Rowan Thorne

**Age:** 27

**Gender:** **a woman** *(proposal)*. With the farmer as a man, this gives the series two very different leads. Her path is a knight school and a lion, not a dragon academy, so it stays clear of the *Fourth Wing* comparison. A man works too; see the alternatives above.

**Why she's at a knight school at 27:** the schools take a few older, unbonded candidates through a "late oath." Before that, Maren spent years in Hearthmere's river watch, patrolling the border river in the Marches. She is the oldest in her year and unbonded, so she's underestimated twice over. She knows the river, the Old Ruin's island, and the far bank better than any instructor.

**Family:**
- **Her father, Edric Ashdown,** was a bonded knight. When poachers killed his bonded hound-like beast, he died within days: one life between two. Maren was fourteen. She grew up seeing exactly what a bond costs, which is part of why she has never sought one.
- **Her mother, Liss,** runs a ferry on the border river and still crosses to the Saltreach side for trade. She tells the river's stories, including the island's.
- **Her younger brother, Tam** (19), is apprenticed to a smith in a Hearthmere river town and worships the knights his sister is trying to become.

**Flaw: control.** Maren trusts only what she can control: her training, her body, her temper. Restraint is her strength, but it hardens into coldness and a refusal to depend on anyone. A bond, which shares one life, is the thing she fears most. Her arc runs from control to trust, ending when she offers her own life into the binding to save the lion in chapter 37.

**What she wants:** to become a knight on her own merit, without being chosen by a beast. Underneath that, she needs to let something matter more to her than her own control.

**How she speaks:**
- Measured and precise, and few words. She thinks in distances, angles, and exits.
- Dry humor, mostly at her own expense.
- Formal with superiors, blunt with peers. Under pressure her sentences get shorter, never louder.
- In her head, she counts: steps, breaths, odds.

**Sample voice:** "Twelve paces to the bank. The beast hasn't moved. Neither will I."

## The farmer

*(Called "the farmer boy" in earlier notes. At his age, "the farmer" fits better; see the note at the end.)*

**Recommended name:** **Calder Fenn** · *Alternatives:* Tobin Reed, Jory Mosse, Hal Garrow

**Age:** 32

**Family:**
- **His mother, Ada Fenn,** who sings the old songs ("before the six, there was one") and knows more of them than anyone in the village.
- **His daughter, Wren** (9). Her mother, Calder's wife **Senna**, died of fever five years ago. Calder raises Wren with his mother's help.
- **His younger sister, Bel,** married into the next village, closer to the Dragon Clan's hold.
- The family farm sits on the Saltreach side of the border river, in the shadow of the Clan's lands. They sell grain to the Clan's hold.

**Flaw: passivity.** Calder has spent his life keeping his head down: paying the Clan's dues, avoiding the knights across the river, letting stronger people decide. It kept his family alive. Then the bonds come to him without his choosing, and he can't keep his head down anymore. His arc runs from letting things happen to choosing: warning a stranger at the other lock in chapter 36, fighting at the Old Ruin, and in Book 2 bringing the truth to the Clan.

**What he wants:** to know **why the dragons came to him** (established). Underneath that, he needs to accept that he is no longer ordinary, and that his choices now matter to many more people than his own.

**How he speaks:**
- Plain and earthy, with short sentences. He thinks in weather, soil, harvests, and animals.
- Wry, tired humor. He swears mildly.
- He talks to animals the way other people talk to friends, which turns out to be useful.
- With the dragons, he argues out loud, which makes people think he's mad.

**Sample voice:** "Three of them in my head and not one can tell me why. Grand. Wonderful. Pass the bread."

## The ancient lion

**What people call it:** **the Grey Mane**, or "the monster of the Old Ruin."

**Its true name** *(learned only through the bond, in chapter 37)*: **Orrun** · *Alternatives:* Ashkaran, Veyl, Thamor

**Sense of it:** old, heavy, and patient until it isn't. Through the bond, its mind comes across as slow and deep, like stone settling, with grief underneath, for kin it can feel beneath the earth.
