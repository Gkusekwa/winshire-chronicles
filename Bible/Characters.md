# Winshire — Character Profiles

*Profiles for the two protagonists, their beasts and the supporting cast. **Established:** the ages (the student 26–30, the farmer 28–36), the student is a woman, the farmer has a young daughter, and **all names in these profiles** (including family members), as drafted in Book 1. Personalities, backstory details, flaws, and voices began as proposals and are now drafted in Book 1; the chapter numbers say where. Names that were considered and not used are in **Names considered** at the end.*

## The student

**Name** *(established)*: **Maren Ashdown**

**Looks** *(from the drafts)*: tall and dark, darker than her fair brother Tam.

**Age:** 27 *(established range 26–30)*

**Gender:** **a woman** *(established)*. With the farmer as a man, this gives the series two very different leads. Her path is a knight school and a lion, not a dragon academy, so it stays clear of the *Fourth Wing* comparison.

**Why she's at a knight school at 27:** the schools take a few older, unbonded candidates through a "late oath." Before that, Maren spent years in Hearthmere's river watch, patrolling the border river in the Marches. She is the oldest in her year and unbonded, so she's underestimated twice over. She knows the river, the Old Ruin's island, and the far bank better than any instructor.

**Her years** *(fixed in review; settled after the full audit)*: she joined the river watch at 17 and served **eight winters** on it, then came to Greyhold, where she has been **two years**; she is a second-year, aged 27.

**Family:**
- **Her father, Edric Ashdown,** was a bonded knight. When poachers killed his bonded hound-like beast, he died within days: one life between two. Maren was fourteen; he died thirteen years ago. His night on the island he told only Liss. She grew up seeing exactly what a bond costs, which is part of why she has never sought one.
- **Her mother, Liss,** runs a ferry on the border river and still crosses to the Saltreach side for trade. She tells the river's stories, including the island's. She carries Calder's family across as refugees (64).
- **Her younger brother, Tam** (19), is apprenticed to a smith in a Hearthmere river town and worships the knights his sister is trying to become.

**Flaw: control.** Maren trusts only what she can control: her training, her body, her temper. Restraint is her strength, but it hardens into coldness and a refusal to depend on anyone. A bond, which shares one life, is the thing she fears most. Her arc runs from control to trust, ending when she offers her own life into the binding to save the lion in chapter 73.

**What she wants:** to become a knight on her own merit, without being chosen by a beast. Underneath that, she needs to let something matter more to her than her own control.

**How she speaks:**
- Measured and precise, and few words. She thinks in distances, angles, and exits.
- Dry humor, mostly at her own expense.
- Formal with superiors, blunt with peers. Under pressure her sentences get shorter, never louder.
- In her head, she counts: steps, breaths, odds.

**Sample voice:** "Twelve paces to the bank. The beast hasn't moved. Neither will I."

**In Book 1's end** *(from the drafts)*: Maren and Calder meet in the cavern after midnight (78).

## The farmer

*Established: the protagonists are adults. The student is a woman (26–30); the farmer is 28–36 and has a young daughter. The notes call him "the farmer."*

**Name** *(established)*: **Calder Fenn**

**Age:** 32 *(established range 28–36)*

**Family:**
- **His mother, Ada Fenn,** who sings the old songs ("before the six, there was one") and knows more of them than anyone in the village. She can read and write (Varrhold's dues-school), and writes Calder's account (62).
- **A young daughter** *(established)*: **Wren** (9). Her mother, Calder's wife **Senna**, died of fever five years ago. Calder raises Wren with his mother's help. Wren makes everyone say Lisska's words twice, wants to meet the lion-woman ("Tell her I said hello", 56), and meets Maren at Greyhold's gate (69): "You're her."
- **His younger sister, Bel,** married into the next village, Upper Rush, closer to the Dragon Clan's hold (see the supporting cast).
- The family farm sits on the Saltreach side of the border river, in the shadow of the Clan's lands. They sell grain to the Clan's hold.

**Flaw: passivity.** Calder has spent his life keeping his head down: paying the Clan's dues, avoiding the knights across the river, letting stronger people decide. It kept his family alive. Then the bonds come to him without his choosing, and he can't keep his head down anymore. His arc runs from letting things happen to choosing: warning a stranger at the other lock in chapter 70, fighting at the Old Ruin, and in Book 2 bringing the truth to the Clan.

**What he wants:** to know **why the dragons came to him** (established). Underneath that, he needs to accept that he is no longer ordinary, and that his choices now matter to many more people than his own.

**How he speaks:**
- Plain and earthy, with short sentences. He thinks in weather, soil, harvests, and animals.
- Wry, tired humor. He swears mildly.
- He talks to animals the way other people talk to friends, which turns out to be useful.
- With the dragons, he argues out loud, which makes people think he's mad.

**Sample voice:** "Three of them in my head and not one can tell me why. Grand. Wonderful. Pass the bread."

## The ancient lion

**What people call it:** **the Grey Mane**, or "the monster of the Old Ruin."

**Its true name** *(established; learned only through the bond, in chapter 73)*: **Orrun**

**Size** *(settled by the author)*: about 10 ft at the shoulder in Book 1, towering over people. It grows toward dragon size over the series.

**Before the bond** *(settled by the author)*: the lion and Maren can't speak. It tries to kill her more than once while she earns its respect.

**Sense of it:** old, heavy, and patient until it isn't. Through the bond, its mind comes across as slow and deep, like stone settling, with grief underneath, for kin it can feel beneath the earth. (Its look, power and multiplication: `Bestiary.md`.)

## The three dragons

The Dragon Clan names its dragons. Calder uses those names because they're the only ones the dragons answer to. *(Names established: Aurveth, Kharrak, Lisska. Personalities as in `Story.md`.)*

| Placeholder | Name | Sense of it |
|---|---|---|
| **The General** | **Aurveth** | She. The oldest and largest. Slow, precise speech; has outlived three partners (Ennis, Ragnald, Sigrun); guides Calder rather than commanding him. |
| **The Vanguard** | **Kharrak** | He. Broad, scarred, battle-trained. Speaks in short, hard bursts; thinks restraint is cowardice; fiercely protective once he trusts. |
| **The Far-Seer** | **Lisska** | She. Lean, quick, restless. Jokes to avoid grief, notices everything, and wanders into trouble. |

**Sizes and eyes** *(fixed in review)*: Lisska is about 12 ft at the shoulder and 40 ft long; Kharrak about 16 ft; Aurveth about 20 ft. Eyes: Aurveth gold, the size of a cartwheel hub; Kharrak gold; Lisska pale green. Calder's bond marks: bronze, rust and green (`World.md`).

**Aurveth's partners** *(from Chapters 8 and 70; fixed in review)*: she had been bonded three times before Calder, and is "more than three hundred years old". Her first, **Ennis**, a warden of the bridge, she lost from memory; his face and name come back to her in Ch 70 (proposal: he was cut out of her by the first knights' binders before he died). Her second was **Ragnald Holm**, sixty-one years ago; she survived his death, lying a year on Coldtarn's shore. Her third was **Sigrun Holm**, chosen at the Roosts about two years after Ragnald's death, at about 17; Sigrun rode her 59 years and was 76 at the mill. Sigrun thought she had been waiting a long time for something.

**Mind-speech on the page:** each dragon should sound different even in italics:
- *Aurveth:* long, measured sentences.
- *Kharrak:* fragments.
- *Lisska:* questions and teasing.
