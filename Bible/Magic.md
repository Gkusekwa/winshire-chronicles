# Magic

How magic works in Winshire, where it comes from, how people use it and fight with it, and what it costs. Written after Book 1 was drafted, to carry the series from Book 2 on. Everything below is *(proposal)* unless marked *(canon)*: where it rests on something already established in `World.md`, `Story.md`, `Series_Outline.md` or the Book 1 drafts, that's noted. Proposals taken from the Book 2 and Book 3 plans (`Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md`, `Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md`, both drafted, review settled) say so; where those plans' review questions were approved, the proposal is now canon.

The governing idea in one line: **all magic in Winshire is borrowed from the Titans, and everyone has forgotten who they're borrowing from.**

---

## 1. The source: the Deep *(canon: accepted by the author)*

**What it is.** Under every kingdom, very far down, a Titan sleeps *(canon: each kingdom was founded, without knowing it, over a sleeping Titan)*. A sleeping Titan breathes. What comes up from it through the rock, slowly, over centuries, is the thing every kind of magic in Winshire draws on. The scholars of Isvaren call it **the Deep**. Nobody else uses that word.

| Who | What they call it | What they think it is |
|---|---|---|
| Isvaren colleges | the Deep; its rise and fall are "the tides" | A natural current in the world, like groundwater. Measurable. |
| Hearthmere | "the crown's gift"; to the licensed, simply "the draw" | A blessing of the gods on the crown, held in trust and licensed. *(canon: the lamps were said to be the gods' gift to the crown, Ch 57)* |
| The Wyldmarch | the earth's breath | The breath of the old ones, sleeping. They're closest to the truth. |
| Saltreach | "the stuff", "the draw", "the weather" | A commodity. Some years it's cheap, some dear. |
| Sarovel | the Grace | It flows through the chosen: the bonded and the royal line. |
| Kessmoor | the Bleed | A poison that leaks out of the ground and drives people mad. Banned. |

**How it behaves.**
- **It rises slowly and pools.** It comes up strongest over the sleeper and thins with distance. It collects in old places: deep wells, caves, and above all **old-realm stone**, the dressed grey stone of the Oneroad, its milestones and bridge-forts, Greyhold's inner wall, the catacomb road. The old realm built in that stone *because* it holds the Deep. *(Book 1: "the stone knows him" — the grey stone moving for Orrun.)*
- **It has tides.** It swells and ebbs over years, and over hours with the moon. Isvaren keeps tide-charts going back four hundred years.
- **It hums.** Where it runs strong, the ground hums like a struck bowl and beasts feel it first *(canon: the hum on the island and in the archive; the Long Barn beasts turning to the north wall)*.
- **It can be used up locally.** Draw too much from one place and the place goes **dead**: wells sour, crops fail, lamps won't take. Dead ground recovers over a generation or more. Every kingdom has old dead ground somewhere and a story to explain it.

**Why Hearthmere was rich.** Hearthmere didn't live on the slow breath of a sleeper like everyone else. It woke its Titan and **chained it**, and a chained, waking Titan doesn't breathe the Deep: it **bleeds** it *(canon: "We've been bleeding something for three hundred years," Hale, Ch 49; Hearthmere's magic is the chained Titan's harnessed power)*. That's why Hearthmere had lamps without oil in every street and the crown licensed every word. The supply was huge and secret, and the licence kept anyone from asking why. When the Titan flew, the bleed stopped, and Hearthmere's magic failed at once *(canon, Ch 79)*. Hearthmere now has *less* than its neighbours. A Titan's own former ground is close to dead for a lifetime.

**What a waking does (the Book 2 engine)** *(proposal; stages fixed Oct 2026)*:
0. **The stir.** While the Last Titan sits on a temple and draws on it, before any relic is taken, the local Deep tugs and quickens: tide-glasses climb, charms take a little too hard, beasts turn toward the temple, ground near it goes thin. These are the signs the researcher learns to read, and how he predicts a relic's taking.
1. **The surge.** When a sleep relic is taken and its Titan wakes, the local Deep floods for days or weeks. Magic in that kingdom goes wild: charms work too well and won't stop, lamps burn white, licensed workers burn out, beasts go mad, the ground heaves.
2. **The drain.** When the waking Titan rises and leaves, it takes its breath with it. The kingdom's magic fails, as Hearthmere's did.
So every waking in Book 2 is two disasters: a flood of uncontrolled power, then a famine of it.

## 2. The disciplines (classes of magic)

People work the Deep through **disciplines**, the classes of magic. Isvaren counts **six**: two foundations (**charms** and **runes**, below), two higher disciplines (**drawing** and **shaping**), one that only reads the Deep (**tide-reading**), and one that is forbidden (**binding**); the last four are in section 2a. Each kingdom favours some and forbids others.

Two other kinds of power are described here too, because everyone confuses them with the disciplines, but neither is one: **bond gifts**, which come from a beast and can't be taught, and **old magic**, which only the elder Titans ever worked.

### Words (charms)
Spoken workings. A short fixed phrase in the old tongue (or a garbled descendant of it), said with intent, shapes a small draw of the Deep for one purpose: light, heat, mending, keeping. *(canon: the licensed bone-setting words; the lamp-words)*
- **Cheap and weak.** A charm does one small thing. It needs the Deep to be present: in dead ground, words are only words.
- **Learnable by anyone** with the words and practice. Isvaren teaches them as grammar; Hearthmere licensed each one; the Wyldmarch passes them mother to child.
- **They don't carry.** A charm works on what the speaker can touch or see close by. There's no throwing fire across a field.
- **Many voices make a bigger charm.** The same words said by many people at once, at the same hour, facing the same way, add up. This is the principle behind the noon oath *(canon: "every voice at the noon bell is a hand on the chain")* and behind every chapel litany, sea-chant and harvest song that ever actually did anything.

### Runes (inscription)
Workings written into a material: carved in stone, struck into iron, inked into skin. The runes are **the hooked letters** of the Oneroad milestones and the arch verse *(canon: "square and spare, with odd little hooks at the ends of the strokes")*. It is the old ones' own writing, taught to people in the one realm. Each rune is a word fixed in place.
- **Runes hold.** A spoken charm lasts as long as the breath. A rune lasts as long as the thing it's cut into, drawing on the Deep slowly and steadily, like a wick. This is how imbued objects work (section 5).
- **Runes are slow to make and need masters.** Few people can still write the script correctly. A wrong stroke does nothing, or the wrong thing. Isvaren's colleges hold most of the knowledge; Hearthmere's archive held some *(canon: Hale taught Maren to read a little)*.
- **Hidden runes.** Serious workings put their runes where they can't be seen or defaced: inside the curve of a chain-link, on the underside of a stone, under a seal. *(Echo of Odo's crossed hammers inside the collar: a smith's mark where you'd never look.)*

### Bond gifts
*(canon: bond gifts come from a magical beast and reflect its nature)* A bonded person doesn't draw from the Deep. They draw from **their beast**, and the beast draws from whatever it is. Dragon-bonded people stand heat and smoke; Orrun's bond gives Maren earth-sense and, in time, a little of his stone.
- **They need no words or runes.** They work through the bond, by feeling and will, the way you move your own hand.
- **They grow with the bond and the beast.** As Orrun grows toward dragon size, the gift grows *(canon: the lion's power grows with its size)*.
- **They cost the beast first.** Overuse tires the beast, and through the shared life, the person.
- **Why bond gifts are "wild" to Isvaren.** They can't be written down or taught, so the colleges rank them below learned magic *(canon proposal in World.md)*. The researcher will come to suspect the opposite: bond gifts are closer to the source than anything the colleges teach.

### Old magic
**Old magic** *(canon: the elder Titans put the sleepers to sleep with old magic, bound into the temples and relics)* is not a discipline. It is what the elder Titans did themselves, with nothing between them and the Deep. People can't do it. They can only find its leftovers (temples, relics, the stone of the old realm) and copy its forms. The hooked script is one such form: a beast's language taught to people, which people kept after they forgot whom they'd learned it from. Copying a form isn't working old magic. A copied stroke is a rune like any other: it's fed by the Deep, and it fails on dead ground. Only what the elders made themselves (the temples, the relics, the lock under Greyhold) is old magic.

## 2a. The higher disciplines

The other four of the six disciplines (what everyone outside Isvaren calls "the arts"): two that work the Deep with more force and far more toll than charms or runes (drawing and shaping), one that only reads it (tide-reading), and one that is forbidden almost everywhere (binding).

### Drawing (energy manipulation)
Pulling the Deep up through the worker's own body and letting it out as raw force: **heat, light, motion, and the pressure the colleges call "weight"**. No words, no runes: breath, stance and will. A drawer stands on the ground, pulls, and releases through the hands.
- **What it can do:** heat iron red in the hand; throw a man off his feet at a few paces; blow a door off its hinges; make a flash that blinds a room; hold a gate shut against a ram for as long as the drawer can stand. At the highest level, a stone's throw of range and a burst of force like a falling tree.
- **What it can't:** fire or lightning across a field, flight, anything lasting. Drawing is spent the moment it's released.
- **The toll is the worker's.** The Deep goes *through* the body, and the body pays: heat leaving the limbs, then the blood, then burn-out. A drawer who works on dead ground pulls from themselves instead and can kill themselves in one release.
- **Grounding:** drawers must stand on earth or stone. On a ship, a bridge, or a horse at the gallop they can't draw. Running water under them breaks the pull outright.
- **Who:** rare. Perhaps one in a hundred of those who can feel the Deep at all. Isvaren trains them as **wardens** (its law-enforcers); Sarovel's court mages are drawers; Hearthmere licensed a handful for siege-work and had none left that anyone knew of by Book 1. (Book 1's final battle has hired drawers on the Restoration's side: §10a.)

### Shaping (transfiguration)
Changing what a material **is**, for a while: stone soft as clay, iron that flows cold, wood hard as iron, water that holds a shape, glass that bends. Done with runes laid on the thing and words said over it while the worker holds it. Shaping is how quickened edges are set, how old-realm stone was cut so close you can't see the joins, how the lock-discs were carved.
- **Lesser shaping** works on dead matter and doesn't last: the clay sets back to stone within the hour unless runes hold it.
- **Healing** is shaping on living flesh, and is the only living shaping most kingdoms allow: bone-setting words, wound-closing *(canon: Hearthmere's licensed bone-setting)*. It goes only as far as the body would go itself, faster.
- **Greater shaping** changes a living thing's form or nature. It is forbidden everywhere but Sarovel's court, and the stories about what it does to people (and beasts) are the worst stories in Winshire. A **binding** is, at bottom, a greater shaping done on a beast's self.
- **The toll** is split between the worker and the thing shaped. Shaped stone is weaker forever after; shaped flesh ages.
- **Bond-shaping:** Orrun's way with stone is shaping without runes or words, through his nature and through the bond *(canon: the stone opens for him, Ch 75–79)*. It's what the colleges say is impossible.

### Tide-reading (measure)
Not a working but a reading: feeling and measuring the Deep's rise and fall, where it runs and pools, where it's been drained. The instruments are **tide-glasses** (sealed glass with a drop of quicksilver under a rune that rises with the Deep) and **tide-charts**. Isvaren's College of Tides practises it. It's how Idony Vael sees the hole under Hearthmere, and in Book 2 how he learns to see a waking coming.

### Binding (forbidden)
See section 6. Banned in every kingdom but practised by the Restoration and, under another name, by Sarovel's crown.

### Summary
| Discipline | Works through | Toll | Who | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Charms (words) | Spoken old-tongue phrases | Small, from place and worker | Almost anyone | Licensed (Hearthmere), graded (Isvaren), folk (Wyldmarch), sold (Saltreach), banned (Kessmoor) |
| Runes (marks) | Script cut or struck into material | Slow, from place | Scribes, smiths, masons | Everywhere; called "engineering" in Kessmoor |
| Drawing (energy) | The worker's body, from the ground | Heavy, from worker | Rare | Wardens, court mages; feared |
| Shaping (transfiguration) | Runes + words on a held material | Split, worker and material | Masters | Lesser: allowed; healing: licensed; greater: forbidden |
| Tide-reading | Glasses and charts | None | Scholars | Isvaren |
| Binding | Smoke, words, will, collar | From the beast | Binders | Forbidden (practised in secret) |
| Bond gifts | The bond | From the beast, then the person | The bonded | Not a discipline; can't be taught |

## 3. The toll: what magic costs

Every working takes something from somewhere. Characters call it **the toll**.
1. **From the place:** draws drain the local Deep. Overuse makes dead ground.
2. **From the worker:** heat leaving the body, hunger, shaking hands, nosebleeds; in the worst cases a worker who draws too hard, too fast, **burns out**: grey hair overnight, the hands never steady again, the words never working for them again. Licensed workers in Hearthmere were forbidden more than so many workings a day for this reason; they thought it was for their health.
3. **From a beast:** a binding (section 6) takes the toll from the bound beast's self.

The toll keeps magic small in everyday life and dangerous in war.

## 4a. Who works magic: society

**The knack.** Anyone can learn a charm and have it work, a little, where the Deep runs. To do more (read runes so they answer, draw, shape) a person needs **the knack**: feeling the Deep the way some people feel weather coming. About one in twenty have it, unevenly. It runs in families but skips. Nobody can give it to anyone.

**Who works magic, kingdom by kingdom.**
- **Hearthmere:** the **licensed** (registered workers with a crown licence for each word they may say: lamp-tenders, infirmary surgeons, keeping-wives, and, formerly, a few siege drawers). Unlicensed workers are **hedge-workers** and fined or jailed. Knights may learn battle-words where licensed; most don't bother. After Book 1 the licensed are a class without a trade, and hedge-workers selling old words that don't work are everywhere.
- **Isvaren:** magic is **rank**. The colleges rule; a seat on a college council is a seat in government. A worker's ring (section 4b) is worn on the hand and decides where they may sit, whom they may marry, which doors open. The knack without a patron gets you a low ring and a lifetime of copying. Bond gifts are "wild" and the bonded are outsiders.
- **The Wyldmarch:** no colleges, no licences. Every hearth has its charms; **Keepers** know more, including runes cut in wood and bone. Workings on beasts need the beast's leave. Binding is the worst crime there is.
- **Saltreach:** magic is **trade**. Contract-workers sell charms by the job, guaranteed or not; trading houses keep a drawer on retainer the way they keep a lawyer; the Tide Council stamps warranted charms. Beast-matter and honey-smoke are bought and sold.
- **Sarovel:** magic is **grace**, the right of the chosen. Only the bonded and the royal line may work it openly. Unbonded workers serve the court as **hands**: they do the work and the chosen take the credit. Greater shaping is a court secret.
- **Kessmoor:** spoken magic is a crime with a prison sentence; drawing is a hanging matter. **Engine-wrights** cut runes in iron for a living and would be insulted to be called workers of magic. Beast-matter is an industry.
- **The Dragon Clan:** charms for the hearth and the herd; the dragon-bonded have their gifts; no schools. The Clan distrusts learned magic as a lowland thing.

**Common attitudes.** Everyone uses charms; almost nobody understands them. Runes are respected as old and serious. Drawers are feared. Binders are hated. The bonded are envied, and in Isvaren looked down on, and in Sarovel worshipped. Nobody anywhere knows where the Deep comes from, except, in Book 2, one tide-reader and two people who've been under the river.

## 4b. Levels of knowledge

Isvaren's **rings** are the only scale the whole of Winshire recognises; other kingdoms map their own titles onto them. A fellow wears a plain iron ring on the hand of their working, with notches for their grade. *(The ring motif is deliberate: the Restoration's ring of six, the control relic's six hollows, the rings of script on the lock-discs.)*

| Ring | Isvaren title | Can do | Elsewhere | Roughly how many |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | **Hand** | Common charms reliably; feels the Deep | Hearthmere: licensed worker. Wyldmarch: any grown hearth-keeper. Saltreach: contract-worker. | Thousands |
| Second | **Reader** | Reads the runes and can tell what a working is for; repairs simple runework | A good smith, mason, archivist *(Hale was a Reader)* | Hundreds |
| Third | **Scribe** | Writes runes that answer; makes simple imbued objects (wards, lamps, keys) | Master smiths; Kessmoor engine-wrights | Fewer |
| Fourth | **Fellow** | Lesser shaping; the beginnings of drawing; teaches | Wardens of Isvaren; Sarovel court mages; Saltreach house drawers | Dozens per kingdom |
| Fifth | **Master** | Greater runework (quickened edges, many-voice workings, wards for walls); healing beyond the ordinary; strong drawing | A handful in each kingdom | A handful |
| Sixth | **Keeper of the Script** | The whole hooked script; can read the oldest stone and make workings that last centuries. The chains under Hearthmere were Sixth-ring work. | No equivalent | Two or three living, all in Isvaren |
| Seventh | **The Unbound** | Old magic: working the Deep with nothing between. | The elder Titans. No person has ever worn a seventh notch. | None |

*(Settled, Oct 2026: the name belongs to the seventh ring. Book 1's working title is now* The Watch Below.*)*

A ring is earned by examination before a college council and can be broken (taken away) for crimes. Idony Vael wore a Second ring until his was broken (Book 2 ch 40), and reads like a Sixth.

## 5. Imbued objects

An imbued object is a material with runes in it that hold a working.

**How they're made.**
1. **The right material.** Old-realm stone, iron, bone, scale, heartwood. Iron holds runes well and takes the toll without cracking; that's why chains, collars and keys are iron. Beast-matter (scale, horn, a dragon's shed plate) holds them best of all, which is why Kessmoor and Saltreach want it.
2. **The runes,** struck or cut where they won't be worn away.
3. **The quenching:** the working has to be set while the material is open. Iron is quenched in water from a place where the Deep runs strong, while the words are spoken over it. A great working needs many voices at the quenching.
4. **Feeding:** a rune draws slowly on the Deep forever. In dead ground an imbued object weakens and finally fails.

**Common kinds.**
- **Wards:** marked doorposts, shields, boundary stones. They don't stop a blade; they turn a working aside.
- **Lamps and stones:** Hearthmere's lamps without oil were imbued glass fed from the bleed. They're glass now.
- **Keys:** the long black keys of the Watch are imbued. They don't fit a lock so much as answer one. *(Canon: two keys open the black door, the archivist's and the Watch-captain's; the Watch-captain's also turns the knight's lock, once for each of the Watch on the night they're sworn. Corvin's key for the Ruin's lock is older, its bow cut with the same hooked marks as the boss (Ch 74); where he got it is a Book 2 thread. See `World.md`.)*
- **Edges:** quickened blades (section 8).
- **Kessmoor's heartstones:** see section 7.

**Far-stone** *(settled Oct 2026, as drafted in Book 3 ch 16)*: the old road's stone in the far south-west is full, its breath never drunk. A flake of it is a pinch of live ground you can carry: a tide-glass climbs on it, and a working that needs live ground can be done on it, as far as the flake's breath goes.

### The chains and the seal under Hearthmere
*(Book 1 canon: six chains, links taller than a man, between anchor-stones at each end of the cavern; two locks, carved stone discs with iron bosses, under the school and under the Old Ruin; turned in the same breath at midnight, they opened the seal. The oath said at noon facing the inner wall was "a hand on the chain.")*

What they actually were:
- **The chains** were forged by Hearthmere's first knights about three hundred years ago with craft bought from an Isvaren college *(proposal from `Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md`)*. Every link carries hooked runes struck on the inside of the curve, where the Titan would never see them and nobody would think to look. The runes say one thing over and over: *hold.*
- **The chains fed on what they held.** The harder the Titan pulled, the more of its own bleed ran into the runes, and the stronger the chains held. It was a cage that grew stronger the more it was fought. This is why the Titan lay still for three hundred years, and why the chains began to fail only when the bleed and the oath both faltered at once.
- **The anchor-stones** were old-realm stone, the same as the inner wall, set in the cavern walls at each end, with iron eyes. When the Deep in them was drained by the bleed going upward to the lamps, they grew brittle. Hearthmere's lamps were weakening Hearthmere's chains for three hundred years, and nobody knew.
- **The oath** was the living part of the seal: a many-voiced charm said daily at noon, facing the wall over the way down. *Keep the watch below* fed the runes. *And do not ask* was there to keep the voices from doubting, because doubt weakens a many-voiced charm. When the Restoration's paper set the whole kingdom asking, the oath weakened, the chains slackened, and the lamps flickered *(canon: Ch 45–49)*. The oath's words were taken from the arch, where they had been the old ones' charge to the lion *(canon: Ch 70)*: the first knights turned a guardian's charge into a jailer's charm.
- **The two locks** were the seal's lid, designed so that no one person could ever open it. Each lock is a disc of old-realm stone carved in rings of the hooked script. Turned, the rings line up into a sentence; both sentences have to be read at once for the seal to release. It was a safeguard against a traitor. It made the Restoration's plan require two men in two places. The legend of the man in two places is what the seal itself demanded.
- **What's left of them.** The broken chain-ends are still on the Titan's legs *(canon)*. Their runes still say *hold*, and they still feed on what they hold. In Book 2 the researcher realises that whoever can speak to those runes has a grip on the Titan, which makes the chain-ends something Corvin wants.

### The sleep relics and the control relic
*(canon: old magic, made by the elder Titans; five sleep relics, one in each remaining temple, each holding one sleeper's sleep; a control relic in Hearthmere's deepest vault; Hearthmere broke the Last Titan's sleep relic to wake and chain it.)*
- **They aren't imbued objects.** No runes, no quenching. People can't make them, read them, or unmake them. They can only move or break them.
- **What a sleep relic is, as the researcher understands it:** a sleeper's sleep, kept outside its body, the way you'd keep a candle-flame in a lantern. Take the relic from its temple and the flame goes out; the sleeper wakes.
- **What they look like:** alike in form, hollow eggs of grey old-realm stone, warm, each carved with its own sleeper *(proposal, `Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md`)*.
- **What the control relic is** *(proposal, from `Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md`):* a stone ring with six hollows. The Restoration's ring of six was copied from it, long ago, by somebody who saw it and didn't understand.

## 6. Bindings

*(canon, Bestiary: a true bond forms only by the beast's choice. A binding is coercion by the forbidden discipline of binding (honey-smoke, words, will and collar), chains and pain, imitating a bond from outside. No mind-speech, no shared life. If the binder dies, the beast is simply free; if the beast dies, the binder lives. A binding can be the place a bond happens if someone gives instead of taking — Maren and Orrun, Ch 73.)*

How it works, as magic:
1. **Honey-smoke** opens the beast. It's resin from a Wyldmarch tree the Keepers call sleepwood, cooked with honey; its smoke loosens a beast's self the way sleep does *(canon: honey-smoke opens a beast, Ch 73)*. The Wyldmarch forbids its export; Saltreach smuggles it.
2. **The binding words:** a short charm in the old tongue, repeated (*"You're chosen now"* is the Restoration's common-tongue version: it's the translation that gives it away).
3. **The binder's will:** the binder lays a hand on the beast's head and pours his will into the opening while holding himself shut. The toll is taken from the beast: its own self goes out to make room.
4. **The collar** holds the working in place when the binder isn't touching the beast. Odo's collars carried the six on the outside for show and plain iron inside, with no runes. The Restoration's real binders strike their own runes inside the collar after Odo's work. *(This is why a bound beast whose binder is gone but alive goes slack and follows any hand: the collar still holds the shape of the binding with no will in it. When the binder dies, the will the collar was holding for is gone, and the beast is free. Reconciles Bestiary with Old Grey.)*
5. **Breaking a binding:** kill or drive off the binder, then strike the collar off and let the beast come back slowly. Some never do.

Sarovel's royal "bonds" with unicorns are bindings done with court ceremony *(canon)*. Book 2 is where that's found out.

## 7. Kessmoor: engines

*(canon: Kessmoor has banned magic and explores arcane-like technology.)*
- **The ban** *(Atlas canon: the town of Orrin, now the Scald; engineers drilling for ore broke into the outer chambers of the temple under it)*. Within living memory *(proposal: forty years ago)* Kessmoor's engineers at **Orrin**, near the Hearthmere border, sank shafts deeper than anyone had dug, for ore and to draw the Deep in bulk for the forges. They broke into the outer chambers of the temple under the town. The sleeper's breath, held there for an age, came up the shafts all at once. In one night the ground of Orrin fused to glass, and those who lived went mad within the week. Kessmoor calls what came up **the Bleed**, calls the place **the Scald**, keeps a cordon round it, and has banned spoken magic ever since. It has never understood what its engineers found.
- **The workaround.** Kessmoor still uses runes. It just calls them engineering. Its **runic engines** are imbued machines whose runes are fed not by the Deep but by **heartstones**: beast-matter (dragon plate, scale, horn, the crystallised heart of certain beasts) that holds a store of power and can be spent like coal. So Kessmoor doesn't draw the Deep; it burns beasts. *(Ties to canon proposal: its technology depends on materials harvested from beasts.)*
- **Why that matters in Book 2:** Kessmoor's cage for its Titan *(Book 2 plan proposal: the only capture that holds)* is a heartstone engine of runes, fed by the deaths of hundreds of beasts. It works because it never touches the Deep the Titan breathes. It's monstrous, and it works, and every kingdom learns the wrong lesson from it.

## 8. Fighting with magic

The rule for writing battles: **magic tips fights; steel and beasts win them.** A charm takes a breath to say and works at arm's length. Runes have to be prepared beforehand. The toll is paid in the middle of the fight. No one in Winshire throws lightning.

**What a fighter can actually do:**
| Kind | What it is | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Wards** | Marked shields, armour, door-posts. They turn workings aside: a binding-word slides off a warded collar; a curse-knot fails on a warded door. | Don't stop steel, fire or teeth. Fail in dead ground. |
| **Edges** | Quickened blades: runes struck into the steel by a master, set at quenching. They cut scale and plate that ordinary steel won't, hold an edge, sometimes burn. | Rare, expensive, a smith's life's work. Sarovel's knights carry them; a few Hearthmere families have one. Every use takes the toll from the wielder. |
| **Drawing in battle** | A drawer on the ground: a blow of force at a few paces, a flash, heat in an enemy's blade, a gate held shut. | Must stand on earth or stone; burns the drawer; one or two releases and they're spent. A drawer is a prize and a target. |
| **Shaping in battle** | Softening a wall, sealing a door, hardening a shield; mostly siege-work, slow. Bond-shaping (Orrun) is fast. | Takes time and runes; the toll weakens what's shaped. |
| **Battle-words** | Charms said in a fight: a word to blind, a word to stop bleeding, a word to make a horse stand, a word of silence that stops another charm in the speaker's mouth. | One breath, short range, small effect. Licensed in Hearthmere *(canon: Pell's licensed horse-standing word, Ch 9; the gate knights' staunching-word, Ch 33)*. |
| **Many-voice workings** | A company chanting the same words at once: a ward over a whole wall, a fog over a river, the noon oath. | Need discipline, time, and voices that believe. Break when the voices doubt. |
| **Bindings** | Bound beasts in battle: silent, fearless, feel no pain, one handler drives a pack *(canon)*. | Kill the handler and the pack is free; drive him off and it stands slack. |
| **Bond gifts** | Dragonfire, the dragon-bonded standing in smoke; Orrun's earthwork (the ground heaving, stone opening, stakes pulled out of the earth) *(canon)*. Growing in Book 2 toward Orrun's many lions of stone *(Book 2 plan proposal)*. | Cost the beast and, through the shared life, the person. |
| **Engines** | Kessmoor's: marked crossbows that throw farther, armour that hardens on impact, the cage. | Burn heartstones fast. Fail when the heartstones run out. |

**Counters every soldier knows:**
- **Running water breaks a spoken charm.** A word said across a river doesn't land. *(This explains old customs in Book 1 for free: the river as a border, "they never cross", the ferry as neutral ground. And it's why the oath's runes were under the river, not across it.)*
- **Dead ground** strips every charm and starves every mark.
- **Silence-words** and **warded iron** stop a binding.
- **Honey-smoke on the wind** turns a battle: it opens every beast it reaches, bonded or not, and a bonded beast in the smoke feels its partner's self pulling away *(new danger for Lisska and Orrun)*.
- **Kill the handler.**

**Magic and war in Book 2:** each waking's surge makes magic in that kingdom wild for a while, so battles there go wrong in strange ways: wards that blaze, charms that won't stop, bound beasts breaking free in the flood. Then the drain makes the same kingdom's magic fail, and armies that relied on it are suddenly fighting with steel alone.

## 10. The rules (for the writer)

1. All magic is borrowed from the Titans: the Deep is a sleeper's breath; bond gifts come from beasts; old magic was the elder Titans' own.
2. People work the Deep only through the six disciplines: charms, runes, drawing, shaping, tide-reading (which only reads it) and binding (forbidden). Bond gifts come through a bond, not the Deep, and are not a discipline. Only the elder Titans worked old magic, with nothing in between.
3. Every working takes a toll from the place, the worker or a beast.
4. Charms don't carry. Range is touch or a few paces. Only a master drawer can throw force farther, a stone's throw at most, and it costs them.
5. Running water breaks spoken charms. Dead ground stops learned magic: charms don't take, runes and imbued things go hungry and fail, wards gutter, and a drawer draws on themselves. It doesn't stop bond gifts, which come through the bond and not the ground, or old magic, which isn't the Deep's to stop.
6. Runes hold as long as their material and their feeding.
7. Many voices make a stronger charm, and doubt weakens it.
8. Relics are old magic: people can move or break them, never make or read them.
9. A binding is never a bond. It can become the place a bond happens only if someone gives and the beast chooses.
10. Magic tips fights; steel and beasts win them.

## 10a. Magic in Book 1 *(author's direction)*

- **Limited magic users.** In Book 1 magic is part of daily life but small: chores, trades and talk. Lamp-tenders, keeping-wives, licensed surgeons, a hedge-worker fined in Millbrook, Ada's hearth charms, a Clan herd-charm, Saltreach charm-sellers with "warranted" charms, the Halloran house's drawer glimpsed and feared on Gallows Quay (34). Most people know a charm or two; workers beyond that are few and noticed. Maren and Calder are not magic users.
- **Maren and Calder across the series** *(established by the author)*: no casting in Book 1 (only bond gifts: dragonfire is the dragons'; Orrun's earth-sense begins at the bond). In **Book 2** they **begin limited casting**: small, costly, uncertain workings, learned on the road and through their bonds. In **Book 3** they **can use magic, but not as trained workers**: strong, untaught, without the colleges' rings or forms, paying the toll in ways the trained wouldn't.
- **How their casting works, Books 2 and 3** *(settled by the author, Oct 2026, as drafted)*: in Book 2 their workings are ordinary words and runes, learned on the road, with a beast's power going through them. They follow every rule here: live ground only, touch range, broken by running water, paid for by the worker and the beast; Maren can't cut a rune herself, only hold the block while Idony cuts the strokes. In Book 3 the same words and runes, said untaught with a Titan-sized power behind them, are huge and crude and still need live ground. On dead ground only bond gifts work. Book 2's workings come back in Book 3 at its key moments: the stand-word at the line, the herd-charm on the six roads, the *hold* rune in the roof of the hall under Coldtarn.
- **The failing (from night 90).** Hearthmere's licensed words and lamps flicker and fail as the chains slacken; people relight oil lamps; hedge-workers sell words that don't take.
- **The surge (nights 142–143).** As the seal is about to open, the Deep round the river floods: dead lamps flare white for moments, charms take too hard, wards blaze, drawers hit harder than they should. The final battle has magic users in the thick of it on both sides: the Restoration's hired drawers (Halloran's drawer, lent to Delling, at Greyhold's gate, 69; the ring-broken Isvaren Fellow at the Ruin, who dies in the cavern fall, 74, 76) and binders with honey-smoke, Greyhold's last licensed workers and battle-words, Haverly's quickened sword, wards on the archive stair.
- **The drain (dawn, day 144).** When the Titan flies, Hearthmere goes dark for good.

## 11. The researcher (third POV from Book 2) *(canon: a magic researcher is a third POV from Book 2 on)*

**Who:** **Idony Vael**, about thirty-one, a **Second-ring Reader** of the College of Tides at Vael in Isvaren, which measures the Deep's rise and fall across the six kingdoms. Unbonded. A gifted reader of the hooked script, ranked low because he comes from a ferryman's family on the Isvaren lakes and has no patron. Careful, funny when frightened, more interested in being right than in being safe. His ring was a plain iron ring on his working hand; when it was broken it was cut off him on the Library steps (Book 2 ch 40).

**What he knows that nobody else does:** for eleven years his tide-charts have shown a hole under Hearthmere. He started the ledgers at twenty. Not a low tide: a draw, as if something were pumping the Deep out from under one kingdom. His college told him to stop writing about it. He kept the charts.

**How he enters Book 2:** on the night of day 143–144 every tide-glass in the College of Tides breaks at once. The next morning his charts show the hole under Hearthmere gone, and something moving north-east across the tides at the speed of a bird.

**What he's for in the story:**
- He's the one who can explain the magic: the Deep, the toll, the surge and the drain, the runes on the chain-ends, why Hearthmere's lamps went out. The reader learns the system through his curiosity, not through lectures.
- He reads the hooked script, which neither protagonist can do well. He can read the temples, the relics' resting places, and the runes on the broken chains.
- He learns to predict wakings from the surges and drains in his tide-charts. That's a second, independent way to see the pattern the protagonists find through the Oneroad.
- He finds out his own college sold Hearthmere the chain-craft *(Book 2 plan proposal)*, and that its seniors want to sell it again, to any kingdom that can pay, to cage the waking Titans.
- He's unbonded in a series about bonds, the way Maren started out unchosen. His arc runs from knowledge for its own sake to knowledge as responsibility. In Book 3 he could be the one who understands the old magic well enough to deal with the elder Titans.

**Voice:** close third like the others, but his chapters think in measurements, quotations and footnotes he doesn't write down. He notices numbers the way Calder notices beasts and Maren notices people.

**Where he joins the others** *(see `Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md`, drafted, review settled)*: alone in Vael at first, tracking the surges; he meets Maren at the Hollow Ring (Book 2 ch 18) and travels with her on the Titan's road from Isvaren to Sarovel; he crosses Calder's separate road at Vael and again at the end. Maren and Calder don't travel together *(canon, `Series_Outline.md`)*.

**Settled by the author:** his name is **Idony Vael**; he's a man; he **survives to the end of the series**.

**His state after Book 2** *(canon, author, Oct 2026: as drafted)*: Book 2 ch. 83, 87 has him **burned out** holding the Titan still for the cellars: grey in a breath, hands that shake for good, every working he knows now only words, but his knowledge whole, and tide-glasses still readable because reading them is measure, not craft. (Book 2 plan, review question 12, settled.) He doesn't grey or shake before ch 83.

**Settled (Oct 2026), as drafted:** he stays unbonded through Book 3 and refuses the Mother's mending (the Book 3 plan's question 19).
