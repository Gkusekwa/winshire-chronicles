# Winshire — Story and Characters

*Established facts are stated directly. Story beats marked “working outline” are proposals that can change.*

## The two protagonists

The story has two protagonists and two strands within one overall story, told in alternating chapters. Both strands share a timeline and eventually converge.

### The student (Maren) — knight strand

The student begins unbonded at a school that trains future knights. *Established:* she is **a woman, aged 26–30.** Her name, family, and motivation are proposed in `Characters.md`. They may be underestimated where bonds bring status, but the school accepts unbonded students.

The central question is: **Can someone become a great protector without being chosen by a beast?** The eventual bond should deepen that question, not erase the student’s skill and choices before it.

Knights are protectors. Training includes swords, fists, staves, and bows, as well as defending people, teamwork, reading danger, understanding beasts, and using force responsibly. Knight schools and magic schools are separate. A knight school may teach basic magic where local law allows; magic schools teach the discipline in depth.

**Working student-path outline:**

1. Establish school life, friendships, rivalries, training, and the student’s unbonded status.
2. Send the student on protection duties beyond the school.
3. Introduce a disturbance involving beasts, an unsafe route, or a disturbed ruin.
4. Bring the student into a frightening first encounter with the ancient lion-like beast.
5. Develop trust and respect through repeated encounters and difficult choices.
6. Form the bond only after a meaningful sacrifice; continue to show the beast’s violent nature and the cost of bonding.
7. Expand the student’s responsibility to protecting people and beasts as the wider threat emerges.

The school’s name and location, and the student’s personal details and personal conflict, remain open. The series ending is set in `Series_Outline.md`. The Restoration is the established antagonist shared by both paths; how the student first encounters it and becomes involved remains to be developed.

### The ancient lion-like beast

**Established:** The beast is ancient, huge, violent by nature, and as powerful as a dragon. It can multiply itself. It bonds only through respect and sacrifice. Ancient beasts regard bonding as a taint.

The beast remains independent and dangerous after bonding; it is not a tame mount or convenient weapon. The bond may restrain or redirect its violence, but does not erase its nature. The sacrifice could come from the student, the beast, or both. *Established:* the lion is kin to the Titan chained in Hearthmere's catacombs (see `World.md`). *(Established: at the end of Book 1, when the student kills the first twin, the lion learns that its kin is now a world destroyer. Its kin was not always one; ages in chains made it a destroyer. In Book 3 the lion becomes a Titan without becoming a destroyer, the opposite of its kin.)* That gives it a reason to stay near Hearthmere, and gives the student, a Hearthmere knight-in-training, something to answer for before the lion can respect them. Its name, origin, multiplication rules, gift to the student, and reason for being feared remain undecided.

### The farmer (Calder) — Dragon Clan strand

The farmer is a farmer outside the Dragon Clan. *Established:* he is **aged 28–36 and has a young daughter.** See `Characters.md` for his proposed name, family, and voice. The Clan lives on the lawless northern edge of Saltreach, where the League's writ barely reaches. It breeds dragons, keeps them under control, and bonds only with dragons.

The civil war has been brewing since the old chief died. **The war is between the dead chief's brother and the dead chief's two sons:** uncle against nephews, each side claiming the right to lead the Clan.

- **Coldtarn** *(from Chapter 8)*: the Clan scatters its chiefs' ashes on Coldtarn, a black tarn that never freezes, at the new moon after an eleven-day pyre. The eldest son pours by custom; the champion pours "when there's doubt". At Haldor's, Ulric claims the pour and Arn reaches for his sword; Teo makes them pour together, one hand each. The dragons sit silent on the rim and then cry all together.
- **Two fires** *(from Chapter 6)*: during the thirty days of mourning the hold splits into Ulric's men (east, plain grey) and Arn's (west, grey with a red thread), with an empty chair between the two high tables. Teo walks the space between them. Ulric really did lead the shore at Gullhaven in the coast raids; the songs give it to Haldor.
- **The uncle's claim:** even while his brother ruled, the uncle always had more followers, and he is the better warrior in the Clan. Many believe leadership should go to the strongest, not to the chief's blood.
- **The sons' claim:** the **elder son claims by birth** as the chief's heir.
- **The spark:** the **younger son is killed**, apparently by the uncle's faction, and the long-running struggle becomes open war. The elder son is left to lead his side alone.
- **The battle (Book 1):** in the battle that follows, the Clan's three bonded warriors die; their dragons are enraged and cause havoc. The farmer is injured in the chaos. No one in known Winshire history has bonded with multiple beasts.
- **The truth:** the killing was **the Restoration's doing**, so that it could gain the Clan's dragons in the chaos. The killer was **Rook**, a Restoration agent hired into the uncle's men *(established)*. He dies at the Old Ruin before he can talk, so the proof is lost and the truth waits for Book 2. Until the reveal, the uncle is blamed.
- **How the truth comes out:** through **a conversation between the twins, overheard by the farmer, right after the twins are revealed and before Book 1's final battle begins.** Book 1 ends soon after, with one twin dead, so the farmer carries the secret into Book 2. *(Working proposal: he overhears it while hiding from Restoration hunters, or the Far-Seer leads him close enough to listen.)*
- **Book 1: no winner.** The civil war runs through the whole of Book 1 with no winner, only damage and casualties on both sides. It never resolves; it bleeds.
- **Book 2: the farmer brings the truth, and uncle and nephew unite.** The farmer takes what he heard to the Clan **because the civil war is hurting his own people and village.** Once they know, the uncle and the elder son join against the Restoration. The outsider the Clan hunted becomes the one who ends its war, not for the Clan's sake, but for his village's.

**Bond rupture:** When either bonded partner dies, the other usually dies too; a beast usually withers when its human dies. The bond is so strong that losing it feels like having part of yourself torn away. The warriors’ deaths tear through all three dragons at once. Each dragon is already grieving, enraged, and beginning to wither as the battle continues.

**The accidental threefold bond:** In that same battle, the three dragons are unintentionally drawn toward the injured farmer and pushed into bonding with him. The pull is so sudden that neither he nor the dragons understands what is happening until the bonds have formed. He does not seek or capture them, and they do not calmly choose him in a deliberate ceremony. The three bonds form almost simultaneously, interrupting the dragons’ expected deaths. The dragons were bred and kept under the Clan’s control, but the bond surge overrides that system. The farmer experiences the pain and grief of three ruptured bonds at once; the connection feels like a curse before it can feel like a gift. Why this happens, and how he survives holding three bonds, are central mysteries.

### The three dragons

These are distinct working personalities. Their names are established (Aurveth, Kharrak, Lisska; see `Characters.md`). Exact appearances, histories, and gifts are open; any gifts below are possibilities, not established mechanics.

- **The General — the elder strategist.** Ancient and wise, with eons of experience. Across its long life it has bonded with three humans in separate periods; its latest partner is one of the three warriors who dies in the civil war. It is not a battle dragon; it served as a general, reading the field, planning campaigns, and directing others. It speaks slowly and precisely, remembers the cost of past wars, and tends to see the farmer as a person to guide rather than a weapon to command. Its calm hides the shock of losing its latest partner and being pulled into a new bond before it can process the loss. How it survived the endings of its two earlier bonds is an open question and may be an exceptionally rare clue to bond rupture.
- **The Vanguard — the challenger.** A direct, battle-trained dragon who believes action is better than hesitation. Proud of its strength, quick to take offense, and protective once trust is earned. Its weakness is that it mistakes restraint for cowardice and can push the farmer toward dangerous confrontations. It gives the group a forceful frontline presence. **Possible gift:** a short, powerful burst of flame or heat, potent at close range.
- **The Far-Seer — the restless scout.** Curious, alert, and hard to keep still. It notices tracks, distant movement, and small changes others miss. Humor and mischief help it avoid talking about grief. It excels at scouting and escape, but its curiosity can lead the group into danger before the others are ready. **Possible gift:** heightened perception or wind-assisted movement that helps detect and evade danger.

**His want** *(established)*: beyond surviving and protecting his village, the farmer needs to know **why the dragons came to him.** "Why me?" drives his choices after the disaster and leads him into the history of the old realm. *(Working proposals: in Book 1 the question takes him to the Old Ruin and its carved verse, which is why he is there at the climax; in Book 2 the answers begin to come from the history Hearthmere hid; the final answer, that he was always able to carry more than one life, arrives only at the end of Book 3.)* What the three dragons want from one another now that they share a bond remains to be developed. His path should focus on his outsider status, the sudden burden of the bonds, grief, and the work of living with three distinct dragon personalities.

**Established rule: keep him distinct from familiar dragon stories.** His story must not read as “farm boy finds dragon” (as in *Eragon*) or as a dragon-rider academy story (as in *Fourth Wing*). He never discovers an egg, seeks out a dragon, or trains to ride one: the bonds happen to him, unintended, in a battle he has no part in, and they arrive as a curse rather than a reward.

## The antagonist: the Restoration

**Established:** The antagonist is a restoration faction, *the Restoration* as a working name, that wants to bring back the old realm of Winshire. Its aims are to:

1. **Overthrow the current regimes** of the six kingdoms.
2. **Gather beasts by capture,** through raids, roadblocks, and camps.
3. **Find an ancient, powerful beast** to aid its cause.

Its events center on the areas near the two protagonists.

**Working proposals:**

- **Why they believe it.** The Restoration teaches that Winshire was whole and strong before it split into six quarrelling kingdoms, and that the old realm's power came from the ancient beasts. To restore Winshire, they need that power again. Their grievance is real: Sarovel's unchosen, Kessmoor's exiled bonded, and Saltreach's lawless ports are all failures of the current regimes, so the Restoration can recruit people the kingdoms have wronged. It should be tempting, not cartoonish.
- **The central irony.** The Restoration claims to bring back the age the ancient beasts remember, yet it captures beasts and wants to bind an ancient one to its cause. To the ancient beasts, that is the worst form of the taint. They are destroying the very thing they say they're restoring.
- **Bond-forcers are its tools.** Rather than a separate radical group, the bond-forcers could be the Restoration's method: capturing beasts and binding them (coercion that imitates a bond; a true bond can't be forced) or harvesting their gifts. Their captured beasts become the army that will overthrow the regimes.
- **Its link to the Dragon Clan war** *(established)*. The Restoration arranged the killing of the chief's younger son that sparked the open war, so it could gain the Clan's bred and controlled dragons in the chaos. The killer was Rook, a Restoration agent hired into the uncle's men. The truth comes out in a conversation between the twins in Book 1; until the Clan learns it in Book 2, the uncle is blamed. The accidental threefold bond ruins the plan. The farmer now holds the dragons it wanted, and he proves that one person can hold more than one bond.
- **Its target: the Titan beneath Hearthmere** *(established)*. The Restoration is after the ancient Titan locked in Hearthmere's catacombs. That is the "ancient, powerful beast" its plan depends on. *(Established for the series: the Titan is freed at the end of Book 1 and turns out to be a world destroyer. The Restoration opens the seal to capture it and bind it to its cause, and loses control of it.)*
- **It exposes Hearthmere's secret** *(established)*: the Restoration reveals that Hearthmere chained a Titan and built its magic and history on it, as part of its case against the regimes. Here the Restoration is right, which makes it harder to dismiss.
- **Why it wants both protagonists.** The farmer is proof of multiple bonds and holds three dragons. The ancient lion is exactly the kind of ancient power the Restoration seeks. Both protagonists end up standing between the Restoration and what it needs, which brings their paths together.
- **How it appears near the protagonists.**
  - *Near the student:* poachers and hunters on Restoration pay in the outskirts, roadblocks on knightly patrol routes, a beast camp hidden in the forest, and hunters disturbing the Old Ruin, which wakes the ancient lion.
  - *Near the farmer:* agents inside the Clan's rival factions, raids on farms for beasts and supplies, and hunters tracking the three dragons after the battle.
- **Leadership.** *Established:* the Restoration is led by **twin brothers**. Their identity is revealed **right before the battle at the end of Book 1**, as the Restoration's first open war against the kingdoms is about to break out. The Dragon Clan's civil war comes earlier and does not reveal them. For most of the story, people speak of the Restoration's leader as **one person who can be in two places at once**. *Working proposals:*
  - **The legend before the reveal.** Sightings of "the leader" arrive from both sides of the border on the same day: a speech in a Saltreach port and a raid in Hearthmere at the same hour. Rumor turns this into a supernatural power. Some say the leader has an ancient beast's gift; the reader has just seen the ancient lion multiply itself, which makes the rumor feel plausible and points suspicion in the wrong direction.
  - **Why they hide as one.** A leader who seems to be everywhere is more frightening and harder to catch, and a single legendary figure is easier to follow than two men. The deception is also their best defense: anyone hunting "the leader" is always looking in the wrong place.
  - **Their fates** *(established)*: one twin is killed at the conclusion of Book 1, **by the student, just as his lock on the seal gives way, while his brother leads the charge at another location.** *(Established: the twins are **Cassian and Corvin Vale**; Maren kills **Cassian**, the public face; **Corvin**, the strategist, survives until Book 3.)* In that moment the lion learns that its kin is now a world destroyer. The surviving twin now has a personal reason to hate the protagonists. He continues through Book 2 and is killed in Book 3. See `Series_Outline.md` for proposals.
  - **Two brothers, one cause.** The twins can share a cause but differ in temperament, for example one the public face who wins people over, the other the hidden strategist who plans raids and captures. That difference gives the protagonists something to work on: the protagonists might reach one brother but not the other, or turn them against each other.
  - **A mirror of the realm.** Two halves of one leader echo a land that was one realm and is now divided. They are also a counterpoint to the Dragon Clan, where a brother turned against his dead brother's sons and tore the Clan apart: these brothers stayed united, and that unity is what makes them dangerous.
  - **Twinned with the protagonists.** The alternating structure lets each strand see one brother first, so before the reveal, each strand only ever meets "the leader" as one man. A reader following both strands may notice small differences between the two "leaders" they met, a clue that pays off at the reveal.
  - **A twist at the end of Book 1.** Because the reveal comes right before Book 1's final battle, the "two places at once" legend runs through almost all of Book 1. The reveal reframes everything the reader has heard: every impossible sighting was true, just not in the way people believed.
- **Where people stand.** Some help the Restoration's raids or hand over beasts; others break its camps and free beasts; many agree with its grievances while rejecting its methods.

## The series

**Established:** the story is a three-book series, self-published first; any games are adapted later.

- **Book 1:** the twins are revealed, the first war breaks out, and the protagonists meet. Then the Titan, known throughout Book 1 as the Last Titan, is freed, and the book ends with the two of them watching it fly.
- **Book 2:** the war continues and the hunt for the Titan begins. Some Titans prove to be world destroyers, and the kingdoms try to capture them for power. Heroes save as many people as they can. The protagonists try to stop the Titan's capture, then learn it means to free five other major Titans to level the world. While everyone hunts it, other Titans are revealed to exist; the Titan travels between temples gathering relics: each sleep relic it takes wakes one of the five Titans put to sleep ages ago, and the last, the control relic, gives it command over them.
- **Book 3:** the Titan War. The kingdoms unite, but one kingdom plots to be supreme. The ancient lion becomes a Titan and the three dragons become Titan-class. The protagonists unite the elder Titans: two join them, and two give their power to the lion and the dragons, then fade away. In the final battle victory seems close, but the Titans win, and everything is destroyed. A remnant of people the protagonists saved survives. The lion and the dragons, now Titans, die protecting people. With no Titans left, the bond between beasts and people breaks, and the Age of Elementals begins. The ending only hints at total destruction. The student and the farmer survive as near-Titans and become the Council of Immortals in the next series, which reveals that some Titans survived.

From Book 2 on, the other kingdoms are introduced while Hearthmere and Saltreach keep growing, with parallel events in both that intertwine and affect the overall story.

The full outline, with working proposals for each book, is in `Series_Outline.md`.

## Themes

- Protection is a choice and a responsibility, not just a title.
- A person’s worth is not determined by whether they are bonded.
- Trust between people and beasts has to be earned.
- Great power calls for restraint and care.
- Legends can hide the truth about the creatures they describe as monsters, but not always. Some legends are true.
- Ambition for power destroys what unity builds. *(Working proposal: the kingdoms unite against the Titans, but one kingdom's plot to be supreme turns near-victory into the end of the world.)*
- What you protect matters, even when you lose. *(Working proposal: in a story that ends in destruction, the lives the protagonists saved are what gives it meaning.)*

The two protagonists argue these themes from opposite sides. The student has worth without power and earns one bond slowly, through respect and sacrifice. The farmer has power before he has learned to carry it, through three bonds that arrive at once, by accident, out of other people’s deaths.

- **Callis** is the seal-carrier (since before Upper Rush). He opens Greyhold's river gate in the third hour of night 143 and takes Cassian down with Thorne's key.
- **The twins** meet under the arch (68): Corvin (still) and Cassian (warm). Rook tells them how he killed Teo. Calder overhears; no one else does.
- **Rook** dies on the spit by the hunters' stake (72), going at Calder with the six-knife so Kharrak will kill him. Calder keeps the knife.
- **The bond (73):** on Hollin Knowe, bond-forcers have the lion in honey-smoke and chain. Maren puts her hand where the binder's was and gives herself into the opening. The lion meets her instead of emptying her. Its name is **Orrun**. Lion and dragon can speak as far kin, so Lisska's message reaches Maren through him.
- **The full verse** (Aurveth, from memory, 70): *Here the road of the one realm crossed the water, / WINSHIRE, where the old ones walked unbound, / and when the old ones went into the light / the last great one lay down to sleep below the stone, / and its own kin kept the door. Let no hand wake it. / Keep the watch below, and do not ask.* "Keep the watch" was the old ones' charge to the lion before it was the knights' oath.
- **The locks:** a carved stone disc with an iron boss under each end (the Ruin's under the tower; the knight's off Hale's ledge under Greyhold). Both turn at midnight on night 143: Corvin's with Calder's hand on his wrist; Cassian's under his own falling weight after Maren kills him. The Old Ruin falls into the cavern; Greyhold's side holds.
- **What Orrun learns (77):** his kin was a sleeper; three hundred years of chains made it a destroyer. It doesn't know him.
- **The ending (79):** they come up in the reeds below Redwatch at dawn. The Titan rises out of the river where the island was, lion-kin, the size of a hill, with stone-and-leather wings and broken chains on its legs; Redwatch's tower cracks; Greyhold's inner wall splits. It flies north-east. Every lamp without oil in Hearthmere goes out; licensed words fail; the sweet wells sour. Maren: "Keep the watch."
- **Deaths:** Joss (50), Hallam and Morrow (60, 62), Pell (69), Thorne and her boar (75), Kesh and Haverly (75), Hale (75), Cassian (77), Rook (72), Sten Raske (60). Survivors for Book 2: Corvin (up the old stair), Callis (down, alive), Tobin, Tam, Odo (legs broken; threw his punches in the river).
