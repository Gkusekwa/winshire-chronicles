---

"Six," said Corvin.

The dust came down out of the dome in a thin grey stream onto the carved stone between them. Small stones with it, pattering. Over their heads, through the rock, through the floor of the tower, the sound of Kharrak digging. Claws in stone. Closer. A second crack ran across the dome from the passage to the far wall.

The men holding Calder looked up.

"Five."

Below the ledge, out in the dark, the six chains groaned.

All of them at once. Going taut, the way they'd gone taut before, but further. Harder. A sound so deep it wasn't a sound, it was the rock round them shaking, and it didn't stop. It held. And held. Like a rope with a horse leaning back on it, a horse the size of a hill.

The candle-flame on the floor beside the iron boss bent sideways and stood up again.

"Four," said Corvin.

He hadn't moved. He knelt on the iron boss in the middle of the stone disc with his long pale hand on the bow of the key and his face turned down to it, calm, the way a man kneels at a wayside shrine. He didn't look up at the dome, or the dust, or Calder.

*Kharrak,* said Calder. *Kharrak, now, now, now…*

---

"Three."

The dome came in.

Not all of it. The part over the passage. Where the crack was. It came down in one great slab, rock and earth and stone, and the noise of it was the loudest thing Calder had ever heard, louder than the river going out, and through the hole where it had been, out of the dark and the dust, came Kharrak.

Head first. Rust-red, filthy, bleeding from a long cut down his side, with his claws still out from the digging and the fire coming up in his throat. He came through the hole in the dome into the chamber with the floor of the tower coming down round him in pieces, and the light of his fire went over everything, white, and the men holding Calder let go.

They didn't decide to. They saw a dragon come through the roof and their hands opened.

Calder went for the key.

---

He was a pace from it. He'd been a pace from it the whole time, with four men holding him. He went across the pace on his knees and got his hand on Corvin's wrist.

Corvin looked at him.

For the first time. Straight at him. Their faces a hand's breadth apart. Calder's hand on his wrist, round it, gripping, with everything he had.

"Two," said Corvin.

He didn't pull against Calder's hand. He didn't try to shake it off. He knelt there with Calder holding his wrist, and looked at him with his grey eyes, and Calder pulled, and pulled, and the hand didn't move off the key. It was like pulling at a tree.

Not strength, Calder thought. He's not stronger than me. He's a thin man with a clerk's hands.

He just won't.

*Kharrak…*

Kharrak was in the chamber. Behind him. Rock still coming down. Calder felt him coming, felt the heat on his back, felt the red tide going up and over, felt Kharrak see the key and the hand and the wrist…

"One," said Corvin.

---

He turned it.

With Calder's hand on his wrist. With Calder pulling back against him with all his weight. He turned the long black key in the iron boss, and it turned as if it wanted to, as if it had been waiting three hundred years to be turned and was glad.

And the whole stone disc turned with it.

Calder felt it go under his knees. The great carved floor as wide as a threshing floor, grinding, turning, a quarter of a turn. The rings of letters cut in it sliding past each other. Ring past ring. The letters in each ring meeting the letters in the next, and stopping, in a line. A line of letters from the rim to the iron boss, all at once, where there'd been no line before.

The grinding stopped.

And in the same breath, a long way off, under the river, toward Greyhold, the same sound.

He felt it through his knees. Through the stone disc. Through the rock. Far off. So far off he couldn't tell if it was a sound or only the ground. A great slow grinding turn. The same. In the same breath. As if somewhere a long way away, under the other bank, under the school, another floor had turned under another hand at the same moment as this one.

*Two hands.*

Corvin felt it too. Calder saw him feel it. Kneeling on the iron boss with his hand on the key and Calder's hand on his wrist, he lifted his head, very slightly, the way a man lifts his head to listen to a bell a long way off.

And he smiled.

It was the only time Calder ever saw him do it. A small smile. Not like his brother's. Not warm. Satisfied. The smile of a man at the weighing-post when the sack comes out at the weight he said.

---

The seal opened.

He'd thought it would be a door. Something that swung, or slid, or lifted.

The floor went. The stone disc dropped. All of it. Out from under him. Not breaking: dropping, whole, the way a trap-door drops, and he was on it, kneeling, and then there was nothing under his knees at all. And the chamber floor round it went too, the rock round the rim cracking away in a great ring and going down after it. The passage too, and the ledge beyond it.

And above.

He felt it above. Through the marks. The tower. The round room at its foot where Kharrak had dug through. The arch. The lion's long smooth hollow in the threshold. The verse over the arch, six lines, *let no hand wake it.* All of it, cracking, going down. The fort round it. The broken walls. The great fire in the middle of the fort, falling into the hole. The Old Ruin, that had stood on its island in the river since before there was a Hearthmere, since there was a bridge, falling into the dark under it all at once with a sound like the end of the world.

He fell.

---

He fell for a long time.

He didn't know which way was up. Rock falling round him, past him, with him. Dust. Darkness, and in the darkness, now and then, fire, Kharrak's, white, lighting the falling stone for a moment like a lantern swung in a mill. He heard Kharrak roar. Close. Then further. He felt Kharrak's wings jam in the falling rock and tear free and jam again.

He felt the ground of the cavern coming up at him out of the dark, a long way down. He couldn't see it. He knew it was there. The way you know the bottom of a well is there when you drop a stone.

*I'm sorry,* he thought. *Wren. I'm sorry.*

Something came down through the falling rock above him.

Fast. Faster than the rock. Wings folded, head down, like a stone thrown at the bottom of the world. Bronze. Huge. With one gold eye and the ruin where the other had been, and blood all down the plates of her chest where the ironback's horn had been in her.

He felt her come. Through the bronze mark. He felt her tear free of the ironback in the fort as the fort went in under her, and fold her wings, and dive into the hole after him.

Her forefoot closed round him in the dark.

Round his whole body, the way his hand would close round an egg. Claws as long as his arm, curved over him like the bars of a cage. Holding.

She couldn't stop.

She opened her wings.

---

He felt them open. He'd felt her wings open a thousand times, on the hill, in the sky over the Marches, over the river at midwinter. He'd never felt this.

She opened her wings in the cavern under the Old Ruin, in the dark, among the falling stone, the first wings that had ever opened in that air in all the years since there'd been a cavern. They caught. The air was there. More air than he'd thought, more dark, more room. A great dark, going on and on in every direction past where any light could reach. They caught the air and she turned, and the falling stone went past them, and she was flying.

Not well. Not long. She was hurt and heavy and the ironback's horn had been in her chest. She came down in a long slanting rush through the dark with the rock still falling round them, and the sound of it going on above, and her wings beating once, twice, three times, and then the ground.

A slope. Rubble. She hit it hard on her hind legs and her chest and slid, and he felt her forefoot keep him off the stones, curled round him, while she slid down the rubble on her bronze plates with a noise like a ploughshare on rock. A long way. Then she stopped.

She lay there.

He lay in her forefoot.

The rock went on falling. Above them, behind them, out in the dark, the rest of the Old Ruin coming down. It went on and on. Then smaller. Then stones, one at a time, rattling down the slope. Then nothing.

The dust settled.

---

*Calder,* said Aurveth.

"Yes."

*Are you…*

"Yes." He didn't know if he was. He moved his arms. His legs. Everything hurt. Everything moved. "Yes. Are you?"

She didn't answer.

He felt her through the bronze mark and he didn't need her to. The horn-wound in her chest, deep, pulsing. Her wings, the left one wrenched, the joint torn. Her back, the old scars from the mill opened again. She lay on the rubble with her great head down on the stones and her breath going in and out of her, slow, ragged, and he felt every bit of it.

"Aurveth."

*I'm here,* she said. *I've been here before.* A long pause. *Not here. But hurt like this. I'm old. I've been hurt like this.*

Her forefoot opened.

He crawled out of it onto the rubble.

---

It was dark. Not black. Somewhere above, very far up, there was light: a ragged hole, orange, where the island had been, with fire still burning at the edges of it, a long way up. It didn't reach down here. It lit the dust hanging in the air above them, and the edges of things, and nothing else.

Kharrak came down the slope.

Out of the dark above them. Not flying. Sliding, clawing, falling and catching himself, in a shower of loose stone, with his wings half open and jammed with rubble and his head down. He came down the slope of fallen rock onto the stones beside them and stopped, and stood there, swaying.

Cut. Burned. One horn broken off short. Blood running down his side from the ironback and from the rock and from the digging, and his claws torn. Alive.

He put his head down to Calder.

*You're alive,* said Kharrak.

"Yes."

*I felt you fall.* The red tide was low. Lower than Calder had ever felt it. *I couldn't reach you. I was in the rock.*

"She caught me."

Kharrak looked at Aurveth, lying on the stones.

He lay down beside her, close, against her side, and put his head on her neck. She let him.

---

He heard the ironback go past.

Out in the dark below them. Something huge coming down the slope of rubble from above, not sliding, falling, end over end, plates and horn and the great humped back, and past them, close enough that the wind of it went over his face, and on, down, into a deeper dark below the slope.

It didn't make a sound when it hit.

The man in grey who'd stood on its neck with his arm up didn't make one either.

---

He looked for Corvin.

He couldn't see much. The dust. The dark. The fire very far up. But across the cavern, on the far side of the great hole the Old Ruin had fallen into, high up on the wall of the dark, there was a light.

A lantern. Small. Yellow. Moving.

Going up.

He watched it. It went up slowly, back and forth, back and forth, the way a light goes up a stair cut into the face of a cliff. A stair he hadn't known was there. An older one. Cut into the far wall of the cavern, going up to somewhere he couldn't see. Three figures with it. One tall and thin, going first, not hurrying, not looking down.

Corvin had known it was there. Of course he had. He'd read the verse. He'd read the cut pages, or the ones that hadn't been cut, or whatever the Restoration had that Hearthmere didn't. He'd known the floor would go. He'd knelt on the iron boss with his hand on the key and counted down to one and turned it, and when the floor went he'd been a step from the edge of it, and the stair.

The lantern went up the far wall and round a corner of the rock, and was gone.

He didn't look back once.

---

Calder stood up on the rubble.

He stood at the bottom of the slope of the fallen Ruin with the two dragons lying behind him, and looked out into the dark.

There were the chains.

He could see them now. His eyes had found them. Going up past him into the dark above, toward the hole and the fire, where they'd been fixed to whatever had been above. Six. Black. Each link taller than a man. Taut. So taut they hummed. A deep note, all six together, that went through the rock and through his boots and through his teeth.

And going down. Ahead of him. Into a deeper dark at the bottom of the cavern, further than the slope, further than anything. Down to where they were fastened to whatever they'd been fastened to for three hundred years.

Something was moving down there.

Not the dark moving, or water turning over. Something he couldn't see the edges of, that filled the deep dark at the bottom of the cavern the way the dark filled it, lifting, the way a hill would lift if a hill could stand up.

The chains groaned.

Up at the top of the slope, where the six chains went up into the dark toward the hole, there were stones. Great anchor-stones, set in the cavern wall, each as big as a house, with the chains running through iron eyes in them. He could see the nearest one in the light of the fire far above.

It was cracking.

A long dark line running across the face of it, from the iron eye outward. Getting longer as he watched, like ice on a pond in the morning when somebody's walking out on it.

*Two hands,* he thought. *Here and there. And it wakes.*

*And it will not sleep again.*

---

He felt something.

Far off. Not above. Not from the hole where the Ruin had been, where the fire was. The other way. Across the cavern, through the rock, toward the far side of the river. Toward the Hearthmere bank. Toward Greyhold.

Down.

A small bright fire. Green. Frightened. Coming toward him.

He stood on the rubble and stared into the dark toward it.

*Lisska?*

No answer. No words. Too far still, through too much rock. But the feeling. He'd know it anywhere. He'd felt it go west over the Brackenwood at first light with a bundle in her claw, going further and further until it was nearly nothing.

Now it was coming back. Through the dark, under the river, from the far side of the cavern, from under the school. Down. Toward him.

He didn't understand it.

*How?* he thought. *She can't be under the school. She can't be down here. How can she be coming from there?*

Beside it, so close to it he could hardly tell them apart, there was something else.

Not his. He couldn't feel it the way he felt her. He felt it through her, the way he'd felt Morrow through Aurveth at the ford. Something very large, and very old, and warm, like the ground in summer. Coming with her. Through the dark.

With it, smaller, almost nothing, so faint he'd have missed it if Lisska hadn't been holding on to it the way she held on to him: somebody.

A person.

Coming.
