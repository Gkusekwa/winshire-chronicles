# Decisions while drafting Chapters 41–50

*Historical record; merged into the Bible. The chapter notes and the Bible are current.*

Extracted from each chapter's notes block. Items marked (proposals) are the drafter's calls for the author to confirm. Later rulings: see Rulings_41-79.md.

## Chapter 41: The Freeze

POV: Maren
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Deep winter. The Wend freezes from the edges in, and a refugee family tries to walk across. Maren goes out on the ice after them. It breaks. Something huge drags her out of the water and is gone before she can see it.
Echo of the other's story: A dragon lands on the ice upriver and takes off again.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapters 39–40: the lion now lies on Maren's side of the stream; the Wend is in winter spate with ice running and no boat will cross. Maren's trips start from the Redwatch boathouse. The collar is still on.
Draft notes: Days seventy-four to seventy-nine. Decided here (proposals):
- The freeze: the running ice jams at the bend below the island on night 74 and the river locks from the edges in. By day 78 there's a white road from the Saltreach bank (the Eelweir) to the island's east shore, and the ice reaches out from the island's west shore toward Redwatch, but a channel of black open water still runs close under the Hearthmere bank where the current is fastest. It skins over at night. It freezes hard on night 80.
- The ice runs from day 62. For the first ten days Ottery can still take her over in the punt at dusk along the slow edges (Lisska sees her there on day 72); after that nobody crosses. By day 79 Maren hasn't seen the lion for a week.
- Day 79, dawn, Redwatch: a family from the Saltreach bank, the Haddows (a man, a woman, a boy of about eight and a baby), have walked the white road onto the island in the dark and are trying to cross the last channel, which has skinned over in the night. Ottery shouts them back. They don't hear, or won't.
- Maren goes out with a rope and a boathook from the boathouse, flat on the ice the way the river watch teaches. She gets the boy and the woman with the baby back to the solid edge. The man goes through. She goes in after him with the rope round her. The rope parts on the ice edge.
- Under the ice, the current takes her downstream under the skin. Something seizes her by the coat and the shoulder from above, through the ice, and drags her up onto the island's shore. Heat, a smell of fur and cold stone, a sound like a millstone. When she can see again there's nothing there but a broken hole in the shore ice the size of a cart and long furrows in the snow going into the trees. The man, Haddow, is on the shore beside her, alive.
- She doesn't tell Ottery what she felt. She tells him the current threw them up. He looks at the furrows and says nothing.
- Echo: on day 77, from the Redwatch tower, she sees a dragon come down on the white ice far upriver, above Harrow's End, stand there a moment as if testing it, and go up again. Rust-red, she thinks.

## Chapter 42: Crossing the River

POV: Calder
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Calder crosses the ice to the Old Ruin, hiding the dragons, and narrowly misses Maren and the lion. Each finds only signs of the other.
Echo of the other's story: Footprints, a broken Restoration chain, the lion's scent that makes all three dragons go still.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 40: Calder crosses alone, on foot, when the river freezes. He carries a warm green scale Lisska shed, inside his coat. Three dragons landing would bring Greyhold's knights; Aurveth is afraid of the island ("it cut me"). From Chapter 41: the white road from the Eelweir to the island's east shore; Kharrak tested the ice on day 77.
Draft notes: Days seventy-seven to eighty-two. Decided here (proposals):
- Calder winters at Upper Rush with Bel, Joss, Ada, Wren and the children. The dragons have moved down from the old hatching caves to Crow Scar for the freeze, two leagues east of the Fenn farm and within speaking range of the river.
- Kharrak tested the ice on day 77 without being asked ("Holds. Thick. Tested.") and came back pleased with himself.
- He crosses on the night of day 81, from the Eelweir, on foot, alone, by starlight. The dragons lie on the hill above the Fenn farm, a league and a half off, so he can still hear them.
- On the island, in order:
  - the fort and the arch: he can't read the verse; Lisska asks him to push it to her the way she pushes her memories to him, and he manages it; Aurveth reads the first line through him and stops;
  - the spit: the hunters' rusted body-chains still lie in the snow; the stake still stands in the paving at the foot of the landing stair with the stub of the collar-chain round its head, cut clean (the collar itself is still on the lion). He works an arm's length free from one of the body-chains, where two links have rusted thin, with the ring of six on every link;
  - the stream on the west shore: a small fire's ashes, still warm, a woman's boot-prints going down to the reeds and out across the west ice toward Redwatch, and over and beside them huge round prints and a long pressed hollow in the snow where something lay on this side of the water.
- He follows the great prints north as far as the edge of the oaks. He hears it breathing, close, in the dark ahead, not asleep. The three dragons, a league and a half away, go utterly still through the marks. He backs away.
- He kneels at the stream bank on the way back to look at the fire, and Lisska's scale slips out of his coat into the snow by the warm ashes. He doesn't notice until he's back at Upper Rush.
- Echo: when he gets back to the hill above the Fenn farm at dawn, the dragons smell the lion on his coat and go still, all three, and Aurveth puts her head down to it and won't lift it until the sun is up.

## Chapter 43: Footprints

POV: Maren
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Maren finds a man's tracks on the island and a dragon scale the size of a shield. She doesn't report either: her first lie to the orders.
Echo of the other's story: The scale is warm.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 40: the warm dragon scale Maren finds on the island is Lisska's, dropped from Calder's coat. From Chapter 42: on night 81 Calder crossed from the Eelweir, cut a length of chain from the stake on the spit, found her warm fire and the lion's lying-place, followed the lion's prints north to the oaks, heard it breathing awake, and backed away. From Chapter 41: the channel froze hard on night 80; Ottery forbade the ice.
Draft notes: Days eighty-one to eighty-three. Decided here (proposals):
- Dusk on day 81: Maren's first visit since the freeze, two days after the channel. Ottery walks her across the newly hard channel with a pole and a lantern and waits in the reeds. The lion comes, lies at ten paces on her side. She leaves at full dark. (Calder comes two hours later.)
- Day 82, dusk again: the snow has held every print. Over her own, a man's: big, heavy, hobnailed Marches boots, the kind farmers wear on the far bank. They came from the east shore, went to the arch, to the spit, to her fire, and north along the lion's path, then back, then out across the east ice toward the Eelweir.
- On the spit: someone has taken an arm's length out of one of the hunters' rusted body-chains, prising two thin-rusted links open with a blade, recently. The bright scratches show through the rust. (The stake still holds the stub of the collar-chain she cut in Chapter 21; the collar is on the lion.)
- By her fire, half in the snow where the man knelt: a scale. Green as a beetle's back, broad as a round shield, thin as slate, edged like a leaf. It's warm, like a hearthstone the morning after.
- The lion: when it comes, it goes first to the man's prints and smells them, long, with its lip going back. Then it goes to the scale in her hands, smells that, and goes very still, and looks east across the river toward the Saltreach hills for a long while. Then it lies down, at ten paces, as before.
- The first lie: Rowan's standing order (since Ferriby) is that anyone going near the island reports any sign of men. When Pell asks at the Lesson Wall on the morning of day 83, "Anything on the island? Tracks? Hunters?", she says, "No. Nothing. Only the lion." She keeps the scale wrapped in a shirt in her chest with the hammer and chisel.

## Chapter 44: The General's Silence

POV: Calder
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Near the ruin, Aurveth feels something vast and wrong beneath the earth, and won't speak of it.
Echo of the other's story: Through the ice, the dragons hear knights singing at Greyhold: the oath, at midwinter.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapters 28, 38, 40 and 42: Aurveth remembers the white bridge, the milestones and the grey cat on the tower steps; she has lost her first partner's face and believes it was cut out of her; she knew the lion's smell on Calder's coat though she has never been near it, and put her head down in the snow. Hale and the black door carry on through Maren's chapters.
Draft notes: Days eighty-three to eighty-eight (midwinter night, full moon). Decided here (proposals):
- For six days after the crossing Aurveth hardly speaks. She lies at the back of the cave at Crow Scar with her eye open.
- On midwinter night (day 88) she asks Calder to come with her: "not to the island; near it". She glides down with him on her neck an hour before midnight, under the full moon, low over the Fellwood, and lands on the frozen river a league above the island, in the shadow of the Saltreach bank under the hill, where no tower can see.
- She lies down flat on the ice with her jaw and throat against it and her wings spread, the way a man lies with his ear to the ground to hear horses. She stays like that for most of an hour. Through the bronze mark Calder feels it come up into her: something below the river, below the island, below the earth. Vast. Bound. Turning. Like the bottom of Coldtarn, if Coldtarn were alive and couldn't get out. And a wrongness under it, like a bone set badly.
- She won't speak of it. "Do not ask me. Not yet. I will tell you when I can bear to." The phrase "do not ask" stays with him.
- Echo: at midnight the sound of singing comes across the ice from Greyhold, four leagues and more downriver, faint and clear on the still air: the whole school on the wall singing the midwinter oath. Lisska, on the hill, hears it best and repeats the words to Calder: "Keep the watch below, and do not ask." Aurveth, on the ice, shudders from end to end.
- Kharrak's response: he wants to dig. Aurveth says, very quietly, that if he puts a claw into that island she will break his wing herself.

## Chapter 45: The Paper

POV: Maren
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: At Millbrook's midwinter fair, the Restoration publicly exposes Hearthmere's secret: a Titan chained beneath the kingdom, its power harnessed for centuries, most of Hearthmere's history built on it. Hearthmere calls it a lie.
Echo of the other's story: The Restoration names "the dragon farmer" as proof that the old powers are returning.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapters 31, 35 and 37: Absalom Reede, the bookbinder at the Kettle, runs the Restoration meetings in Fennick's tithe barn; Tam wears the iron ring; the Watch list ("From the closing"), the black door, Hale's "the Last Titan", the lullaby ("the last great beast lay down below"). From Chapter 43: Maren's first lie, the scale in her chest. From Chapter 44: that night Greyhold sings the midwinter oath on the wall.
Draft notes: Day eighty-eight, midwinter (full moon). Decided here (proposals):
- Before dawn on midwinter morning the same printed sheet is found nailed to Greyhold's river gate, inside the outer wall, where nobody from outside could reach without passing the watch. Rowan has it taken down. Every student has already read it.
- Millbrook's midwinter fair: frost fair on the frozen back channel below the mill, braziers, chestnuts, skating, a bear-baiting the knights banned years ago and nobody stopped. Greyhold sends twenty of the orders to keep the peace, Maren among them as a runner. Callis leads, with a dozen knights of the gate (Wynn and his hound Ash among them).
- At noon Absalom Reede climbs the mill steps and reads the sheet aloud, quietly, to a crowd of six or seven hundred. The claims:
  - Below Greyhold, running under the river toward the Old Ruin, the crown keeps the last of the great beasts, the Titan of the old songs, in chains, and has for three hundred years.
  - Hearthmere's lamps that burn without oil, its licensed magic, its harvests and its wealth are drawn from it.
  - The orders' oath, "keep the watch below, and do not ask", is a jailer's oath. The knights stand guard over a prisoner and are not told.
  - "The bonded sit in front and the unchosen at the back, on top of a beast in chains. That is your kingdom."
  - Proof that the old powers are waking: the farmer in the Marches whom three dragons chose in one night, and the monster on the island that a Greyhold girl sits with.
- Callis tries to arrest Reede. The crowd closes round Reede. Tam is in it, with the ring out on his coat. Stones, a brazier over, Callis knocked down on the ice, a skater's leg broken. Reede is gone in the confusion.
- At dusk a herald from Ashford (eighteen leagues; the paper went up there too in the night) reads the crown's answer at the Kettle's door and at Greyhold's gate: "a lie, a slander against the crown and the orders, put about by enemies of the realm. Any person repeating it shall answer to the magistrate."
- That midnight Maren stands on the wall with the whole school and sings the midwinter oath, and the words are different in her mouth.

## Chapter 46: The Forbidden Verse

POV: Calder
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: The accusation reaches Harrow's End. Ada breaks her promise and sings the verse her grandmother made her swear never to sing: the last one sleeps "where the oath is kept." Calder thinks of the knights' oath.
Echo of the other's story: Across the ice, every lamp in Greyhold goes out at once, then comes back.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapters 22 and 40: Ada's hidden third verse has "the shape of" the carved verse; "not till I see what it's for"; if she sang it before then, she'd be "calling something I couldn't send back". Calder has never seen "Keep the watch below" on the arch. From Chapter 44: he heard the knights sing the midwinter oath across the ice through Lisska ("Keep the watch below, and do not ask"), and Aurveth won't speak of what she felt under the river ("Do not ask me"). From Chapter 45: the Restoration's paper.
Draft notes: Days eighty-nine and ninety. Decided here (proposals):
- The paper comes over the ice from Millbrook in a carter's coat on the night of day 88, and up the river lane to Harrow's End and Upper Rush on day 89. Old Fisk brings a copy to Upper Rush, because he can read and most can't. By nightfall every house in Harrow's End has heard it, and has heard its last part too: that the dragon farmer is proof the old powers are waking.
- The song as everyone knows it (Chapter 2) is two verses and the hush. Ada's grandmother's verse is a third, sung between the second verse and the hush. Ada sings it in Bel's kitchen on the night of day 90 with the door barred and the children asleep in the loft. Wren isn't asleep.
- The hidden verse:
  *The last one sleeps where the oath is kept,*
  *in iron and dark beneath the stone,*
  *and the watch above has never slept,*
  *and the watch has forgotten what it keeps.*
  and a hush that goes with it:
  *Hush now, and do not ask it.*
  *Hush now, and do not turn the key.*
  *When two hands open what one hand shut,*
  *it wakes, and it will not sleep again.*
- Calder hears "do not ask" for the third time since midwinter night and puts it together: the knights' oath, Aurveth's silence, the paper's "beneath Greyhold". The last great beast sleeps where the knights keep their oath.
- Aurveth, two leagues off at Crow Scar, silent since the ice, hears the verse through him. She says one thing, the first since the ice: *That is what I touched.*
- Echo: while Ada is still sitting with her hands in her lap afterwards, Lisska, who has come over from Crow Scar after dark and is lying under the window, says every light at Greyhold has gone out. Calder goes into the yard. Six leagues and more down the frozen river, Greyhold's long wall is dark from end to end. It stays dark for about as long as it takes to say the hush twice. Then the lights come back, all at once.

## Chapter 47: Lamps

POV: Maren
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Hearthmere's magic flickers. The beasts are restless. Riots in Millbrook; knights ordered to arrest Restoration speakers. Tobin disappears from Greyhold.
Echo of the other's story: Rumour: the dragon farmer was seen on the island at the turn of the year.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapters 31, 33–35 and 45: Absalom Reede; Tam's ring; Vale on the Tidesteps and the two-places legend; the paper on the river gate; Callis's broken collarbone; Tobin missing from the Millbrook muster. From Chapter 46: on the night of day 90 every light at Greyhold went out, as Ada finished the verse, and came back.
Draft notes: Days ninety to ninety-six. Decided here (proposals):
- Night 90, an hour before midnight: Maren is in the Long Barn doing late rounds when every lamp in Greyhold goes out at once. The lamps that burn without oil are the crown's licensed magic and have never failed in living memory. In the dark every beast in the barn rises at the same moment, silent. The ground under the barn floor hums, faintly, through her boots. Then the lamps come back, and the beasts lie down.
- Days 91–96, it gets worse: lamps dim and flicker at odd hours; the warming-stones in the infirmary go cold for a night; the old surgeon's bone-setting charm fails on a groom's wrist, and he swears and sets it by hand. Greyhold says: the cold. Nobody believes it.
- The beasts: Kesh won't settle even with Haverly in the yard; the hounds won't go near the north end of the yard; Captain Thorne's boar breaks its stall door; the cat nobody owns sleeps on the archive stair three nights running.
- Tobin is gone. His bunk is stripped; his practice sword is left on Maren's cot with a scrap of paper tied to the hilt: "You know it's true. Come and find us. T." She burns the note.
- Day 93: Rowan's order from the council at Hallowmere: the orders will arrest any person who reads or repeats the paper aloud. Maren is on the detail that goes to Millbrook. They take the Kettle's potboy, Davin, for reading it at the jetty. The town riots: the Kettle's shutters smashed, a knight's horse stabbed, stones. Odo Marl's smithy is shut and Tam isn't there. The detail falls back to Greyhold with Davin in a cart and three knights hurt.
- Echo, day 95: a refugee boy on Liss's ferry, from the Eelweir, tells Liss (and Maren) that old Fisk saw the dragon farmer walk out across the ice to the Old Ruin round the turn of the year (it was night 81), in the dark, alone, and come back before dawn. Maren knows it's true and says nothing.

## Chapter 48: Two Sides of a War

POV: Calder
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Calder tries to make Ulric and Arn stop. Neither will.
Echo of the other's story: Arn mentions a Hearthmere student who keeps company with a monster: "the kind of madness we could use."
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 16: Arn holds Varrhold; Ulric went to Skarrow; the other bonded pairs shut themselves in the Roosts under the no-kin law. From Chapters 36 and 38: the forced binding on Gallows Quay; the camps up the Wend with bound beasts drilling. From Chapter 46: the Restoration's paper names Calder as the sign; Ada's verse. From Chapter 24: the coin man; Gorm Askell; Sten Raske.
Draft notes: Days ninety-two to ninety-eight. Decided here (proposals):
- After the verse and the paper, Calder decides to do something for the first time instead of waiting. He'll go to both Varr and ask them to stop, because the Restoration grows on their war: every burned farm sends more people to the camps and the rings.
- Day 93: he flies to Varrhold on Aurveth, openly, in daylight, with Kharrak and Lisska behind. They land in the hold's yard. Nobody shoots. Arn comes out.
- At Varrhold:
  - Lisska goes to the stable roof where she used to sit to annoy Teo.
  - On the Roosts ledges above, the bonded pairs who wouldn't fight are watching. The oldest of them, Hallam Brecc, an old rider with a black dragon called Morrow, comes down the cliff path to stand behind Calder, not beside him.
- Arn listens. Calder tells him about the camps, the bound beasts and the ring of six on the chain. He doesn't tell him about Rook: he has nothing but a face and a bucket, and Arn would ride on Skarrow that night. Arn says the Restoration is a Saltreach mob and the only thing he'll stop for is Ulric in Coldtarn. He wants the dragons back "where they belong". Calder says no.
- Arn's line (the echo): he's heard of a Hearthmere student, a woman, who sits with the Old Ruin's monster and keeps company with it. "That's the kind of madness we could use. Give me her, and a beast like that, and three dragons that know who they belong to, and I'd have Skarrow by spring."
- Day 96: Skarrow, Ulric's steading on the east side of the Scarfell, in the snow. Ulric meets him alone at the gate with Raske behind. Ulric: he didn't kill the boy; he'll stop the day someone shows him who did. Until then he won't be called murderer and kneel. Calder nearly says Rook, and doesn't. Ulric sees him nearly say it.
- Flying home, Kharrak says: *You knew something. At the gate. You didn't say it.* Calder: "I don't know anything about him." Kharrak: *You keep saying that.*

## Chapter 49: The Archive

POV: Maren
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Maren confronts Hale. He confirms it: the knights' duty "below" is guarding the chains.
Echo of the other's story: Hale admits the inner circle fears the dragon farmer almost as much as the Restoration.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapters 25, 35 and 37: Warden Osric Hale, the archive under the north tower, the black book and its list ("From the closing", 311 names), the black door three steps down with a lock the size of a man's hand, the long black key, "the Last Titan", *let no hand wake it*, "because he's three". From Chapter 45: the paper on the river gate. From Chapter 47: the lamps, the beasts facing the north wall, the cat on the archive stair, Tobin's sword, Davin taken. From Chapter 43: Maren's lie to Pell; the scale in her chest.
Draft notes: Days ninety-six to ninety-eight. Decided here (proposals):
- Davin is held in the cells under the gatehouse, waiting for the council's word from Hallowmere. Maren is put on the rota that carries his bread. On day 96 he asks her through the grille: "You swear it too. Every day. Don't you want to know what you're swearing?"
- Night 96: she goes to the archive without being sent for. Hale is there, sober, with the black book shut. She puts it all on the table in front of him: the paper, the lamps, the twenty-nine beasts at the north wall, the cat, Tobin. She asks him straight: "What's below?" He tells her.
- What Hale knows (what the Watch is told when it's sworn, and what he saw):
  - The first knights found it sleeping under the hill, "in the old days, before the orders had a name". They woke it, and they chained it, and they built the inner wall over the way down. The orders began as its wardens.
  - The lamps that burn without oil, the warming-stones, the licensed words: all of it comes up from below. "It's not a well. A well fills up again. We've been bleeding something for three hundred years, and lighting our lamps with it."
  - The Watch goes down once, on the night it's sworn. Hale went down forty years ago with Merrow holding the lantern. He never saw the beast. He saw chain: links as tall as a man, going down into the dark, and the dark breathing. He heard it.
  - Every knight's oath is part of the keeping. That's what the books say, and the Watch believes it. "Every voice at the noon bell is a hand on the chain." When knights stop believing it, the chain slackens. Hale thinks that's why the lamps fail: Tobin gone, the paper read in every alehouse, the oath said with doubt in it.
  - He doesn't say anything about two locks. He doesn't know where the chains end. "The books stop. Somebody stopped them."
- The echo: the inner circle fears the dragon farmer almost as much as the Restoration. The black book's margin lists six old signs that come before a chain fails. The last one is *one comes with three.* The council at Hallowmere has written to Rowan twice about him. "You can hang a printer. What do you do with a man three dragons lay down for?"
- Hale asks her whether the farmer has crossed. She says, "I don't know." It isn't a lie, quite. She thinks of the scale in her chest. It's the second thing she's kept back.
- Hale says he'll take her to the door. Not tonight. "When the moon's gone. Six nights. They watch the stair less in the dark of the moon. Don't ask me why. I think they're frightened."
- Day 97, the noon bell: she says the oath facing east toward the inner wall, and for the first time knows what she's saying. She says *keep the watch below.* Her mouth won't make *and do not ask.*

## Chapter 50: Bel

POV: Calder
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Joss goes looking for the Restoration camp that holds his other grey and is brought back dead. Bel blames Calder for keeping quiet. He finally shows her the chain and tells her everything: the first thing he has ever chosen to say.
Echo of the other's story: The chain's maker's mark matches the broken chain Calder found on the island in the winter.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 2: five links of beast chain from the ashes at Upper Rush, the last link cut, the ring of six. From Chapter 4: Joss's greys, Mabbin and Old Grey, roped and led off; "I'll find them and I'll bring them home." From Chapters 36–38: Mabbin home; the camps with soup at the front and bound beasts at the back; the empty eyes. From Chapter 42: the arm's length of chain from the island spit, wrapped in his scarf. From Chapter 10: Rook on the bucket; the six on his knife. From Chapter 48: the trip to Varrhold and Skarrow; Kharrak: "You keep saying that." Calder nearly named Rook at both gates.
Draft notes: Days ninety-eight to one hundred and one. Decided here (proposals):
- Day 98: the Ashby widow's girl brings word that the new camp below Ewe Hollow, where the Ewe Beck comes down to the Wend, four leagues south, has a big grey dray-beast with a split left ear pulling its water-sled. Old Grey's left ear was split by a gate-hook when he was a yearling. Joss says he'll go. Calder says wait, he'll think of a way. Joss: "You've been thinking since the summer, Cal."
- Day 99: Joss goes before light with a halter and his knife. Calder sends Lisska. She finds the camp and the grey beast, but not Joss, and shows him when she's back within reach. Calder doesn't go in. He tells himself it's because the dragons near a camp would bring the whole Restoration down on Upper Rush.
- Day 100, noon: a cart comes up the lane from Ewe Hollow with two men in grey and a camp woman. Joss is in it under a blanket. He went into the beast lines in the night with a knife to cut the grey loose; the grey's handler woke; the grey "took fright". His chest is broken; a hoofprint the size of a bucket. They return his knife and the halter, they say the beast was bought fair at Wendmouth with papers, and the camp man says to Calder: "You're Fenn. The one realm grieves with you. Our door is open." Kharrak's tide comes up on the hill above Bel's; Calder holds it. The men go away unharmed.
- Bel blames him. He knew what was in those fields and never told Joss about the eyes. Joss went in thinking Old Grey would know him.
- Night 100: Calder tells her everything, at the table, with Ada by the fire and Wren on the ladder, in scene: the chain from the ashes, Rook and the knife, the ford, the camps, the eyes (Lisska has seen Old Grey's), the room on Gallows Quay, the island, the dragons, Aurveth on the ice, the verse. It's the first thing he has ever chosen to say, the whole of it. Bel asks why he kept quiet. He says that naming it meant naming what was coming for the farm and for Wren: "I thought if I didn't name it, it'd stay down the road a while longer. It didn't. It came up the lane in a cart."
- The echo: the two chains side by side on the table. Bel scrubs the rust off with ash and her thumb, the way she'd scrub a pot, and on the inside of the last link of each there's the same small smith's stamp under the six: a crossed hammer. One maker. The men who took Joss's greys put the chain on the lion. (Proposal: the crossed hammer is Odo Marl's stamp, from his Millbrook smithy. Calder doesn't know that. Maren could recognise it later.)
- Bel: "Get him out. Old Grey. Don't you leave him in that field."
- Day 101: they bury Joss on the hill above the hollow, Marches fashion. Mabbin and Sorrel stand at the paddock gate. The dragons watch from the ridge behind Bel's. Kharrak: *Tell me when.* Calder: "The night after the dark of the moon."
