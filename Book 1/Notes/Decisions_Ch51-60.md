# Decisions while drafting Chapters 51–60

*Historical record; merged into the Bible. The chapter notes and the Bible are current.*

Extracted from each chapter's notes block. Items marked (proposals) are the drafter's calls for the author to confirm. Later rulings: see Rulings_41-79.md.

## Chapter 51: The Door

POV: Maren
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Hale takes Maren to the black door at the end of Greyhold's archive. Behind it, far down, the sound of chain. She puts her hand on the door, and across the river the lion roars.
Echo of the other's story: The roar is heard in Harrow's End; the dragons answer it.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 35: the black oak door, three iron bands, no handle, the lock as big as a man's hand, three steps down at the far end of the archive. From Chapter 49: Hale's confession; "when the moon's gone"; the Watch goes down once, on the night it's sworn; the chain; the oath as a hand on the chain; *and do not ask* won't come out of her mouth. From Chapter 37: the lion lies in the arch under the verse. From Chapter 27: Captain Thorne and her black boar. From Chapter 31: Thorne lifted her hand to the farmer the morning after the burning.
Draft notes: Days ninety-nine to one hundred and two. Decided here (proposals):
- Day 99: the council's word comes for Davin. He's to go to Hallowmere for trial before the crown's magistrates. A closed cart takes him on day 100. Maren carries his last bread. He says: "Did you ask?" She doesn't answer, but he sees her face. "You did," he says.
- Night 100: the island. Ottery takes her over the hard ice. The lion is in the arch under the verse again. She tells it, out loud, what Hale told her, knowing it can't understand a word. It watches her the whole time and doesn't look away once.
- Night 102, the dark of the moon: Hale takes her to the door after the last bell. He has the long black key in his hand, and he doesn't put it in the lock. "It's turned once for each of the Watch, on the night they're sworn. I'll not turn it for you. I'll not make you that." They stand on the bottom step.
- What she hears: nothing, and then, a long way down, chain. Iron drawing over stone, link by link, slow, for a long time. The ground hums. She puts her palm flat on the door. The oak is warm, warm as a flank. Through her hand: no voice, no bond. Something very large turns its attention toward a hand on a door, the way you'd notice a fly on your skin in your sleep.
- Across the river the lion roars. It's the first time she's heard it since the night it woke (Chapter 19). It comes through the stone. Every beast in the Long Barn answers. Below the door, the chain goes taut with a sound like a ship's cable, and is still. Hale pulls her hand off the door.
- The echo: from the north-east, faint, across the ice and the Saltreach hills from Harrow's End, three answering roars. Dragons. Maren hears them from the top of the archive stair.
- Captain Thorne comes down the stair with a lantern: she's Watch. Hale hides Maren in the stacks. Thorne: "Did you hear it? Below?" Hale: "The whole school heard it." Thorne: "It moved, Osric. The chain moved. And the dragons answered." Then: "Rowan wants the island watched from tomorrow. The girl's beast." She goes. Hale lets Maren out.

## Chapter 52: Kharrak

POV: Calder
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Calder lets Kharrak burn a Restoration beast camp to free its captives. It is the first time he chooses violence. The Restoration names him an enemy.
Echo of the other's story: Across the river, Greyhold's bell rings at night: the knights saw the fire.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 50: Joss killed under Old Grey's forefoot; the camp past Ewe Hollow where the Ewe Beck comes down to the Wend; the young man with the soft beard ("Our door's open"); Bel: "Bring him home"; "The night after the dark of the moon." From Chapters 36 and 38: the binding room and its smoke; bound beasts fight silent and don't feel pain; the binder drives by will. From Chapter 51: the lion's roar on night 102 and the dragons' answer.
Draft notes: Nights one hundred and two and one hundred and three. Decided here (proposals):
- Night 102: at Upper Rush Calder feels the lion's roar come up through the ground before he hears it. On the hill the three dragons get up and answer it before he can stop them. Aurveth, after: *That came from below as well. Not only the island. From under it.*
- Night 103: they go. Bel gives him the good halter at the door.
- The camp: grey tents for the refugees along the beck; the camp's own lines behind, on the slope, fenced: the handlers' tents, the store wagons, a long low tent with smoke-holes that is the binding tent, the beast lines (bound hounds, two greyharts, a wyvern, Old Grey), and a pen of new-bought beasts not yet bound (two young wolves, a marsh-cat, a yearling greyhart).
- Calder's choice: Kharrak may burn the fence, the binding tent, the stores and the handlers' tents. Not one refugee tent. Lisska goes first, low, screaming, to wake the refugees and drive them down the beck. Aurveth lands at the beast lines with Calder.
- The handlers set the bound hounds on Calder. They come silent. Aurveth kills two. Kharrak takes the man in grey who drives them, and Calder sees it happen and doesn't look away. Three handlers die that night. It's the first death Calder has chosen.
- Old Grey's handler stands at the end of the line with his hand on the grey's neck and drives him at Calder. Calder stands. Aurveth puts her foot on the ground between them and the handler runs. With the handler gone, Old Grey stops. He doesn't come back. He stands there empty. Calder puts the halter on him and he follows the hand that leads him. (Proposal: a bound beast whose binder is gone doesn't wake. It goes slack and follows any hand. Whether it comes back is unknown.)
- The unbound pen: Calder opens it and the wolves and the marsh-cat and the yearling go into the forest. The bound beasts at the lines stand where they are when he cuts them loose. They don't go anywhere.
- The young man with the soft beard is among the refugees on the beck ice, watching. He shouts after them, not gently now: "Enemy! Enemy of the one realm!" The refugees take it up.
- Calder walks Old Grey home up the river lane, five leagues (the grey is too big to carry). A league along it, Lisska hears Greyhold's bell first and then he does, faint, across the ice from the Hearthmere bank, ringing in the night. The knights saw the fire.
- Kharrak, at dawn: *You chose.* Calder: "Yes." Kharrak doesn't gloat. *It doesn't feel the way I thought it would.* Calder: "No."

## Chapter 53: The Pit

POV: Maren
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Maren understands why the lion lingers at the Old Ruin: what lies chained below is its kin. She goes to it knowing her kingdom imprisoned its family.
Echo of the other's story: Fire on the far bank: the farmer's dragons fighting off Restoration hunters.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 25: Hale asked whether it digs; the hollow at the north end "dug, and filled, and dug again". From Chapter 49: the cut pages, the stubs in the binding; the margin's signs, "*A thing that was sleeping comes to the old place, and will not leave it.*" From Chapter 51: her hand on the door; the lion's roar; the chain going taut; Thorne: "Rowan wants the island watched." From Chapter 52 (Calder's side): the camp at the Ewe Beck burned on night 103; Greyhold's bell.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and three to one hundred and five. Decided here (proposals):
- Night 103: Greyhold's bell. Maren on the wall with everybody else, seeing the glow at the mouth of the Ewe Beck, straight across the river on the Saltreach bank, three leagues off. Haverly: "That's dragon-fire. Nothing else burns that colour." Nobody says the farmer's name. Everybody thinks it.
- Day 103, morning: Rowan sends for Maren to her room at the top of the north tower, for the first time since the trial. She puts her on Pell's patrol at Redwatch, which is to watch the island day and night from the tower and the ice. "You'll go closer than anybody. You'll tell me what it does. You'll not go further than you've been." Rowan doesn't say why. Maren doesn't ask.
- Day 103, afternoon: Hale sends for her. In the binding of the black book, on one of the cut stubs, there's a fragment of drawing the knife missed: a corner of a page, brown ink, the edge of something drawn very large: a forefoot. Four splayed toes, claws drawn in. Under it, half a word in the old script. Hale doesn't know what it is. Maren does. She's put her own hand in those prints in the snow.
- Day 104, dusk: the island, with Pell's patrol at the tower and Ottery on the ice. The lion isn't at the stream or in the arch. She finds it at the north end, in the hollow, digging. It has dug through the frozen ground and down into old fill, deep, a pit the size of a barn floor, and at the bottom of the pit there's stone: worked stone, dressed blocks, the same grey as the inner wall at Greyhold. It's digging toward Greyhold. It's been digging all winter.
- She understands. The thing under the river is chained, and the lion came to the old place and won't leave it, and the lion's foot is in the drawing. It isn't guarding anything. It's trying to get down. To its own. To its kin.
- She doesn't go away. She goes down into the pit, slowly, and sits on the fill at the edge of the dressed stone at ten paces, while it digs. When it stops and looks at her, she says, out loud, "We did it. We did that to yours." It can't understand. She says it anyway. It looks at her a long time and goes back to digging, and she stays.
- The echo: night 105, from the top of Redwatch, the patrol sees fire on the far bank, above Harrow's End, in the hills: dragon-fire, three sources, and torches scattering. Pell: "They've come for him." Ottery: "And they're not getting him." Pell writes it down. The knights don't move.
- Maren doesn't tell Rowan about the pit. Her report says the beast keeps to the north end and digs. That's true. It's the third thing she's kept back.

## Chapter 54: The Gathering

POV: Calder
Part: Part Four: The Frozen River
Beats: Restoration forces mass in the Marches. The civil war is still bleeding the Clan, and Harrow's End is in the path.
Echo of the other's story: Sellswords say they've been paid to bring in "the knight with the lion" as well as him.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 52: the camp at the Ewe Beck burned; "Enemy of the one realm"; Old Grey home, empty. From Chapter 53 (Maren's side): fire on the hills above Harrow's End on night 105, a hundred men with torches put to flight. From Chapter 48: Hallam Brecc and Morrow; the eleven bonded pairs on the Roosts ledges; Arn and Ulric won't stop. From Chapter 38: the camps on the drove road up the Wend.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and five to one hundred and twelve. Decided here (proposals):
- Night 105: they come for him at Upper Rush. About a hundred: Restoration men with rings, bound hounds, and hired swords from the coast. They come up from the river lane toward Bel's in a line with torches. Lisska sees them. The dragons meet them on the hillside below the farm. Calder tells them: drive them, burn the ground in front of them, kill only what won't turn. Some don't turn. Eleven dead on the hill by morning; the rest scatter to the river lane.
- Kharrak brings one back alive in his claws: a sellsword, a coast man called Brask, from a Wendmouth free company. He's not a believer. He wears no ring. He's paid: ten silver a man to come up the river; two hundred for the farmer, alive; "and the same again for the knight with the lion. Over the water. A woman. Greyhold." Calder lets him go at dawn with his boots and nothing else.
- Days 106–108: Calder moves out of Bel's house to Crow Scar with the dragons, so that whatever comes looking for him comes there. He goes down to Upper Rush only by night, to see Wren.
- Lisska scouts the roads. The camps on the drove road are emptying into one: a great camp at the Ram's Head, halfway up the Drover's Way to Varrhold, where the cart road from Cotter's Cross comes in. Thousands. Companies coming up from Cotter's Cross with carts and bound beasts. More every day.
- Day 109: Hallam Brecc and Morrow come to Crow Scar, the first time a bonded pair has left the Roosts since the ford. Hallam says the Clan is still killing itself: Arn's riders burned a Skarrow steading two nights ago, Ulric's burned Teo's ford-house. "Neither of them has turned his head to look south." He's come to see for himself what's on the road.
- Aurveth notices it, on day 110, looking down at the Ram's Head from the tops: every road the Restoration is using bends toward the river, and comes down to it opposite the island. Harrow's End is the last village on the way. "They're not coming for the Marches. They're coming through it."
- Day 111: the meeting in the burned shell of the Wheel at Harrow's End. Fisk, Wat Garrow, the Ashby widow, the Hollins. Who's staying, who's going. Liss Ashdown's ferry is still taking people over from the Saltreach jetty at Tolly's Staithe whenever the ice lets her. Nobody decides. (Sets up 62 and 64.)
- Night 112: Wren at the paddock gate with Old Grey: "You're always going." "I know." "Take me." "No." She asks him whether the lion-woman is in trouble too. He says he doesn't know. He thinks: two hundred silver, the same as me.

## Chapter 55: Thaw

POV: Maren
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: The river goes out all at once, with a noise like a city wall coming down. The lion lets Maren close enough to touch. No bond, no words, but it lets her stay.
Echo of the other's story: The Restoration offers a reward for the dragon farmer.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 29: the hinged collar, the pin hammered over; she came at its neck with iron and it read her as a hunter; the hammer and chisel in her chest. From Chapter 53: the pit at the north end, the old grey stone, the lion scraping; Pell's patrol at Redwatch; the report that says only "it digs". From Chapter 41: the Haddow crossing; Ottery and the long pole. From Chapter 50 (Calder's side): the crossed-hammer stamp under the six. From Chapter 31: Odo Marl's smithy in Millbrook; Tam.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and thirteen to one hundred and twenty. Decided here (proposals):
- There are no cannon in this world, so the break-up is described in its own terms. The river people say *the river's going out*. It sounds like a city wall coming down, a mile long, all at once.
- Days 113–117: she goes to the island every dusk with Ottery. The lion is always in the pit, scraping. On day 115 she brings the hammer and chisel and leaves them, wrapped, on the stone at the edge of the pit, and doesn't touch them. It smells them, a long time, with its lip back. It doesn't come at her.
- Day 117, full moon: a south wind, and rain. The ice goes grey. Ottery says three days, maybe two.
- Dusk on day 119: Ottery says no. She goes anyway. He goes with her, cursing, as far as the island's reeds. While she's in the pit the river goes out. The ice breaks from upriver down, past the island, in one long roar, and heaves, and the floes go grinding past both shores. Ottery gets back across the west channel just before it goes and is cut off on the Hearthmere side. She's on the island for the night.
- The night: rain, the floes grinding and booming in the dark on both sides. She sits in the arch of the tower. Toward midnight the lion comes out of the pit and lies down in front of the arch, five paces off. Then closer. She puts out her hand and lays it on the mane at the shoulder. It lets her. She leaves her hand there. It's warm and coarse and wet, and under it she feels the ground humming. She stays. It stays.
- Dawn, day 120: she unwraps the hammer and chisel and lays them on the stone in front of it and waits. It looks at them. Then it lowers its head and lays its neck along the ground beside her. She sets the chisel to the pin and strikes. Three blows. The pin comes out. The hinged collar falls open. The lion stands up and shakes, and the ground shakes with it, and it walks away down to the water and drinks.
- On the inside of the collar, under the six: a crossed-hammer stamp. She knows it. It's on every horseshoe and pot-hook in Millbrook. Odo Marl's mark. Tam's master.
- Ottery comes for her at noon through an open lead in the punt. He sees the lion on the shore without the collar. Says nothing all the way back to Redwatch.
- The echo: at Redwatch, Pell has the paper the morning rider brought from Millbrook. Not the old wanted bill. A new one, from the Restoration, nailed at the Millbrook jetty overnight: two hundred silver for CALDER FENN, THE FARMER, ENEMY OF THE ONE REALM, alive, for the burning of the camp at the Ewe Beck and the murder of its keepers. "They've turned on him," says Pell. "Their own sign."
- She tells Pell about the collar. That's the truth. She still doesn't tell him about the stone.

## Chapter 56: Wren and the Far-Seer

POV: Calder
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Lisska perches on the crag above the farm and tells Calder what she sees across the river, and Wren makes him repeat every word. Calder realises his daughter is safer anywhere he isn't.
Echo of the other's story: Lisska reports the knight-student touching the beast's mane.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 54: Calder living on Crow Scar, coming down to Upper Rush by night; Lisska's crag above Bel's farm; Wren at the paddock gate with Old Grey; "Is the lion-woman in trouble too?" "Then somebody should tell her." From Chapter 55 (Maren's side): the river goes out on night 119; Maren stranded on the island; the touch; the collar struck off at dawn on day 120. From Chapter 24: Lisska's far sight; she saw the woman at the stream in the summer.
Draft notes: Night one hundred and nineteen to day one hundred and twenty-two. Decided here (proposals):
- Lisska's eyes: she can see a sparrow on a roof at a league, a face at two. From the crag above Upper Rush the island is two leagues and more off, across the river. She can see the arch, and the lantern in it. She can't see faces from there in the dark, but she can see shapes.
- Night 119: Calder is at Upper Rush when the river goes out. The noise wakes the whole house. Wren won't go back to bed. He takes her up the crag path to where Lisska sits, wrapped in his coat. Lisska tells him what she sees and Wren makes him say every word aloud, and then say it again.
- What Lisska sees: the ice going, a man on the reeds (Ottery) who gets off it in time; a lantern in the arch of the tower on the island; the woman sitting in it; the lion lying down in front of her, ten paces, then five, then with its head on the step. "She's put her hand on it. On its mane. On its neck. It's letting her." Wren: "Say it again."
- Near midnight Lisska also sees, on the forest path from the east, four men with a hooded lantern, coming down toward Upper Rush the way Calder comes every night. They've been watching his path. Kharrak goes and they run. One drops a coil of rope and a sack. Calder knows what the sack is for.
- Dawn, day 120: Lisska sees the woman kneel with iron in front of the lion, and the lion put its head down, and the collar come off. Wren is asleep against Calder by then and doesn't hear it. Calder tells her at breakfast, every word, and she makes him say it twice.
- Calder understands it then, on the crag at dawn with Wren asleep against him: they came down his path. If he weren't there, they'd have no path. He tells Ada that day: when the ferry runs, Wren goes over. Ada says yes. Bel won't decide.
- Day 121: Old Grey turns his head toward Wren's voice at the paddock gate. Only that. Wren tells everyone.
- Day 122: Calder goes up to Crow Scar and doesn't come down. He sends Lisska each night to sit on the crag over the house instead. Wren asks Lisska to tell him what the lion-woman is doing. Lisska does.

## Chapter 57: Tobin

POV: Maren
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Maren finds Tobin at a Restoration hedge-meeting. He begs her to join them. She hears the leader speak and he is warm, not precise; she tells herself it's the crowd.
Echo of the other's story: The speaker promises the dragon farmer will "come home to the one realm."
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 47: Tobin's blue-corded practice sword and note ("You know it's true. Come and find us. T."); Odo's smithy shut; Tam gone. From Chapter 55: Odo Marl's crossed hammers inside the lion's collar; the Restoration's bill for Calder Fenn, "enemy of the one realm". From Chapters 5 and 33: the thin man at the roadblock and on the smithy roof at Ferriby: long pale face, short dark hair, grey eyes; still, precise, never raises his voice; he knew her name. From Chapter 31: Tam is her brother, Liss's son, apprenticed to Odo; Absalom Reede; the iron rings "when you stand up". From Chapter 34 (Calder's side): Vale on the Tidesteps, warm, three thousand people.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and twenty-one to one hundred and twenty-four. Decided here (proposals):
- Day 121: Pell sends her back to Greyhold with the report about the collar. She rides on past Greyhold to Millbrook. Odo's forge is cold, the doors chained (by the crown's men). She looks at the hinges on the forge door: crossed hammers.
- Day 122: a boy at the Millbrook jetty pushes a scrap of paper into her hand and runs. It's tied with blue cord. *The long hedge above Fennick's top field. Moonrise tomorrow. Come and hear him. T.*
- She tells nobody. She goes on the night of day 123, on foot, in a plain cloak, from Greyhold after the last bell, over the wall by the midden the way the grooms do.
- The hedge-meeting: a long field under a thorn hedge above Fennick's farm, a league inland from Millbrook. Six or seven hundred people. Lanterns on poles along the hedge. A hay-wain drawn across the top of the field for a platform. No masks. Men with rings at the gates and along the hedge with cudgels.
- Tobin finds her at the edge of the crowd. He's in grey, with a ring on his finger, and he's thinner and happier than she's ever seen him. "You came." He begs her: "You know. Hale told you, didn't he? I can see it on you. Come over. They want you. You don't know how much they want you."
- On the wain: Tam, with a torch, at the front corner. He sees her. He doesn't come down. Odo Marl beside him, huge, bald, with his arms folded.
- The speaker: Vale. The same face as the roadblock and the smithy roof. She knows it at once. But he laughs. He jumps down off the wain into the crowd. He takes people's hands. He calls an old woman *mother*. He raises his voice and it breaks with feeling. He looks straight at Maren in the lantern light with nothing in his eyes but friendliness, and doesn't know her. The thin man on the smithy roof knew her name. She tells herself: the crowd. Seven hundred faces. He sees a thousand a night.
- The speech: the chained beast under Greyhold; the lamps; "every one of you chosen"; and the farmer. "They'll tell you he burned our camp at the Ewe Beck. He did. Three of ours are dead. And his sister's man is dead under a beast in our lines, because a fool of a handler thought a beast was worth more than a grieving man. I'll not have it. I forgive him. Hear me: I forgive Calder Fenn. He'll come home to the one realm. All of them will. Every one." The crowd roars it. Maren thinks of the bill on the Millbrook jetty: *to be brought in alive.*
- Maren to Tobin, at the end: "Your people put the collar on it." She shows him nothing; she says it. He doesn't understand. She goes.
- Tobin, after her: "When they throw you out, and they will, come and find us. We'll still be here." She doesn't look back. On the wain, Tam watches her go.

## Chapter 58: The Stranger

POV: Calder
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Calder sees Rook again, the quiet man from Ulric's hall, among Restoration men. He starts to wonder about Teo's death, and has no proof at all.
Echo of the other's story: From the heights he sees Greyhold's walls being raised and armed.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: Calder recognises Rook by his forgettable face, soft clerk's hands with a pale scar across the knuckles, Wendmouth boots, and the six-dot mark on his knife (Chapter 10). He realises Rook was sitting three paces from the foot of the cliff path when Teo turned and said the time and place of the ford out loud. From Chapters 17, 21 and 24: Rook was with the island hunters (Maren noted only his boots with brass eyelets) and crossed to the farmer when word of the bonds reached the far bank. Calder has told the dragons about the man on the bucket; Kharrak: "Then we'll find out. All of them." From Chapter 48: he nearly said it at Varrhold and at Skarrow, and didn't. From Chapter 54: the Ram's Head camp; Hallam Brecc; the road to the island; Brask of Delling's company. From Chapter 34: Vale on the Tidesteps.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and twenty-three to one hundred and twenty-six. Decided here (proposals):
- Day 123: Hallam Brecc comes to the Scar again and asks Calder for a count. Nobody from the Clan has been near the Ram's Head. Hallam will carry whatever Calder sees to both Varrs himself.
- Night 124: Calder goes to the Ram's Head on foot, alone, with a winter's beard and Joss's old carter's coat and a whip, among the carts coming up the road from Cotter's Cross. The dragons wait a league off in the Fellwood. Nobody looks at one more carter.
- What he sees: the town of tents; the beast lines, a quarter-mile of them; companies drilling by torchlight; Delling's company's flag; a cart of new collars with the six on them, and on the inside of one, when he turns it over, the crossed hammers.
- Rook: in the inn yard, under a lantern, with a ledger, paying out silver to the captains of the companies from a chest. The same face. The soft hands, the white scar across the knuckles. The brass-eyeleted boots. The knife at his belt, the ring of six on the pommel. A sellsword says something and Rook laughs: "You would. Truly. I'm hopeless."
- Rook goes into the inn's back room. Through the window: a man sitting at a table writing, very still, with a long pale face. It looks like the man from the Tidesteps. It doesn't move like him. Calder can't make it fit and doesn't try. (He hasn't put the twins together. This is for the reader.)
- Rook comes out again and walks past the cart where Calder stands, close enough to touch. He stops at Calder's carthorse and says to the carter beside him, "Good beast. Look after her." He doesn't look at Calder. Calder can't breathe.
- What Calder works out walking back: Rook was three paces from the cliff path when Teo said the ford, and the hour, out loud. Rook was gone before light on the morning of the ford. Rook pays the Restoration's swords. The killing at the ford started the war, and the war has fed the camps all winter. It fits. All of it. And none of it is proof. He has a face, a bucket, a laugh, and a man paying silver in a yard.
- Kharrak: *Let me take him. Tonight. Out of that yard.* Calder: "And then there's a dead clerk at the Ram's Head and a camp of five thousand coming up the road after us, and Arn says I killed one of Ulric's men, and Ulric says the same." He wants Rook alive, in front of both Varrs, at once.
- The echo: day 126, Aurveth takes him high over the river to look at the island and the road, and he sees Greyhold from above for the first time: scaffolds on the river wall; new timber hoardings going up on the towers; carts of stone in the yard; great throwing-engines on the river towers, their arms turned toward the Saltreach bank. Knights drilling in the yard in hundreds.
- He decides: he'll bring them both to the ford. Teo's ford. Where it started. Hallam will carry the word. He'll say it to both their faces, at once, in the same breath, so neither can hear it alone.

## Chapter 59: Orders

POV: Maren
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Knight-Captain Rowan orders Maren to lure the lion into a trap so the Restoration can't take it. She refuses.
Echo of the other's story: The order also covers "the farmer with the dragons, if he crosses."
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 53: Rowan's room at the top of the north tower; "You'll not go further than you've been"; the report that says only "it digs". From Chapter 55: the collar struck off "at the beast's leave"; Pell's "Is there anything else?" From Chapter 51: Thorne in the archive: "Then we'd better start deciding what to do about it." From Chapter 57: the hedge-meeting; Odo's hammers; Vale's "I forgive Calder Fenn". From Chapter 54 (Calder's side): two hundred silver for "the knight with the lion".
Draft notes: Days one hundred and twenty-four to one hundred and twenty-eight. Decided here (proposals):
- Days 124–125: back on Pell's patrol at Redwatch. The river's high and full of floes; Ottery takes her over in the punt only twice, at first light, when the night frost has slowed the melt and the floes run thinner. The lion is in the pit, which is flooded to the stone now. It stands in the water and scrapes. Since the collar came off it lets her come to five paces. Once, she lays her hand on its foreleg. It lets her.
- Day 127: Rowan sends for her. Thorne is in the room, by the fire, with the ledger.
- Rowan is inner circle but not Watch: she was told the secret as Captain of Greyhold, when she took the school. She knows what's under the hill. She knows Maren knows. "Hale's a bad liar, and you're a worse one."
