# Decisions while drafting Chapters 61–70

*Historical record; merged into the Bible. The chapter notes and the Bible are current.*

Extracted from each chapter's notes block. Items marked (proposals) are the drafter's calls for the author to confirm. Later rulings: see Rulings_41-79.md.

## Chapter 61: Lucan

POV: Maren
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Haverly warns Maren that Rowan will take the lion without her. They talk properly for the first time. He chooses his orders; she understands why.
Echo of the other's story: Haverly says the Clan's ford meeting ended in blood again.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 59: Rowan's trap (a pit-net and the crown's beast-chain on the island's west shore, a drugged carcass, forty knights in the reeds, Thorne's boar and the greyharts); three refusals; Maren confined to the midden, the barn, the refectory and the dormitory; the gate told to stop her at the midden wall. From Chapter 60 (Calder's side): Greyhold's boats sat at anchor across the mouth of Hollow Beck at dusk on day 130 and watched the ford's dead come down; Hallam Brecc killed. From Chapters 11, 23 and 47: Lucan Haverly, passed his trial, grey with the silver pin; Kesh, bonded since she was a fledgling six years ago; Kesh goes to pieces when he's on the gate.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and thirty-one and one hundred and thirty-two. Decided here (proposals):
- Night 131: Haverly comes to the Long Barn after the last bell, where Maren is on the late muck. He was in the boats at the mouth of Hollow Beck. He tells her what came down the beck: men in Clan grey with red thread and without, horses, and an old man's stick. "It ended in blood again. The ford. Whatever the farmer did up there, it ended like the first time."
- Then the warning: the trap is set for the night of day 135, at dusk, the first night the river's low enough. Rowan's sending Callis and Wynn's hounds to drive it from the north end toward the west shore and the net. "She'll do it without you. She told the captains you'd made it worse."
- They talk properly for the first time. Haverly's father and grandfather were knights of the gate at Hallowmere; he was chosen at fifteen on his own trial day, the first time he held out his fist; he's been told all his life he deserves what he has. He's been thinking since the ford's dead came past his boat that maybe he doesn't. And still: he'll be in the reeds on night 135.
- Why: "Because if I'm not, somebody worse is. Because the orders are the only thing that makes the grey anything but a coat for people like me. If the bonded at the front start choosing which orders to keep, then we're just the front, and the people at the back are right about us." And: "I'm not brave enough to be you, Ashdown. I'm brave enough to be in the reeds."
- She understands. She doesn't agree.
- He leaves the Long Barn door unbarred on the midden side. He doesn't say so. She sees him not bar it.
- Day 132: Kesh, at the noon bell, screams and goes up off his fist and won't come down for an hour. He stands in the yard with his arm out the whole time. Maren watches him not move.

## Chapter 62: Before the Storm

POV: Calder
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Harrow's End prepares to flee. Calder stays near the Old Ruin, where the answers might be, and because the Restoration is hunting him: every league between him and Wren keeps her safer.
Echo of the other's story: Smoke from Greyhold's forges; the knights are arming.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 60: Hallam Brecc dead at the ford; Morrow lying down beside him; the ten of the ledges on the hillside; Hallam's charge: "Tell them. Again. Write it down if you have to. Nail it on a door." The iron ring from the dead shooter; the two chains. From Chapter 54: the meeting in the Wheel; Fisk: Liss Ashdown will take them. From Chapter 56: Wren and Ada go over when the ferry runs; Bel undecided; Old Grey turned his head. From Chapter 58: the Ram's Head muster; Greyhold's hoardings and engines. From Chapter 28: Aurveth's memory of the white bridge, and the great grey cat on the tower steps that her first partner bowed to. From Chapter 46: Ada's third verse, "When two hands open what one hand shut".
Draft notes: Days one hundred and thirty-one to one hundred and thirty-four. Decided here (proposals):
- Day 131: Calder goes back to the ford. Hallam's stick is gone; the beck has it. The ten of the ledges have kept the vigil on the hillside all night. Morrow dies at dusk on day 132, the third day, with her head on Hallam's chest. The ledges carry them both to Coldtarn. One of the ten, **Gunna Tarne**, an old rider with a grey-green dragon called **Tamsin**, tells Calder the ledges believe him now. They saw the shooters' boots. They'll carry his words.
- Day 132, night: Ada writes it down for him at Bel's table, on the clean backs of the Restoration's papers. She learned her letters as a girl at the Varrhold dues-school. Everything: the bucket, the six, the ford, the ledger at the Ram's Head, the gorse, the boots, the ring. Two copies. He signs each in crooked capitals, and Ada writes beside it. The ledges carry one to Varrhold and one to Skarrow. He keeps the ring and the chains. Hallam said nail it on a door; Ada says no, in the Clan you say it aloud, so Gunna Tarne will read it in both yards, under the law, so nobody can say it was hidden.
- Days 132–134: Harrow's End packs. The Ram's Head tents start coming down on day 133; Lisska sees the first companies moving onto the Drover's Way. The village goes south down the river lane to Tolly's Staithe, to Liss Ashdown's ferry, in twos and threes. Fisk won't go. "Sixty years on this weir."
- Bel decides on day 134. She'll cross with the children from the Staithe, and Liss will take the greys over too, one a crossing: Mabbin, Sorrel, Old Grey. "Joss went to bring him home. Home's where I am." The crossing is set for day 137.
- Calder won't cross and won't go north. He stays on Crow Scar, near the Ruin, for two reasons he says aloud to Aurveth: the answer is on the island (the verse, Ada's third verse, the cat on the tower steps), and every league between him and Wren is a league the men with sacks have to come past him.
- Aurveth on the cat: the lion is the cat she remembers on the tower steps. Older than her first partner. Her first partner bowed to it. "It was there before the chains. It was there when there was a bridge. It has been waiting a long time for something."
- The echo: from the top of the Scar on day 134, black smoke over Greyhold, going straight up in the still air from a dozen chimneys: the forges working day and night. Lisska goes close enough to see: smiths in the yard at open forges, arrowheads by the barrel, a ring of red-hot iron being hammered for a new chain.

## Chapter 63: The Net

POV: Maren
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Maren breaks the knightly oath to protect the lion and is cast out of Greyhold.
Echo of the other's story: Word arrives that the Restoration's leader is gathering forces at the Old Ruin.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 61: the trap is set for dusk on day 135; Callis with three hounds drives from the north end; forty knights in the west reeds; Kesh to mark; Thorne's boar at the south end; the small door on the midden side of the Long Barn left unbarred. From Chapter 59: three refusals; confined; the gate told to stop her. From Chapter 55: the collar, the scale, the hammer and chisel and Tobin's blue-corded sword in the chest under her cot. From Chapter 49: "and do not ask" won't come out of her mouth. From Chapter 9 (Pell's lesson): subdue, don't kill.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and thirty-three to one hundred and thirty-six. Decided here (proposals):
- Night 134: she goes out through the unbarred door and over the midden wall with Tobin's practice sword and the hammer and chisel in a sack. She walks the River Road three leagues upriver to Redwatch in the dark. Ottery is in the boathouse. He looks at her a long time and gives her the punt. "I never saw you." She's on the island before first light on day 135.
- Day 135: she spends it with the lion at the pit. It lets her sit against its shoulder. She can't tell it anything. She can only be there.
- Dusk, day 135: Callis's hounds come in at the north end, three of them, baying, with Callis and two knights on foot behind. The lion stands. Maren stands in front of it. The hounds won't come past her (she's mucked them out since the autumn; they know her smell). Callis orders them on. They won't. He lifts his crossbow at the lion.
- The oath-breaking: she strikes Callis. With Tobin's blunt sword, across the forearm, the way Pell taught them (subdue, don't kill), and the bolt goes into the oaks. A student raising a weapon against a knight of the orders in the field is the oath broken, whatever else. Then she runs down to the east shore and the lion follows her, and at the water's edge she turns and points across the east channel, and it goes into the river. It doesn't understand her. It goes anyway. It swims the east channel and comes out on the Saltreach shore below Harrow's End and goes into the Fellwood.
- Kesh marks it. Maren sees the falcon go over high above the lion, and hover, and then turn away west toward the reeds, toward Haverly, as if she'd lost it. She hadn't. Haverly lets it go. Maren doesn't know whether he did.
- Thorne takes Maren in the east reeds. She doesn't fight.
- Morning, day 136, the river gate: Rowan in front of the school. "Maren Ashdown raised her hand against a knight of the orders in the field. That is the oath broken. She is no longer of Greyhold." Her grey is taken. Grieve brings her chest out of the dormitory and puts it on the cobbles by the gate himself, without a word. Hale stands at the top of the archive stair. Haverly's in the front line with Kesh. The gate shuts behind her.
- The echo: as she stands outside the gate with her chest, a rider comes in hard from Redwatch past her: boats coming down the Drover's Way on carts, the Restoration's banner at the Ram's Head, and Vale himself at the head of the column (Fisk's boy rowed over to tell Ottery). "They're gathering for the Old Ruin. Three days. Four at most." The gate opens for him and shuts again.
- She sits on her chest outside the gate. Then she picks it up and walks down the River Road toward the ferry, toward Liss.

## Chapter 64: The Staithe

POV: Calder
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Ada, Wren and Bel's family cross to Hearthmere as refugees on Liss Ashdown's ferry. Calder watches from the bank. Wren tells the ferrywoman about her father's dragons.
Echo of the other's story: On the Saltreach jetty, while they board, Liss says her daughter was cast out of the knights yesterday for loving a monster.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 3: the ferry runs on an old guide-rope from the stone jetty and the red-doored ferry house at the Greyhold landing to the timber jetty at Tolly's Staithe; a long flat-bottomed boat, broad as a cart; Liss knows the Fenns by name from the trade. From Chapter 62: Bel's decision; three crossings for the greys; Calder stays. From Chapter 56: "Tell her I said hello." From Chapter 54: Brask: "two hundred for her and two hundred for you, like you were a pair". From Chapter 38: Wren tied her washed blue ribbon on Lisska's horn. From Chapter 63 (Maren's side): Maren cast out on the morning of day 136.
Draft notes: Day one hundred and thirty-seven. Decided here (proposals):
- They come down the river lane from Upper Rush through the night, six leagues, and reach the Staithe at first grey: Bel with the handcart and the children, Ada, Wren, and the three greys on halters. Calder walks with them as far as the Staithe in Joss's carter's coat. Lisska keeps watch above, high, out of sight of the Greyhold towers. Kharrak and Aurveth stay on the Scar.
- The Staithe is nearly empty: old Tolly, Greta with her eel-smoke, a few families waiting. Greyhold on its hill across the water, with its scaffolds and the smoke from its forges.
- Liss brings the ferry over on the rope at first light. Three crossings for the beasts, the people with the first. Liss looks at Calder once, a long look, and knows him from the trading houses' bill and from twenty years of buying Fenn barley. She says nothing about it.
- On the jetty, while they load the first crossing, Wren tells Liss about Lisska and Kharrak and Aurveth, everything, the way she's heard it, and that her father talks to them out loud and they answer. Liss listens to all of it.
- The echo: Liss, coiling the bow-line, quietly, not to anyone: her daughter came to the ferry house last night with a chest on her back, down the landing steps from the school. Cast out of the knights yesterday. "For loving a monster, they're saying in Millbrook. She'd say it was for not being able to say a thing she didn't mean." Calder understands who she is.
- He asks Liss, on the last crossing, to tell her daughter something. Not his message. Wren's: "Tell her a girl called Wren says hello." Liss says she will. Then Liss, at the rope: "And you? Anything from you?" He can't think of anything to say. "Tell her the old one says it's the cat from the bridge." Liss doesn't understand. She says she'll tell her.
- Wren on the ferry, last sight: she stands at the rail and waves, not to him, up, to the sky where Lisska is. Lisska comes down low over the river, against orders, once, and goes up again. Liss watches her go over with her mouth open. The blue ribbon is still on her horn.
- Calder stands on the Staithe jetty until the ferry reaches the stone jetty at the Greyhold landing. Then he goes back north.

## Chapter 65: The Red Door

POV: Maren
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Maren with Tam in Millbrook, then alone on the island near the lion. Liss tells her about the Saltreach refugees she carried, and a girl called Wren.
Echo of the other's story: Wren's story: her father talks to dragons out loud and they answer.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 63: cast out at the river gate on the morning of day 136; her chest (the hammer and chisel, the collar in two halves, the scale, her father's brown cloak); the lion swam the east channel and went into the Fellwood; the Restoration is three or four days from the Old Ruin. From Chapter 64 (Calder's side): Liss carried Ada, Wren, Bel and her children and the three greys on day 137; Wren's account of the dragons; Calder's two messages: "A girl called Wren says hello" and "the old one says it's the cat from the bridge"; Lisska went low over the river with the blue ribbon on her horn. From Chapter 57: Tam on the hedge-wain with the torch; Odo Marl. From Chapter 3: the ferry house with the red door, where Maren was born.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and thirty-six to one hundred and thirty-nine. Decided here (proposals):
- Night 136: the ferry house. Liss doesn't ask. Maren sits on the floor by the fire all night with the scale in her lap.
- Day 137: Liss makes four crossings for the Fenns and their greys (the people, then one beast at a time) and is done after noon. That evening she tells Maren about the refugees she's carried since the autumn, and the girl called Wren, and Wren's story, twice: her father talks to dragons out loud and they answer. Then the green dragon, low, with a blue ribbon on its horn. Then the two messages. Maren holds the scale. She knows now whose it is. "The cat from the bridge" means nothing to either of them.
- Ada and Wren and Bel's family go on to the refugee camp at Millbrook, below the town. Maren doesn't go to see them. She thinks about it.
- Day 138: Millbrook, looking for Tam. The town is tight as a drum. Odo's forge still chained. She finds Tam in the Kettle's yard, splitting kindling where Davin used to. She puts the collar on the block in front of him, inside up, with Odo's hammers showing. Tam knows the mark. Odo told him the collars were for the camps, for beasts "brought in to be cared for". Tam goes white. He won't come home. He tells her, not meaning to, that when the Ram's Head army crosses to the island, Millbrook will rise, and the barns will open, and the knights will be caught between. Vale has promised that no Millbrook house will burn.
- Night 138: Liss's skiff, upriver alone in the dark, past Greyhold's lights and Redwatch's, to the island. The lion has come back to the old place. It's at the pit. She sits with it, against its shoulder, all of day 139.
- She tells it about Wren. Out loud, twice, though it can't understand. A girl on the far bank whose father talks to dragons.
- Dusk, day 139: from the island's east shore, across the channel, she sees fires at Harrow's End and carts coming down the hill behind it, and boats being run down to the water at the Eelweir. They'll come in the morning. Before first light on day 140 she takes the skiff back across the west channel and the lion swims after her, and they go west over the fields toward the Brackenwood.

## Chapter 66: The Ruin

POV: Calder
Part: Part Five: Thaw
Beats: Calder leaves the dragons in the Scarfell and goes to the Old Ruin alone to find the answer in the verse. The Restoration arrives in force, and he hides in the tower.
Echo of the other's story: On the island, signs that the knight and the lion left only hours ago.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 62: Calder stays near the Ruin; "the answer's there"; Aurveth: the lion is the cat on the tower steps; Fisk: "I'll be in the reeds with a boat. I know every channel round that island." From Chapter 42: the arch and its verse (Aurveth read the first line through him, *Here the road of the one realm crossed the water*, and stopped); the spit; the lion's prints. From Chapter 46: Ada's third verse. From Chapter 53 (Maren's side): the pit at the north end down to dressed grey stone. From Chapter 65 (Maren's side): Maren and the lion left the island across the west channel before first light on day 140.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and thirty-nine and one hundred and forty. Decided here (proposals):
- Day 139: Calder sends the three north to the old hatching caves above Coldtarn, out of sight. If they're seen over the island the whole Ram's Head comes down on it at once, with nets and smoke, and he can't let them be taken. Kharrak refuses; Aurveth makes him go. Lisska cries. The feeling will carry; the words won't, not at that distance.
- He can't read the old script. He takes paper and a stick of charcoal to take a rubbing of the verse under the arch, the way farmers take a rubbing of a boundary stone in a dispute, to bring back to Aurveth.
- Night 139–140: Fisk rows him over from the Eelweir in the dark, round the north of the island by a back channel, while the Restoration is running its boats down onto the landing below. Fisk lands him on the island's north shore and goes back into the reeds.
- The signs (the echo): at the pit, a long pressed hollow on the bank still warm, and beside it a smaller one, a person's, where someone sat against the lion. Down at the west shore, a skiff's keel-mark in the mud, fresh; a woman's boot-prints going down to it; the lion's great prints going into the water beside them, the edges still sharp and wet. Hours. Not more.
- At the arch he takes the rubbing, by touch, in the first grey. Then, inside the tower, in its ground floor, he finds a stair going down, choked with fallen stone and old earth, and at the bottom of what he can see of it, a slab of grey stone set in the floor with an iron plate in it as big as a man's hand, and a keyhole. He doesn't know what it is.
- The boats come across the east channel at first light. Hundreds of men. He hides in the tower: up the broken stair inside the wall, to the upper chamber where the floor is half gone, under a fallen beam. From there he watches the Restoration take the island: the fort, the arch, cages, ladders, smoke, men with honey-smoke pots going north to the pit with chains, and finding nothing there.
- He stays hidden all day 140 with the rubbing inside his shirt, and the chains, and Joss's knife.

## Chapter 67: Muster

POV: Maren
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: The Restoration gathers on the island and along the Saltreach bank: boats, ladders, cages. Greyhold arms and shuts its gate. Outcast, Maren watches from the Brackenwood.
Echo of the other's story: The Old Ruin is full of fires and men.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 65: Maren and the lion left the island before first light on day 140 and went west over the fields toward the Brackenwood; Tam: when the Ram's Head army crosses to the island the knights will go up to Redwatch to hold the bank, and Millbrook will rise behind them; Liss, Ada, Wren, Bel and the children at Millbrook (the refugee camp by the water-meadows). From Chapter 63: the gate; Grieve and the chest. From Chapter 62 (Calder's side): Greyhold's forges; the great chain.
Draft notes: Days one hundred and forty and one hundred and forty-one. Decided here (proposals):
- The Brackenwood's eastern edge stands on a long rise, Hollin Knowe, two leagues west of the river. From the old beacon-stones on the top of the knowe you can see the whole reach: Redwatch, the island, Harrow's End on the far side, and downriver the smoke over Greyhold.
- The lion doesn't want to be there. It paces the edge of the trees all of day 140, looking east toward the island, and twice starts down the slope and stops. It doesn't go. It comes back and lies down where she is. She doesn't know why it stays. She doesn't ask it to.
- It hunts at night and brings back nothing; she eats the bread from her sack and drinks from a spring under the beacon-stones.
- What she sees on day 140: the island full of boats from the Eelweir; smoke in the fort; men on both shores. Cages going over on rafts. On the Saltreach bank, the hill behind Harrow's End black with tents. Ladders, long ones, being carried down to the west shore of the island. Boats drawn up there, facing Hearthmere.
- Day 141: Greyhold's gate opens at dawn and the orders ride out up the River Road toward Redwatch: two hundred knights and more, greyharts, hounds, Thorne's boar in a wagon-cage, the great new chain under canvas on a dray-wain. The gate shuts behind them. She sees, from the knowe, that Greyhold's left with a handful on its wall. Exactly what Tam said.
- She thinks about going down to warn them. She thinks about what they'd do with an outcast who came out of the Brackenwood with a lion. She thinks about Millbrook, and Liss, and a girl called Wren in a camp by the water-meadows. She doesn't know which way to go.
- The echo: at dusk on day 141, the island is full of fires. Every part of it, the fort and the shores and the oaks, red points in the dark, so many that the island looks like a coal. The lion stands on the beacon-stones and looks at it and makes a sound she's never heard: low, long, broken, like a question nobody answers.

## Chapter 68: Under the Arch

POV: Calder
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: At the Old Ruin, right before the battle, the twins are revealed: the leader in two places was always two men. Hidden, Calder overhears them speak of the killing they arranged in the Dragon Clan. Rook is with them.
Echo of the other's story: They talk of "the knight's lock" beneath the school, without saying what it opens.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: Rook is with the twins. Calder's overhearing confirms what Chapters 10 and 58 suggested: Rook killed Teo at the ford. From Chapter 66: Calder hidden under the fallen beam in the half-floor of the tower, above the room with the choked stair and the slab with the iron lock-plate; two guards at the tower door; the still man crouching with his hand in the lion's hollow. From Chapter 58: the still man in the back room at the Ram's Head, and Rook reporting to him; Vale laughing on the Tidesteps. From Chapter 60: the gorse, Hallam. From Chapter 33 (Maren's side): the still man on the smithy roof at Ferriby.
Draft notes: Night one hundred and forty-one. Decided here (proposals):
- Calder has been under the beam since the morning of day 140. He has water from the rain that comes in through the broken roof, and nothing else.
- The second man comes over from the Saltreach bank at dusk on day 141, in a boat with lanterns, with a crowd at the Eelweir cheering him off: Vale, laughing, on his feet in the bow. He comes up to the fort to the arch, where the still man is waiting under the verse. They stand together under the arch, in the firelight, a pace apart. The same face. One still, one moving.
- Their names, as they say them to each other: **Corvin** (the still one) and **Cassian** (the warm one). The leader in two places was always two men.
- Rook comes up from the spit to report. They stand in the arch below Calder's crack in the wall and talk. What Calder hears:
  - Corvin: the knights have come up to Redwatch, all of them, with the chain; Greyhold has a handful on its wall; Millbrook's ready.
  - Cassian on the farmer: "I'd still rather have him than burn him." Corvin: "We'll have the dragons either way. A man's a halter."
  - The Clan. Rook on the second ford: the law held the bonded long enough. Cassian, uneasy: "The old man." Rook: "He came down. Nobody thought he would." Corvin: "It did what it was for. The Clan won't stand together again this side of the summer."
  - The first ford. Cassian asks, quietly, as if he's never asked: "The boy. The first one. Varr's son." Rook: "Went in the water like a stone. He never drew. He thought I'd come to hold his horse." Corvin: "Leave the armband this time?" Rook: "On the alders. Plain grey. And the axe in the shallows." Cassian turns away.
  - The echo: Corvin says that tomorrow (day 142) Millbrook rises and Cassian takes Greyhold's gate, and the night after (143) Cassian goes down "under the school", "to the knight's lock." He'll stay here with this one (he taps the floor of the tower with his foot). "At the hour. Not before. Not after." Cassian: "Two hands." Neither says what the locks open.
- Calder lies above them with his face to the crack and hears Teo's death told like a cart-load delivered. He doesn't move. He holds Joss's knife so hard his hand goes dead.
- After, Cassian goes back to the boat to cross downriver to the Hearthmere side in the dark (toward Millbrook). Corvin stays under the arch. Rook sits down on the step of the tower door, below Calder, and whittles.
- Calder, at the end: he could drop on Rook from the stair. He doesn't. He has it now: not proof, but the words, out of their own mouths. He needs someone to hear them who isn't him.

## Chapter 69: The Gate

POV: Maren
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: The Restoration's first open war against the kingdoms breaks out: it crosses the river and drives toward Greyhold and the catacombs. Pell dies holding the gate.
Echo of the other's story: Rumours from the Saltreach side: the dragon farmer has gone to the Old Ruin.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 67: the orders on the bank at Redwatch facing the ladders; fewer than forty left on Greyhold's wall; Millbrook ready to rise behind them; Liss, Ada, Wren, Bel and her children at Millbrook (the camp by the water-meadows). From Chapter 68 (Calder's side): Cassian crossed to the Hearthmere side in the night to lead the rising and take Greyhold's gate; "our friend" is inside. From Chapter 9: Pell, long-boned, grey, two fingers gone; subdue, don't kill. From Chapter 63: Thorne took Tobin's sword; Maren has no weapon.
Draft notes: Day one hundred and forty-two. Decided here (proposals):
- Dawn: from Hollin Knowe Maren sees both at once. Upriver, the boats come off the island's west shore with the ladders, and the bank at Redwatch goes up in noise. Downriver, smoke over Millbrook: the rising.
- The lion won't come down off the knowe. It stands on the edge looking at the island. She leaves it there. It's the first time she goes somewhere it doesn't follow.
- She takes a farm cob from the paddock at Hollin Farm under the knowe and rides south across the fields to the River Road. The road is full of people running north toward Greyhold from Millbrook: the camp by the water-meadows was fired at dawn. The rising didn't spare the refugees.
- At Greyhold's river gate: a crowd of refugees and Millbrook folk, and behind them men with rings and the boats from the back channel, and Cassian's men off the river. The gate is shut. Pell is on the wall.
- Pell opens it. He comes down with eleven, students and old knights, and holds the gateway while the refugees go through. Maren, outside, gets people to it. She takes a dead knight's sword from the road. She finds Ada and Wren and Bel's children in the crush, with Liss, who brought them up from the ferry house. Bel has the greys: she won't go in without them, and the gate's wide enough for a dray-beast.
- The echo: Wren in the crush, clinging to Maren's arm, says it over the noise to her as if it were the most ordinary news: Fisk's boy came over in the night; Papa's gone to the Old Ruin. On his own. "He left the dragons. He said they'd be seen."
- Pell sees Maren. He looks at the sword in her hand. "Subdue," he says. "Don't kill." And then: "You'll not be let in." She knows.
- The bound hounds come up the road. Pell holds the gateway with five left. The last of the refugees go through. Old Grey goes through with Bel at his head. Pell shouts to shut it. The gate shuts with Pell outside it. He stands with his back to it. He dies there, in the gateway, at noon, with the bell going over his head and nobody saying the oath.
- Maren on the road outside with Liss. The Restoration falls back from the walls in the afternoon when the orders come galloping back down the River Road from Redwatch: Rowan has turned back with half. The other half is pinned at Redwatch.
- Liss: "Tam." Smoke over Millbrook. Maren goes.

## Chapter 70: The Rubbing

POV: Calder
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: Calder learns what the twins are after: two locks, here and beneath the knight school, opened at the same moment. He sends Lisska to warn whoever is at the other lock. She can't speak to anyone but him, so she carries what he can give her: the cut chain from the first night, wrapped in a strip of his shirt with one word burned into it with a coal, TWO, and Wren's blue hair-ribbon. Then he stays to fight.
Echo of the other's story: His choice to warn the student, a stranger he knows only by rumour.

Draft notes: Night one hundred and forty-two to dawn on day one hundred and forty-three. Decided here (proposals):
- Day 142: the island empties as the army crosses to Redwatch. Corvin stays at the fort with a few hundred; the guards stay on the tower stair; Rook leaves the step at dusk to go to the west shore.
- Night 142: Calder goes up, not down: through the broken top of the tower and down its outside wall by the ivy on the river side, and north through the oaks to the reeds. Fisk is there, the last of his three nights. "Thought you were dead. Was looking forward to getting drunk."
- Fisk lands him on the Saltreach shore above the Eelweir. He climbs to the hill above the Fenn farm and calls them the only way he can at that distance: he stops holding them back. They feel it and come. Before midnight all three are on the hill.
- Aurveth reads the rubbing. She can read it whole now: what's gone from the stone she remembers, because she saw it whole from the bridge with her first partner, and the rubbing brings it back. The verse in full:
  *Here the road of the one realm crossed the water,*
  *WINSHIRE, where the old ones walked unbound,*
  *and when the old ones went into the light*
  *the last great one lay down to sleep below the stone,*
  *and its own kin kept the door. Let no hand wake it.*
  *Keep the watch below, and do not ask.*
- What Calder puts together, out loud, to them: the thing under the river (Aurveth's throat on the ice), the lock under the tower, the "knight's lock" under the school, Ada's "two hands", Corvin's "the same breath, here and there". The twins will open both at midnight tomorrow and let the thing out, to take it. "Its own kin kept the door": the lion is its kin. The cat on the steps was keeping the door. "Keep the watch below" was never the knights' oath first. It was the old ones' charge to the lion.
- He can't be in two places. He'll be at the Ruin, against Corvin. Somebody has to be under the school. He knows one person on the Hearthmere bank who might be: the woman with the lion, cast out, whose mother runs the ferry. She knows the island; she knows something under it. He can't speak to her. Lisska can't either.
- What he gives Lisska: the five links from the first night (the Restoration's own chain); a strip torn from his shirt with TWO burned into it with a charred stick from his own field that Kharrak breathes alight, the only word he can write besides his name; and the ribbon already on her horn. Wren's. He hopes the ferrywoman told her. He hopes she'll know whose it is.
- Kharrak: *She won't understand.* Calder: "Then she won't. But she'll know someone over here is trying to tell her something." Lisska goes at first light on day 143, west, low, toward the Brackenwood, where the prints pointed.
- He stays. With Aurveth and Kharrak, on the hill, through day 143. Tomorrow night they'll go to the Ruin.
