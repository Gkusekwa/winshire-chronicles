## Chapter 71: Millbrook

POV: Maren
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: Millbrook burns. Maren pulls Tam out of the smithy and finds Tobin on the other side of the fight.
Echo of the other's story: Dragon fire over the Old Ruin, two colours of it. She doesn't know yet that a third is missing.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 69: Pell dead in the gateway; Rowan back with half the orders; "Tam." "I'll bring him." From Chapter 65: Tam in the Kettle's yard; Vale's promise: "No Millbrook house'll burn"; Odo's forge chained by the crown. From Chapter 57: Tobin at the hedge-meeting; "When they throw you out, come and find us." From Chapter 68 (Calder's side): "our friend" inside Greyhold will take Cassian down. From Chapter 33: twice a rider with Greyhold's seal cleared the river for a raid.
Draft notes: Evening of day one hundred and forty-two to dusk on day one hundred and forty-three. Decided here (proposals):
- How Millbrook burns: not by the Restoration's plan. The knights who came back with Rowan rode down the River Road at dusk to drive the rising out of the town, and the rising fired the thatch in front of them to stop the horses, and the wind took it. By full dark the whole jetty road is alight.
- Maren goes in from the river side, along the back channel, in the smoke.
- Odo's forge: the crown's chain on the doors has been broken off. Tam went in to get Odo out; Odo had gone in for the punches, the stamps, the crossed hammers, to throw them in the river so nobody could make another collar with his mark. The roof's coming down. Odo's under a beam. Tam won't leave him. Maren gets them both out through the back, Odo on a door between them. Odo's legs are broken. He makes no sound on the door. In his fists are the punches, the crossed hammers and the six; at the back channel he tells Maren what the collars were sold as, and throws both into the water.
- Tobin: at the barricade at the foot of the jetty road, with a dozen ring-men and a stave, holding it against the knights. They meet in the smoke. He sees her, and Tam, and Odo on the door. He lowers the stave. "I didn't know about the camp. Nobody said about the camp." He lets them through. Then, when she's past: "The friend with the seal. It's Callis. He's had it since the summer. He opens the river gate tomorrow night. In the third hour." He goes back to the barricade.
- Night 142: she gets Tam and Odo to the ferry house. Liss is there with Wren, who wouldn't stay inside Greyhold without her; Ada stayed with Bel. Liss and Tam.
- Day 143: she tries to get the word about Callis to someone inside. The gate won't open for her. She shouts it up at the wall. A student on the wall hears and looks at her, and goes away; she doesn't know if anyone believes it or who it'll reach. She tells Liss to tell Hale, by the water-gate, if she can.
- The echo: dusk on day 143, as she starts back toward Hollin Knowe, from the River Road she sees fire over the Old Ruin, upriver. Dragon-fire. Two colours: one white at the root and orange-red, one a deep slow bronze-gold, like a furnace door. Two. She doesn't know what that means, or what it means that there are only two. She runs.

## Chapter 72: The Spit

POV: Calder
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: In the fighting at the Ruin, Calder corners Rook. Rook dies before he can say anything useful; the proof dies with him.
Echo of the other's story: The bells of Greyhold stop.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: Rook dies before he can talk. The six-dot knife may survive as Calder's only physical link (with the chain) to Teo's death, useful in Book 2. From Chapter 70: the full verse; the plan to stop Corvin at the tower before midnight; Lisska gone west with the chain, the word and the ribbon. From Chapter 68: Rook told Teo's death under the arch; "I need him alive." From Chapters 13 and 21: the stake on the spit where the hunters chained the lion. From Chapter 71 (Maren's side): Callis opens Greyhold's river gate tonight.
Draft notes: Dusk to the fourth hour of night on day one hundred and forty-three. Decided here (proposals):
- At dusk Kharrak and Aurveth come down on the island from the north: the fire Maren sees from the River Road. Calder's on Aurveth's neck. They burn the camp in the oaks and the boats on the east shore, not the fort. The men on the island break for the boats. A few hundred stand with Corvin in the fort, with bound hounds and two wyverns and crossbows.
- Calder is down on the ground by the eastern gate when he sees Rook: going the other way, down through the oaks to the spit, alone, fast, toward a boat. Leaving.
- Calder goes after him on foot. Kharrak is over the fort; Aurveth is holding the eastern gate.
- He corners Rook at the spit, at the foot of the landing stair, by the stake where the hunters chained the lion. The boat's gone; somebody else took it. Rook stands with his back to the stake and the river behind him and Joss's knife in front of him, and smiles, and says, "Fenn. The barley man." And: "You'd laugh to see me with sheep."
- Calder tells him he's coming to the Clan. Alive. To both yards. To say it. Rook says, pleasantly, that he won't. "Nobody believes the barley man. They'll not believe me either. I'm hopeless." He has the six-knife in his hand.
- Kharrak comes down on the spit behind Calder, with the red tide up, because he felt Calder's blood go. Rook looks at the dragon. He knows what Kharrak is. He goes for Calder with the knife anyway, fast, not to kill: to make the dragon do it.
- Kharrak does it. Calder shouts *no* and it's done. Rook is dead on the gravel by the stake. Whatever he knew is gone with him. All he said was the sheep line, and "Good beast," to Kharrak, at the end, with the dragon over him.
- Calder takes the knife out of the gravel. Plain, round pommel, the ring of six. He puts it in his coat with the chains and the ring.
- Kharrak, after: *I'm sorry.* Calder: "I know." Then: "He meant you to." Kharrak: *I know that too.*
- The echo: from the spit, across the water, from downriver, Greyhold's bells: the alarm, ringing and ringing. Then, all at once, they stop. Calder doesn't know what that means. Aurveth does. *They've stopped because nobody's left at the ropes.*
- The fort: Corvin isn't under the arch. The guards on the tower door are gone. The stair has been dug out to the slab. Corvin has gone down. Lantern-light far below. It's the fourth hour of night. Two hours to midnight.

## Chapter 73: Hollin Knowe

POV: Maren
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: Bond-forcers trap the lion at the edge of the Brackenwood. To save it, Maren offers her own life into the binding in its place. The lion accepts, and the bond forms: for the first time they understand each other. Its name is Orrun.
Echo of the other's story: Lisska lands beside her with a cut chain, a strip of cloth that says TWO, and a child's blue ribbon. Maren knows the ribbon from her mother's story: Wren's. The dragon farmer is warning her.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 71: dusk on day 143, dragon-fire over the Old Ruin in two colours; she runs for Hollin Knowe. From Chapter 67: the lion on the knowe, facing the island, wouldn't come down. From Chapters 36, 39 and 57: the forced binding (honey-smoke, a man kneeling with his hand on the beast's head; "You're chosen now"); bound eyes; the man in grey who drove the hounds from the barge at Redwatch. From Chapter 65: Liss's story of Wren and the green dragon with the blue ribbon. From Chapter 70 (Calder's side): what Lisska carries. From Chapter 26: words carry three or four leagues between bonded.
Draft notes: Night one hundred and forty-three, from the second hour to the fourth. Decided here (proposals):
- How a forced binding works (as Maren sees and feels it): the honey-smoke opens a beast, the way sleep opens a sleeper; the binder lays his hand on its head and pours his will into the opening, and the beast's own self goes out to make room. It takes most of a night for a beast that size.
- The bond-forcers: a dozen of Delling's company and the man in grey from the barge at Redwatch, the one who drove the hounds with his arm. They came over the river at Redwatch in the fighting and swept the fields for it. They've found it on the knowe in the dusk, ringed it with honey-smoke, and got chain on it while it was dazed. The binder is kneeling with his hand on its head when she comes up the slope.
- She goes through them. With the river watch sword. Subdue, don't kill, unless you have to. She has to, twice. She knocks the binder off the lion's head.
- The opening is still open. Smoke. The lion's eyes going. She puts her own hand on its head where his was. The pull takes hold of her: everything in her going out, into the opening, the way the river goes out through an open sluice. It will take all of her. She knows that. That's what the binder's will was keeping him from: his hand was on it, but he wasn't giving, he was taking. She gives.
- She doesn't pull back. In her head, with no words to give it, she offers it: *All of it. Take it. Not him. Me.*
- It accepts. Instead of emptying her, it meets her. The opening closes over both of them. The bond forms.
- First words, in her head, slow and deep as the ground: *You.* Then: *You came back with iron, and put it down.* And its name, the way a beast gives a name: not said, known. **Orrun.**
- The chains break. The earth on the knowe heaves, the beacon-stones roll, and Orrun stands up out of the smoke with the chains falling off it in pieces. The bond-forcers die or run. The man in grey dies under the beacon-stones.
- She understands it now. Not everything. What it feels. What it has felt all winter: the door, the kin below, the scraping, the woman who sat at ten paces and talked and talked and it couldn't answer.
- The echo: Lisska comes down at ten paces in the heather in the light of the burning honey-smoke pots, with a bundle in her front claw and a blue ribbon on her left horn. Maren knows the ribbon. Wren's. *The dragon farmer is warning her.* Lisska lays the bundle down and backs away. Maren unwraps it: the five links with the six and the crossed hammers; the strip of shirt with TWO burned on it.
- And then, because Orrun is beast and kin and Lisska is beast and kin, they can hear each other. Lisska can't speak to Maren, but Orrun can hear Lisska and tell her. What Lisska says: *Two locks. Under the tower on the island. Under the school. Midnight. The still one at the tower. The other one under the school. Both, or nothing. He said tell her. He said she'd know about two.*
- She knows about two. The black door. Hale's key. Callis and the river gate. She heard Greyhold's bells come faint over the fields and stop, a little while before Lisska came.
- They go. Orrun, and Maren on his back, the first time, and Lisska over them. Four leagues to Greyhold. Two hours to midnight.

## Chapter 74: Fire at the Ruin

POV: Calder
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: Calder and the dragons fight Corvin Vale at the Old Ruin's lock, and can't stop it opening.
Echo of the other's story: Through the earth, the dragons feel something vast beneath them begin to pull against its chains.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 72: Rook dead on the spit; the six-knife in Calder's coat; Greyhold's bells stopped; the stair under the tower dug out past the slab and going on down; Corvin's lantern far below; the fourth hour of night, two to midnight. From Chapter 70: the full verse; "both, or nothing"; Lisska gone west. From Chapter 49 (Maren's side, unknown to Calder): Hale's chains, links taller than a man, six of them, the dark moving. From Chapter 54: the great humped shape at the end of the Ram's Head beast lines that Calder didn't go near.
Draft notes: From the fourth hour of night to just before midnight, night one hundred and forty-three. Decided here (proposals):
- The dragons can't go down the stair; it's a man's width. Calder goes down alone with a torch. Aurveth and Kharrak hold the top, because the Restoration is coming back: boats from the Eelweir, two thousand men, and on a raft of eight barges lashed together, the great humped shape: a bound **ironback**, a plated beast the size of a barn, with a man in grey standing on its neck.
- The stair: past the slab (a seal-stone; Corvin's men have prised it up and laid it aside), down a long way, worn in the middle like a chapel stair, cold. It opens onto a ledge high in the wall of a cavern too big for his torch to find the far side of. Below the ledge, in the dark: chain. Six chains, each link taller than a man, black, rusted hard, going down. They're moving, slowly. The dark below them moves.
- Off the ledge a passage leads to a round chamber cut in the rock: the Ruin's lock. In the middle of its floor, a great disc of grey stone the width of a threshing floor, carved with the same hooked letters as the verse, and in its centre an iron boss with a keyhole. Corvin stands by it with a lantern and a long black key and ten of Delling's men.
- Calder goes at them. He's a farmer with a dead man's knife. He gets two of them before they have him down and his arms held. Corvin doesn't let them kill him. "A halter," he says. "My brother would be sorry."
- Corvin talks while he waits, a little, the way a man talks to a beast he's going to keep: the one realm; Hearthmere's crime; the Titan as the one power the six crowns can't stand against; "my brother believes it. I believe in the hour." He knows Rook is dead; he says nothing about it at all.
- The echo: the ground moves. Up through the rock, through the chamber floor, through Calder's knees. Far below, the six chains groan and go taut and slacken and go taut, with a sound like ships' cables in a gale. Through the bronze mark Calder hears Aurveth, above, on the hill of the fort: *It's pulling. Against them. It knows. Something has told it.* And Kharrak: *I can feel it in my teeth.*
- Above, the ironback comes ashore. Kharrak and Aurveth fight it in the fort. Calder feels it through the marks in pieces. Kharrak, cut, roaring, starts tearing at the tower floor with his claws over the stair, trying to dig down to Calder.
- Corvin looks at the lantern-clock he's brought, a candle marked in hours. "Ten breaths before the hour," he says, and puts the key in the lock. Calder can't move. It isn't open yet. It's going to be.

## Chapter 75: The Stair

POV: Maren
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: Greyhold is breached. Kesh is killed, and Haverly dies in Maren's arms: one life between two. Hale, dying, gives her the long black key to the black door at the end of Greyhold's archive. She and Orrun go down.
Echo of the other's story: Far below, someone is already at the second lock.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 73: the bond; Orrun; Lisska's message through him (two locks, midnight, both or nothing); the five links and TWO. From Chapter 71: Tobin: Callis has the seal and opens the river gate tonight; Liss to warn Hale by the water-gate. From Chapter 69: Pell dead; Ada, Bel, the children and the three greys inside; Wren at the ferry house with Liss. From Chapters 49 and 51: the archive, the black door three steps down, warm to the hand; the long black key, turned once for each of the Watch; "I'll not make you that." From Chapter 61: Haverly and Kesh; "I'm brave enough to be in the reeds."
Draft notes: Night one hundred and forty-three, from about the fifth hour to the last before midnight. Decided here (proposals):
- Orrun runs four leagues across the fields in under the hour, faster than any horse. Lisska over them.
- Greyhold: the river gate is open. Callis opened it at the third hour with the seal and his own key to the wicket, and Cassian came in off the river with three hundred. Thorne died at the gate trying to shut it again, with her boar. The bells stopped when the bell-tower was taken.
- Orrun comes in through the open gate with Maren on his back. Everything in the yard stops. Knights, Restoration, refugees at the Long Barn door. Then the yard breaks open round him.
- The Long Barn: the refugees barricaded in it with Grieve and a pitchfork, Ada and Bel among them, the three greys, the twenty-nine beasts. Old Grey stands in the barn door, empty-eyed, and nobody gets past him.
- The north tower stair: Haverly is on it. Holding it, on the steps down to the archive, with Kesh, against Cassian's rearguard, so nobody else can follow them down. He's been holding it for an hour. A bolt takes Kesh off his fist as Maren comes across the yard. He goes down a moment after with a spear in him. Orrun clears the stair. Maren gets to him. He dies on the steps with his head in her lap and the falcon beside him. "I was brave enough for the stair." One life between two.
- The archive: the black door open, and through it, the stair going down; then shut again from the inside and locked. Cassian and Callis and twenty men went down an hour ago. Hale is on the floor by the three steps with a sword-cut, dying. Liss got to him by the water-gate at dusk; he went to the door and was there when Callis came, and tried to stand in front of it.
- Hale gives her the key. "I said I'd not make you that. I'm making you it now. Go through. Be what you have to." And: "There's a lock at the bottom. In a room. Under the chains. It turns. At the hour. Don't let it."
- The echo: through the bond, through the floor, far below, Orrun feels it: someone already at the second lock. A hand on iron. Waiting.
- The door is a man's width. Orrun can't go through it. He puts his shoulder to the door-frame and the earth moves for him: the stone round the door opens like a mouth, the arch cracks and widens, and the stair beyond it groans wider as he goes down it. Lisska stays at the top of the archive stair, in the yard, with her wings spread over Haverly's body.
- They go down.

## Chapter 76: Seven

POV: Calder
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: Corvin's lock turns. The floor of the Old Ruin falls in, and Calder and the dragons fall with it into the catacombs.
Echo of the other's story: Two locks, one moment.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 74: the round chamber off the ledge; the stone disc as wide as a threshing floor, carved in rings of the old letters; the iron boss; Corvin's long black key; the hour-candle; Calder held by four of Delling's men; Kharrak digging down through the tower floor; the ironback in the fort; the six chains going taut; "Seven."
Draft notes: Midnight, night one hundred and forty-three. Decided here (proposals):
- The count goes down. At "three" Kharrak comes through the dome of the chamber: rock and earth and the floor of the tower coming down with him, and daylight-less sky above, and fire. The men holding Calder let go. Calder goes for the key. He gets his hand on Corvin's wrist.
- At "one" Corvin turns the key anyway, with Calder's hand on his wrist. It turns as if it wanted to. The whole stone disc turns with it, a quarter-turn, grinding, the carved rings of letters sliding past each other.
- The echo: in the same breath, from far away, under the river, toward Greyhold, the same sound. A great grinding turn, through all that rock. Two locks. One moment. Calder feels it through his knees. Corvin, kneeling, smiles for the only time.
- The seal opens. Not a door: the whole floor. The disc drops away under them, and the chamber floor round it, and the ledge, and the tower above, and the fort above that. The Old Ruin falls into the cavern.
- Calder falls. Kharrak falls, wings jammed in the collapsing rock. Aurveth comes down through the hole from the fort with the ironback still locked on her, tears free of it, and folds her wings and dives, and catches Calder in her forefoot a long way down, in the dark, among the falling stone. She can't stop. She opens her wings in the cavern, the first time in three hundred years anything has flown in it, and comes down hard on a slope of rubble far below, by the foot of the chains.
- Corvin: Calder sees his lantern going up, not down, on the far side of the hole: a second stair, an older one, cut into the rock, that he must have known was there. Two of his men with him. He doesn't look back.
- Kharrak comes down out of the falling stone onto the rubble beside them, cut and burned and alive. The ironback falls past them into the dark and doesn't make a sound when it lands.
- They're at the bottom of the world. The six chains go up past them into the dark, taut, and down into a deeper dark ahead. Something is moving at the bottom of them. The anchor-stones the chains run through are cracking.
- Lisska: the feeling of her, very far off, in the other direction. Under Greyhold. Down. Coming toward them through the rock. Calder doesn't understand it.

## Chapter 77: Midnight

POV: Maren
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: Beneath the school, Maren kills Cassian Vale just as his lock gives way. At the same moment, his brother opens the Old Ruin's lock, and the seal opens. Through the new bond, Maren feels Orrun learn what its kin has become: a world destroyer.
Echo of the other's story: The other lock opening, felt as the seal breaks.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 75: the black door; Hale's key; Orrun opening the stone; Callis and Cassian and twenty men went down an hour before; "a man with his hand on iron, waiting"; Lisska over Haverly's body at the top of the north tower stair. From Chapter 49: Hale's ledge, the six chains, the dark moving. From Chapter 57: Cassian on the hedge-wain; he didn't know her face. From Chapter 33: the still man on the smithy roof knew her name. From Chapter 63: Callis's broken arm. From Chapter 69: Pell: "Subdue. Don't kill. Unless you have to."
Draft notes: The last minutes before midnight, night one hundred and forty-three. Decided here (proposals):
- The school's side matches the Ruin's: the long stair; Hale's ledge high in the wall of the cavern with the six chains going down past it; a passage off the ledge carved with the old letters; a round chamber; a carved stone disc with an iron boss and a keyhole. The knight's lock.
- In the chamber: Cassian kneeling on the boss with a long black key in it and an hour-candle beside him; Callis with his arm splinted and a sword in his good hand; eighteen men.
- Orrun comes through the passage and the men break. Callis doesn't. He comes at Maren. She does what Pell taught. She takes his sword arm, the good one. He goes down and stays down. She doesn't kill him.
- Cassian, counting, looks up and sees her, and the lion, and his face lights up with real gladness. "*Ashdown.* The knight with the lion. You came. You came home." He doesn't know her face. He knows the lion. He goes on counting.
- She asks him to stop. He won't. "It's the one realm, Maren. It's under your feet." "Three." She has to. She puts the river watch sword into him at "one", as his hand turns.
- His hand turns anyway. His weight falls on the key and it goes round with him, and the disc turns, a quarter-turn, grinding. The lock gives way under a dead man's hand.
- The echo: in the same breath, through the rock, from far off under the river, the other lock turning. Two hands. One moment. The seal breaks: not here, there. The floor here holds. Far off, she feels something fall: a whole island going down into the dark.
- Through the bond, at the moment the seal breaks, Orrun's kin wakes all the way, and Orrun reaches for it, the way he's been reaching all winter through the stone of the pit, and it's there, and it's open, and he touches it.
- What Orrun learns, and Maren with him: it isn't what he kept the door for. It was a sleeper once, old and great and kin. Three hundred years of chains have made it something else. There's nothing in it now but the chains, and the hate of the chains, and a pull toward somewhere far off that only it knows, and the wish to have the world down. A destroyer. It doesn't know him. Or it knows him and doesn't care.
- Orrun's grief comes through the bond into Maren and she goes down on her knees on the turned stone beside the dead man.
- Lisska comes down the stair behind them, all the way, through the widened stone. She lies down beside Orrun. She looks east, under the river, toward the Ruin. Toward Calder. She wants to go.

## Chapter 78: Underground

POV: Calder
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: The catacombs shake. Coming from opposite ends beneath the border, Calder and Maren meet for the first time.
Echo of the other's story: The stranger he warned, now in front of him.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 76: Calder, Aurveth (horn-wound in the chest, left wing torn) and Kharrak (one horn broken) on the rubble slope under where the Old Ruin was; the six chains taut; the anchor-stones cracking; Corvin gone up an older stair; Lisska's feeling coming from the west, from under the school, with something large and warm and a person. From Chapter 77 (Maren's side): Cassian dead on the knight's lock; Callis down; Orrun has touched his kin and found a destroyer; Lisska wants to go to Calder. From Chapter 53: the course of dressed grey stone at the bottom of the pit, running under the river toward Greyhold. From Chapter 70: the five links wrapped in a strip of shirt with TWO burned on it.
Draft notes: The first hour after midnight, night one hundred and forty-three into one hundred and forty-four. Decided here (proposals):
- The way between: under the river runs a gallery of the same dressed grey stone as the inner wall and the bottom of the pit, a road for the wardens of old, roofed, wide enough for a cart, going from under the school to under the island along the side of the great cavern. Orrun knows it by the stone. Maren rides him along it with Lisska ahead. It's cracking as they go; the ground is heaving.
- The cavern shakes the whole time. Stones come down from the dark. The six chains sing. Something at the bottom of them is lifting.
- Lisska's voice comes back into Calder's head when she's within three leagues: *Calder! Calder, I've got her, I've got her, she's with me, she's got the lion, she understood, she understood, Calder…*
- They meet on the cavern floor at the foot of the rubble slope, in the light of Kharrak's fire, with the chains going up into the dark over them and the deep dark beyond moving.
- Lisska first, at a run, round Calder, and then Orrun comes out of the gallery mouth with Maren on his back, and stops, ten paces off. Exactly ten. Maren gets down.
- First words. He says, "You got it." She holds out the five links in the strip of his shirt. "Your dragon told my lion. My lion told me." He doesn't understand that. She says: "I met her. At the gate. At Greyhold. She held on to my arm." Then: "She says hello. She said it to my mother, and to me. Twice."
- The rest goes fast, the way things are said when the roof's coming in: Cassian dead at the school's lock; Corvin gone up the old stair; Rook dead on the spit; Hale, Haverly, Pell, Thorne. Two hands: both turned. And what Orrun touched. Maren tells him that. *It isn't kin any more. They made it a destroyer.* Aurveth, lying on the rubble, closes her eye.
- They have to get out. Aurveth can't fly with the wing. The gallery's coming down. Orrun: the stone will move for him. Up. Not back toward the school. Up through the rock under where the island was, toward the river, the way the lion dug all winter in the other direction.
- The first anchor-stone breaks while they're talking. A chain comes loose from the wall above and falls past them into the dark, a link at a time, with a sound like a city's bells being thrown down a well.
- The echo: Calder, looking at her across the ten paces in the firelight: a stranger. Hair tied back, burned hands in rags, a river watch sword, blood to the elbow. He's never seen her face before closer than across a river. He warned her, and she's here.

## Chapter 79: Keep the Watch

POV: Maren
Part: Part Six: The First War
Beats: The Last Titan tears free of its chains and flies. Hearthmere's magic, failing since midwinter, goes out for good. The book ends with the two of them watching it go.
Echo of the other's story: Both stories, together for the first time.
Status: first draft
Carried forward: From Chapter 78: Orrun opening the rock overhead; Aurveth can't fly (left wing torn, horn-wound in the chest); Kharrak holding her up; five chains left; the first anchor-stone broken; Maren and Calder have met. From Chapter 77: what Orrun touched: a destroyer, made one by three hundred years of chains, pulled toward somewhere far off and wanting the world down. From Chapter 49: Hearthmere's lamps without oil, warming-stones, licensed words, the sweet wells, all drawn from below. From Chapter 13: "Keep the watch below, and do not ask." From Chapter 70 (Calder's side): the full verse; "its own kin kept the door".
Draft notes: The small hours to sunrise, day one hundred and forty-four. Decided here (proposals):
- The way up: Orrun walks up into the rock and the rock opens ahead of him in a long rising passage, the way water opens round a swimmer's arms, and closes behind them a little way back. Aurveth walks, with Kharrak's shoulder under hers. Calder walks at her head. Lisska goes ahead with the lantern in her claw. Maren on Orrun.
- Under them, the chains go: the second, the third, the fourth, each one a long falling thunder through the rock. Then the fifth. Then the sixth, last, and a silence.
- They come out at first grey in the reeds on the Hearthmere shore below Redwatch, where Maren and the lion came ashore before light on day 140. Orrun knew the place.
- The river: where the island was there's a great dark hole in the river, and the Wend is pouring into it from both channels, roaring, in a whirlpool a quarter-mile across. Smoke and steam over it. On both banks, everybody has stopped: Redwatch's knights, the Restoration's boats, the men on the Saltreach bank at Harrow's End. The fighting stops by itself.
- The Titan comes up through the river. Up out of the hole, out of the whirlpool, with the Wend falling off it in sheets. Lion-kin: Maren knows the shape from Orrun, and from the forefoot on the cut stub. But the size of a hill. Grey as old stone, a mane like a ridge of broken rock, plates of stone along its back, and wings that come up out of the water folded and open, slowly, the width of the river, made of something like stone and something like leather. The broken ends of the chains still on its legs, links taller than men, trailing like threads.
- The ground on both banks heaves when it moves. Redwatch's tower cracks. Downriver, Greyhold's inner wall, the old grey stones, splits from top to bottom.
- It doesn't look at Orrun. Orrun roars at it, the question, the last time. It turns its great head once toward the reeds, toward the lion, and away. Maren never knows whether it saw him.
- It flies. North-east. Over the Saltreach bank and Harrow's End and the Fellwood and the Scarfell, slowly, then less slowly, with the dawn coming up behind the hills under it, until it's a mountain in the sky, and a hill, and a bird, and nothing.
- Hearthmere's magic fails: on the Hearthmere bank, every lamp at Redwatch that was burning without oil goes out at once. Downriver, the lamps on Greyhold's walls. Orrun feels it through the ground: the thing that came up from below draining away like a river after a flood. Maren knows, without seeing it, that in Hallowmere the lamps are going out too, and the sweet wells are going sour, and every licensed word is only a word.
- The end: the two of them on the shore at sunrise, with the lion and three dragons, watching the empty sky. Calder asks what it was. Maren tells him the name the Watch had for it. "The Last Titan." Then, with her hand on Orrun's shoulder, she says the old words back to herself and means something new by them: "Keep the watch."
