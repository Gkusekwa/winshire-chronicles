# Voice samples: the opening of Chapter 1

*The same moment written three ways, to choose the series voice. Names like Greyhold and Corwin Brandt are still proposals.*

## A. Grounded and plain (recommended base)

The yard at Greyhold had been swept before dawn, and by the second bell it was mud again. Maren stood in the line of the unbonded with her practice sword point-down in the dirt and watched the beasts come in.

They came in with their students the way dogs come in with farmers, close at the knee, though none of them were dogs. A grey cat the size of a calf. Something long and low with a badger's stripe that Instructor Pell called a marten and nobody believed. The Holm girl's hawk, which hated everyone, sat on her fist and screamed at the swallows.

Maren counted them. Eleven this year. Last year, nine.

"You're doing it again," said Tobin.

"Doing what."

"Counting."

She didn't stop. He grinned and looked off across the river, where the Saltreach bank was a low brown line under smoke. Someone over there was burning stubble. Or someone over there was burning something.

## B. Lean and hard-edged

Twelve beasts in the yard. Eleven students standing with them. One beast hadn't picked yet: the brindled thing by the well, all shoulder, watching the line of the unbonded like a buyer at market.

Maren didn't look at it. Looking was begging.

She was twenty-seven. Oldest in the yard by six years. She'd held the river watch through four winters and she could put a boy twice her weight on his back before he'd finished choosing a guard. None of that was on the list the beasts read from.

The brindled thing yawned. Walked. Stopped in front of Corwin Brandt, who had never had to wait for anything, and sat down on his boots.

The yard cheered. Maren cheered with it. You learned that too.

## C. Mythic and lyrical

In Hearthmere they say a beast chooses the way rain chooses a field: it doesn't ask, it doesn't explain, and afterwards everyone pretends they knew. Maren Ashdown had heard this said at three funerals and one wedding, and she had come to Greyhold anyway, at twenty-seven, to be passed over in front of people.

The choosing was held in the long yard under the east wall, where the flagstones had worn hollow from four hundred years of people standing still. Old knights watched from the wall. New beasts walked the line below. And past all of them the river went by, brown and in no hurry, carrying the Saltreach smoke down to the sea.
