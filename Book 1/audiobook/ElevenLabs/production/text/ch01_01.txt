Part One. Two Banks. [long pause]

Chapter One.

The Unchosen Yard. [long pause]

They say the river at Greyhold was here before the kingdoms and will be here after them, and that it has never once taken a side. It runs brown between two countries that don’t trust each other and carries the silt of both down to the same sea. In spring the fishermen on both banks curse it in two accents. In winter it freezes from the edges in, and for a week or two a person could walk across into /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/, and every year someone tries, and every year the river takes one of them back.

The school sits on the /ˈhɑːθmɪə/ bank where the water bends. It was a fort before it was a school and a quarry before it was a fort, and the yard is still quarry floor under the sand, so anyone who falls in it hard enough learns the difference. Over the gate, in letters cut deep enough to hold snow, the charter of the knightly orders says that the bonded and the unbonded serve as equals. The letters are very old. Most of the students have stopped seeing them, as you stop seeing a crack in your own ceiling.

/ˈmɛərən/ Ashdown saw them every morning. She made a point of it. [long pause]

She fell in the yard twice before the second bell.

The first time was her own fault. The second time was /ˈluːkən/ Haverly’s bird.

“Again,” said Instructor Pell.

She got up. Sand in her collar, sand in her teeth, a hot line along one forearm where the quarry stone had come through. Haverly was back on his mark already, practice blade low, smiling the way he smiled at the tavern girls in Millbrook and at the chaplain. Behind him, on the fence post nearest the well, Kesh was settling her feathers back into place as if nothing had happened.

Nothing had, really. The falcon hadn’t come near her. She had only opened her wings, all at once, at the moment /ˈmɛərən/ committed her weight to the lunge, and eight winters on the river watch had turned her head toward it. On the river, a movement at the edge of your eye was a bolt until it proved it wasn’t. Half a heartbeat. Haverly had needed less.

“Eyes on the man,” Pell said.

“The man brought a bird.”

A few laughs from the fence. Pell didn’t laugh. He was a long-boned, grey-haired man who had lost two fingers on the coast road below Stillhaven before most of this year’s students were born, and he did not laugh in the yard on principle.
