[sadly] He lasted four days after that. The healers said every word their licences allowed over him and kept warming-stones at his feet, and when none of it took they called it grief, because they had to call it something. /ˈmɛərən/ had been fourteen. She had watched a strong man go out like a lamp with nothing wrong with him, and she had understood, sitting on the end of his bed on the fourth morning, exactly what a bond was. A door you couldn’t close again. One life with two people standing in it. [pause]

[thoughtful] She had never wanted one. She’d told herself so often enough that she mostly believed it. [long pause]

[calm] The shadows came over the yard just before noon.

Nobody shouted. You didn’t, at Greyhold. But every head along the fence went up together, and the cat in the shade was suddenly not asleep, and Kesh dropped flat on her post with her wings half open. /ˈmɛərən/ was counting before she’d decided to.

[tense] Five. Six. Seven. [pause]

[in awe] Clan dragons, high up and in no kind of order, following the river south as geese follow it, as if the water were a road someone had drawn on the land for them. The sun came through their wings the colour of a cut. They made no sound that reached the ground. That was the thing nobody told you about dragons until you’d seen them: how quiet they were, how the size of them filled the sky without disturbing it, like weather. [pause]

[calm] One banked low over the water, low enough that she saw its rider, a dark knot strapped in at the shoulders, a pale smear of a face turned toward the school. Then it levelled and followed the others over the far bank, and they were gone into the brown haze that had hung over /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ since dawn.

Someone let out a long whistle.

“Seven,” said Tobin. “War party.”

“That’s a funeral,” said a boy behind him, one of the Weir cousins. “My uncle runs barges to the /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ side. Their old chief’s dead. Three days ago.”

“Dead how?”

“Old, I think. Just old.” The boy shrugged. “My uncle says they’ll be fighting over his bones by harvest. Brother against sons. They’re like that over there.”

“Better their bones than ours,” said someone, and that got a laugh that ran all the way down the fence.
