Or she would be sitting in some /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ kitchen, waiting to see what the smoke meant.

Burning stubble, probably. It was the end of summer. Farmers burned stubble.

/ˈmɛərən/ watched it anyway, until Pell called the yard to the noon line and she had to turn her back on it. [long pause]

They said the oath at noon. They said it every day, bonded and unbonded together, standing in their lines facing east toward the river and the old grey stones of the inner wall, and they said it the way you say anything you learned before you understood it. Which is to say, without listening.

/ˈmɛərən/ listened. She always had. It had been her father’s oath first.

[slowly] I will hold the river. [slowly] I will keep the road. [slowly] I will stand between the weak and what would harm them, [slowly] and I will keep the watch below, [slowly] and I will not ask what I keep.

[calm] Halfway through the fourth line, she felt the beasts move.

[tense] She didn’t see it. She was facing the wall, like everyone else. She felt it on the back of her neck, like a change in the wind: a ripple running down the line of the bonded behind her, a stir of claws on sand. Then it was gone, and the oath was ending, and Tobin beside her was already scratching his ear. [pause]

[calm] She looked back. The beasts were where they had been. The Darrow girl’s hound was lying down. Kesh was preening. The cat was asleep again, or pretending.

[hushed] Only every one of them had turned its head the same way. Not toward the river. Down. Toward the sand under the yard, and whatever was under the sand. [pause]

[calm] Then the Darrow girl clicked her tongue, and the hound looked up at her, and the moment broke.

[quietly] The watch below. Nobody knew what it meant. /ˈmɛərən/ had asked once, in her first month, and Pell had looked at her as you look at a horse someone has just told you is lame. “Below the river,” he’d said, and walked off. As if the river had anything below it but mud.
