[calm] She’d asked the chaplain, who said it meant the dead. She’d asked an old knight at the muster, who said it meant the poor. Haverly, when she was new and still asked people things, had told her cheerfully that it was a joke from the old days, and that the founders had kept their wine in the cellars.

The cellars of Greyhold were locked, and had been for as long as anyone could remember. Students didn’t go into them. That was simply how it was, like the oath, and the gate, and first lance.

[quietly] I will not ask what I keep.

[calm] The bell stopped. The lines broke up and went in to eat. [long pause]

She went up to the river wall after supper, as she did most evenings when the light was going. Below her old Wick the lamp-tender was going round the yard with his licence on a cord at his neck, saying the dusk words at each post, and the lamps came up from their daytime glow to full white behind him one by one, with no oil in them, as they had every evening of her life. The river wall was the old fort wall, broad enough to walk two abreast, and from the north corner you could see a long way up the water to the Old Ruin, a dark lump on its island where the river split. She had poled past that island a thousand times in her years on the river watch and never once set foot on it. Nobody did. There were stories about it. There were stories about everything on this river, and her mother knew all of them.

Haverly was already there.

He was sitting on the parapet with his boots hanging over the drop and Kesh on his knee, feeding her strips of something from a cloth. He looked up when /ˈmɛərən/ came round the corner and didn’t look embarrassed, which she had to give him.

“I wondered if you’d come up,” he said.

“It’s a wall. I’m allowed on it.”

“I know.” He fed the falcon another strip. “You should have had first lance.”

“Tell Pell.”

“I did.”

That stopped her. She hadn’t expected it.

“He said I’d earn it by autumn.” Haverly frowned down at the bird. “I will, too. I’m not saying I won’t. But you should have had it today, and I wanted you to know I know that.”

He had a frank, open face that had never had to hide anything. He would make a good knight, she thought. He would make a good knight and never once wonder why.
