“Ten and a half. He says the uncle wants the hold and the sons want the hold, and when two lots want the same thing you get a war.” She licked her spoon. “He says the uncle’s the best fighter in the Clan, so he’ll win.”

“Eat.”

“Will he?”

/ˈkɔːldə/ pulled his boots on. They were still damp from yesterday and would be damp tomorrow. “Whoever gets the hold,” he said, “the barley still needs cutting. And Bramble’s foot still needs seeing to. And you’ve still got porridge.”

She looked at him over the bowl, a long, level look that was entirely Senna’s. Then she ate the porridge. [long pause]

The cow had an abscess in her hoof and no opinions about the Clan. He liked her for it. Ada had said her cow-words over the foot two mornings running, which worked about one time in three, and this was not one of the three, so now it was the knife.

[dryly] He had Bramble tied short to the ring in the byre wall and her off-hind foot up on his knee, and he was talking to her the whole time because she liked that, and because his father had talked to cows and /ˈkɔːldə/ had never found a reason to stop. “There,” he told her. “That’s the bad bit. Hold still. Hold still, you great soft lump. Nobody’s cutting anything off you that you’ll miss.”

[calm] She leaned on him. She was very good at leaning. He had the knife in and the muck coming out, and was feeling quite pleased with both of them, when the light went.

Cloud came slowly. This was a hand closing over the yard.

Bramble screamed, the long, cracked, outraged bellow of a cow who has been lied to, and kicked, and put him flat on his back in the byre muck with the knife still in his fist.

He lay there and looked up through the open half of the roof.

[in awe] They were low. Lower than he’d ever seen them come over the farms. Seven of them, black against the white sky, going south-west from the hills toward the river, and he could see the plates along their bellies and the shine on the nearest one’s claws, folded up neat as a sleeping dog’s. They went over without a sound. Then the wind of them came down a moment later, all at once, and every loose thing in the yard went flying, the hens and the straw and Wren’s washing off the line.
