[calm] /ˈkɔːldə/ got up on one elbow and watched them reach the Wend and turn, all seven together, and follow it south, past the trees of the Old Ruin’s island, along the border, toward the grey smudge on the far bank that was Greyhold.

[dryly] “Well,” he told the cow. “There goes the harvest.”

[dryly] Bramble looked at him with deep dislike.

[calm] He knew what it was. Everyone over forty would know. When a Clan chief died, the Clan flew the border, the whole length of the Marches, once, on the third day. It was meant to be a mourning. It was also meant to remind everyone on both banks, and especially the knights, that a dead chief didn’t mean a weak Clan. /ˈkɔːldə/’s father had seen it done for Haldor’s father. He’d said the dragons had come over so low that you could have hit one with a thrown stone, if you’d wanted to die that particular way.

Seven, though. His father had said three.

/ˈkɔːldə/ wiped his hand on his trousers, which did nothing, and went to get Wren’s washing out of the plum tree. [long pause]

In the afternoon he took the last of the old barley to the mill, because Hob had sent twice for it, and because the mill was where you went if you wanted to hear things without being seen to want to.

Harrow’s End was thirty-one houses, a mill, a shrine to the gods at the crossroads, and one long argument about water that had been running since before anyone could remember. The houses were river stone at the bottom and tarred timber at the top, with turf on the roofs that the goats got onto every spring. Hollow Beck ran down through the middle of it from the /ˈskɑːfɛl/, fast and brown, and turned Hob’s wheel, and went on into the Wend opposite the Old Ruin, where nobody went. The wheel had a charm-tag nailed to its hub that Hob had paid a Wendmouth contract-man two silver for, warranted, and it squealed exactly as it always had.

There were eight men at the mill and none of them were grinding anything.

“Seven,” Hob was saying when /ˈkɔːldə/ came in with the sack. “Seven. For a funeral.”

“It’s a big funeral.”
