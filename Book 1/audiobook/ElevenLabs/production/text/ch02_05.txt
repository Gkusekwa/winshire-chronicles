[calm] The fire ticked. Somewhere outside a goat complained about the roof.

“Who were the old ones?” said Wren. She knew. She asked anyway. It was part of the song.

“The gods.” Ada didn’t look up. “Before they went into the light. They walked about like you or me, the old women say, only bigger, and nobody could tell them where to go.”

“That’s what unbound means.”

“That’s what unbound means. Which is more than I can say for you. Bed.”

“And the last great beast?”

“Sleeps.”

“Where?”

“Under somewhere.” Ada smiled a little at the fire. “That’s the point of a song, love. You don’t find out.”

“What happens when the six are one again?”

[thoughtful] Ada didn’t answer that one. She never did. She sang the last verse faster than the others, /ˈkɔːldə/ had noticed years ago, as you walk faster past a dog you don’t know, and when Wren asked about it she always found something that needed doing. Tonight it was the kettle.

[tenderly] /ˈkɔːldə/ put Wren to bed in the loft, under the eaves where the warm from the fire collected, and she was asleep before he’d got the blanket straight. He stood there a while. She slept with her fists up by her face like a baby, still, at nine. Senna had slept like that.
