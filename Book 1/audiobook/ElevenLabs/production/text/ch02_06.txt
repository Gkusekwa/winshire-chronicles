[calm] He stood with his hand on the byre door and did nothing for a long breath, which was the thing he always did, and which had kept him alive and out of trouble for thirty-two years. Then he thought of /bɛl/.

[urgently] He was running before he’d decided to. [long pause]

[calm] It was two leagues up the river lane to Upper Rush. He’d never run it before. By the time he got there his chest was a fist and there were four other men from Harrow’s End strung out behind him on the lane with lanterns, Garrow and his son and Hob, of all people, and one of the Tanner boys, and the fires had mostly done what fires do.

[tense] Upper Rush was four farms in a hollow above the river. Two of the barns were gone, just gone, black ribs standing up in a red glow, and the third was going, and there was no point in water. People stood around in their nightclothes in the yard of the nearest farm, holding things they’d grabbed without thinking. A woman with a clock. A boy with one boot. [pause]

[calm] /bɛl/ was in the middle of them, filthy to the elbows, with her husband Joss beside her and their two little ones wrapped together in one blanket at her feet. She saw /ˈkɔːldə/ and her face went through about five things and landed on angry, which was how he knew she was all right.

“You ran,” she said.

“I walked fast.”

[tenderly] “You ran. You’re purple.” She hit him in the chest, not hard. “We’re all right. We’re all right. The house is all right.”

[calm] “What happened?”

[sadly] Joss answered. He was a big, slow, gentle man who bred dray-beasts, and his voice had gone strange and thin. “They came just on dark. Twenty, maybe more. Masks. No colours. They didn’t come for the houses.” He looked at the burning barn. “They came for the beasts. They knew which barns. They had them roped and out before we were even down the ladder, and then they fired the barns behind them. Like you’d shut a door.” [pause]

[calm] “All of them?”

[sadly] “My two greys. Hollin’s ox-beast. The Pellow girl’s hound, the one that finds the lambs in fog.” Joss swallowed. “They had a cage for the hound. On a cart. A cage.” [pause]

[calm] “It’s the Clan,” said someone in the crowd. Hollin, whose ox-beast was gone. “It’s /ˈʊlrɪk/’s men, getting their hands in before the war. Or the sons’. They want beasts for the fighting and they don’t want to pay for them.”
