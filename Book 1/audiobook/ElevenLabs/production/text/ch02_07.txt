There was a growl of agreement. It had a shape to it that was easy to hold.

“The Clan’s got dragons,” said Hob. “What’s an ox worth to a man with a dragon?”

“Eat it, maybe.”

That got a bad laugh. /ˈkɔːldə/ didn’t laugh. He was looking at the yard.

It was churned to mud, as any yard is after twenty men and a lot of frightened animals have been through it. But across the mud, plain in the light of the burning barn, ran two deep ruts. A cart, heavy, iron-tyred. They went out of the yard and down the slope.

[tense] Not up toward the lane and the hills and /ˈvɑːhəʊld/. Down. Toward the river. [pause]

[calm] The Clan didn’t use carts. The Clan didn’t need to. Anything the Clan wanted, it carried home in the air.

[tense] He walked along the ruts a little way, out of the lantern light, and something turned under his boot. He bent and picked it up. A length of chain, five links, as long as his forearm, the last link cut clean through. The metal was dark and good and heavier than it should have been, and each link had a mark stamped into it that he didn’t know: a small ring of six dots, neat as a smith’s, like six seeds laid in a circle. [pause]

[calm] He’d seen chain like it once, years ago, on a /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ barge that came up the Wend selling things nobody in Harrow’s End could afford. Beast chain. The kind you used on something that might pull a wall down to get at you.

“/ˈkɔːldə/?” /bɛl/ had followed him. She stood at the edge of the light with her arms wrapped round herself. “What’ve you got?”

He closed his hand. “Nothing. Bit of harness.”

She looked at his fist, and then at his face, and she was his sister and had been looking at that face all her life.

“Say it, then,” she said.

“Say what?”

“Whatever you’re not saying.”

The barn roof went in behind them with a long, soft roar, and sparks went up into the dark, and everyone in the yard stepped back at once.

“It’s late,” /ˈkɔːldə/ said. “You should get the little ones inside.” [long pause]

He stayed till the fire was down to embers and the men from Harrow’s End had done what they could, which was nothing, and then he walked down to the river to wash the smoke off his face.
