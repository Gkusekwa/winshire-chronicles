The Wend was wide and black here and moving fast. On the far side, a long way off, the /ˈhɑːθmɪə/ bank was a low line against a slightly lighter sky, and on that line, small and steady, well down the river, were lights. A row of them, high up, too regular for houses. Lanterns on a wall. Crown lamps, Ada called them, that burned all night on nothing at all, though she’d never been closer to one than this.

Joss had come down behind him. He stood with his big hands hanging and looked across the water at them.

“Greyhold,” he said. “The knights’ school. They’ll have seen it. They’ll have watched the whole thing.”

“Probably.”

“Three barns going up a few leagues from their wall. And they’ll have stood there and watched.” Joss spat in the river. “They’ll not cross.”

“They never do,” said /ˈkɔːldə/.

He didn’t mean anything by it. It was just a thing everyone said, like the thing about red smoke, and it was true in the same way, which is to say it had always been true so far.

[quietly] The lanterns on the far wall didn’t move. After a while one of them went out, and then the others, one by one, as if someone over there had finished looking and gone to bed.

[calm] /ˈkɔːldə/ walked home in the dark. Two leagues. He didn’t run it this time. The chain was in his coat pocket, and it knocked against his hip with every step, and by the time he got to his own door it had got so heavy that he’d almost made up his mind to tell someone about it.

He hung the coat on its peg and went up to check on Wren. She was still asleep, fists up by her face. Downstairs, his mother had banked the fire and left the kettle on the hob for him, the way she always did.

[thoughtful] He didn’t tell anyone. It was the kind of thing he was good at.
