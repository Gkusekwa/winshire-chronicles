Chapter Three.

The Ferry. [long pause]

The ferry’s guide rope had been spliced so many times that there was probably not an inch of the first rope left in it, and every morning for thirty years Liss Ashdown had put her hand on it before she did anything else. It was the nearest thing to a charm that ferry people bothered with. A word said over running water goes downriver with everything else, Liss said, and you might as well say it to the eels.

She was touching it now, in the grey before sunrise, when /ˈmɛərən/ came down the landing steps.

“You’re not on the list,” Liss said, without turning round.

“I’m not crossing for trade.”

“I didn’t think you were.” Her mother looked at her then: the boots, the plain wool coat over the school shirt, no badge, no sword. “Does your Instructor Pell know you’re out of the walls?”

“He’ll know if I’m not back by the second bell.”

“Then we’d better be quick.” Liss took her hand off the rope. “Get in. Mind the eel boxes. And you’re poling, since you’ve come to be nosy.” [long pause]

The ferry was a long flat-bottomed boat, broad as a cart, with a rail along one side and a ring at either end where it ran on the rope. At the Greyhold landing there was a stone jetty and a little ferry house with a red door, where /ˈmɛərən/ had been born. At the far end there was a timber jetty at Tolly’s /steɪð/, on the /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ bank, and a few huts, and the smell of smoke and eels. In between was the Wend.

[quietly] It was quiet at this hour. Mist lay on the water in a flat white skin, just deep enough to hide your feet, so that standing in the ferry felt like standing on a cloud that smelled of weed. /ˈmɛərən/ set the pole and leaned and the boat moved out, and the rope came up dripping out of the mist ahead of them and slid through the rings with the small hiss it always made. She had learned to pole on this boat before she could read. It didn’t take thinking. Her body did it and left the rest of her free, which was why she’d come.

[calm] “You heard about first lance, then,” she said.

“Everyone heard about first lance.” Liss sat on an eel box with her hands in her sleeves. “Weir’s lad was telling the whole landing by supper. Said you put that Haverly boy on his back and Pell gave it to him anyway.”

“It’s not Haverly’s fault.”

“I didn’t say it was.”

“You were going to.”
