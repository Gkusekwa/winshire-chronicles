[calm] /ˈmɛərən/ leaned on the pole. The boat slid. Somewhere off to the left a heron got up out of the mist with a noise like someone shaking out a wet sheet.

“I’m not arguing.”

“No,” said Liss. “You’re counting strokes. You think I don’t know what your face does.” [long pause]

[in awe] They were halfway across when the sun came up behind the /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ bank and turned the mist gold, and then the mist began to lift in long slow tatters, and the river came out from under it brown and wide and full of light. Upriver, past the bend, three leagues off and a little to the east, the Old Ruin’s island sat in the middle of the water like a ship that had run aground in another century. From here you could make out the line of the trees and, above them, the stump of the tower. Nothing else. [pause]

[calm] /ˈmɛərən/ looked at it the way she always looked at it, which was without meaning to.

She had poled past that island a thousand times. Eight winters on the river watch, out of Northwatch and Redwatch, up and down this stretch in the long patrol boats with three oars a side and a lantern in the bow. You learned every bar and snag on the river. You learned the island too, from the water: the gravel spit at its south end where the current split, the east channel that was faster and the west channel that silted every summer, the place where the old bridge piers broke the surface in a dry year like the backs of drowned animals. You learned not to look at it too long at night. You never landed.

Nobody landed. It was the first thing they taught you, before they taught you to row.
