“I know it’s the law. I’ve known it’s the law since I was seventeen. I’ve had it read to me off a board at Northwatch every spring.” She leaned on the pole. “No boat of the river watch shall put in at the island of the Old Ruin, nor any person land there, on pain of dismissal. I know what it says. I asked why.”

“And what did your captain say, when you asked him?”

“He said it was the law.”

“Well, then.”

“Ma.”

Her mother sighed and settled deeper into her coat, which was what she did before a story, and /ˈmɛərən/ felt something in her own chest loosen a little, because that was what it did before one of her mother’s stories, even at twenty-seven, even with the bell coming.

[thoughtful] “The law’s old,” Liss said. “Older than your captain, older than his captain. I don’t know where it comes from and I don’t think he does either. But the river folk didn’t need a law. We’ve always kept off it. When I was little, your grandmother told me the island was minding something, and you don’t walk into someone else’s house while they’re minding something. That’s all. Minding something.”

[calm] “Minding what?”

[hushed] “She didn’t say. She didn’t know. Nobody knows. There’s a story.” Liss watched the island. “There’s always a story. Before your grandmother was born, a boy from the /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ side swam out to it on a bet, on a hot day, middle of summer, and climbed up the spit and went in under the trees. His friends waited on the bank. He didn’t come out. They waited till dark and then they went home and told his mother, and she went out in a boat with a lantern, and she found him sitting on the spit with his feet in the water. Not hurt. Not a mark on him. He came home with her and he never said a word about what he’d seen. Not that night and not ever after. He lived to be old, they said, and every year on that day he went down to the bank and looked at the island, and then went home.” [pause]

[calm] The rope hissed through the rings.

“That’s not a story,” /ˈmɛərən/ said. “That’s half a story.”

“The good ones usually are,” said her mother. [long pause]
