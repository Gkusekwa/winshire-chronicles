Tolly’s /steɪð/ had been awake for hours. Fishing people get up before everyone and then pretend they haven’t, and by the time the ferry bumped the jetty the eel traps were already up and emptied, and the smoke house was going, and Greta, who smoked the eels, was standing in the door with her arms folded, watching the ferry come in as if it owed her money.

“You’re early, Liss.”

“I’m on time. Everybody else is late.” Her mother stepped onto the jetty with the rope’s end and made it fast without looking. “Where’s Tolly?”

“Out on the weirs since before light. You’ll have seen the fires.”

“We saw them.”

“Everybody saw them.” Greta’s eyes went to /ˈmɛərən/ and stayed there, and moved over the coat and the school shirt under it, and came back to her face. /ˈmɛərən/ knew that look. The far bank had a dozen ways of looking at someone from Greyhold, and this was the polite one. “This your girl? The knight?”

“Not a knight yet,” said /ˈmɛərən/.

“Not one at all, from what I hear.” Greta jerked her head toward the river. “Weir’s boy runs his mouth on both banks. First lance to the falcon.” She almost smiled. “Come in, then. Kettle’s on. You’ll want to know what happened, since your lot stood on the wall and watched it.” [long pause]

The smoke house was hot and dark and smelled so strongly of eel that after a while you stopped smelling it and started tasting it instead. There were half a dozen people in there, fishing folk and a woman /ˈmɛərən/ didn’t know with a baby, all talking at once, as people do after a fire, when nobody has slept and the worst is over and the news is still new enough to be worth having.

/ˈmɛərən/ sat on an upturned crate in the corner with a cup of something hot and brown, and listened.

It came out in pieces. Upper Rush, two leagues upriver of Harrow’s End: four farms in a hollow above the water. A crowd of men just on dark, twenty or thirty, with masks and no colours. They’d known which barns. They’d gone straight in and brought the beasts out roped, a pair of grey dray-beasts and an ox-beast and a hound in a cage, and then they’d fired the barns behind them. Nobody killed. Nobody even much hurt, apart from a man who’d tried to stop them and been knocked down with the flat of a sword.

“The flat,” said Greta. “Mark that. They didn’t want blood. They wanted the beasts.”
