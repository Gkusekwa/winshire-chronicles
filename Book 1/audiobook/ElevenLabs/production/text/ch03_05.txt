“It’s the Clan,” said a big man by the door. “Everyone knows it’s the Clan. The old chief’s three days dead and they’re already taking what they’ll need for the fighting.”

“The Clan doesn’t hide its face,” said the woman with the baby.

“The Clan doesn’t need a cart, either.” That was a thin old man who’d come in behind them, still wet to the knees, with the look of a man who hasn’t slept. Tolly. /ˈmɛərən/ knew him from the river; he’d sold the watch eels for years and sworn at their wakes for longer. He sat down heavily on a sack. “Hollin’s lad came down from Upper Rush in the night. They had a cart. Big one, iron tyres. He saw where it went.”

“Went where?”

“Down to the water.”

[tense] “Not without a light, they don’t, not on this water. They’d be on the bar below Millbrook before they knew it.” Tolly drank at last. “And she wasn’t rowing. She was poling.
