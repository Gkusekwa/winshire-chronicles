[calm] “Anyway,” said Greta, “it’s done now. And /bɛl/ Fenn’s all right, and Joss, and the little ones, thank the gods, even if Joss has lost his greys. And that brother of hers turned up, if you’ll believe it. /ˈkɔːldə/ Fenn. Ran it.”

“Ran what?”

“Ran from Harrow’s End. Two leagues in the dark, when he saw the fire. Hob the miller’s lad says he got there purple in the face and couldn’t speak for a quarter of an hour.” Greta shook her head. “/ˈkɔːldə/ Fenn. Never ran anywhere in his life. Never did anything in his life, that one, except pay his dues and mind his cows.”

“He’s got a little girl,” said the woman with the baby. “His wife died. The fever, five years back.”

“Everybody’s got something,” said Greta. “Doesn’t make them run.”

“I know Ada Fenn,” Liss said from across the room. /ˈmɛərən/ hadn’t known she was listening. “Brings me honey in the spring. Sings like a split reed and knows every song on the river. Her son came over once, years ago, with a cart of barley for the Millbrook mill, and wouldn’t look at the knights on the landing. Wouldn’t look at anyone. Polite. Quiet.” She shrugged. “Not the running sort. But it was his sister.”

“It was his sister,” Greta agreed, and that seemed to settle it, and the talk went on to other things.

/ˈmɛərən/ sat with her cup going cold and thought about a quiet man who didn’t look at knights, running two leagues through the dark toward a fire because it was his sister’s. She found she could picture it very clearly, though she had never seen him. A big man, she thought, slow, purple in the face, too late to do anything when he got there, there anyway.

[thoughtful] She thought: I stood on the wall.

[calm] She picked up the cup and drank the cold brown stuff to the bottom. [long pause]

On the way back the sun was full up and the river was bright and busy. Two barges were coming up from Millbrook under sail with the wind behind them, and a /ˈhɑːθmɪə/ patrol boat was out in the middle of the stream with six oars going and a knight in the bow, rowing hard upriver toward Upper Rush as if there were still something to be done there.

“Now they go,” said Liss.

/ˈmɛərən/ said nothing. She was poling. The boat moved. The rope came up out of the water ahead and slid through the rings and went back down behind.
