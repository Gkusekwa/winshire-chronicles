“There’s something else,” her mother said after a while. “I nearly forgot. Old Penner from Millbrook came across yesterday with his boat. He had a message from your brother.”

“From Tam?”

“Who else is your brother?” Liss pulled a folded scrap of paper out of her sleeve and held it up without giving it to her. “He’s coming home for the harvest fair. Six days. He says the smith’s letting him off for three days, which I’ll believe when he’s sitting at my table, and he says, and I’m reading this exactly, Tell /ˈmɛərən/ I’ve something to show her that’ll make her eyes pop out.”

“That sounds like Tam.”

[dryly] “It sounds like trouble is what it sounds like.” Liss folded the paper and put it away. “Last time he had something to show you it was a dead pike the size of a dog, and he kept it under his bed for a week.”

[calm] “He was nine.”

“He’s nineteen. I don’t know that it’s made a lot of difference.” But her mother was smiling. Then the smile went, and she looked upriver past the bend at the island, and /ˈmɛərən/ followed her look without meaning to.

It sat there in the sun, small and green and harmless, with the stump of its tower and the water splitting around it white at the south end. A place you could have walked round in an hour. A place you’d never think twice about, if you’d been born anywhere else.

[quietly] “I’ll tell you one more thing about that island,” Liss said quietly. [quietly] “Since you asked. And then I’ll not say another word about it, because it’s a fine morning and you’ve a bell to make.”
