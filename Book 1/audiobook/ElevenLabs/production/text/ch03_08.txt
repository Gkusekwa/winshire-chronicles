[hushed] “Before you were born. Before we were married. He was a boy on the river watch, like you were. They were chasing poachers in the fog and he lost the others and came up on the spit before he knew where he was. He was there all night. He never told anybody but me, and he only told me because I asked him why he wouldn’t go near it after, not even in the boat, not even to fish.” Liss watched the island. “He said he didn’t see anything. He said he lay on the spit all night in the fog, with the water going by on both sides, and he could hear something breathing in the trees. Slow. Like a bellows in a forge. All night long. And in the morning the fog lifted and there was nothing there, and he got back in the boat and rowed home, and he didn’t sleep for three days.” [pause]

[calm] “He never said.”

[tenderly] “No. He wouldn’t have. He was a knight; he’d sworn not to ask what he kept.” She said it lightly, like a line of an old song. Then she looked at /ˈmɛərən/, and her face was not light at all. “You’re poling, girl. We’re drifting.”

[calm] /ˈmɛərən/ set the pole. The boat moved. Her arms did what they had always done, and the rope hissed through the rings, and the Greyhold landing came up out of the bright water toward them, stone and red door and the high grey wall above, and she counted the strokes all the way in, and got to forty-one, and did not look upriver again. [long pause]

She made the second bell with half a minute to spare and her boots soaked to the ankle.

Pell was at the gate. He looked at the boots. He looked at her. He had a way of looking at you that made you feel he’d already read the whole of the report you were about to give him and found three errors in it.

“Ashdown.”

“Instructor.”

“You’ve been across.”

There was no point lying to Pell. “Yes, Instructor.”

“And what did you learn on the far bank?”

She thought about the masks and the cart and the barge with no light on it, poled not rowed, slipping down the deep channel under this wall. She thought about a river-watch boat that would have been out on the water, and had seen nothing. She thought about a quiet farmer who didn’t look at knights.

“That they think we watched,” she said.

Pell was quiet for a moment. The yard behind him was filling up; she could hear the beasts, and somebody laughing, and Tobin’s voice saying something about breakfast.
