Chapter Four.

The Chain. [long pause]

A burned barn, they say on the /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ bank, smokes for three days and tells lies for thirty, and the only way to get the truth out of it is to shovel.

/ˈkɔːldə/ had been shovelling since first light.

He’d walked back up from Harrow’s End before dawn with a spade over his shoulder and Wren trotting beside him, because she had woken when he came in and had refused, with a nine-year-old’s terrible patience, to go back to sleep until he promised she could come. Ada had said nothing at all, which was worse than anything she might have said, and had packed them bread and cheese and a jar of honey for /bɛl/, and stood at the door with her shawl round her until they were out of sight.

Now the sun was well up and the hollow at Upper Rush was full of people and smoke. The two worst barns were black ribs and grey ash, still too hot in the middle to stand in. The third had kept its gable end and nothing else. Men from the four farms and a dozen from Harrow’s End and the /steɪð/ were dragging out what could be dragged out: iron from the harness, a plough-share, a scorched cartwheel, the bones of the hay-rack. Old Tolly from the /steɪð/ was poking along the edge of the ash with a stick like a heron looking for frogs, and muttering, and every so often picking something up and turning it over and throwing it away again.

/bɛl/ had given up being angry at about the second hour and gone on to being practical, which in /bɛl/ was harder to watch. She had the little ones, Pip and Nan, set to carrying cups of water to the men, and she had Wren carrying them too, and she was cooking something in a pot over a fire made from bits of her own barn, because, she said, she was damned if she’d waste good wood.

“You’ll want to eat,” she told /ˈkɔːldə/, when he came over for water.

“Later.”

[dryly] “Now. You’ve the face you get.” She looked at him. “Like a cow that’s swallowed a nail.”

[calm] “That’s a good face.”

“It’s your face. I’ve known it longer than you have.” She handed him a bowl. “Eat.”

[tenderly] He ate. It was barley and bacon and ash, and it was good. Wren sat down next to him on the low wall with her own bowl and leaned against his arm and didn’t say anything, which was how he knew she’d been more frightened by the night than she was letting on. He put his free arm round her. She smelled of smoke. Everything did.
