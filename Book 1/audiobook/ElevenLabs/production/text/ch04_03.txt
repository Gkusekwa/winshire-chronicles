[sadly] “I know she’s frightened. I’m frightened.” Joss wiped his face with the back of his wrist, leaving a long black smear. “They had her mother on a rope, Cal. I saw it from the ladder. Mabbin and Old Grey, both of them, roped, and a man walking in front with a lantern like he was taking them to market. Walking. Not even in a hurry. And she was out here in the dark and heard her mother go.” He gripped the gate. “I’ll find them. I swear on my children, I’ll find them, whoever had them, I’ll find them and I’ll bring them home.” [pause]

[calm] “I know you will.”

“You don’t. You’re just saying it.”

That was true. /ˈkɔːldə/ let it stand. Behind them, /bɛl/ was calling the children; behind that, somebody was laughing too loudly at nothing.

“Let me try,” /ˈkɔːldə/ said. [long pause]

He went in through the gate and shut it behind him and didn’t walk toward her. That was the first thing. You didn’t walk toward a frightened animal; you walked near it, and let it decide what you were for. He went along the hedge a little way and stopped and stood there with his hands loose and looked at the alders, and the river beyond them, and the far bank, which was a brown line in the haze. He started talking.

[quietly] He didn’t talk to her. That was the second thing. He talked about the weather, the way his father had, low and steady, nothing in it to be afraid of. He said it was going to be fine later, you could tell by the swallows, and the barley would want cutting at Harrow’s End by the end of the week if this held, and old Bramble had kicked him into the muck yesterday when the dragons came over, and he’d deserved it, honestly, because he’d been showing off with the knife. He said Sorrel’s mother had been the best puller in the Marches and everybody knew it and so did she, and he’d seen her once take a wain of stone up the hill out of Cotter’s Cross that two oxen had given up on, and walk it up like it was empty, and stand at the top and look back down at the oxen like they’d embarrassed her.

[calm] The mare stopped screaming.
