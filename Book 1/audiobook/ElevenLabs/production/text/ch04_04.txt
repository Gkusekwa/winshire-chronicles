[quietly] He kept talking. He talked about the swallows again. He talked about his mother’s honey, and how the bees had swarmed in the spring into the Garrows’ chimney and Davy Garrow had tried to smoke them out and smoked out his own grandmother instead. After a while he sat down on the grass with his back to the hedge, which every horseman he’d ever met would have told him was a fool’s thing to do near a frightened beast that size, and he kept talking, and the mare came three steps closer and stopped and blew through her nose.

[low and steady] “There,” he said. [low and steady] “That’s it. Nobody’s taking you anywhere.”

[tenderly] She came the rest of the way. It took a long time. When she got there she stood over him, enormous against the sky, shivering, and put her long soft face down and breathed on his hair. Her breath smelled of grass and fear.

[quietly] He put his hand up, slowly, and she let him. He sat there with his palm flat on the side of her face and felt her shaking go through his arm, and he went on talking about bees.

[calm] When he looked up, Joss was at the gate with his mouth open, and beside Joss there was a young man on a tall bay horse, in a Clan riding coat with the black hound of the /vɑː/ stitched on the shoulder, watching him with great interest. [long pause]

There were a dozen of them, Clan riders, come down the slope from the lane without anyone hearing them over the work and the smoke. Hard men mostly, older, in leather and wool with axes at their saddles. The young one at the front had a long face that was too thin for him yet, and dark hair tied back, and his father’s nose, and he looked like a man who’d been awake all night for a reason he was angry about.
