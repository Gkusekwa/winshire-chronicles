There were riders on the /ˈhɑːθmɪə/ bank road. A long column of them, small at this distance, going north at a steady pace with the sun on their helmets and a banner at the front he couldn’t read, and out on the water a long patrol boat with six oars going, pulling hard upriver against the current, toward Upper Rush, toward them, as if there were still something to be done.

“Now they come,” said /ˈteɪəʊ/.

“They never cross,” said /ˈkɔːldə/.

“No. But I’ve never seen that many on that road, either. Not in my life.” /ˈteɪəʊ/ watched them. “Somebody over there is frightened of something. I’d like to know what.” He turned back. “You’ve a good eye. You saw the ruts in the dark when nobody else looked. If you see anything else, anything, you’ll come to the hold and ask for me. Not my uncle. Not my brother. Me.” He held out his hand. “Will you?”

/ˈkɔːldə/ looked at the hand.

[tense] The chain was in his pocket. Five links, as long as his forearm, the last one cut clean through. Dark, good metal, heavier than it should be, with a mark stamped on every link that he didn’t know. He could feel the exact weight of it. It would be the easiest thing in the world to put his hand in his pocket and take it out and put it in /ˈteɪəʊ/ /vɑː/’s hand and say: [quietly] this was lying in the yard. This isn’t Clan chain. Whoever this is, they came to take beasts, and they came ready to hold something that fights. [pause]

[calm] He thought about Hollin saying [quietly] ask your uncle , and the riders going still. He thought about whose men the masked men might be, if they weren’t Clan, and whose men they might be if they were. He thought about a chief’s son with his uncle’s warriors behind him and his own grief in front of him, taking a thing like this up to /ˈvɑːhəʊld/ and putting it on a table, and what happened next. He thought about Wren.

[thoughtful] He thought: it’s not my business. It’s never my business. That’s why we’re still here.

[calm] He took /ˈteɪəʊ/’s hand. “If I see anything,” he said.

“Good.” /ˈteɪəʊ/ held on a moment longer than he needed to. “Come to the harvest fair. I’ll be at yours; I’ll be at all of them, this year. My uncle wants every village in the Marches to see a /vɑː/ smiling at it before the moon’s out, and I’m the only one of us who can manage it.” He let go. “I’ll buy you a drink. You can tell me about bees.”
