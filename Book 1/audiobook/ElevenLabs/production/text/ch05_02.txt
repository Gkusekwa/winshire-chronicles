[calm] They rode out of the gate in two lines with Haverly at the front, his falcon on his fist and the banner on the pole behind his saddle, and the sun coming up over the /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ bank. /ˈmɛərən/ rode at the back with Turnip and Tobin and the Darrow girl, whose brindled hound loped beside her stirrup with its tongue out like it was the best day of its life. It probably was. Hounds loved patrols. They loved anything that went somewhere.

“Rear,” said Tobin. “Of course you’re rear.”

“Someone has to be.”

“Someone cleans the privies too. Doesn’t make it an honour.” He was riding a bay that was nearly as bad as Turnip and sitting it worse. “You know why they put us at the back? Ambush. Lose the back, you’ve lost nobody.”

“They put me at the back,” /ˈmɛərən/ said, “because I can see the whole line from here, and I’ll notice if something goes wrong before Haverly does. Pell told me yesterday.”

“Did he?”

[dryly] “No. But it’s true.”

[calm] Tobin laughed despite himself. The Darrow girl smiled down at her hound and said nothing. She almost never said anything. She was seventeen and had been bonded since she was twelve, and when she looked at the hound you could see the two of them talking, or something like talking; it was like watching two people share a joke across a crowded room. /ˈmɛərən/ didn’t look at it for long. She never did. [long pause]

Turnip stopped dead twice before Redwatch. The second time /ˈmɛərən/ leaned down and blew in his ear, as Ottery did with the boat ponies, and he walked on, which might have been the breath and might have been that he’d finished thinking.

At Redwatch she asked Pell for leave to water the horses, and Pell looked at her for a moment as if he knew precisely what she wanted, and gave it.
