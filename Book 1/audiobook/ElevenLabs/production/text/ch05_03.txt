Redwatch was a square tower of red sandstone half a league back from the river, with a boathouse on the bank below it where the river watch kept its long boats. /ˈmɛərən/ had served out of it for two winters. She knew the smell of it, tar and wet rope and cold stone, before she’d even turned Turnip down the track. Sergeant Ottery was sitting in the boathouse door mending a net with his feet up on a barrel, exactly as she’d left him two years ago, as if he’d been sitting there waiting for her the whole time.

“Ashdown,” he said, without surprise. “Look at you. Nearly a knight.”

“Nearly.”

“First patrol. I saw them go by. I’d have waved, but my arm’s tired.” He looked at Turnip. “They gave you the turnip horse.”

“They did.”

“Course they did.” He didn’t get up. He’d never got up for anyone below a captain. “What do you want? You’ve the face you used to get when you’d found a body in the reeds.”

She sat down on the bollard opposite him. “Two nights ago. The night Upper Rush burned. Who had the boat?”

[tense] Ottery’s needle stopped. [pause]

[calm] “Nobody had the boat,” he said.

“Somebody always has the boat. There’s always one out at night. That’s the whole point of the river watch.”

[tense] “Nobody had the boat.” He started mending again, slowly, as if the net were very interesting. “Rider came at dusk. Before the fires. Greyhold seal on the order. Boats in and stay in till dawn; the orders are moving on the river tonight and don’t want to be mistaken for anyone else. So we brought the boats in, and we stayed in. Then the fires went up, and we stood on the bank and watched them like everybody else, and nobody moved on the river tonight but a barge going down with no lights on her, quiet, in the deep channel, which none of us is supposed to have seen. Because we were in. Like we were told.” [pause]

[calm] The river slid by below the boathouse, brown and quiet.

“Who signed it?” said /ˈmɛərən/.

“I didn’t look. It had the seal. You don’t look past the seal, girl, not on the river watch.” He glanced up at her. “Have I just told you something I shouldn’t have?”

“I don’t know yet.”

“Well, when you do,” said Ottery, “do me the courtesy of forgetting where you heard it.”
