[tense] It was a good place for one. That was the first thing /ˈmɛərən/ thought, and she disliked herself a little for thinking it. The road came round a long bend with the wood close on one side and a steep bank down to the water meadows on the other, so that anything coming either way had to slow, and couldn’t leave the road without breaking a leg. In the middle of the bend somebody had pulled a heavy farm cart across the road and chained its wheels. Behind it, and in front of it, and lounging against the shrine’s little stone roof, there were fourteen men. [pause]

[tense] She counted them twice. Fourteen. Good boots, plain coats, no badges, no colours. Swords, mostly short ones, the kind you could use in a crowd. Two crossbows. Nobody was masked. That was the second thing she noticed, and she found she’d been expecting masks, and was angry at herself for expecting them. [pause]

[calm] There was a queue in front of the cart. Two drovers with a flock of sheep, a pedlar with a handcart, a family in a wagon with a cow tied behind, and a heavy carrier’s dray with a crate on the back. The men at the cart were going through each one, slowly and politely, looking under covers, looking into sacks, lifting the cow’s tail as if they expected to find something under it.

“What in the gods,” said Tobin.

Pell held up his hand, and the patrol stopped. He sat his horse for a long moment, looking. Then he rode forward alone at a walk, and the men at the cart turned round and watched him come, and one of them, a square, greying man with a clean-shaven face, put down the sack he was holding and came to meet him with his hands open and in plain view.

/ˈmɛərən/ couldn’t hear them. She could see. She watched the square man reach into his coat, slowly, with his other hand still open, and take out a folded paper, and hand it up. She watched Pell read it, all the way down, and then read it again. She watched his face not change at all.

Pell rode back.
