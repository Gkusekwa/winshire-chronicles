[perfectly flat] “They have a licence,” he said, to all of them. His voice was perfectly flat. [perfectly flat] “From the magistrate at Ashford. To stop and search traffic on the River Road for stolen beasts, in light of the raids, and to hold any beast that answers the description of one reported stolen, against a receipt, until the magistrate has looked into it. It’s sealed. It’s dated three days ago. It appears to be genuine.” He looked at Haverly. “Your bird. Can she take a message to Greyhold and bring one back?”

[calm] “Faster than any horse,” said Haverly, and for once it wasn’t boasting; it was just true.

Pell wrote on a scrap from his saddlebag, leaning on the pommel. Haverly held up his fist and Kesh stepped onto it and then onto his forearm and stood very still while he tied the scrap to her leg, and the two of them looked at each other for a moment, and then she went up off his arm in three hard beats and over the trees and was gone south.

“Now,” said Pell, “we wait.” [long pause]

They waited at the bend for most of two hours, and while they waited the roadblock went on doing what it was doing, politely, under the eyes of a Greyhold patrol.

They let the drovers through, after counting the sheep. They let the pedlar through, after turning out his entire handcart in the road and putting it all back again. They let the family through, cow and all, after the cow had been looked at from every side by a man who plainly knew cattle. Then they came to the carrier’s dray, and the crate.

It was a beast crate, /ˈmɛərən/ could see that from where she sat: slatted, iron-bound, with straw in the bottom and air holes in the lid. Whatever was in it was small, and quiet, and every so often it moved, and the whole crate creaked.

“Papers,” said the square man to the carrier.

The carrier had papers. He had them out before he was asked; he’d been holding them for half an hour. The square man read them slowly. Then he lifted a slat with a knife and looked in. Then he looked at the carrier, kindly.

“This doesn’t match,” he said.
