“Beasts,” the old drover was saying. “That’s all anyone’s after, this year. Beasts for this, beasts for that. Over our side it’s the Clan, mostly, and now the old chief’s dead it’ll be worse.”

“Worse how?”

“Brother and sons.” The drover spat. “Haldor had a brother, /ˈʊlrɪk/, who’s the best fighter in the Clan and knows it, and has the most men in his hall, and has wanted his brother’s chair since they were both in skirts. And Haldor had two sons, the elder who’s proud as a cockerel and the younger who’s well liked, and the elder can’t stand the brother and the brother can’t stand the elder and neither of them can stand the younger for being liked. You mark me. They’ll be at each other’s throats before the barley’s in.”

“Masked men,” said Tobin. “Upper Rush. Who were they?”

The drover gave him a long look. “Nobody over our side knows who they are. Some say Clan. Some say not.” He nodded at the roadblock. “Some say them.”

“Who are they?”

[lowered voice] “Depends who you ask.” The old man lowered his voice, which made /ˈmɛərən/, who’d been pretending not to listen, listen harder. [lowered voice] “In Wendmouth they call them the Restoration. Say there’s a man going round the ports and the villages, both sides of the river, telling people the six kingdoms are rotten and it’s time the land was one again, like in the old days, before the six. Fine speaker, they say. Gives you hope and then gives you a job. Half the dockhands in Wendmouth have been to hear him.”

[calm] “One man?”

“One man.” The drover nodded, slowly, enjoying himself now. “And here’s the thing. My nephew heard him speak on the quay at Wendmouth, middle of the day, middle of the market, a month back. Hundreds of people. Spoke for an hour. And the very same day, the same hour, near enough, there was a raid on a farm down past Stillhaven, on your side, and the farmer swears blind it was the same man led it. Saw his face by the torches. Same man. Tall, quiet, didn’t raise his voice the whole time.”

“Stillhaven’s forty leagues from Wendmouth,” said Tobin. “More.”

“So it is.”

“Can’t be in both, then.”
