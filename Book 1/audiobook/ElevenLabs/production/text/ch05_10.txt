[hushed] “They’ll never give you a horse worth riding, you know,” he said. [quietly] “Not if you put every one of them on his back.” [pause]

[tense] She didn’t answer. She didn’t look at him. She looked straight ahead, between Turnip’s ears, at the road, and rode on round the bend, and only when she was sure she was out of sight did she let out her breath. [long pause]

[calm] They came back at dusk, all eight of them, and nobody talked much on the way. At the bend by the shrine the cart was gone and the men were gone, and there was nothing to show they’d ever been there but some trampled grass and a track going off into the trees on the north side, two wheel ruts deep in the leaf mould, heading for the river.

/ˈmɛərən/ reined Turnip in and looked at the ruts. Pell stopped beside her.

“Instructor,” she said.

“I know.”

“They’re heading for the water. They had a beast in that crate that matched nothing, and they’re heading for the water. And the river watch…” She stopped.

“Go on.”

She didn’t go on. She thought about Ottery, and the seal you didn’t look past, and the face he’d had when she asked. She thought about what it would mean, said out loud in the road in front of Tobin and Haverly and the Darrow girl. She found she couldn’t make herself say it.

Pell waited, and when she didn’t speak, he nodded as if she had.

[tense] “When you get in,” he said, “you’ll sit down with paper and write down everything you saw today. Every face. Every word that drover said. How many men, how many crossbows, which one talked and which one decided. The track, the ruts, the crate. All of it.” He looked at the trees. “You count things, Ashdown. Count those. And then you’ll give it to me, and nobody else, and you’ll forget you wrote it.” [pause]

[calm] “And then?”

“And then we’ll see what happens to it,” said Pell, “and that’ll tell us something too.”

He rode on. After a moment, Turnip followed without being asked, which was the first time he’d done that all day. [long pause]
