There were Clan men in from the outlying farms, loud, in mourning grey with black armbands. There were drovers. There were a few /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ traders with their purses inside their shirts, and a charm-seller from Stillhaven going table to table with a tray of knotted cords and stamped tin tags for dry barns and fat ewes, every one of them warranted, he said, by the Tide Council itself. He didn’t try /ˈkɔːldə/’s corner. Farmers from Harrow’s End had a look. And at the long table under the window, taking up more of it than they needed to, there were six or seven men who weren’t any of those things: hard, easy, weathered men with good boots and bad manners and long knives, and a look about them like dogs that had been fed by too many different hands.

Sellswords. /ˈkɔːldə/ had seen them before, at Cotter’s Cross, at the hiring yard. You saw more of them every year.

One of them was complaining. He had a voice made for complaining, high and aggrieved, and he didn’t care who heard it.

“Bank road,” he was saying. “Bank road on the /ˈhɑːθmɪə/ side. I’ve used that road ten years, the stretch from Northwatch down to Millbrook, and I’ve seen a knight on it maybe twice. Twice, in ten years. Now they’re on it every day. Columns of them. Patrols with students, gods help us, children on horses with birds on their fists. Stopping carts. Asking questions.” He drank. “What’s that about, eh? What’ve they got to be frightened of, over there? Nothing ever happens over there.”

“Upper Rush,” said one of the others.

[angry] “Upper Rush is on this side. They watched it burn. They didn’t cross.” The complainer slapped the table. “And now they’re riding about like it was their barns. You know what I think? I think they know something. I think something’s coming, and they know it, and they’re not telling anyone, and in a month there’ll be no honest work left on that road for a man who wants to move a crate without some knight’s brat lifting the lid on it.”

[calm] “What’s in your crates, Nolly?”

“Nothing that’s anybody’s business.”

They laughed. /ˈkɔːldə/ ate his stew. He thought about a column of riders on the far bank road, going north in the sun with a banner at the front, and a long patrol boat pulling hard upriver against the current. [quietly] Now they come, /ˈteɪəʊ/ had said. [quietly] Somebody over there is frightened of something.
