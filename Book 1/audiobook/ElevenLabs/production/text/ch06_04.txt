[calm] He thought about a chain under a stone in a byre, and was glad, very glad, that it wasn’t in his pocket. [long pause]

He came over the last rise in the afternoon of the second day, with the rain blowing through, and saw /ˈvɑːhəʊld/ for the first time in four years.

It sat in a hollow under the cliffs where the /ˈskɑːfɛl/ broke off in a long black wall, with Hollow Beck coming down past it in white falls from somewhere above. A turf rampart went round it, high as a house, with a single gate. Inside the rampart, the long-houses stood in a ring: black stone, low, turf-roofed, with smoke coming out of the roof-holes. In the middle, bigger than any of them, stood the chief’s hall, and its roof shone even in the rain, because it wasn’t turf. It was dragon-scale. Hundreds of years of shed scales, collected and pinned in overlapping rows like slate, green and bronze and rust-red and black, so that the whole roof looked like the back of some great beast asleep in the hollow.

And above it, in the cliff face, were the Roosts.

[in awe] He’d forgotten the Roosts. Or he’d remembered them smaller than they were. They were caves: dozens of them, cut or worn into the black rock in rough rows going up the cliff, each one big enough to stand a house in. The mouths were scorched black. And in the mouths, and on the ledges between them, and turning slowly in the grey air in front of them, there were dragons. [pause]

[in awe] More than he could count. Some were asleep on the ledges with their wings folded and their tails hanging down the cliff. Some were coming in from the moor, low and heavy, with sheep in their claws. Two were fighting, or playing, in the air above the falls, rolling over each other with a sound like canvas tearing. The noise of them came down the hollow on the wind: hissing, and a deep grinding sound like rocks in a river, and every so often a long cry that made the hair stand up on his arms. [pause]
