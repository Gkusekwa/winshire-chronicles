The hold was full of grey. Grey cloaks, grey armbands, grey ash from the long fire still burning in the middle of the yard where Haldor’s pyre had stood, and would stand, banked and fed, until the new moon. People stood about it in knots, talking low. /ˈkɔːldə/ drove Sorrel round the outside of the ring to the steward’s barn, as you were supposed to, and on the way he saw it.

The yard was split.

[tense] There was no fence. Nobody had built anything. But on the east side of the pyre, toward the gate, the men in grey were big, older, hard, with axes at their belts and the look of men who had fought in the Clan’s raids on the /ˈsɔːltriːtʃ/ coast twenty years ago and never really stopped. On the west side, toward the hall, the men were younger, and their armbands had been sewn with a thin line of red, and they stood closer together, and they watched the east side the whole time. In between them there was a space, ten or twelve paces wide, round the fire. Nobody stood in it. People walked round it. [pause]

[calm] He’d heard about it at the Ram’s Head. He hadn’t believed it until now.

The steward was a sour old man with ink on his fingers who counted /ˈkɔːldə/’s sacks twice and wrote them down and said the barley was damp, which it wasn’t, and gave him a token for the levy and told him he could feed his beast in the lower stable and get himself a bowl in the hall if he wanted one, the dead chief would have wanted it, and was there anything else.

“No,” said /ˈkɔːldə/.

[dryly] “Then move your cart. There’s a wain from Ewe Hollow behind you and they’ve brought oats, gods help us. Oats. For a chief.” [long pause]

[calm] He went into the hall because he was hungry and because it would have been rude not to, and because some small, stupid part of him wanted to see it.

It was long and dark and full of smoke, with tables down both sides and a great hearth in the middle and the scale roof going up overhead into shadow. Light came through it here and there where a scale had slipped, in green and bronze spots on the floor. It was crowded and loud. And it was split, like the yard. He saw it as soon as his eyes adjusted. The east tables and the west tables. Grey and grey-with-red. The space down the middle, round the hearth.
