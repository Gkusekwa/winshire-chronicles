At the far end, on the dais, there were two high tables, side by side, with a gap between them. In the gap, on its own, stood a big carved chair with a grey cloak thrown over it. Empty.

At the right-hand table sat a big man with a grey beard cut short and a face like the /ˈskɑːfɛl/ on a bad day. He was eating as if the food had insulted him. Around him sat a dozen men who looked like smaller copies of him. /ˈkɔːldə/ had seen him once, years ago, at a dues-gathering, and remembered him as the largest man he had ever seen. He wasn’t. It just felt like that.

/ˈʊlrɪk/ /vɑː/. The old chief’s brother. The best warrior in the Clan, everyone said, and the man with the most men in his hall. Everyone had always said he should have been chief, and then said it quietly.

At the left-hand table sat a young man of perhaps twenty-five, lean and dark, with Haldor’s hawk nose and a face that had been handsome before grief and temper had got into it. He wasn’t eating. He was drinking, steadily, with his eyes on the empty chair, and the young men round him kept glancing at him like a pot that might boil over.

Arn /vɑː/. The elder son.

[tense] And /ˈteɪəʊ/, the younger, wasn’t at either table. /ˈteɪəʊ/ was coming down the middle of the hall, through the empty space round the hearth that nobody else would walk in, with a jug in one hand and two cups in the other, smiling at someone on the east side and someone on the west side and stopping to say something to an old woman by the fire that made her laugh. He walked down the middle of that hall as you’d walk across a frozen river, easy and careful at once. [pause]

[calm] He saw /ˈkɔːldə/ in the doorway and his face lit up.

[dryly] “Fenn! The bee man!” Half the hall turned round. /ˈkɔːldə/ wanted to be underneath the floor. “You came. You actually came. I said you would. I told /ˈɪmrə/, I said, he’ll come, he’s the kind who pays his levy himself.” /ˈteɪəʊ/ put a cup in his hand and filled it. “Did you bring the mare? The sand-coloured one? Tell me you brought the mare.”

[calm] “She’s in the lower stable.”

“She came up the Way? Under the Roosts?” /ˈteɪəʊ/ whistled. “Brave beast. Most horses won’t come past the falls. Come on. Come and eat. Sit with me.”

“Where do you sit?”

/ˈteɪəʊ/ looked down the hall at the two high tables and the empty chair between them. Something went across his face, very quick, and was gone.
