And when the thirty nights are over, there’ll be a man in that chair, and /ˈteɪəʊ/ will be standing between two fires with nothing in his hand but a jug. [long pause]

[calm] “You’ve met nobody yet,” /ˈteɪəʊ/ said afterwards, outside in the wet dusk. “You can’t come to /ˈvɑːhəʊld/ and meet nobody. Come on.”

He took /ˈkɔːldə/ up the cliff path.

It was cut in the rock, steep and wet, with an iron rail on the outside that /ˈkɔːldə/ held onto so hard his hand ached. It went up past the lowest of the Roosts, close enough that he could smell them: hot stone and something like iron and something like burnt hair. The noise was worse up here. The dragons on the ledges turned their heads to watch them climb, and their eyes caught the last of the light and shone.

On a broad ledge a third of the way up, under a cave mouth black with old fire, three people sat round a brazier with their backs to the rock. The wind kept laying the brazier smoke flat across them, and none of them so much as narrowed an eye. Behind them, filling the cave, were three dragons.

“Late,” said the woman nearest the fire. She was old, grey-haired, with a weathered face and a war-leader’s stillness and an old scar through one eyebrow. “And you’ve brought a farmer.”

“I’ve brought the farmer,” said /ˈteɪəʊ/. “The bee man. I told you about him. He settled Joss’s dray-mare at Upper Rush by talking to her about bees. /ˈkɔːldə/ Fenn, /ˈsɪɡruːn/ /həʊm/. /ˈsɪɡruːn/, /ˈkɔːldə/.”

/ˈsɪɡruːn/ /həʊm/ looked at him. She had grey eyes and she used them like a ruler: as if she’d read the whole of him already and found it about as she’d expected.

“Fenn,” she said. “Harrow’s End. Good barley. Your father paid his dues on time for twenty years and never once looked me in the face.”

“Neither do I,” said /ˈkɔːldə/.

“I know. I’ve seen you do it.” She almost smiled. “Sit down.”

Behind her, in the cave, the largest of the three dragons opened one eye.
