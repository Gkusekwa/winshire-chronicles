The third rider dropped off the ledge above, landing on the stone beside the fire with a thump. She was young, perhaps twenty-five, small and wiry and brown, with her hair blown loose and a grin like someone who’d just got away with something. Right behind her, so close it felt like the cliff was falling, came the third dragon, lean and dark green and quick, folding herself down onto the ledge in one long movement like a cat settling, and sticking her long narrow head straight over the rider’s shoulder to look at /ˈkɔːldə/ from a hand’s width away.
