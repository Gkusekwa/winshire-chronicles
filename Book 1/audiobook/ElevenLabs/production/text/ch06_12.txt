They sat on the ledge till it was full dark, the five of them and the three dragons, and /ˈteɪəʊ/ and /ˈɪmrə/ talked and Dag cleaned his axe and /ˈsɪɡruːn/ listened and /ˈkɔːldə/ mostly listened too. Below them the hold’s fires glowed in the hollow, and the great pyre-fire in the middle of the ring, and on either side of it, east and west, two other fires had been lit, one by each table, and you could see from up here how the people were gathered round them in two crowds, with the dark space between.

[quietly] “Two fires,” said /ˈsɪɡruːn/ quietly, at one point, looking down. [quietly] “/ˈvɑːhəʊld/’s had one fire for four hundred years.”

[calm] Nobody answered her.

“It’s thirty days,” /ˈteɪəʊ/ said at last. “Thirty days of bread and ale and singing. That’s a long time. Anything can happen in thirty days. They’ll come round. Uncle /ˈʊlrɪk/’s not a fool, and Arn’s not a fool when he’s sober. I’ll get them in a room. I’ll get them at the ford, where nobody’s watching, and make them talk till they’re sick of it.” He said it as if he believed it. Maybe he did.

“And if they don’t come round?” said /ˈɪmrə/.

[tense] /ˈteɪəʊ/ looked at the empty dark between the two fires. “Then I’ll stand in the middle,” he said, “and they’ll have to go through me.” [pause]

[calm] Dag /rɔːk/ stopped cleaning the axe.

[hushed] Behind them, in the cave, /ˈkærək/ made the millstone noise again, very low. /ˈɔːvɛθ/’s eye opened, gold in the dark, and looked at /ˈteɪəʊ/, and stayed on him a long time. [long pause]
