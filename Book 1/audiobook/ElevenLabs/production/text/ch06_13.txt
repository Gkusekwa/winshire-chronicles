[in awe] The hold lay below him in the grey morning with its scale roof shining wet and its two fires smoking on either side of the pyre. Up on the cliff, the dragons were coming out of the Roosts to hunt, dozens of them, peeling off the ledges one after another and dropping into the air with their wings opening like sails. Three of them came out of the same cave a third of the way up: one great and bronze and slow, one rust-red and heavy, one dark green and quick, and they went out over the moor together in a long line, the green one darting ahead and circling back, ahead and back, like a dog that can’t believe how slowly its people walk. [pause]

[calm] He watched them until they were specks against the cloud. Then he clicked his tongue to Sorrel and turned her down the road toward the river, and home, and Wren, who would want to hear everything, and would want to know, above all, whether the green dragon had asked about her.

[tenderly] He’d tell her yes. It was even true.
