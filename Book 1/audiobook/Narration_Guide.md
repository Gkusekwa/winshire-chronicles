# Narration guide: The Watch Below

For whoever voices the book: a person, or an AI voice directed by these notes. The scripts carry the same directions in machine form.

## The read

- British English, warm and unhurried; intimate, not an announcer. About 150–160 words a minute for narration.
- The prose is lean and rhythmic. Let the sentences end; don't run them together and don't push for drama. Short sentences are meant to land short.
- Read every word as written. Add nothing (no sound effects, no added breaths, no "he said" the text doesn't have) and drop nothing.

## The five kinds of words

The ebook marks these with italics; the audiobook has to mark them with the voice. The annotations say which is which for every span.

1. **Narration.** The default.
2. **Speech** in quotation marks. Characters are told apart by small changes of rhythm, pitch and energy (see `Cast.md`). No invented accents, no caricature.
3. **Bond speech.** Beast and bonded human, mind to mind, in italics with no quotation marks: *Then what?* said Aurveth. This is the book's signature sound. Bring it a little closer and quieter than speech, slightly slower, as if heard inside the head. Each beast keeps its own voice: Orrun slow and deep, Aurveth long and measured, Kharrak in hard fragments, Lisska quick and teasing. When the human answers through the bond, the same closeness applies to them.
4. **Thought and remembered words.** A character's own unspoken thought, or words heard again in memory. Quieter still, without the bond's sense of a second presence.
5. **Written words and verse.** The charter, inscriptions, the paper, the rubbing; the old songs and the verse. A slightly slower, plainer read; verse keeps its line endings.

## Directions

Most paragraphs are plain narration. About a quarter carry a direction, one of: action, tension, dread, quiet, reflective, tender, grief, humour, anger, urgent, awe. Sometimes it comes with a short note ("Wren half asleep"). These are judgements about the scene, not keywords. Follow them lightly. A direction is a colour, not a performance.

Where the text says how a line is said (*very quietly*, *shouted*, *high and wild*), that is noted on the line and should be honoured.

## Pauses

| Where | Silence |
|---|---|
| Between paragraphs | 0.5 s |
| After a paragraph marked grief, dread, awe or tension | 0.8 s |
| Scene break | 1.6 s |
| After "Chapter …" | 0.8 s |
| After the chapter title | 1.8 s |
| After a part title | 2.0 s |

## Names

Use `Pronunciation_Guide.md`. Names marked *confirm* are working choices until the author settles them; once settled they must not drift across the three books.

## Credits

The opening and closing credits are in `00_Opening` and `99_Closing`. They say plainly that the book is narrated by a digital voice; change that line if a human narrator records it.
