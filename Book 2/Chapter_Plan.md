## Premise

The Last Titan is free, and the world finds out what Hearthmere did to it. Hearthmere's lamps are dark, its wells are sour and its ground is all but dead; the Restoration was right, and it has lost the beast it opened the seal to take. Every kingdom now wants a Titan of its own *(canon)*. **Maren Ashdown**, outcast, bonded to the grieving lion Orrun, takes the Titan's road, because Orrun won't let it go: temple to temple, kingdom to kingdom, keeping it out of every crown's chains and getting people out of its way. **Calder Fenn**, outlawed, with one dragon who can't fly, carries the truth about Teo Varr's death to a Clan that won't believe a barley farmer, because the war is still burning his village *(canon)*. When the Clan believes him, it gives him a task of his own: bring home the young dragons Corvin Vale stole from the Roosts, and the men who paid for Teo. That takes him down Corvin's money-trail through Saltreach, to a dead capital in Hearthmere and to the Tide Council's table. And in Vael, on the night every tide-glass in his college breaks at once, a low-ranked scholar called **Idony Vael** watches the hole he's charted under Hearthmere for eleven years close, and something start to move across the world at the speed of a bird. The two protagonists go their separate ways. Their roads cross only where they must, and Idony's work runs between them. Behind the Titan, at every temple, something that has slept since the old realm fell wakes up *(canon)*, first in a flood of wild magic and then in a famine of it. A year after it flew, it comes home for the sixth relic, under Hearthmere, and gets it *(canon)*.

### The rotation *(proposal)*

- **S and F keep strict alternation with each other**, ignoring R chapters: the sequence of S and F chapters always runs S, F, S, F through the whole book. The book opens on S and ends on F.
- **R chapters slot in between**, at a density set by the part: **one in five** in Parts Two and Seven, about **one in three or four** in Part Three (Isvaren, 4 of 14), **one in four** in Part Four (3 of 13), and **one in six** in Parts One, Five and Six (2 of 12 each). There are never two R chapters in a row. **One deliberate silence**: no R chapter from 61 to 71, across his capture (63).
- **Why:** with the two protagonists apart, the book needs a thread between them. **Idony is that thread**: alone in Parts One and Two, with Maren on the Titan's road from Isvaren to Sarovel (because the next temple is where his tide-glasses work), and crossing Calder's road at Vael and again at the end. His density follows the map: highest where the lore is his own.

### The echo device *(proposal)*

- **Apart, which is now almost all the book:** as in Book 1, rumours, sightings, news and signs of the other, with the reader knowing more than either. Added carriers: **Lisska**'s visits (only the six in **Lisska's flights**, plus short hops in the Marches); **Calder's letters**, more legible as the book goes on; and **Idony's work** (a glass rising, a rune he's read, a line in his notes). An echo has to be something the POV character could plausibly see, hear or be told by then.
- **R chapters:** echo **either protagonist**, usually as a reading. Orrun's bond-shaping shows on his glasses as a small moving draw he calls **"the walker"**; three dragons together stir the Deep in a way he can't explain until he meets Calder.

## The temples, the relics and the five wakings

**Canon:** six temples, one per kingdom; seven relics once, because Hearthmere's temple held two (the Last Titan's sleep relic, broken when it was chained, and the control relic); six remain: five sleep relics and one control relic (the sixth relic); each sleep relic taken wakes its sleeper; the control relic lies in Hearthmere's deepest vault, can't be reached until the five relics have made the Titan strong enough, and commands the five; each kingdom was founded, unknowing, over its sleeper. Temple names and sites are from `Atlas.md` (proposals there). The surge and drain are from `Magic.md` §1 *(proposal)*. **The stir** is new here *(proposal for `Magic.md` §1, not yet in the Bible)*.

**Three stages at every temple** *(proposal)*:
1. **The stir.** The Last Titan comes to a temple, lies on it and draws. The Deep runs toward it like water toward a drain: the old stone over the temple fills and hums and its lamps burn white, while the country round goes dead, lamps dimming and charms slipping. It lasts as long as the Titan sits there. This is the pre-relic sign, and the one Idony learns to read: from its shape he can say when the Titan will go down for the relic (16, 29, 33, 36, 44, 58, 70, 74).
2. **The surge.** When the relic is taken and the sleeper's sleep goes out, the local Deep floods (`Magic.md` §1): charms take too hard, wards blaze, drawers burn out, beasts go mad, and even dead ground lives for a while. From the surge he can say when the sleeper will rise (22, 36–37, 45–47, 60–61, 73).
3. **The drain**, when the sleeper rises and goes: the kingdom's magic fails (27, 37, 63).

**Why this order** *(proposal)*: the Titan flies north-east out of the Marches, and the Hollow Ring lies east-north-east of the Old Ruin. From there it goes anticlockwise round the old realm along the Oneroad's ends, which are also the Deep's great tide-lines (41): west to Vael, west again to the Scald, south to the Sun Stair, east along the coast to the Bell, and closes on the hub.

## Magic in the action *(proposal; from `Magic.md`)*

- **Hearthmere is dead ground** from chapter 1: no charms, no healing words, imbued things failing. Haverly's edge is dark there, because runes hold as long as their material *and their feeding* (Magic §10 rule 6); it is dark again in the Ring's dead league (20) and wakes only in Vael's surge (37). Fights there are steel and beasts, which is why the crown buys a heartstone cage: engines don't need the Deep. The Saltreach bank isn't dead (5, 10), so Calder's first workings happen on home ground. **Old magic is the exception:** the lock's lit sentence (4, 81) and the relics aren't fed by the Deep, so dead ground doesn't touch them.
- **Live ground at the Ring:** in the league of dead ground only the old-realm ring-stones are live, so the Wardens must stand on them to draw, and that makes them Maren's target (20). Her finding-knot works only through Orrun there (18, 23).
- **Stir, surge, then drain**, in every kingdom (table above): battles in a stir are drawn toward the temple; battles in a surge go wrong in strange ways and even dead ground wakes; battles in a drain are steel alone.
- **Drawers and Wardens:** Isvaren's Wardens at the Ring (20), broken by running water; Corvin's hired drawer at the Library (38–39); Sarovel's court drawers (60); Saltreach house drawers stranded on the reef (73). Always grounded, short range, one or two releases, and burning.

- **Running water breaks spoken charms:** the beck at the Ring (20); the ferry in the flood (43); the reef at the turning tide (73); Calder's fire-word at sea (73); Maren sending the Marches over the Wend (72). **Saltreach's sea-charms** are therefore runes cut into hulls and oars, not words (70).
- **Charms are touch-range:** Calder's herd-charm lands on one lead cow (26); his hearth-word needs his hands on the boy (33); Maren's stand-word goes into the bank under her hands (48).
- **Quickened edges:** Haverly's sword, dark on dead ground (4, 5, 20, 84) and woken once, in Vael's surge, for the *hold* rune (37). It costs whoever uses it.
