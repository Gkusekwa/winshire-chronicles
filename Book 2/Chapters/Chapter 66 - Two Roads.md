---

She came in at dawn.

From the west. Over the end of the spit. Low, so low over the grey water that her wingtips threw up spray, and slow, slower than he'd ever seen her fly. Labouring. Every beat of her wings like somebody lifting a sack they couldn't lift.

He'd felt her coming in the night. Waking against Aurveth's side on the shingle with the thread in his chest suddenly not empty any more. Something at the end of it. Small. Tired. Coming.

He was on his feet at the water's edge when she came over the last of the sea.

She didn't make the shingle.

She came down short. Ten paces out. Into the sea, with a splash, and went under, and came up, flailing, too tired to fly and too tired to swim.

Kharrak was already in the water.

He waded out. To his chest. And put his head down and took her by the scruff of the neck in his jaws, very carefully, and lifted her out of the sea, dripping, and carried her up onto the shingle, and put her down at Calder's feet.

She lay there with the sea running off her.

*I'm back,* she said.

---

"I know," said Calder.

He was on his knees beside her. He had his hands on her. Her neck. Her side. She was so thin. Thinner than after the Scald. Every rib. Her wings lay out on the stones like wet washing.

*Twenty-five days,* said Lisska. *I counted.*

"I know. I felt."

*I waited for an answer. In the wood. In the city. On the island. On the tower.* She shut her eyes. *She wouldn't write one. And then she did.*

"Rest."

*In a minute,* said Lisska. *I've got to tell you first. I've been saying it in my head the whole way so I'd get it right.*

---

She told him.

Lying on the shingle with the sea running off her and his hands on her neck and Kharrak's head lowered over both of them. Close enough now for the bond to carry it. Not words, all of it. Pictures. Coming through the thread into him, one after another, as Aurveth's bridge had come, but fast, tumbling, a small green dragon who'd seen too much trying to get it out before she fell asleep.

A city on a bay. White. Burning.

A thing standing in the bay. White-gold. With a horn going up higher than anything, and light round its head like the sun.

Hundreds of grey horned beasts in the water round it.

A cliff falling into the sea.

Three lions on a stair. Holding a cliff. Exactly alike.

The lion-woman on her knees on the shingle with her hands in the stones.

And a ship.

---

*A ship,* said Lisska. *At a stair under the palace. Men on it. And they had the reader.*

He went still.

*The one with the stick. Who went with her. Who reads.* Her voice at the back of his head was very small. *They were dragging him. Up a plank. He was pulling back. He's not strong. He dropped his stick in the water.* A pause. *She shouted. I heard her. From across the bay. His name. He couldn't have heard.*

"What did you do?"

*I went at them,* said Lisska. *I went low over the water. I was going to knock them off the plank. With my wind.* She shivered. *They had crossbows. On the deck. Lots. They shot at me. I went up. I went in again. They shot again.* Very small. *I couldn't get near. I followed it to the end of the bay. Then it went out on the sea. East. And I came back. Because she was there.*

He sat on the shingle with his hand on her neck.

Aurveth, behind him, said: *That's what I felt.*

He turned his head.

*Two nights ago,* said Aurveth. *When I woke. When I looked south-west and wouldn't say.* She was very quiet. *That was her turning for home with it. At the end of the thread. I didn't know what it was. I knew it was bad.*

---

The letter was on her leg.

He untied it with his fingers. She held still for him. She was asleep before he'd got the string off.

He knew it before he unfolded it. His own page. The Blackwater one. Grey paper, the back of a Council notice from Corsa, with his charcoal on it. *The man who pays them is at Solenne with you.* He'd written that. *Be careful of him.*

He turned it over.

Her hand. Square. Big. Plain. Written so he could read it.

He read it on the shingle. Himself. At once. Without his finger. He didn't need his finger for her hand any more.

Idony taken, onto a ship, going east. The ring under Greyhold, below the chains. Corvin knowing where. And the last line, on its own.

*Going home. Keep your coast.*

---

He sat with it a long time.

The sun came up over the sea behind the reef. The salt-wives came past him along the spit in their line, with their cups, and poured the brine back into the sea at the end of the shingle for the Lady, and went back to the pans. Kharrak lay down along the stones beside Lisska with his wing half over her.

*The ring is under Greyhold.*

The held breath. The dead-still place on the reader's chart, under the river hill, where all the lines went in and nothing moved. *Nobody knows what that is,* the reader had said, on the frost, with his finger on it.

Now somebody did.

And so did the man who'd paid for everything. The man who'd paid for the ford and the gate and the war and the four young ones in a trench of smoke. Who'd sat at a king's table in Sarovel and been given dinner. Who had the roll. And the reader. And the words in the reader's head.

The ship had gone east.

Along the coast. West of here. To the Wend's mouth, and up the river. Home.

---

He saw it on the shingle.

Two roads.

All year he'd been on one. His. The Clan's errand, from the black water, at the pour, with Arn's hand and Ulric's hand on the urns. *Bring the four home. Bring back the names of whoever paid.* He'd followed it from the Fleece to the mint to the camp in the Fells to the Library hill to Blackwater. He'd followed the coin. The man who paid.

And she'd been on the other. The Titan's road. Round the ends of the wheel he'd drawn on the frost. The Ring. Vael. The Scald. The Sun Stair. Going round, toward the middle.

He'd thought they were two roads.

They weren't. They'd never been. The man who paid was going where the Titan was going. To the middle. To the round place at the end of the bridge, and under it, and below the chains.

His road had caught up with hers.

---

He wanted to go.

He sat on the shingle with the letter in his hands and wanted it so badly he couldn't sit still. To get up on Kharrak's back now, this morning, and go north. Over the Ashlings, over the Marches. To the river. To Greyhold, where Liss was. Where Maren was going, on foot, with a lame lion, from the other end of the world.

*Liss says come home.*

He'd written it for Liss. He hadn't thought it was for him.

---

He looked out at the reef.

Two leagues off the end of the spit. Black. Low. The line of white where the waves broke on it. He couldn't hear the bell. It wasn't low water. But he could feel it.

He'd been able to, a little, since the night on the rock. Nothing like what Aurveth had shown him. A little. Through his feet, through the shingle. A slow cold pull, going in toward the reef, under everything. And under it, something breathing. Shorter now. Quicker.

The reader's numbers. On the back of his own letter. *After that the coast. The reef by Gullhaven. A month after. Your coast.*

A month after the Sun Stair.

Three weeks, then. About. Less.

He looked at the spit behind him. At the houses along the shingle, a league of them. At the white crosses on the roofs. Four hundred and twelve. At the salt-wives at the pans, and the oyster-men on the flats, and the children on the jetties, and the smoke going up from the smoke-houses into the morning.

Four thousand people. On a spit of stones. With nowhere to go but up.

His own coast.

---

Arn found him at noon.

Still on the shingle. Still with the letter. Arn came down from the root of the spit where the dragons were and stood beside him and looked at the reef with his hands in his belt.

"Ada says you've had word," he said.

Calder gave him the letter.

Arn could read. Better than Calder. He read it slowly anyway, both sides, and gave it back.

"Go," he said. "If you want."

Calder looked at him.

"The Clan can lift them," said Arn. "Off the roofs. Twenty pairs. Forty baskets. We've had a month's practice. We don't need you to do it." He looked at the reef. "Go to the river. Go to her. Go after the man who paid. That was your errand. The black water gave it you."

---

"No," said Calder.

Arn waited.

"I said I'd keep it," said Calder.

He didn't know he was going to say that until he'd said it. *Keep your coast.* She'd written it on the back of his own page, in her square hand, plain, so he could read it.

"It's my coast," he said. "It's my people. Not the Clan's. Mine. The Marches folk and the Saltreach folk. I've been on this side of the river all my life." He looked at the spit. "Four thousand people who think a woman under the water keeps their stones. And a Council in Corsa that's sold the reef to the highest bidder. And a fleet coming." He folded the letter. "Somebody's got to be on the spit who knows what's coming. Who's felt it." He put the letter in his coat. "I'm staying."

Arn looked at him.

"Then when the coast's done," said Arn, "the Clan goes to the river."

Calder turned his head.

"All of us," said Arn. "Red and grey. Every pair on the ledges. When the Bell's up and the spit's off, we'll go to the river. To Greyhold. To whatever's under it." He looked at Calder. "Tell her that."

---

He kept Lisska a week.

She needed it. She slept for two days on the shingle against Kharrak's side with his wing over her, and woke, and ate a whole sheep, and slept again. On the fourth day she flew a little, round the harbour, and came down, and slept again.

*I'll go again,* she said. That evening. *When you've written it.*

"When you're well."

*I'm well.*

"Say it twice."

*I'm well,* said Lisska. *I'm nearly well.*

---

He wrote it at the Anchor-and-Net that night.

At the table with the bad wine on it, with Ada across from him. She didn't fold her arms this time. She didn't say anything at all. She sat and watched him write, and when he'd finished she read it and nodded and gave it back.

A page. The Clan's promise, first. In big letters. *WHEN THE COAST IS DONE THE CLAN WILL COME TO THE RIVER. ALL OF THEM. ARN SAYS.*

Then the rest. The crosses on the roofs. The salt-wives and their cups. The letter he'd written to the Tide Council, three times, so they couldn't pretend. The rider waiting on the Tidehall's steps for an answer that hadn't come. A Corsa fishing boat, the day before, that had come in to the harbour with word of green sails off Corsa: the Strand house's fleet, gathering.

*I am staying for the Bell,* he wrote. *It is my coast.*

*Keep the door.*

He signed it. He gave it to Ada. She read it and gave it back without a word, and he folded it and put it in his coat for the day Lisska was ready.

---

He went out on the shingle at dusk.

To the end of the spit. Where the salt-wives poured the brine in the mornings. He stood at the water's edge with the reef out there in the last light, and the line of white where the waves broke on it, and felt it breathe in, under his feet, slow and cold and getting shorter.

He took her letter out again.

He didn't read the first two lines. He knew them. He turned it to the last one.

*Going home. Keep your coast.*

He read it. Once.

Behind him, along the spit, the salt-wives were coming back from the pans in the dusk with their arms white to the elbow, talking, laughing, past the roofs with the white crosses on them, going home.
