---

Arn kept the Clan on the spit.

He said it at dawn, on the shingle, to every rider. The wave was the Clan's work. The reef was the Council's. Nobody went out to the reef. Every pair stayed by its roofs. Every basket ready.

He looked at Calder.

"You're going out," he said.

"Somebody's got to see it," said Calder. "Who'll tell it after."

Arn looked at him for a while. Then he nodded, once, and went back to his roofs.

---

Aurveth stayed on the shingle.

She couldn't fly two leagues out and two back and then a day of lifting. She knew it. She lay at the spit's end with her chin on the stones and her old gold eyes on the sea, and Ada sat beside her on an upturned boat with her shawl round her.

*I'll be in your head,* said Aurveth. *As far as it reaches.*

"I know."

*Watch the tide,* she said. *Not the beast. The tide.*

---

He went out at mid-morning.

On Kharrak. With Lisska under them, low over the water, her shadow going along the grey sea beside his. Out past the harbour mouth and the packed reefwyrms. Over the oyster beds. Toward the line of breakers, two leagues off, getting lower every hour as the tide went out.

Beyond them, the fleet. Eleven ships in a line, sails furled, with their scorched hulls. The engine-barges in the middle with their chimneys smoking. The harpoon-ships with their bolt-throwers wound back.

And Vey's small black ship. With two shapes on its deck, unhooded now. Black-green. Watching the sea.

And beyond the fleet, the island that wasn't an island. Lying in the deep water. Not moving.

---

The tide went out all morning.

He circled. High enough to see. Low enough to see well. The breakers came down. Lower. Further apart. The water inside their ring went flat as a pond.

A little before noon, something black came up out of the middle of it.

The reef.

He'd seen it once. At dusk, with Aurveth, before he lay on it with his face on the barnacles. A hundred paces and more. Weed, brown and green. Pools. The very old smell under the salt.

It came up out of the sea and the last of the water ran off it in sheets.

Then the bell.

---

It came up through everything.

The rock. The water. The air. Kharrak's body under him. His own. Up through his legs and his back and his ribs into his teeth. Long. Low. A note out of the reef, out of somewhere under the reef, so deep it wasn't a sound so much as a weight.

Kharrak flinched in the air.

Under them, Lisska shuddered all along her length.

---

The Titan stood up.

Out of the deep water beyond the breakers. He saw it come up. Water pouring off it in sheets, in rivers, off the long rough ridge of its back. The plates. The great head coming up last, streaming. The chain-ends hanging off its legs, twelve, iron, black with weed.

In its back, four places where the stone had grown over something.

It walked onto the reef.

Through the breakers, which broke round its legs and went white. Up out of the sea onto the black rock, and the rock cracked under its weight with a sound he heard over the bell.

It put its head down.

Into the reef. Its jaws open. Its head going down into the rock as if the rock were water.

---

The horns went in the fleet.

All along the line. He heard them over the bell. Then the bolt-throwers.

Five harpoon-ships, five bolt-throwers, all at once. A sound like trees breaking. The harpoons went out over the water, each as long as a mast, every one cut with runes from the point to the barbs, white in the noon light, with chain paying out behind them off the decks in a black line to the engine-barges.

Three went in.

Under the plates of the Titan's flank. Where nothing should have gone in. He saw them bite. The runes on them blazed as they went, and they went in under the plates as if the plates were cloth, and held.

The Kessmoor engines roared.

On the barges. All four. Black smoke out of the chimneys, a sound like a forge in a storm, the great drums on the barges' decks turning, hauling. The chain came taut. Off the water. Three black lines from the barges to the Titan's flank, dripping.

The Titan's head came up out of the reef.

---

For a moment it was held.

He saw it. He couldn't believe it, and he saw it. The Titan standing on the reef with three harpoons in its side and three chains going taut to the barges, and its head up, not down. Held.

Then Sable and Hask went in.

---

Off Vey's black ship. Low over the water. Two wyverns, black-green, fast, their barbed tails out straight behind them, each with a rider on its neck and something in its claws.

People. The Strand house's drawers. In green. One in each claw.

They couldn't draw on a deck. He'd heard Vey say it to Arn, at the sheds. A drawer drew on the ground under him. A ship's deck over the sea was nothing to draw on. So the wyverns carried them out, two at a time, and set them down on the bare reef, on the wet black rock, a long stone's throw from the Titan's feet, and went back for more.

Eight drawers. On the reef. On their knees on the rock with their hands flat on it.

And Vey's own boat, rowed in through the breakers, and Vey stepping out of it onto the reef in his grey gloves.

---

They drew.

He felt it from the air. The reef was full. The cup was full under it. The drawers knelt on it with their palms on the stone and drew, and poured what they drew into the harpoon-chains, and the runes on the chains lit white from end to end.

The chains shortened.

He saw them. Link by link. The runes going white and the chain coming in, off the water, as if the links were eating each other. Three taut white lines.

The Titan went down on one knee on the reef.

---

It turned its head.

Slowly. And looked along the chains. At the barges.

Then it pulled.

The nearest barge went over. Engine and all. He saw it lift, and tip, and the iron engine slide off its deck into the sea with a roar of steam, and the barge go over on top of it, and men in the water. The second barge came across the water on its chain like a sled across snow, and hit the reef's edge, and broke in half.

The harpoon-ships loosed again.

Two more harpoons in. Under the plates. Five chains now. Three white, two black.

And the Titan's head came round toward the drawers.

---

He was low over the water. On Kharrak. Close.

He saw the head come round. A wall of grey stone the size of a church, streaming, with the eyes in it, and the drawers on their knees on the rock in front of it with their hands flat, drawing, not looking up.

He put his hand on Kharrak's neck.

Where the heat was. Where it came up through the plates behind the jaw, so hot it hurt his palm. He'd done it once before, in the Iron Fells, on a boy's chest. Ada's hearth-word. The lighting word. Two fingers. Three turns. The word on the third turn, and the catch.

He meant to put a wall of fire on the sea. Between the head and the drawers.

He said it.

---

Nothing.

Not a flame. Not a spark. Not a breath of smoke. The word went out of him into Kharrak's heat and out of Kharrak into the air over the sea, and the sea took it, and it was gone.

Running water. Under him. The biggest running water there was.

*That's a kettle word,* said Kharrak.

He banked. Hard. Down over the reef's edge. In front of the head.

*I'll do it.*

---

Kharrak's own fire.

Not a word. Not a charm. What dragons had had before there were words. It came out of him in a long gout, gold, down across the reef's edge between the Titan's head and the drawers, and the pools on the rock boiled, and steam went up white in a wall.

The head pulled back.

For a breath. From the fire. As any beast pulls back from fire.

It was enough. Two of the drawers got up off their knees and ran across the rock for Vey's boat.

Only the dragons' own fire worked at sea. He'd known it. Gunna had said it at the caves, a year ago, about something else. *Our fire's older than your words, farmer. The sea doesn't know it to put it out.*

---

The Titan put its head down anyway.

Into the reef. With five harpoons in its side and the drawers pouring into the chains and the engines roaring.

The reef broke open.

He saw it from the air. The rock splitting under the head, across, along, a crack running the length of the reef and opening, and the sea pouring in, and under the reef: a hall.

Columns. Old. Grey. A dome, broken in, with the sea going in through the break in a waterfall. And hanging in the dome, on a chain as thick as a tree, green with three hundred years and more, a bell.

A real bell. Bronze. The size of a house. Swinging. Ringing, as the sea poured past it.

The Titan's head went down past the bell into the hall.

And came up with the egg.

---

Warm stone. He could see it glow through the water streaming off the jaws. The size of a cart-wheel's hub. Held in the Titan's teeth as gently as a dog holds a pup.

It had been under an hour. From the reef coming up.

The Titan lifted its head. High. And turned it, slowly, over its own shoulder, and laid the egg down into the plates of its back, among the others. And the plates closed over it.

Five.

---

The tide turned.

He felt it before he saw it. Aurveth, in his head, as far as it reached: *Now.* He looked down. At the reef's edge. At the line of the sea round it.

It was coming back.

Over the edge of the reef. Round the rocks. Round the drawers' knees. Running. Water going in over the reef's top in a hundred little streams between the pools, fast, as the tide came in over a flat.

Running water.

---

It broke them.

All at once. He saw it go. The drawers knelt on the reef with their hands on the stone and the sea came in round their knees, between their hands and the rock, running, and the drawing broke. Like a rope cut.

The runes on the harpoon-chains went dark.

All five. White to black in a breath. The chains went slack and fell into the sea.

And the pour came back into the drawers. All of it. What they'd drawn and couldn't send, coming back up their arms into them, with nowhere to go. Three of them went grey where they knelt. In a breath. Grey hair. Grey faces. They fell over in the water and didn't get up.

The rest were on their feet, knee-deep, waist-deep, wading for the boat.

---

Vey was getting them in.

One by one. Standing in the sea to his waist at the boat's side in his grey gloves, taking each drawer by the collar and putting him over the gunwale into the boat. The oarsmen holding it off the rocks.

The last drawer. A boy. Grey to the eyebrows.

Calder brought Kharrak down.

On the reef's edge. On the last of the dry rock, with the sea coming over it. Ten paces from Vey.

"Captain," he shouted. "Get on."

Vey looked round. Water to his waist. His spectacles gone. His face very calm.

"I'm paid for the reef, Master Fenn," he said.

He put the last drawer over the gunwale into the boat and pushed it off the rock with both hands. The oarsmen pulled.

---

The surge came up through the reef.

He felt it come. Through Kharrak's feet on the rock. Through Aurveth in his head, two leagues off, lifting hers. Up. All at once. Out of the broken hall under the reef, out of the sea, out of the stone.

Every rune in the bay went white.

On the harpoons, in the Titan's side. On the hulls of the fleet. On the chains in the sea. White. Blazing. The sea itself, round the reef, white for a breath, as if the sun had come up under it.

The bell rang once more.

Loud. From under the water. Not low now. A clang that went through him like a blow.

The reef cracked across.

Between Kharrak and Vey. He saw it open. A black crack across the rock, ten paces long, a pace wide, five paces wide, and the sea pouring into it.

Vey went with the sea.

Calder saw his grey gloves last. Up. On the water. Then not.

---

Sable and Hask came in screaming.

Over the place. Low. Round and round over the crack in the reef where the sea was going in white. Their riders shouting. Looking.

There was nothing to look for.

They went round three times. Then they went back to the black ship, slowly, and landed on its deck, and folded their wings.

---

The Titan walked off the reef.

Into the deep water. North-east. Slowly. With the harpoons still in its side and the slack chains trailing off it into the sea behind. The fleet's chains parted one after another. Snap. Snap. Like threads.

It went a league. Toward the Saltings.

Then it lay down in the sea. And was still.

---

He looked for Lisska.

He'd lost her. He didn't know when. At the bell, he thought. At the first note. She'd been under him in the air and then she hadn't, and he'd been too busy to look.

He called her.

Through the bond. As loud as he'd ever called anything.

Nothing. The bond was full of the bell. Like a room full of noise. He couldn't hear her in it. He couldn't hear himself.

He shouted. Out loud. Her name. Over the sea.

---

She was on the reef.

At the far end. On the last of the rock, with the sea coming in round her legs. Lying flat on the wet black stone with her wings spread out across it and her chin down, her eyes shut. She'd been there since the bell. Out of reach of his voice.

Kharrak came down beside her and roared.

She opened her eyes.

*It was ringing in me,* she said. Slowly. Getting up. With the sea at her chest. *All of me. In my bones. Like the Roosts, when they cry.* She shook her head. *I couldn't hear you. I could only hear it.*

"I know," said Calder. "Come on."

She came up off the reef with the sea pouring off her and flew home under them, low, all the way, without a word.

---

Aurveth was waiting at the spit's end.

Ada beside her. The whole town on its roofs behind. The Clan's dragons along the shingle. The surge coming up through the stones under his feet as he got down off Kharrak, so strong he staggered.

Every rune on the spit was blazing. The oar-runes on the burnt boats, again. The ward-cuts on the chapel's door. The harbour-master's charm-board. White.

The fish-oil lamps in the windows burned ordinarily. They always had.

*That was the first breath,* said Aurveth.

"Vey's dead."

*I know. I felt the crack.* She turned her old head and looked out at the dark shape lying in the sea a league off. And past it. At the reef. Where the sea was going in.

*The next one's the sleeper,* she said. *Under the Bell. Within the day.*
