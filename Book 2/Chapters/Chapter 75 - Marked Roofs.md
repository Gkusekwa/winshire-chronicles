---

Sable's rider came ashore at dawn.

In Vey's boat. Rowed by two of Vey's men, through the stranded and the half-stranded reefwyrms in the harbour, which hardly moved now. She stepped onto the jetty and walked up the shingle to where Arn was standing by his red.

A lean woman of thirty. Freckled. One ear burned off at the top long ago. A wyvern-rider's leather coat with the company's mark on the shoulder, a grey glove on a black field.

"Nell Tallis," she said. "Sable's." She looked at Arn. "The company's got no captain. Is the Clan's coin still good?"

"For the children," said Arn.

"For the children," said Nell Tallis.

---

She told them what she'd seen.

After dark. She'd flown Sable high, looking for the captain on the water. Round and round over the place where the reef had cracked. High, so she could see further.

"And I saw the coast," she said. "West. Past the Wend's mouth. The Hearthmere shore." She looked west, along the coast, toward where it went away into the morning haze. "Lit. White. Every lamp on it. Town after town. Lights going along the coast as far as I could see, like a string of pearls. And right on the rim, where Stillhaven must be, a glow like a bonfire."

"How long?"

"An hour," said Nell Tallis. "Then dark again. All at once. Like a door shut." She looked at Calder. "What was it?"

Calder thought of Maren. On her landing, with her lion. Under the dead lamps, that weren't dead for an hour.

"The middle," he said. "Answering."

---

He sent Ada up the hill at first light.

In Tamsin's basket. Over her protests. She said she'd seen the sea now and she'd see what it did next, thank you. He said she'd see it better from the hill. She said she could see perfectly well from the Anchor-and-Net. Gunna lifted her off the shingle in the middle of the sentence.

Nan Gorrie was already up there. On her bench at the salt-store's back door, with her white eyes on the sea she couldn't see.

---

The Titan went at mid-morning.

He felt it first. Through Aurveth, lying on the shingle beside him with her eyes shut. She lifted her head.

*It's going,* she said.

He looked. A league off, in the sea toward the Saltings, the dark shape that had lain still all night was moving. Rising. The sea pouring off its back. It stood up in the shallows with the harpoons still in its side and the broken chains trailing, and it walked. North. Through the shallows. Up out of the sea onto the shore where the Saltings came down to the beach. Over the marsh. Inland.

Away. Over the Ashlings, north, into the haze.

*Home,* said Aurveth.

---

The sea went out at noon.

Not a tide. He knew tides now. This was the whole bay. Draining. Toward the Bell. As if somebody had pulled a plug out of the bottom of the sea.

The harbour emptied in a quarter of an hour.

He watched it go. From the spit's end. The water inside the spit's arm going out through the harbour mouth in a rush, brown, and the reefwyrms going with it, and then the water gone and the reefwyrms lying on the mud in their hundreds, thrashing. The jetties standing on their legs over nothing. The boats on their sides.

Then the oyster beds. Bare. A league of them. Grey and black and shining in the noon light.

Then the fleet. The Strand ships heeling over on the mud where the bay had been, their keels showing.

Then, two leagues off, the reef. All of it. More of it than he'd ever seen. Standing up out of a drained sea like a hill.

---

People came down off the roofs.

He saw them. Off the white crosses, down the ladders, out of the doors, onto the shingle. Onto the mud. To look. Walking out onto the bare harbour floor among the thrashing reefwyrms, onto the oyster beds, with baskets.

It was oyster season.

"Back!" Arn was on his red. In the air over the spit. Roaring. He had a voice for it. "Back on the roofs! Get back on your roofs!"

Hessa Pane was running along the shingle with her tin cup in her hand, hitting the salt-wives with it. On the arms. On the backs. Driving them up the shingle toward the houses. "Up! Up! You heard him! Up!"

Some went back.

Some didn't.

---

Then the reef rose.

---

It was its back.

He understood it as he saw it. The reef had never been a reef. The whole of the Drowned Bell, the weed and the barnacles and the pools and the black rock and the broken hall underneath, lifted. Out of the drained bay. All at once. Water and weed pouring off it in falls.

The temple was on its back. The broken dome. The grey columns. The bell, hanging in the dome on its great green chain, swinging, ringing as it came up into the air.

Then the head.

Out of the mud at the reef's near end. Blunt. Grey-green. Crusted with barnacles like a sea-wall. Eyes like rock pools, flat and wet and full of the sky.

Then more of it. And more. Coming up. Out of the bottom of the sea. Until it stood in the drained bay to its belly, and its back was a hill with a temple on it, and the bell rang.

The size of an island.

The size of Gullhaven.

---

It went down.

Forward. South. Into the deep water beyond where the reef had been. Its head going down first, then its back with the temple on it, the bell ringing all the way under.

And the sea came back.

---

All at once.

He saw it come. A line on the sea, far out, where the leviathan had gone down. Grey. Then higher. A wall. The height of the smoke-houses. White along its top. Coming across the drained bay toward them over the oyster beds, faster than a horse.

Arn's horn went.

One long note. The Clan's call. Up.

---

Every pair went up off the shingle.

Forty-odd dragons. Red and grey. Off the stones in a roar of wings, into the air over the spit.

And every white-crossed roof had its people on it.

He saw that, as Kharrak went up under him. Four hundred and twelve crosses. People on every one. A month's practice. They'd done it every day. When the horn went, the roof. When the dragon came down, the basket. They'd laughed at it. They'd queued for it. Salt-boys on dares.

They were on the roofs.

The dragons came down on the crosses.

---

The wave hit the spit.

Over the reef's place. Over the oyster beds, and the people on them, and the baskets. Over the fleet: he saw four of the Strand ships lifted off the mud and rolled over and gone. Over the harbour mouth. Onto the spit.

The net-sheds went first.

The unmarked ones. Wooden. Thin-roofed. He saw them go down into the white water like paper boats. Then the houses along the spit's middle, the little ones, the ones Arn's riders had stamped on and tapped and shaken their heads at.

The marked roofs stood.

The smoke-houses with their stone walls and flat slab roofs built for fish-weight. The chapel. The salt-store. The big houses on the spit's top. Every white cross. Standing in the white water with a dragon on each and the baskets going on and the people going in.

The marks were right.

---

He lost count.

He was on Kharrak. Down on a cross. A smoke-house, the sea going round its walls, eight people on the roof in a knot, a woman with a baby. Into the basket. Six. Up. The other two hanging on to Kharrak's harness. Up. Over the harbour that was sea again, over the flooded road, to the hill above the salt-store. Down. Out. Up.

Back.

Another cross. Another. The smoke-house he'd left going down behind him as he lifted off the next one. Lisska beside him with a basket of her own, smaller, four at a time, fast, in and out. Back and forth. Back. Back.

The second wave came while he was over the spit's end.

It took more.

---

He saw Ulric go in.

The second wave. A net-shed roof near the spit's middle, one of the few marked ones, with a family on it. The wave hit it as Ulric's grey came down. A girl went off it. Seven, eight. A salt-girl, with her white arms. Into the water.

Ulric went off his dragon after her.

Dived. Off the grey's neck. Into the grey sea. Under.

Calder saw the water close over him.

He came up. A long way off. Went under. Came up. Further. He had something.

He had her by the hair.

His grey came down out of the air over them with its forelegs out, and took them both up out of the sea in its claws, the man and the girl, streaming, like a hawk with a fish, and carried them to the hill.

---

Tamsin carried eleven.

He saw that too. The chapel roof. By the harbour-master's. Too many on it: people had run there from the unmarked houses when the horn went, because it was the chapel, because it was stone. Twenty. More. The water to the chapel's eaves.

Gunna brought Tamsin down on it.

The old dragon. Three hundred and twelve years. Grey-green, her burned wing scarred black along its trailing edge. Down on the chapel roof, which held.

The basket took six. Gunna put five more on Tamsin herself. On her back, behind Gunna. On her forelegs. One on her neck in front of Gunna with her arms round it. All holding the war-harness.

Eleven.

Tamsin lifted.

Slowly. He saw her wings go down, and down, and the old dragon go up off the chapel roof with eleven people on her and the sea coming over the roof behind. Over the harbour. Low. So low her feet were in the water. Over the flooded road. To the hill.

She landed on the grass. And lay down on it, and didn't get up for a long time.

"Never once dropped anything," said Gunna. Getting down. To nobody.

---

Aurveth was on the salt-store.

She couldn't fly the hill and back over and over. She knew it. So she'd gone to the salt-store at the spit's root, the fortress-roofed one, the strongest roof on the spit, and lain down on its flat stone top with her wings spread.

And people came to her.

Off the flooding road. Off the houses round the store that weren't going to stand. Climbing up the store's walls on its iron rungs, onto the roof, onto her. Onto her back, her tail, her folded wings.

Sixty of them. He counted them later.

She lay there through both waves. With the sea going round the salt-store's walls, white, to the top of the door. With sixty people on her. With her old gold eyes half shut.

She didn't move.

---

The sheds.

He saw them only once. From high up, turning over the harbour for the hill. Behind it, on the flats. The salt pans. Square, in rows, gone under the sea.

And the sheds along their edges. Thin roofs. Children on them. Every child in Gullhaven over seven.

And wyverns.

Five. Black-green. Small, fast, low over the water, their barbed tails out straight. Sable. Hask. Three more he didn't know: the rest of Vey's company, that Vey hadn't brought to the reef. Coming down on the shed roofs, which wouldn't take a dragon, and wouldn't have taken a dragon's weight, and took a wyvern's.

Three children in each wyvern's claws. Four if they were small. Up. To the hill. Back.

Seven trips each.

The last shed went as Hask lifted the last two off it.

---

It went quiet at dusk.

The sea came back in. No wave. Back to where it had been, and over where the spit had been, and it lay there grey and flat and ordinary, with the evening on it.

The spit's seaward half was gone.

He saw it from the hill. The spit had been a league of shingle curved like an arm. It was half an arm now. The rest was sea. And standing up out of the sea, where the spit's end had been, roofs. Stone roofs, flat, with white crosses on them. The smoke-houses. Standing in the sea with the water round their eaves. Empty.

---

They counted on the hill.

By torchlight. Arn's riders with their lists. The harbour-master with his book, wet through, his hands shaking, reading out the names of the houses one by one. People answering. Or not.

About three thousand eight hundred had been on the spit at dawn.

Three thousand five hundred and some were on the hill.

Two hundred and ninety drowned.

The unmarked houses. Those who'd gone down onto the mud for the oysters and hadn't come back up. Some who'd been on marked roofs and fallen, or jumped, or been taken by the second wave between one basket and the next. Two of Arn's riders and their dragons, who'd gone down into the water off the spit's end with baskets full of people and not come up. Old Asta Dunn of Ulric's ledges and her dragon, who'd gone into the second wave after a family on a roof that wasn't marked, and been rolled.

The harbour-master closed his book.

"It would have been all of us," he said.

He said it to nobody. Then he said it to Arn. Then he sat down on the grass with his book in his lap and didn't say anything else.

---

The boats on the hill were dead.

The ones that had been dragged up in the morning. He went past them in the dark with a torch. Every hull. The runes on them, that had burned five nights ago, and blazed white last night in the surge.

Dark. Not scorched now. Nothing. Cuts in wood.

The leviathan had gone south, into the deep sea, with the temple on its back, and the drain was going after it. Out of the coast. Out of Saltreach. Every charm-cut on every hull from here to Corsa, going dull.

He thought of Corsa. Of the Tidehall's copper roof. Of a soft-voiced man in green.

*The Lady keeps the stones.*

---

Hessa Pane was at the edge of the torchlight.

On the hill's seaward edge. Looking down at the sea where the spit's end had been. With her white arms folded. With her tin cup in her hand.

She stood there a long time.

Then she held it out. Over the edge. Toward the sea, which was too far down to reach.

And poured her brine on the ground.
