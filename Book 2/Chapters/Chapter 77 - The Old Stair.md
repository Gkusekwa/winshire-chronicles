---

Joby Marsh came down the jetty road before dawn.

With a lantern. She heard him knock, and Liss open the door, and an old man's voice in the kitchen, low, hurried. She was on her feet before Liss called her.

He was a groom. Of Greyhold. Sixty, bent, with a face like a walnut. He'd held Hale's horse at the gate when she was a student. He'd kept the servants' gate open for her the night she went to the stair.

"The burial ground," he said. "Ours. Outside the west wall." He held the lantern up. His hand was shaking. "Dug. In the night. Men. Lanterns. I saw them from the loft."

"Dug where?"

"A slab," said Joby Marsh. "Lifted. By a grave." He looked at her. "Garran Vale's. I dug it myself. Thirty-odd years back. He asked for the place."

---

She went up the jetty road in the dark.

With Orrun. Joby behind her with his lantern, puffing. Up past the dead lamps on their posts. Up to the river wall's walk, where she could see the west side of the hill.

The light was coming.

Grey, over the Saltreach bank. The river below her, wide, fast. The new rope going across it from post to post, and Liss's boat already on it, and Tam's lantern going out in the bow as the light came.

She looked south. Down the valley. Out of habit.

And saw them.

---

Specks.

Far down the Wend. Low over the water. Where the river came round the last bend before Millbrook, a long way off, in the grey. Coming north. Up the valley. One. Three. More behind them.

Dragons.

Coming home up the Wend.

She stood on the wall and watched them for as long as she could spare. She couldn't see thread or colour. She couldn't see who. Too far. Small and dark over the river in the dawn, a long ragged line, coming.

*Then come,* she'd written.

---

Orrun lifted his head.

Beside her on the wall walk. Not looking south. Looking up. At nothing. At the grey sky over the downs, to the south-west.

*It has stood up,* he said.

She looked at him.

*Over the hills. Where it lay down. It has stood up, and it is flying.* His mane rose all along his neck. *It is coming.*

"How long?"

*A day. Two.* He didn't take his eyes off the sky. *It is very heavy. It has five in it.*

---

Thurl had his rod on the cellar wall.

She went past the cellar door on the way to the gate, and he was there already, in the grey light, with the rod against the old stone and his eyes shut. He opened them when he heard the lion.

"The draw's moved," he said. "It was south. To the sea. Since the lamps." He took the rod away. "It's south-west now. And moving." He looked at her. "It's coming, isn't it."

"Yes."

Thurl nodded. He put the rod back on its hooks.

"Then I'd better write to Ironhythe," he said. "Once more."

---

Rowan was at the gate.

Maren told her plainly. Corvin. The eddy. The stair under the grooms' ground. Idony. The vault.

Rowan listened to all of it without a word.

"I'll send knights down after him," she said.

"No."

Rowan looked at her.

"The knights are for the cage," said Maren. "And the people. When it comes." She looked at Orrun. "He fits a stair. They don't, against forty men in the dark." She thought. "Put three on the stair's head. With hounds. Nobody comes up it who didn't go down it."

Rowan looked at her a while longer.

"Wynn," she said. Not loud. "Take two. And Ash and Sorrow."

---

Tam brought the lamp.

Liss's. The old oil lamp, brass, with its glass chimney. The one she'd kept all those years when every other lamp in Hearthmere burned out of nothing, because she said you never knew. The one she'd lit every night since the spring.

He brought it up the jetty road with a jar of oil under his arm and put both in her hands.

"She says bring it back," said Tam.

"I will."

"She says it twice," said Tam. He looked at her. Then he put his black hand on her shoulder for a moment, hard, like a blow, and went back down to the forge.

---

The grooms' burial ground was below the grooms' yard.

Outside the west wall. Where the hill went down in a long slope of rough grass to the Brackenwood's edge. No stones. Low mounds, and wooden boards at their heads with names burned in them, most of them gone grey and unreadable.

One stone.

Plain. Grey. A groom's work, the letters cut a little crooked. GARRAN VALE. Nothing else. Not a date. Not *knight*. Not a word.

And beside it, the slab.

---

Dragged aside. A big square of old grey stone, the inner wall's stone, lying on the grass. And under where it had been, the drain: a cut through the turf, a spade wide, a spade deep, filled with rubble. The rubble dug out and thrown aside.

And under the drain, steps.

Going down.

---

Wide.

She hadn't expected that. She stood at the edge of the cut and looked down into it. Wider than the water-gate stair. Wider than the stair under the school. Old grey stone, worn in the middle of each step as if by something very heavy, a long time ago. A high roof, cut round, going down into the dark.

And on the walls, the hooked script.

In rings. All the way down, as far as Joby's lantern reached.

The turf round the cut was trampled. Boot-prints. Many. A drag-mark, as of a hurdle or a sledge. A smell she knew, faint, sweet, in the cold morning air. Honey-smoke.

---

Orrun stood at the stair's head.

He put his head down into the cut. Then his shoulders. He stood there half in and half out of the earth with his mane brushing the roof and was quiet for a long time.

*I know this stair,* he said.

"How?"

*It was made for my kind,* he said. *Before the knights. Before the gate. Before the noon words.* He went down a step. Another. He fitted it. Head and mane and shoulders, with a hand's breadth to spare. *For the ones who kept the door.*

She lit Liss's lamp.

Wynn was behind her with his hounds. Joby with his lantern. She looked at them once.

Then she went down after the lion.

---

The stair went down in long straight flights.

With landings between. Square. The flights all the same length, forty steps; she counted. Then a landing. Then forty more. Turning back on itself. Down.

The walls were carved.

All the way. Rings of the hooked script, cut deep, in bands round the walls and over the roof. She held the lamp up to them as she went. She had Idony's list in the black book in her coat. She stopped on the second landing and took it out and held it up to the wall in the lamplight beside the hooks.

She found words.

Not sentences. Words. She found them as she'd found them in the fallen carving at the Sun Stair, with the sea lapping at the boat. One at a time, going down the list with her finger.

*Keeper.*

*Below.*

*Door.*

And on the fourth landing, cut larger than the others, by itself, over the way down, a word she'd found once before. In the lock's sentence. In the back of the black book.

*Together.*

---

On the sixth landing there were the six.

Carved in a ring on the wall. As at the Ring. As at the Sun Stair. The six shapes. The stag. The coil. The burrower. The horned one. The reef-backed one.

And the sixth. The largest.

And at the door of it, small, cut in the old stone, the lion.

Lying down. With its head up.

Orrun stopped and looked at it.

He didn't say anything. He looked at it for a long time in the light of Liss's lamp. Then he went on down.

---

It got warmer.

Every flight. She took her coat off on the eighth landing and carried it. The air coming up the stair from below was warm, and still, and smelled of nothing. Not damp. Not earth. Like the air in a room where someone has been asleep for a long time.

The walls hummed.

She could feel it through her boots. In her teeth. The same hum as the water-gate stair the night of the lamps. Full. Drawn through.

And through Orrun, the pull. Down. The held breath. She could feel it in him, like a hand on the back of his neck.

---

On the eleventh landing she saw their light.

Far below. Down the well of the stair, where the flights turned back on themselves and you could look down between them into the dark. A glow. Yellow. Lanterns. A long way down. Moving.

And a voice.

Faint. Coming up the stair through the warm air. Reading. In the old tongue. Slow. A voice she knew.

Idony's.

She leaned over the edge of the landing with Liss's lamp and listened. She couldn't make out the words. Just the voice. Going on. Reading something off a wall, as he'd read carvings to her a dozen times. Patient. Tired.

Then it stopped.

The light went round a turn of the stair below and was gone.

---

She went down faster.

Three flights. Four. Orrun ahead of her, his mane brushing the roof. Five.

And the stair ended.

---

A hall.

Round. Domed. Old grey stone carved in rings, every inch of it. Wide enough for four lions. And out of it, seven ways.

Seven doorways. In a ring round the hall's edge. Each one high and round-roofed, like the stair. Each one going down. She held up the lamp and looked at them, one after another. Seven dark mouths. Each with steps going down into it.

The floor of the hall was dust.

And the boot-prints went into all of them.

---

She stood in the middle of the hall and turned round.

All seven. Boot-prints going into every doorway. Not many into each. Two. Three. As if somebody had split forty men into seven parties at the foot of the stair and sent one down each way.

Callis, she thought.

He'd been a knight of the gate. He knew how to lose a hound on a scent.

"Which?" she said.

Orrun stood in the middle of the hall with his head up. Turning. Slowly. From one doorway to the next.

*All of them,* he said. *They all go down. They all go toward it.* He was quiet. *I can feel it under all of them.*

---

She took the one with the most prints.

Four sets. One of them dragging a foot. She thought of Idony, on his piece of spar.

It went down. Steps, then a slope, then steps. It turned. It branched. She took the branch the prints took, and then there were no prints, only stone, and she went on.

There was water on the floor.

A little. A film of it, shining in the lamplight. Then more. Then she could hear it. Ahead. Running.

---

A stream.

Through the old ways. Coming out of one wall and across the passage floor in a channel cut for it, fast and black and cold, and into the other wall. A pace wide. Running.

Orrun stopped at the edge of it.

She felt him go thin. Not much. As she'd felt him at the Scald when the canals ran past him. The earth in him, his hold on the stone, thinning where the running water went through it.

*I don't like this,* he said.

"Can you cross?"

*Yes.* He stepped over it. Slowly. One forefoot. The other. He shuddered as he crossed, all along his length. *I don't like it.*

She stepped over after him.

The draught off the water caught the lamp. The flame leaned. Shrank. Went blue.

Went out.

---

Dark.

Complete. She'd never been in dark like it. Not in the cavern, which had the lock's light. Not on the stair. This was the dark under everything. She held up her hand in front of her face and couldn't see it.

She heard the water running. She heard Orrun breathing. She felt his side against her shoulder, warm.

She sat down. With her back against him. On the wet stone.

*I'm here,* said Orrun.

"I know."

---

She lit it again with Ada's word.

She hadn't known she could. She sat in the dark with the lamp between her knees and the jar of oil, and filled it by feel, and put two fingers on the wick. Ada's hearth-word. The one Ada had taught her on the edge of the Scarfell, fifteen tries and nothing but smoke. Two fingers. Three turns. The word on the third turn, and the catch.

It didn't catch.

She did it again. With her other hand flat on Orrun's side. On the old stone, through the lion. Two fingers. Three turns. The word.

It caught.

Small. A blue spark on the wick, then yellow. Then the lamp, burning, in the dark under the world.

Her fingers went numb. All of them. Both hands. She sat there holding them in her lap and couldn't feel them.

It was enough. She could see.

---

They went on.

For a long time. She didn't know how long. Down. Along. Turning. The ways branched and branched again. She chose. Orrun chose. They followed the pull. It was under all of them. Every way went toward it. None of them got there.

Twice she thought she heard voices.

Once, far off, through stone, a sound that might have been a wyvern, or a man shouting, or water.

Once she found a boot-print in mud by a second stream. One. Going the other way.

They didn't find them.

---

She stopped in a small round chamber at a meeting of four ways.

Carved, like all of it. Rings of the hooked script. Warm. Humming. She sat down against the wall with Liss's lamp beside her and her numb hands in her lap.

*Come down after us,* he'd said. On the shingle. *I'd rather she was there too.*

She didn't know which he'd meant. That he wanted her lost down here in the dark, out of his way, while the big one came. Or that he wanted her at the vault when he got there. With the lion. At the door.

She didn't know which was worse.

---

Orrun lay down in the chamber with his chin on the floor.

Above them, a long way up, through all that rock, she could feel it now. Through him. Not a sound. A weight. Coming. In the sky over the Marches, getting heavier.

*We go up,* said Orrun. *When the light comes.*

"And them?"

*They have gone down where they can wait,* said Orrun. *It is what he wants. To wait at the door until the big one breaks it.* He shut his eyes. *We will go down again by our own door. The one you know. The one with the lock.*

She looked at the lamp. At her hands.

"Will we be in time?"

Orrun didn't answer for a long time.

*It is the door,* he said. *I have always been in time for the door.*
