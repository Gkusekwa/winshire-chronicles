---

Liss had taken his hand.

At the post, in the dusk, with the lantern. She'd got out of the boat and stood on the shingle in front of him and looked at him for a long time without saying anything. Then she'd put her hand out. Big, rough, a ferry-woman's hand.

"You're the farmer," she said.

"Yes."

"You write like a child."

"I know."

She'd held his hand a moment longer. Then she'd let it go, and turned round to help Tam lift Odo's chair out of the boat, and that was all.

---

She didn't go up the Way with the others.

Tam took Odo up it in the dark, with the cart, with the goat and Joby Marsh's grandson. Liss stayed. In a fisher's hut at the Staithe, empty, with a fire she lit with a flint like anyone.

"To see my rope," she said.

---

He walked through the envoys' camp at first light.

On the slope above the anchors. Tents. Horses. Flags hanging limp in the still grey air. People standing in their doorways in their good coats, looking across the river at the hill and the chains. They looked at him as he came through. A man with a red dragon walking behind him. The farmer from the Tidehall.

A square red-bearded man in a leather coat with Kessmoor's iron wheel on the shoulder. Pike's deputy, somebody said. Engineer Tolley. He nodded to Calder and didn't say anything.

A thin anxious man in grey with the Blue College's blue at his cuffs, holding a pair of glasses up to the light. Dane's man. He didn't look at Calder at all.

A brown man in a plain coat, with dust on his boots from a long road and a letter in his hand. He came up to Calder.

"You know the lion-woman," he said. In a Sarovel voice. "Tobin sent me. From the low city. With this." He held up the letter. "For her."

"She's over there," said Calder. He looked across the river. "Under."

The brown man looked at the hill.

"Then I'll wait," he said.

At the top of the camp, by the best tent, two he knew. The thin woman in white from the Quill house, who'd sat at the long black table and not moved when nobody moved. And a clerk in Tide Council blue with a ledger open on his arm, writing. Marrow's. He looked up at Calder once, and wrote something down, and went on writing.

---

The Clan was on the hill above Harrow's End.

All of it. He'd seen the fire come down off the Scarfell in the night, and he'd flown up to look, and he'd come back and lain against Aurveth and not slept.

A hundred and twenty dragons. More. Every pair on the ledges. The old ones who hadn't flown in years. The young ones. Gunna's ledges and Hulda Sarr's. Ivar Leck on his dun, Brindle. The boy Leif and his young brown. Arn's thirty and Ulric's half come in from Gullhaven at midnight. Red thread and grey.

Lying on the hillside in the dawn in rows, like a herd, with their heads up. Looking south-west.

---

Rowan sent the knights over at first light.

He saw the barge come across. Under the chains. Wynn at the front with his two grey hounds. Eight knights behind him. And greyharts.

He'd never seen them close. The crown's war-mounts. White, elk-like, nine feet at the shoulder, with great spread antlers and long white faces and dark eyes. They stood in the barge with their heads up and their nostrils wide, not liking the river.

Wynn came up the shingle to him.

"The captain says keep them moving," he said. He looked at the Staithe. At the lower Way. At the people still there. Several hundred. Late crossers. The envoys' servants. The curious, who'd come to see the cage. "Up the Way. Away from the river."

"Yes."

"She says you've done it before," said Wynn. "At the spit."

---

The Titan came down at sunrise.

Aurveth knew first. She lifted her head off the shingle at the Staithe, where she'd lain all night, and turned it south-west.

*Here,* she said.

He looked.

---

Out of the south-west. Over the downs. Low.

The sky went dark under it. That was what he saw first. Not the Titan. The light going. A shadow coming across the downs toward the river, over the fields and the hedges and the empty villages, faster than a horse, and the sunrise going out behind it.

Then it.

Grey. Ridged like a hillside. Wings. He'd seen it fly once, a year ago, the morning it came up out of the river. He'd forgotten. It flew like a mountain falling slowly forward. Its wings out across half the sky, ragged, grey, with the light coming through them in places, the colour of old stone. The twelve chain-ends hanging off its legs, iron, swinging. The harpoons from the Bell still in its side, black.

And in its back, five places where the stone had grown over something.

---

It came down on the river.

Not on the hill. On the river. Half a league upriver of Greyhold. He saw it come down into the Wend in a burst of brown water that went up higher than the river wall, and stand. In the river. To its chest.

Under the chains.

Then it walked.

Down the river. Toward the hill. In the water to its chest, slowly, with the Wend going round it in a brown wave on either side and spilling over both banks. Under the first chain's curve. Under the second.

Toward Greyhold.

---

Rowan's horn went.

From the north tower. One note. Long.

The engines roared.

All four. The two in the yard and the two on the Saltreach bank, by the anchors, two hundred paces from where he stood. He heard them wake. A sound out of the iron like a forge breathing in. Then the fireboxes flared, red, white, and black smoke went up out of the chimneys in columns, and the great drums on the engines' beds began to turn.

The chains came in.

---

Off the river.

He saw them rise. Six black curves over the water, lifting, as the drums turned and the slack came in. Link by link. Rising off the river and closing in toward each other over the Titan's back as it walked under them, like a net being drawn up out of the sea round a fish.

On the inner wall, the binders.

Twelve. In the College's blue. Standing along the wall's top above the iron eyes with their hands lifted. He couldn't hear them over the engines. He could see their mouths. All moving together. The same words.

He knew what the words were. Everyone on the river knew them. The noon oath. *Keep the watch below.* And the hold-words, woven through it.

The runes on the chains lit.

---

White.

All at once. Every link. From the iron eyes in Greyhold's rock to the anchors on the Saltreach bank, the inside of every curve going white, a line of white running along each chain in both directions, so bright he put his hand up.

The chains came down on the Titan.

On its back. Over its neck. Round its shoulders. Six of them, closing. He saw them touch the stone of its back and go tight, and the white running along them blazed.

It stopped.

---

It stood in the river.

With six chains on it. White. Taut, from its back to the hill and from its back to the anchors on his bank. The Wend going round it brown and high. Its head up. Its great grey head, the size of a church, up out of the water and turned toward the hill.

Held.

He heard the envoys behind him. On the slope. A sound out of all of them at once. Not a cheer. A breath.

---

It pulled.

Once. Toward the hill. He saw it lean into the chains. And the chains held, and the white on them went brighter.

It pulled again. Harder.

Brighter.

*They feed on what they hold,* Liss had said at the post, in the dark. The smith had told her. Odo. The more it pulled, the more of it went into the runes, and the stronger the chains. A cage that got stronger the more it was fought.

The engines screamed. On his bank, two hundred paces off, the iron anchor-posts groaned in their beds of stone. But they were new stone. Live. Saltreach stone, from a quarry above the Staithe, with the Deep still in it.

On the hill, the eyes held.

---

He had people to move.

He went up on Kharrak. Over the Staithe. Over the lower Way. The people were standing. All of them. Several hundred, on the shingle, on the track, on the Way, turned round toward the river with their mouths open, watching a Titan in chains.

Nobody was moving.

"Up!" He brought Kharrak down in front of them on the Way. Low. Kharrak roared. "Up the Way! Go!"

They went.

---

Wynn's hounds worked them.

He watched it from the air. Two grey hounds, Ash and Sorrow, low and silent and quick, going along the edges of the crowd on the Way as they'd go along the edges of a flock of sheep. Turning the stragglers. Heading off a man who'd started back down toward the river to see better. Two hounds against several hundred people.

The people went where the hounds sent them.

The knights on their greyharts behind. Riding slow, along the back of the crowd. And when they came to someone old, or lame, or carrying a child who couldn't walk, a knight leaned down from the saddle and lifted them up behind him onto the greyhart's white back. One. Then another. A greyhart with three people on it, walking up the Way with its antlers up.

Ulric's riders coming down on the Way in front of carts that had stopped. Landing in the road. The carts moving again.

Nobody ran toward the river.

---

The band came at mid-morning.

He saw them from high up. On the Hearthmere bank. Out of the Brackenwood's edge, west of Greyhold, down the River Road. Thirty men. More. With carts. With one figure among them swinging a pot on its chain, smoking. With oxen walking in front of them in a line, heads low.

Onto the landing.

The empty landing under Greyhold's river wall. The ferry house, with its door open. The grooms' cottages. The crown company's camp by the jetty, empty, tents and stores left standing. They came down onto it at a run, with sacks, with their rings on their fingers.

Looting.

With a Titan in chains in the river a few hundred paces off.

---

Arn saw them too.

He was over the Saltreach bank on his red. He turned in the air. Calder saw him turn. And then Arn's red went down. Across the river. Over the chains. Down on the landing like a stone.

And twenty pairs behind him.

---

He'd never seen the Clan come down on anything.

Not like that. Twenty dragons out of the sky at once, red and grey, onto a landing a hundred paces wide, in a roar of wings and a noise out of their throats that he felt in his chest across the river. Not fire. They didn't need fire. They came down on the shingle round the band in a ring, and stood there, twenty of them, with their wings half open and their heads low.

The band broke.

Nobody struck a blow. Nobody loosed a bolt. Thirty men dropped their sacks and ran. Up the River Road, past the dead lamps, back toward the Brackenwood's edge, as fast as they could go. Leaving the carts. Leaving the loot.

The binder threw down his pot.

In the road. And ran after them.

---

The oxen stood on the landing.

The Ferriby oxen. Bound. Heads low. Eyes empty. Standing in a line on the shingle with nobody to drive them.

One of them walked forward into the river.

He didn't know why. Nobody had told it to. It walked down the shingle into the Wend, up to its knees, to its belly, and stopped. With the water going round it.

And its eyes filled.

He saw it from the air. It lifted its head and bellowed and looked round at the dragons and the landing and the Titan in chains, and turned, and walked back out of the river onto the shingle on its own, and stood there blowing.

The others followed it in. One after another. And came out.

That was the last of the Restoration's war. He didn't know it then. He knew it later. Thirty men running up the River Road into a wood, and a pot smoking in the road, and a line of plough-oxen standing in the river with their eyes full again.

---

It held.

For a while.

He hung over the river on Kharrak, high, and looked down at it. The Titan standing in the Wend under six white chains, its head low now. Not pulling. Not fighting. The engines roaring. The binders' mouths moving on the wall. The white running along the chains, steady.

The envoys on the slope behind him had stopped making any sound at all.

*It isn't fighting,* said Aurveth.

From the Staithe. She hadn't moved off the shingle all morning. He could feel her through the bond, watching it with her old gold eyes.

*It's waiting.*

"For what?"

*For the hill,* said Aurveth.

---

Lisska was on the ferry-house roof.

Her old perch. He hadn't sent her there. She'd gone, in the morning, when the Titan came down. Across the river, under the chains, onto the slates of the empty stone house at the bottom of the jetty road. And lain there with her chin on the ridge.

He felt her.

Not words. A picture. Through the bond, from across the river, from the roof, coming up into his head while he hung over the water.

Dark.

Warm. Very far down. Under the house, under the river wall, under the river. Dark, and a stair going down into it, and at the bottom, black water lit gold.

And on the stair, going down, a great grey shape. And a small one beside it. With a dark sword at her hip.

*The lion,* said Lisska. Very quietly. *Far below. In the dark. I can feel him through the stone.* She was quiet. *They're going down.*
