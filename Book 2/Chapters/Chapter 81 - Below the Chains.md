---

The hill shook when it landed.

She was on the water-gate stair. Halfway down. With Orrun ahead of her and the stair's walls humming, and the whole hill shook. Under her. Over her. A long slow shudder, from somewhere above and to the north, coming through the rock and the steps and up through her boots into her teeth.

Orrun stopped.

*It has come down,* he said. *On the river.*

"The cage?"

He was quiet. Listening. Through the stone.

*It is on it,* he said. *I can feel the chains. They are very bright.*

---

The cavern was awake.

She saw it from the stair's last turn. The black water that had never moved, that had lain still under the river for three hundred years, was moving. Shivering. Rings going out across it, one inside another, as if something tapped on it from below.

The knight's lock was blazing. Every ring of its script white.

The Kessmoor scaffolds were shaking on their timbers. Dust coming down off the roof in threads.

And in the cavern's near wall, where the iron eyes went through the rock from the hill above, she could see the backs of the drill-holes. The ends of the great bolts the Kessmoor men had set, black, as thick as her body. And round them, spreading out across the old grey stone of the wall, cracks.

Fine as hair. Dozens.

White. The light of the chain-runes above leaking down them into the rock.

---

There were lanterns at the far end.

At the shut place. Where she'd stood with her palm on warm stone. A knot of yellow light at the end of the cavern, beyond the lock, beyond the black water, on the wet ledge.

And people.

She counted them as she came off the stair onto the ledge. She didn't stop walking. Orrun beside her, between her and the water.

Eight with crossbows and spears. Band men. Rings on. Four with pots, smoking, and short spears in their other hands. Binders.

A man with his arms held against his body.

A tall thin man in black, very still.

And in front of the shut place, with a lantern in his hand, a thin man on a piece of spar.

---

They'd come in by a way she'd never seen.

An arch, in the cavern's far corner, behind the shut place's end. Low. Round-roofed. She'd stood a few paces from it on the night of the lamps and never seen it. It hadn't been there, or it had been stone, and somebody had opened it from the other side.

The old ways.

---

The shut place was written on.

She stopped when she saw it. On the ledge, halfway along. With the black water shivering beside her.

It had been smooth. She'd put her hand on it. Plain grey old stone, without a mark, as if something had smoothed it from the other side.

It wasn't smooth now.

Rings. Coming up out of it. Out of the stone, from inside, like writing coming up through wet paper. Rings of the hooked script, one inside another, gold, lit from within, spreading across the stone as she watched. The same rings as the knight's lock. The same shapes.

As if the thing above, pressing down on the hill, was pressing the words up out of the stone.

---

"Read it," said Corvin.

To Idony. Quietly. He hadn't seen her yet. Or he had, and didn't look round.

Idony held the lantern up. He didn't need it. The stone was bright enough to read by. He held it up anyway, as he'd held a lamp up to every carving she'd ever seen him read. The cavern was shaking. The light shook on the stone.

He put his finger on the outer ring.

And read it. Aloud. Slowly. In the common tongue. Word by word, his finger going round.

---

*One hand shut it.*

She knew it.

The shapes. She knew them. She'd copied them in charcoal into the back of the black book in the spring, from the knight's lock, kneeling on the wet stone with Cassian's body on a plank behind her. She hadn't been able to read them. She'd carried them for a year.

*Two hands open it together.*

She'd found one word in them. Two days ago. On the old stair, with Idony's list. *Together.*

*What is opened together is kept together.*

Idony's finger went round the last of the ring. Slowly.

*Or it is not kept.*

---

He took his finger off the stone.

Nobody said anything.

The black water shivered. The dust came down. Above, through the rock, the hill hummed with the chains.

She stood on the ledge and heard the whole of it for the first time. What she'd carried. A year, in the back of a book, in charcoal, in shapes she couldn't read.

*One hand shut it.*

---

Corvin turned round.

"Ashdown," he said.

He didn't sound surprised.

Then he looked back at the stone. At Idony. "Two hands," he said.

"Yes," said Idony.

"Its," said Corvin. He looked up. At the roof. At the hill. At what was on it. "And mine."

Idony didn't say anything.

"Or mine," said Maren. "And the lion's."

Corvin looked at her.

For a long time. With his long pale face very still in the gold light coming off the stone.

"Then we'll see whose," he said.

---

Callis came at her.

She'd seen him coming. She'd seen him take the sword off the band man beside him, a short sword, and put it in his left hand. The bent one. The wrist she'd broken at the river gate.

He came along the ledge at her.

Badly. He couldn't grip. She could see it. The sword was held in his fist like a stick, with the bent wrist under it, and when he swung it at her the blade turned in his hand.

She didn't draw.

Haverly's edge was on her hip. Dark. Dead ground. It would have been a sword and nothing else. She didn't draw it anyway.

She took the sword off him with her hands.

Caught his wrist as the blade turned. Twisted. The sword went out of his fist onto the ledge and over the edge into the black water, without a sound.

He came again. With nothing. With his crooked arms.

She put him down on the ledge.

He got up.

---

"Keep the lion off the door," said Corvin.

Quietly. To the binders. To the band.

They came along the ledge. Toward the shut place's foot. Spears. Crossbows. The binders with their pots swinging, smoke going out of them white.

Toward Orrun.

---

She felt him want to.

Like heat. All the shadows he had in him. Four. She could feel them. Waiting in the stone of the cavern floor like men waiting behind a door.

"One," she said.

*One.*

It stood up out of the floor.

Between the band and the shut place. Out of the cavern's old grey stone, at the ledge's edge. She felt it go out of her. Like blood. Like a cut opened. Out of her and out of him into the floor, and up, in the shape of a lion, grey, as big as he was, with his mane and his teeth.

Two lions on the ledge.

---

They held it.

Two lions, shoulder to shoulder, with the shut place behind them and the gold rings spreading on it. The band men stopped. The crossbows went off. Bolts hit grey stone hide and fell on the ledge.

The binders came on. Their smoke went out at the lions in white ropes. Their words with it, low, together.

It did nothing.

She'd known it would do nothing. A bonded beast couldn't be bound. And the shadow was the stone of the cavern floor, standing up. You couldn't bind a floor.

A binder came in close with his spear.

Into the shadow's shoulder. Deep. She felt it go in. In her own shoulder, a long way off, like a memory of being struck.

The shadow didn't stop.

---

Then the river came in.

---

She heard it first. Above. A sound through the rock like the sky tearing. Then the roof.

The cavern's near end. Over the stair. Over the Kessmoor scaffolds. Over the cracks round the eyes. The roof split. She saw it open, a black line across the old stone, then wider, then brown.

The Wend.

Coming in. A waterfall. Brown and white, through the roof, the whole river it seemed, pouring down into the cavern in a column as wide as a house and hitting the black water with a sound she felt in her chest.

The black water rose.

All at once. Across the cavern in a wave. Over the ledge. Round her knees. Running. Brown now, cold, fast, the river come down into the dark.

---

Callis went in.

He was on the ledge's edge where she'd put him. On his knees, getting up. The wave took him off it. Into the water.

She saw him go under.

His arms came up. Both of them. Crooked. Useless. They couldn't pull him up. They couldn't hold anything. He went under and came up and went under again, ten paces out, with the river going round him.

On the island in the snow she'd broken one of those arms. At the river gate, the other wrist.

She went in after him.

---

To her waist. To her chest. The cold stopped her breath. The river pulling her off her feet toward the far end of the cavern, toward the shut place, toward the dark.

She got him by the collar.

Hauled. Back. Toward the stair's foot. Against the water. Orrun beside her suddenly, in the flood to his chest, and she got her other hand in his mane and he pulled, and she pulled, and they came up onto the stair's lowest steps with Callis between them streaming.

She dropped him on the steps.

He lay there. Coughing. Water coming out of him. His crooked arms against his body.

He looked up at her.

He didn't say anything.

---

He got up.

Slowly. On the stair. With the water pouring off him. He looked at her once. Then he went back down the steps into the flood, and along the ledge, waist-deep, holding on to the wall with his shoulder because his hands couldn't hold anything, toward the lanterns at the far end.

Toward Corvin.

She let him go.

---

The shadow went back into the floor.

She felt Orrun do it. When the water came. Unmake it. The second lion standing in the flood at the shut place's foot, and then not standing, going down into the water and the stone under the water, back into what it had come from.

And the wound came back.

Into him. She saw it. The place in the shadow's shoulder where the spear had gone in, coming back into his own shoulder. All at once. A dark place opening in the grey hide. Blood. He staggered, in the flood, and nearly went down, and didn't.

She felt it in her own shoulder.

She put her hand on him.

*It is nothing,* said Orrun.

It wasn't nothing.

---

The water rose.

To the stair's fourth step. Fifth. The waterfall through the roof roaring. The lanterns at the far end still there, beyond the flood, high on the shut place's ledge, where it was higher. She could see them. Corvin, standing. Idony with his lantern. The gold rings on the stone, spreading, brighter.

Orrun lifted his head.

Not toward the lanterns. Up. Toward the roof. Toward the hole in it, and the river coming through.

*He is coming,* he said.

"Who?"

He was quiet. Listening. Through the water. Through the stone.

*The farmer,* he said. *Far up. Where the island was. Where the river turns.* He shut his eyes. *Three of them. And him. Coming down. Through the water.*

She looked up. At the brown column of the river pouring in through the roof. At the dark above it.

She couldn't see anything.

*Through the river,* said Orrun. *He is coming down after it.*
