---

The last of the hold went out of the iron.

She felt it go through the ledge, up through her wet boots. Twelve chain-ends hung from the Titan's legs, each link as thick as her body, and for as long as Idony's palm had been on one of them they had been still. Now they went slack. His words dying in them, as a fire dies in a grate when nobody feeds it. The white in the runes went yellow, then brown, then dark, one link after the next down the length of every chain.

The Titan shook its legs.

Once. As a dog shakes water off. The chain-ends swung out over the black water and came back and struck its hide, iron on stone, a sound she felt in her back teeth. Then they hung. Loose. Old iron.

It put its brow to the shut place.

---

She was standing over Idony.

She'd been standing over him since he sat down. She didn't remember deciding to. Her legs had locked under her, a sentry's legs at the end of the night watch, and Haverly's edge was in her hand, and the river was going cold in her clothes.

Soaked to the shoulders. The Wend in her coat, in her boots, in her hair, running down her neck under her collar. Brown water, out of the hole at the near end of the cavern, where the river still came down through the roof in a long fall and roared. Under the roar, the drip of the roof. Under that, the Titan breathing.

Each breath a long time coming in. A long time going out. The air of the cavern moving with it, warm on her wet face, and then still, and then warm again.

Orrun was up in the rock.

She could feel him there. One body again, the four gone back into the stone, and every one of them had taken something with it when it went. He was tired in a way she had never felt him tired. It sat in her own legs and her own shoulders, like a march she hadn't made. And under it, in her shoulder, where the spear had gone into his shadow, an ache that was his and that she carried anyway.

She didn't ask him to come down. He didn't offer.

---

And it pushed.

She'd braced for a blow. It leaned instead. As you lean on a gate that's already open. And the clay that had been old stone, that had held for three hundred years and more, went in under its brow like a bank of wet sand going into the river at the spring flood.

No crack. The stone had screamed for an hour, and now it made a sound she knew from every spring of her life on the river: a long soft slump, a bank giving, the slap of it going under. The last of the gold rings smeared in it and went out. Grit ran off the edges of the hole and pattered on the ledge round her boots.

Inward. Away from them. Into the dark behind.

A passage.

Round-roofed. Short. Old grey stone. Going in.

---

The breath came out of it.

All at once. Past her. A wind out of the passage, out of the dark behind it, warm, long, steady, going past her face and her hair and out across the cavern over the black water.

It smelled of nothing.

Like the air on the old stair. Like a sleeper's breath, let go, after holding it.

It was as warm as the Long Barn on a winter night. It went into her wet coat and found the river there, and she began to shake. All over. Her jaw going. The sword's point ticking on the stone by her boot. She set her heels and stood in it and let it go past her.

She counted it. She couldn't help that. A breath was a thing you counted, on a stair, in a fight, by a bed. Ten. Thirty. It didn't end. At fifty the black water was running away from the ledge in long low lines, as if a hand were smoothing it flat. At seventy the lantern flames on the ledge lay down sideways in their glass and stayed down. After that she was only watching the water.

She felt it go up through the rock behind her. Up the stair. Up through Greyhold. Into the open air. Up in the hill Orrun felt it go past him, and she felt him lift his head to it, as a horse lifts its head to a wind off the river.

She heard later that every lamp left on the hill flared once, white, and went out for good.

The wind thinned. Stopped. The falls came back into her ears, and the drip of the roof, and her own teeth.

---

The vault was small.

She saw it past the Titan's head, by the light of Idony's lantern, where he'd set it down on the ledge. The passage ended a few paces in. And there was a room. Round, domed, no bigger than Liss's kitchen. Old grey stone. Plain. No mark on it anywhere. As if whoever made it had had nothing left to say.

In the middle of it, a plinth.

Like the one under the Ring that she'd heard of and never seen, where the first egg had been. Waist-high. Grey.

And on the plinth, a ring.

---

Grey stone. Old-realm. A cartwheel across. Lying flat. Warm: she could feel the warmth of it from where she stood, on her wet face, as you feel a forge door through a wall.

Six hollows in its rim.

Five of them egg-shaped. Smooth. Waiting. The shape of the thing it had lifted off the plinth under the Sun Stair.

And the sixth, round.

Empty. As if something round had sat in it once, a long time ago, and been taken out.

---

She stood in the opening.

She'd known she would. She'd chosen it upstairs, a breath before, when she took her hand off the stone and sent the lion up into the rock. She'd chosen the cellars. And this was what came with it.

Her, in the door. With a sword.

Haverly's edge. Drawn. In her hand. The hill was live now, live as it had ever been, the held breath going past her up into it. And the letters on the blade were still dark. Old magic. Not hers.

A door was the best ground there was. Pell had said so in the yard, her first winter, with his slate under his arm. One in a door can hold twenty, because only one of the twenty can reach her.

He hadn't said what to do when the one was the size of a church.

The Titan's head came at the opening.

---

It filled the passage.

The brow first. Ridged, streaming, grey as the inner wall had been grey, with the river running off it in sheets. The stone of the passage ground along its jaw on both sides, a long low grinding she felt in the soles of her feet, and grit came down off the roof onto her hair. It smelled of river mud and wet rock and something under both, like a hearthstone an hour after the fire's out.

And the eyes. Each as big as a ferry. Close enough now that she could see they were wet. Close enough to see herself in the near one, small and dark, in a doorway, with a sword.

She cut at it.

Once. Because it was the door. Because she was the only one in it. Because Haverly had died in her arms on the north tower stair with this sword at his hip.

The blade hit its brow and rang.

As a hammer rings on an anvil. The shock of it went up her arm into her shoulder and her teeth. Her fingers opened on the grip by themselves, and she shut them again. The edge didn't cut. It didn't mark. The great grey brow came on toward the opening as if nothing had touched it.

It didn't notice her.

She saw that. Its eyes going past her into the vault. To the ring. She wasn't anything. She was a person in a doorway.

She stepped aside.

Back against the passage wall. The Titan's jaw went past her a hand's breadth from her face, grey, ridged, grinding along the stone, with water running off it onto her boots. She could have put her hand on it. She kept her hand on the sword.

She'd known she would step aside.

---

Corvin was already there.

She hadn't seen him come. While the head filled the passage. Out of the arch along the ledge, with Callis behind him and the roll under his arm, past Idony sitting grey on the stone. Past her, as she stepped aside. Through the gap between the Titan's jaw and the passage wall, where there was room for a man.

Into the vault.

He went to the plinth. Quickly. He didn't run.

His left arm was in its sling. He put his right hand on the ring.

"Now," he said.

To it. Quietly.

---

It took his hand.

She saw it happen. From the doorway, past the Titan's jaw. The ring's stone moved. Under his palm. Rose. As water rises round a hand put into a basin. Grey. Coming up over his fingers. Over the back of his hand. His wrist.

He tried to take it off.

He pulled. She saw him pull. His face didn't change. He pulled, and his arm came back, and his hand didn't.

It stayed. In the ring's rim. Grey. Stone. Part of it.

His arm ended at the wrist.

In grey stone. Smooth. Sealed. Not bleeding.

---

He didn't scream.

He stood by the plinth and looked at it. At the end of his arm. At the grey place where his hand had been. At the grey lump in the ring's rim, with fingers.

Old magic wasn't for people.

The reader had told her. On the shore at Vael. On the road. A dozen times. *It doesn't answer us. It was never made for us. We're not what it's for.*

Callis came in past the Titan's jaw and got him by the coat with his crooked arms and dragged him back. Out of the vault. Out through the gap. Onto the ledge.

Corvin let him.

He was still looking at his arm.

---

The Titan took the ring.

Its jaws closed on it. Gently. As she'd seen it hold the egg at the Scald. As a dog holds a pup. The whole ring, a cartwheel across, with Corvin's grey hand in its rim.

It lifted it off the plinth. The stone came away from the stone with a sound like a millstone lifted off its bed, a long dry pull, and then nothing.

And it drew its head back out of the vault. Out of the passage, grinding. Past her. Into the cavern. And turned its head over its own shoulder, slowly, as she'd seen it do at the Scald, with the river running off its neck.

She came to the passage mouth to watch. She didn't decide to.

---

Its back went away from her into the dark like a hillside. Grey plates, each as broad as a barge, lying over one another as roof-slates lie. And among them, five places where the stone was paler and smoother than the rest. One for every temple it had broken open.

The plates by them moved.

They lifted. Apart. As a rank opens to let a rider through, the men on either side stepping back without being told. She heard them go. Stone on stone, a deep slow scraping, and a darkness opening in the middle of the Titan's back that went further down than she could see.

It laid the ring down into it.

Among the five.

The ring went in with a knock. Low. Deep. She felt it more than heard it, through the ledge, through her boots, in her chest, as you feel a ram hit a gate from across the yard before the sound comes. Then a second sound under it, longer, as something seated home.

The plates closed over it.

Like shields locking. One edge onto the next, all down its back.

The stone grew.

She watched it. Grey. Coming up over the ring's edge from the plates round it, as ice comes out from the bank in the first hard frost, thin and clear, then thick, then white. Over the six hollows. Over Corvin's hand. Until there was nothing to see but a place in the Titan's back where the stone was newer than the rest.

Six.

Up in the hill, Orrun flinched.

She felt it in her own neck. As if a hand had taken her by the scruff.

---

It spoke to Orrun.

She felt it. Through him. He was above her in the hill. She'd felt him there the whole time, in the rock, in five bodies and then in one, the shadows gone back into the stone. The cellars empty. The last of the seventy up and out on the broken hillside, and Joby last, with his lantern.

It spoke to him through the rock.

Once.

---

It had no words of its own.

She didn't know what it was. She heard it as she heard Orrun, in the place behind her eyes where he talked. Bigger than Orrun. Older. A long way down and very close. It came through the rock into him and through him into her, and he made it into words for her, as he made the world into words for her, and that was all she ever had of it.

*You kept it well, little brother.*

A pause. Long. The stone humming.

*It is not yours to keep any more.*

---

Orrun didn't answer.

She felt him not answer. Up there, in the hill, on the broken hillside among the people. She felt him stand there with his head low and the rock round him and say nothing. Her own throat shut on nothing, and stayed shut.

Then he gave her what he saw.

He did it on purpose. She felt the effort of it. He held the hillside up for her, and pushed it across.

---

The hillside.

Broken. The inner wall gone. Rubble. The cellar vaults open to the sky. People everywhere on the slope, sitting, lying, standing, grey with dust. Joby with his lantern. Mother Ketch, on her feet.

And the farmer's three.

On the hillside. In the middle of it. Kharrak with a man in his claws, lifting him down off the rubble. Lisska in the air over the slope. The old bronze one lying across the broken wall with people sliding down her back to the grass.

And all three of them going still.

At once. In the middle of what they were doing. Kharrak with the man still in his claws. Lisska hanging in the air. The old one with her head coming up off the rubble. All three. Heads up. Turned toward the river. Like hounds that hear a horn.

And the farmer standing among them on the broken hill with his hand at his chest.

Where his marks were.

---

Then it reached her.

A pull. Through Orrun, through the place behind her eyes, and down through her into her feet. Going out from the six in the Titan's back. Out through the rock and the river and the hill, in every direction at once.

Orrun leaned into it.

Up on the slope she felt him lean. His whole weight going toward the river. His claws in the turf. And she leaned with him, there at the passage mouth, with nothing to hold, her wet boots sliding a finger's breadth on the stone before she caught herself on the wall.

Haverly's edge went out of her hand. She heard it hit the ledge and didn't look.

The pull went on going out. Further than the Marches. Further than anything she'd walked.

And far off, a long way past the end of it, something turned.

She felt it come back through him. A weight, very far away, shifting. Something as big as the thing in front of her, lifting its head off the ground it had lain on since before there were kingdoms.

Then another. Further. Cold.

She was counting before she'd decided to. As she'd counted beacons going up along the river wall on a raid night, post after post down the valley, each fire telling the next.

Three. Four.

Five, slow, a long time coming, from somewhere under water.

"Five," she said. Aloud. As she'd said four. To nobody. To the rock.

On the ledge behind her, Idony lifted his grey head.

*They have opened their eyes,* said Orrun.

His voice was very quiet. She'd heard him sound like that once before, a year ago, the night he learned what three hundred years of chains had made of his kin.

*All of them.*

---

The Titan turned in the cavern.

She heard it. The water going away from it over the ledge in a long wave, round her ankles, cold. It turned, slowly, in the dark beyond the lanterns, beyond where Kharrak's fire had lit the roof before the farmer went up, beyond where the gold light of the shut place had been.

And went away. Up the cavern. Toward the Ruin end. Into the dark.

With six in its back.

She listened to it go. Its breath, long in, long out, getting smaller. Its back scraping the roof, far off, and stone falling into water. Then only the falls at the near end, and the drip, and Idony's breathing on the ledge, short and uneven, and her own.

---

She went into the vault.

Alone. Into the small round room with nothing written on it. The sword lay on the ledge behind her where she'd dropped it, dark.

She put her hand on the plinth.

Warm. Still warm. Where the ring had been. A round place on the grey stone, paler than the rest, where the ring had lain since before there were knights.

She left her palm there. The river ran off her sleeve onto the stone, and the stone dried it. She watched her own wet handprint shrink at the edges and go, as breath goes off a blade.

Outside on the ledge, Corvin was on his knees in the arch's mouth with Callis holding him up by the coat. Idony was sitting on the stone with his grey hands in his lap.

And above her, through the rock, on the broken hill, three dragons stood still with their heads turned toward the river, as if listening for something only they could hear.
