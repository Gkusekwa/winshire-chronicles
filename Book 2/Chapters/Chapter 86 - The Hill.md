---

Greyhold was gone.

She saw it from the bank as they came along it at dawn. A league. From the slope above Millbrook's woods where the lion had come up out of the ground with the reader on his back. Walking. Her hand in his mane. Idony asleep, or nearly, slumped forward on Orrun's neck with his shaking hands tied into the mane with her belt so he wouldn't fall.

The hill had fallen in on itself.

Where the school had been, a long grey heap. Stone and timber and dust. The north tower lying on its side down the slope like a felled tree. The river wall slid down into the Wend in a long fan of grey rock with the river going round it brown and fast.

Nothing standing.

---

The charter stone was in the rubble.

By the road. Where the gatehouse had been. Lying on its back. Face-up. The old cut letters on it, the charter that had hung over the gate her whole life, with the thing hanging in chains under it this last year.

She didn't read it.

She walked past it.

---

The ferry house was standing.

At the foot of the jetty road. Under where the river wall had been, that wasn't there now. Cracked. From the eaves to the step, a crack she could have put her hand in. The roof half off, slates gone, the rafters showing at one end like ribs. The door hanging on one hinge.

Standing.

She stopped in the road and looked at it.

Orrun lay down across the jetty road behind her.

He'd grown again. In the rock. Going up through it with the two of them on his back and the shadows gone into him. She'd felt it happen and hadn't been able to look. She looked now. He filled the road from the river to the bank. His head on the jetty's end. His back as high as the ferry house's broken eaves.

Nearly Aurveth's size.

The roots of his mane white, all along his neck.

He shut his eyes and slept.

---

The rope still ran.

From the post at the jetty's end, across the river, to the post at the Staithe. Black. Taut. Humming in the current. Thurl's hawser. *Put it down as lost in the river.*

A boat was coming across on it.

At sunrise. One person. Hand over hand. A big plain woman in a ferry-coat with her grey plait down her back.

---

Liss got out on the jetty.

She tied the boat off. She looked at the house. A long look. At the crack. The roof. The door.

Then at Maren.

"You came home," she said.

"Yes."

Liss looked at the house again.

"So did I," she said.

---

The far bank was full.

She saw it from the jetty, in the morning. The Saltreach bank and the Drover's Way. People. Smoke from fires, hundreds, going up all along the Way from the river into the hills, in the still morning air. Carts drawn up in the fields. Cattle. Tents made of blankets. Children on the river bank looking across at the fallen hill.

The Marches. Everyone she'd sent over.

And on the hill above Harrow's End, the Clan. Every pair. Lying on the hillside in rows in the morning sun. Red thread and grey.

---

The envoys came at noon.

On Liss's boat and the crown's barge. Across the river from the Saltreach bank, from the slope above the anchors, where their tents had been.

Every one of them from a broken place.

Not all of the camp. Arn told her on the jetty, before the others were out of the boats. The Tide Council's clerk and the Quill house's woman in white had taken the first barge downriver for Wendmouth at dawn, with their tent and their ledger.

"To write it up for Corsa," said Arn.

"They didn't stay to talk?"

"They came to watch," said Ulric. "They watched."

---

Rowan.

In her white surcoat, torn half off, with the dust of the hill still on it. She stood on the jetty and looked up at where Greyhold had been for a long time.

"For Hearthmere's knights," she said. When Liss asked. "Not the crown. The crown's council is in Hallowmere with no lamps." She looked at the rubble. "I'm what's left of the knights at the river."

---

Arn and Ulric.

On one boat. Together. Arn in black, Ulric with the white scar through his eyebrow and his arms folded. They got out on the jetty and stood there side by side looking at the fallen hill, and neither of them said anything.

---

A brown man in a plain coat with dust on his boots.

He came up the jetty to her with a letter in his hand.

"Perrin Sade," he said. In a Sarovel voice. "From the low city. Solenne. The mud-brick quarter." He held out the letter. "Tobin sent me. To speak for us. And with this. For you."

She took it. She put it in her coat. She didn't open it.

---

A square red-bearded man in a leather coat with Kessmoor's iron wheel on the shoulder.

"Garth Tolley," he said. "Master Engineer Pike's deputy." He had a paper in his hand. Folded. Fresh. "For Kessmoor." He looked at the paper. "The semaphore came down the Iron Road in the night. Tower to tower." He looked at her. "The Scald's cage broke. Yesterday. Mid-afternoon. The thing in it's out. Three engines gone." He folded the paper smaller. "Pike's alive."

"What did she say?"

Tolley looked at the paper.

"*Told you,*" he said. "*Send me the day.*"

---

A thin anxious man in grey with the Blue College's blue at his cuffs.

"Magister Ansel Corrow," he said. Stiffly. "Fourth ring. For Isvaren." He didn't look at the lion. He didn't look at Idony. "A lantern owl came for me in the night. Magister Dane is dead." He looked at the river. "I'm the senior magister of the College south of the Pass."

---

And the farmer.

Last off the barge. With his coat wet to the waist from yesterday still. With his red dragon in the river behind him with only its head out.

He stood on the jetty and looked at the ferry house.

Then at her.

---

They sat at Liss's table.

It was too small. It had been too small for the Fenns, a year ago. Rowan and Arn and Ulric on the bench. Tolley and Corrow on stools from the outhouse. Perrin Sade on the step with his back against the cracked doorframe. Liss at the fire making tea in the big pot.

The farmer at the far end. By the door.

Maren at the other. By the fire.

Idony on the settle. With a blanket over his knees and his hands in his lap. Liss put a mug of tea in front of him and then saw his hands and picked it up again and held it for him while he drank.

---

Each spoke for what they'd seen.

Rowan first. Because it was her kingdom. The cage. The chains. The eyes tearing out of the rock. The engine going over with Dane on it, his hands on the wheel. Letting go.

Tolley. The Scald. The league of iron, the canals, the hearts. A hundred and seventeen days of holding. Then the semaphore, tower to tower, in the night.

Perrin Sade. The Sun Stair. The Horned Sun in the bay. The Bond-King on his stair. The Grace going out of Sarovel like a lamp going out. The low city holding Solenne in a nine-year-old's name, with a loud boy from the Marches shouting at the wells.

Arn. The coast. Ulric beside him saying nothing.

Corrow, stiffly. Vael. The colleges' binding. The Blue tower.

Then Rowan looked down the table.

At the far end.

"Master Fenn," she said. "You've been everywhere it went. What did you see?"

---

He told them.

From the beginning.

She sat at her end by the fire and listened. She'd heard most of it. In letters. In his big square careful hand on the backs of pages, in Lisska's pictures through Orrun, in a word on the wind. She'd never heard him say it.

He said it slowly. One thing after another. As he must have read the numbers to the Tide Council.

A field. A sky full of stars. Three dragons coming down out of the dark to a farmer who'd never asked for anything. The Clan, and the chair under its grey cloth. The ford, and Teo. The mint, and the coin with a ring of six in its rim. The young ones in honey-smoke, with nothing in their eyes. A girl talking about barley until one of them turned its head. The crown's square, and three books, and a man who could read. The Tidehall. The spit. White crosses on four hundred and twelve roofs. The reef standing up. The wave. Ulric going into the water after a salt-girl. Tamsin with eleven in her basket and on her back. The drowned on the shingle, and the pour. The hill, and the cellars, and the lion and the dragons carrying people up through the rock together without a word.

He didn't stop.

The envoys didn't interrupt him. Not one. Tolley with his paper folded small in his fist. Corrow with his eyes on the table. Rowan with her chin on her hand.

He told it to the end.

Then he stopped, and looked down at the table, and didn't say anything else.

---

Idony spoke after him.

From the settle. Without being asked. Grey all through, his hands in his lap shaking under the blanket. His voice was the same. That was the strange thing. It was the same voice that had read carvings to her on a dozen shores. Patient. Tired.

"I want to tell you what my college sold," he said.

---

He told them.

Plainly. With numbers. As he'd always told her everything.

Three hundred years ago, his own college, the College of Tides, had sold Hearthmere's first knights the craft for the chains. The *hold* rune, inside every link. The oath's form, the many-voiced charm said at noon. The words *and do not ask*, so the voices wouldn't doubt. Sold. For a price. It was in the College's books. He'd read the books.

"And this year," said Idony, "the colleges sold them again."

The Undercroft binding at Vael. A hundred magisters on the hill. The gold collar on the Bond-King's unicorn, iron inside, made from Magister Dane's drawings. The runes inside the links of the crown's second cage. Dane's twelve binders with their hands on the iron, six on each bank, saying the noon oath's form with the hold-words woven through it.

"My college has sold chains to every kingdom that asked," said Idony. "For three hundred years. And called it scholarship. And told its students not to ask what for."

He looked at his hands.

"I was one of its students," he said. "I didn't ask."

---

Nobody said anything.

Rowan looked down the table at Corrow.

"Does Isvaren deny it?" she said.

Corrow looked at the table for a long time. His thin face anxious. His hands flat on the wood.

"It's in the College's books," he said.

He didn't say anything else.

He didn't deny it.

---

"Five are awake," said Tolley.

After a while. Into the silence.

"Five. And turned toward here, the farmer says. And the sixth has the ring in its back." He opened his fist and looked at the folded paper in it. "Kessmoor's cage was the best cage in the world. It held a hundred and seventeen days."

"Hearthmere has a dead kingdom," said Rowan. "And a fallen hill."

"Sarovel has no Grace," said Perrin Sade. From the step. "And no king. And a boy of nine."

Corrow didn't look up. "The colleges burned out half their Fifth rings at Vael," he said. "And Magister Dane is dead."

Arn looked at the window. At the far bank. At the hill above Harrow's End, where the Clan lay in rows.

"The Clan has a hundred and twenty dragons," he said. "And not one of them could stop one of those."

---

Silence.

Liss poured tea. Nobody drank it.

No single kingdom could stand against it. Nobody said that. Everybody at the table knew it.

---

Ulric said it.

He hadn't said a word since he got off the boat. He was sitting on the bench beside Arn with his arms folded and the white scar through his eyebrow, looking at the table.

"Then we stand together," he said. "Or we drown one at a time."

Arn looked at him.

"That's my line," he said.

"It was your father's," said Ulric.

---

"Kessmoor'll want it in writing," said Tolley.

"The College will want to be asked," said Corrow. To the table.

"Then ask them," said Rowan.

Nobody agreed to anything.

Nobody left.

---

She read Tobin's letter that night.

By Liss's lamp. The brass one. On the step of the cracked house with the river going past in the dark and the rope humming and the lion asleep across the road.

Perrin Sade had written it for him. In a clerk's hand. She could hear Tobin in every line anyway.

*Maren.*

*Seraphin is well. We hold the low city. The olifants are still standing in the road. Nobody can make them move. We have stopped trying.*

*Mother Ysa is buried on the island. Under the light.*

*Come and see the sea when this is over. I said it twice.*

*Tobin.*

She read it twice.

---

The farmer went back across on the last boat.

At dusk. With Arn and Ulric. He didn't say goodbye. He stood up from the far end of the table when Liss said the boat was going, and looked down the table at her, and nodded once, and went out.

She watched the boat go out on the rope from the step.

Liss came and sat beside her.

"He stayed at the far end," said Liss.

"Yes."

Liss looked at the river.

"So did you," she said.

---

Idony was asleep on the settle.

Under his blanket. With his grey hands on top of it, shaking even in his sleep. Liss had put another blanket over him and left the lamp burning low on the table so he'd see where he was if he woke.

Across the road, the lion slept.

Nearly Aurveth's size. With his chin on the jetty's end and the river going past his nose, and his breath going out in the cold night air, long and slow, like a held breath let go.
