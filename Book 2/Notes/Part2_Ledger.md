# Book 2, Part Two: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as `Part1_Ledger.md`: before writing a scene that touches another POV's days or people, read this file and Part One's. After deciding any fact another chapter could contradict, append one short line under the chapter. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (13, 15, 18, 20, 23, 25)
- Ch 13 (days 17–40): days 17–28 east off the Scarfell through sheep country, onto the Hallow Plains about day 24; 8 leagues a day. Plains: standing stones are beast graves; a stonehorn herd parts round Orrun; horse-clan riders on great elk watch three days and never come close; a Worldsend roc overhead; Ada's hearth-word takes on the second try. Day 31 the Thornwood (a wall of oak, black pine, thorn); the hum felt in Maren's own feet. Night 31: Keeper Hesper (about 50, lean, brown, grey-streaked braid bound with red cord every hand's width, a dozen knot-cords at her belt, leaf-bladed short spear, bare feet, patched grey cloak; alone in this end of the Thornwood twenty years) and the duskcat Hush (black, hound-sized). Hesper calls Orrun "Grandfather" and asks *him* for the fire. The Lions' Barrows: two days in, a long clearing under black pines, barn-long grass mounds in rows, grey unlettered headstones, dozens; the stream Where the Grey One Drank. Orrun remembers none of them; lies down in the empty space at the end of the last row. Hesper's platform: an oak a day north of the Barrows. Her finding-knot (three turns, a twist, a word) unwinds; the fold-ward on the rail goes slack: "as if the ground's being drunk". Hush won't hunt east. The Titan came down on the Hollow Ring (twelve stones in a clearing, four days east of the Barrows; hollow owls nest in them; clans sing there once a generation at midsummer) the morning after the river and hasn't moved; ground going grey outward from the Ring. Orrun chooses to wait for the clans ("This time I choose it"). Rumours: two earth-shakes in the north Thornwood; deer, boar, martens, badgers drifting west in ones and twos; hollow owls flying at noon. Day 38 Corran (20, fox-red beard, hollow owl): the horse clans saw a red dragon going south over the Marches with a man on it. Day 40: elders meet in five days on the Plains at the Speaking Stones.
- Ch 15 (days 45–60): the Speaking Stones, a day west of the Thornwood: seven tall leaning stones in a horseshoe open east, a flat table-stone in the mouth. ~400 of the clans: horse clans (great elk, red-clay bars on cheekbones), forest clans (wolves, bears), river clans (round hide boats carried from the Ysse's streams). Elders: Aun (horse clans; very old, blind, milk-white eyes, white plaits to the ground, led by a girl of ten), Morwen (forest clans, Hesper's; big, slow, brown bear), Tolly-Bright (river clans; small, quick, laughs). Asking: "He lets her sit with him" (Hesper). Orrun on the flat stone: the Stones hum aloud. Answer: kin; don't hunt, chain or drive it; keep watch and keep people out of the grey. Day 48 Lisska (flight 1), speaking through Orrun: the book bought with a road for bad men; Kharrak wanted to burn them; family in the warm caves; Aurveth walked to the end of her ledge; Wren rode Old Grey to the caves, gave Calder the stone ("nobody knows what it's for, that's why it's the right stone"). Hesper's wound-moss in a hide bag on green cord tied round Lisska's neck, knotted (wet it, pack it in, leave it, don't keep looking). Lisska west at dusk d.48. Day 51 Rowan's company: ~30 knights on greyharts, hounds, two falcons, Greyhold's banner; six ox-wains (eight oxen each) with the great chain (links as long as a man); ~100 labourers; Wynn with Ash and Sorrow. Elders refuse passage ("the Thornwood is its own"); Rowan goes in day 53, cutting the path wider. Day 54 Dane's Wardens: a dozen on mountain horses, snow lynx, three stormkites, six mules with the iron binding-lamps in sacking; a thin young man with a box who looks at the ground (Idony, unnamed); in day 55 by a northern path. Day 56–57: a sleepwood grove two days south (forty bone-white trees weeping amber; Hesper's grandmother planted the middle ones); Orrun stays a league off (the knowe smell); Keepers decided (not the elders) to burn every grove to the Ysse; ask the trees' leave, then burn at moonrise; two dozen white pillars; Maren holds Orrun through the bond. Days 58–60 smoke drifts west; beasts leave in tens; Wardens' lynx stagger, eyes empty a moment.
- Ch 18 (days 74–90): Maren, Orrun, Hesper (and an unhappy Hush) entered the grey day 70 from the south along a grey stream. Day 74 Orrun lies at ten paces from the head; days 74–79 eats nothing, calls to it as kin, nothing comes back ("or it hears and there's nobody there"). Faint green at the grass roots in a few-pace ring round Orrun, fading by night, back by day. Day 80 Idony: about thirty, thin, untidy brown hair, inky fingers, once-good coat, plain silver ring with two notches; kneels at ten paces; the field-glass lifts a finger's breadth at the green roots: "He gives it back." "Yes. I think he does." Day 81 Rowan at the stakes: chain over the forelegs on the night the reader names, then carted or held; "In chains. Like the other one." "Better that than loose." She doesn't trust the Wardens' lamps. "You were Watch." Day 82 Lisska (flight 2), afraid, lands ten paces behind Orrun: Calder took the book to Skarrow; Ulric read it himself at his own table and will stand in Arn's yard if the farmer stands beside him; Arn won't open his gate; Calder can write his name, one letter backwards. Lisska leaves dawn day 84 ("before something... like before weather"). Day 84 Hesper's finding-knot: three turns round two fingers, a twist, the water-over-stone word on the twist, the asking held as a shape; tied with one hair from Orrun's mane (iron-grey, white at the root, arm-long). On the grey: string. Day 85 Orrun's chin on her wrist: it pulls down, toward the nearest stone and toward him at once; string when he lifts it; four times; right-hand fingers numb to the second knuckle for two or three days. Maren keeps the knot in her coat. Day 87 Keepers: smoke-men a day west, upwind, plain coats, mules with wax-sealed pots, using the old deer-trails; Rowan's falconer saw them three days before. Day 89 the Titan's breath changes to 11 in, 20 out; Idony: the draw turns on midsummer night, eight or nine days off.
- Ch 20 (day 98): the clearing ~a quarter-league across; a grey beck enters from the north between the 3rd and 4th stones (sunwise from NW) and runs out east. Dusk: breath 11 in, 24 out; the hollow owls all leave east (and don't come back). Six binding-lamps on iron tripods at six N/W stones, each hung on a four-link chain, crystal panes green at the edges; ten Wardens kneel on ten stones (Dane on the tallest, north); the lamps' cold white beams cross into a net; the Titan's eyes go blank. Three chain-teams of about thirty labourers each (from Rowan's ~100, the rest on the stakes), with spare knights hauling too, loop the chain over the forelegs onto paired stakes. An hour after dark: honey-smoke from a line of fires in the green a league west; Maren holds Orrun through the bond. Pask (4th stone, by the beck) throws her off the ladder with drawn weight; Orrun climbs the stone (claws into it like clay) with her on his back; she takes Pask off the stone into the beck; his draw breaks; lamp out; left alive on the bank. Ysolde Marr and the next Warden step down rather than face Orrun; lamps out on the grey. Maren cuts the NW lamp's chain with Haverly's dark steel; panes break like pond-ice; its Warden collapses. Four of six out. Orrun pulls the western line of stakes, his shadow the eastern, two minutes (Idony counted); Maren cold to the bone, nose bleeds into her collar. The Titan stands, puts its head between its forepaws, the ring's middle opens like a mouth; it comes up with a round grey stone in its jaws; wings wider than the clearing; flies west low over the Thornwood; downdraft flattens the grey trees outward in a ring and blows the smoke east. The ring falls into a sink ~forty paces across; stones and Wardens go in; Dane jumps from the north stone and lives. Rowan pinned at the lip under a chain-loop and stake, left leg broken below the knee; Orrun pushes the loop off. Wynn's pale hound Sorrow dead (Ash howling over it). Idony clinging to the 5th stone three body-lengths down the sink; Maren climbs down a chain-loop; crate saved, most glasses broken; one green glass whole: "three together... going ragged... as if one of them were coming loose". Orrun falls asleep where he lies. Hesper and Hush come in at first light.
- Ch 23 (days 100–101): the knot (kept since day 85) pulls on its own in the surge, toward the NE slope of the sink a third of the way down: an old-realm lintel under rubble. Orrun (grey at the mane-roots after his sleep) slides the rubble aside; in the surge the stone moves too easily and he has to hold it. Forty steps down; Hesper's torch, lit with a word that flares to her wrist, goes out at the bottom; the walls glow grey from inside. Round domed chamber as big as Greyhold's yard; the dome broken in the middle (daylight, rubble heap). Plinth: squat waist-high drum, egg-shaped hollow the size of a cart-wheel's hub, empty, warm like a bed just left. Six sleepers carved in low relief round the wall, left to right from the stair: the stag with antlers like a winter wood and a forest lying down with it; a coiled serpent with scales like frost; a long low plated boar-like beast with its snout in the floor; a one-horned beast with sun-rays round its head; a vast island-like hump with weed, a fin and a shut eye; a lion with a broken-rock mane, wall-course plates and folded wings. Over each a tall faceless figure cut to stand out and catch the glow, one hand palm-down over the sleeper's head, the other raised toward the dome's centre; what they held up there fell with the roof (one fragment: an open hand, palm up, fingers curved round something). Orrun: "That is my kin... It was not chained then. It was asleep." Idony: the same hooked word under all six, *sleep*; round the surviving rim of the dome, *Here the breath is kept*. Maren connects it silently to Orrun's "like a breath held" (the lock, ch 4). Hesper's lullaby: "Hush, now. Hush. Like the six who sleep"; "That one's ours. That one's the forest." Idony copies all onto oiled silk; the glow fades by evening of day 101. Maren's fingertips numb only for the evening. Echo at the top of the steps: the three still together, thin at one edge. Maren guesses what they are; doesn't tell Idony.
- Ch 25 (days 104–105): Aun on the shingle: nobody told to go; believers take to the water; elders stay. By noon half the clans are a bowshot out on the lake (hide boats, pole rafts, a roundhouse roof turned upside down with forty in it). The Moot's slope: half a league wide, seven north–south lanes broad as drove-roads, forest to lake. Lanes set at dusk day 104: sixty tame stonehorns three deep across the two western lanes; Keepers' wolves and eleven bears in the middle lanes (a huge pale bear on its hind legs in the third); a duskcat on every roof-ridge; children and old in the big stone-footed roundhouses by the water. Maren and Orrun take lanes 5 (near) and 6 (far); Hesper and Hush the narrow 7th by the forest's edge. Dawn day 105: every bird leaves the Thornwood west in one cloud. Echo: Idony's last reading, "Three. Together. Still"; a river-clan woman takes him onto the lake. Full morning: the ground opens a league north in the forest; the slope heaves; the lake slops over the shingle. The Antlered ("the Old Stag" to the clans; unnamed in prose) rises: bare grey-brown antlers like a winter wood higher than the hill, stag's head the size of a hill, and the trees round it pull up and walk with it. The beast-flood (deer, wild elk, boar, wild wolves, bears, small beasts, a hundred wild stonehorns) comes down the lanes mad-eyed; wild stonehorns break on the tame ones and go into the lake; floods part round the bears; duskcats drop on strays. Orrun raises a shoulder-high turf bank across lane 5; then a second Orrun rises from the turf of lane 6 (empty; the children's roundhouse at its foot) and raises a bank there: half an hour (Idony counted from the boat). Maren in both lanes at once; can't feel her legs from noon to near dawn. Hesper's asking takes too hard in the surge: a wild stonehorn bull turns into the lane-side roundhouse and brings it down on her and Hush; she's singing the lullaby, last line in Maren's tongue. The Antlered walks north-east into the deep Thornwood with its walking forest; out of sight by noon. Forty-odd dead among those who stayed (two horse-clan riders, a Keeper in lane 4 and his wolves); none of the children. Hesper and Hush found together under the roof-tree, Hush across her; Aun kneels and says the lullaby. Orrun won't eat (three days): "She called me Grandfather." Legend at the fires: two lions in two lanes; "the lion-woman can be in two places at once".

## Calder (14, 17, 19, 21, 24, 26)
- Ch 14 (days 25–34): Kharrak hides in a dry gully in the Ashlings, two leagues NE of the Cross. Calder walks in hooded with a borrowed shepherd's crook. The Fleece: three storeys, galleried yard, white ram on green, six white dots painted round the ram's head; top-floor shutters closed. Companies' badges: red hand, black ship, green boar, blue fish, Delling's yellow tower. Tide Council contract board on the beast-pen gate (blue wave over a coin): TEN THOUSAND SILVER, THE GREAT BEAST OF THE MARCHES, ALIVE (means the lion; boards on every Track town). Fake tin "warranted" Titan-charms on red cord, one copper (Calder keeps one). Plan in the taverns: when the Saltreach silver comes, cross the fords below Greyhold into dead Hearthmere and take the Candle Downs. Delling: about 60, heavy, soft-voiced, small careful hands, spectacles on a black cord, yellow tower stitched over the heart; keeps his own books; sits in the back room of the Plough, never the Fleece; has known Corvin two years. Rook's book: black-bound, hymnal-sized, damp-swollen, brass-buckled strap. Delling's price: Calder handed up the Fleece's stairs. Reede seen at a screw-press on a cart in the Fleece's back yard. Corvin glimpsed once at a top window, a lean shape against a lamp. Corvin hangs deserters on the Wendmouth road (three from the black ship at the second milestone). Day 31 noon: the long tremor from the east in the hiring yard. Day 33 bargain: Delling's company (about 200) gets a safe road north over the Scarfell from the Ashlings, down Skarrow's side to the coast and on to Wendmouth, in the Clan's name, three parties a day apart, no burning or taking, ledges' riders to see them over: given **without Arn's leave**. Some of Delling's men were in the gorse at the second ford; one of them shot Hallam. Delling: "a fool, or something I haven't got a word for." Day 34 dawn: north on Kharrak with the book. Brask goes with Delling's first party.
- Ch 17 (days 34–55): night 34 Calder tells Arn the road; Arn: "You gave my moor to the men who shot Hallam"; the bonded keep a bonded man's word given in the Clan's name (old law; Gunna's word from the caves); "you'll have spent everything you had with me"; Arn doesn't ask to see the book. Days 35–37 Delling's company crosses by the old peat road east of the hold in three parties, watched by Hulda Sarr/Harl, old Brede and his blue, and two more pairs; second party (~70) stops under Kharrak's crag, the heavy leader lifts a palm; Brask with the first. Ada reads the ledger at the cave mouth a few pages a night, drying stuck pages on the warm floor under pebbles (Wren helps). Entries: three bags up from the coast, the ring on every bag; the miller at Harrow's End through the coin man, the farmer's road and hour, two silver; the bucket in Ulric's yard, a week, unpaid (my own time); Upper Rush barns, the red hand, twenty; "The drover, Varrhold, paid, for the winter: the uncle's steward, four silver and keep"; "A horse, from the uncle's lines; to the coast for my mother. Returned." (nothing more for the first ford); "The gorse at the ford, the second meeting: sixty, Delling's. Thirty silver the captain, two each the men. One bolt over the count, for the old one: five." Ada knew Hallam young (he brought Morrow to the dues-school). Day 44 Lisska east (flight 1). Wren reads ahead of Ada by night 10. Word sent to Skarrow: "There's a book." Day 54 Lisska home at dusk, knot still tied, with the knights-and-chain news; moss packed into the horn-wound in the setters' spring water; the wound's heat goes down; Aurveth sleeps still. Day 55: a separate older five-line list stuck inside the back cover: "The hedge at the bottom of the Fenn field, one bolt, the Sallow rider. Crane, Delling's, out of Wendmouth. Forty, before the company's hire." Ned Crane walked home over the moor under Calder's road (days 35–37). Lisska silent all night; doesn't joke for weeks. Calder (32) asks Ada for all his letters; starts with C drawn with a burnt stick on the cave floor.
- Ch 19 (days 56–78; plan said 50–60, moved): Skarrow, east side of the Scarfell in a long green valley toward the coast: turf-roofed stone hall as long as the Wheel, byres, folds, ~300 men in plain grey camped round; no dragons (Ulric unbonded; young dragons have stood before him on choosing days and turned away). Orm Garrick (lean, old, white scar through an eyebrow, grey hill-pony). Ulric: broader, greyer; learned letters at the dues-school with Haldor; reads without moving lips, wetted thumb. Night 57 he reads all of it; stops at the drover's wage (his steward paid it) and the horse: a bay mare with a white sock on the near hind, given by his own hand in his own yard, returned lathered in four days. Day 58 in his yard: he'll stand in Arn's yard and be called a murderer, "if the farmer stands beside me. Not in his hall. In his yard." Day 58 Wyldmarch fur-trader (grey-and-black fur coat, red bars on cheeks, eight ponies of pelts and amber): knights with the chain and colleges with snow-cats and lamps went into the Thornwood; a lion-woman went in ahead; the clans call her "the one who sits with it". Days 60–62: Ulric with twenty men; gate shut; Arn in black: "I'll not touch it"; Ulric sits his horse all night, Calder at his stirrup; the ledges (Hulda, Brede, Ivar, Leif and others) stand behind; dawn: "When you're ready to read it, boy, you know where I am." Ulric returns the book ("It's yours. You paid for it"). Day 64 above the Skell Water: two steadings burned half a league apart: an Arn steading (red thread on the gatepost) with plain grey cloth knotted on the door, a shepherd and his boy dead (the Brack shepherd's); an Ulric steading (no thread) with a red thread knotted on the gate, family fled to the high fold. Hulda Sarr speaks: Wendmouth boots with brass eyelets, forty or more, going SW toward the Ram's Head; "Not one Clan boot." Ulric's board with a dried clay boot-print and the grey cloth left on Varrhold's step; Arn rode alone to the Brack steading day 66, stood an hour; took the board into the hall, under the chair. By day 75 Calder writes CALDER (L too big, D backwards; Wren corrects). Day 70 Aurveth: "It is better"; Ivar: another month before she tries the ledge. Day 78 Lisska speaks for the first time since day 55 and goes east (flight 2): "Is the hedge man home now? ... Then I know where he is."
- Ch 21 (day 98): midsummer is the Clan's choosing night (old law: outside all quarrels; young of age stand on the Choosing Ledge below the Roosts before the young dragons let out of the pens). Day 85 Arn's first word to Ulric since the ford (Gorm wrote it): Skarrow's young may come, with Ulric and twenty; Ulric came day 97 with eleven young and camped by the west hearth's outer wall; gate open. Lisska home day 95 (flight 2), speaking a little. Forty young dragons; thirty young people; a young bronze stops before the Skarrow girl with her eyes shut (it's opened in the smoke; she's pushed down the stair by Ulric). Tor Brecc (19, Hallam's nose; bonded two midsummers ago to Skell, a slender nervous young blue who rests his chin on Tor's shoulder). An hour after dark honey-smoke rolls down the cliff from a line of fires a quarter-league NE on the moor. Kharrak goes under, then comes back on his own fury and lights the cliff red. Lisska: "like a boat off its rope"; Calder holds the rope all night, nose bleeds. Aurveth holds herself and him: the first knights' binders smoked her three days on the bridge and cut Ennis out with a knife and a word; "a place that has been cut once heals harder." Raiders on grapnel ropes from below and from the cliff-top (plain coats, dark caps, wet cloths, Wendmouth boots); binders with smoking pots on chains and hinged six-dot collars; two bound wyverns (black-green, barbed tails) with riders. Gunna on Tamsin (both with wet sheets) knocks two binders off the ledge; a pot breaks; burning resin on Tamsin's wing and Gunna's cloak; Ivar Leck and Leif drag them up the stair; Gunna burned on arm, back and half her face; Tamsin's wing's whole trailing edge black; both alive. A straggler binder puts a hand on Skell; Tor kills him with an axe; Skell, half bound, turns on Tor; Tor won't lift the axe, hand open; a bolt from the second wyvern takes Skell under the jaw; Tor goes with him in a breath; they lie together at the lip; someone (Ulric, by the size) lays a plain grey cloak over them. Arn (sword) and Ulric (long axe) hold the stair-head side by side without a word while the young go down. Corvin on the second wyvern, plain face, wet cloth round his neck: "Four. Those four. Leave the rest." Looks once at Calder. Taken: a big bronze (the biggest on the ledge), a black, two reds; south-east over the moor. Calder stops Kharrak following. Dead: Tor and Skell; three of Arn's and two of Ulric's on the stair; four raiders. One binder taken alive (Ulric's knee on his back). At dawn the Roosts' stone hums toward the east; Aurveth: "Something has opened."
- Ch 24 (days 99–102): the captured binder (small, thin, ~40, clerk's hands with pot-burns, Wendmouth boots): hired at Wendmouth, paid in advance by a nameless man; twelve new Saltreach coins (ship / wave, burr on the edge, ring of six struck in the rim, in the die): the coin man's silver from Book 1 ch 24; Ulric keeps them. Night 99 Arn has Gorm fetch the book and reads it alone by the east fire, slowly, finger under the line; stops long at the horse; gives it back next morning without a word. Day 100 one pyre for all: Tor and Skell on an ash-pole sledge drawn by Harl, Brede's blue and two more; three red-thread dead, two grey. Night 101 Ada lights the pyre with the hearth-word before her hand finishes turning. Every dragon of the Roosts on the cliffs; the cry at dawn goes down. Aurveth walks up to the tarn on four legs (half a day). Lisska: "I'd have lifted it... I'm glad he didn't." Day 102 the pour on the black stone, two urns: Arn turns: "Uncle. Pour with me. One hand each." Arn names the man at the Fleece and his ring; the Clan stands against him, red and grey. Ulric: the war between red and grey is over at the black water. The chair stays empty "till we've buried my brother right": till whoever paid is named; then both halves pour for the chair. Ulric's count: "All but four." Errand on the stone: bring the four home (bronze, black, two reds); bring back the names of whoever paid. "Why me?" Ulric gives him a coin: a farmer who's sold to factors twenty years knows a factor's coin; Arn: "You're the only one of us who can follow a coin." The ledges stand behind him at the stone's root. Afternoon: a second Wyldmarch trader (salt and iron, going east): the great one flew west off the stones on midsummer night; the lion-woman's going to the Moot with a Keeper, the lion and a lowlander with a box. In Calder's coat: the book, Rook's knife, the iron ring, Reede's bill, the tin charm, a six-rim coin, the stone.

## Idony (16, 22, 27)
- Ch 16 (days 58–74): day 58 Warden Ysolde Marr's snow lynx Frost, eyes empty for a count of eleven in the sleepwood smoke. Dane: tall, broad, heavy-handsome, short grey beard, Blue College, five notches on his ring. Day 61 the grey's edge: sharp as frost on a pane; nothing rots (Idony keeps a dead wren in his handkerchief); sleeve-wards (grey silk, blue-thread runes) and the lamp-frame runes go dull; Warden Pask's light-word fails. The grey: a near-true circle, a league in radius from the Ring, growing ~120 paces a week (blazed trees). Day 64 the Titan from a rise: breath 11 counts in, 19 out; ten stones visible, two under its flank; hollow owls on the stone-tops; chain-ends on the forelegs. Day 65 the field-glass (cut for *here*) lies dead on the grey and runs full on the warm, humming ring-stone: "full like a cup"; the stir = "a drain with a full cup at its mouth" (the word *stir* only in his silk copy). Dane: stand Wardens on the stones to draw (ten reachable, twelve Wardens, two for the lamps). Day 66 Rowan (ink-stained second finger) and Dane at a fallen grey oak; knights' bonds work on the grey; chain stakes go into ground soft as ash; Idony: stones nearly full, about a month, "near midsummer". Walker off the glasses day 69 (into the grey); day 71 a faint live trail by a grey stream (quicksilver lifts a hair). Day 74 dusk: Maren and Orrun walk into the ring from the south; Orrun lies at ten paces (eleven of Idony's) from the head; the Titan doesn't turn; "She has her hand on its neck."
- Ch 22 (days 99–103): the surge: the grey goes green from the sink outward in lines and patches (a quarter of the league by noon day 99); nothing rots until evening, then everything does (the wren). Sleeve-wards blaze and scorch; Ysolde and two others tear theirs off. Knights' falcons won't settle; greyharts' ears turned east. Hesper's finding-knot winds tight and cuts her two fingers (red rings); she cuts it off with her spear. Keepers' moss: Idony's scalp gash a pink line by morning; Rowan's open fracture below the knee knitting "like a month in a night". Five ring-stones still stand, their tops hazed. Day 99 Dane orders Pask up the east stone to draw and measure; he can't stop; Maren pulls him off onto Orrun; by dawn day 100 his red hair is white and his hands shake for good (burned out; talks of a sister Ell). Four glasses left (three green whole, one cracked); they climb fastest to the south: the sleeper's breath rises toward Lake Ysse, two days south, where Kethra's Moot is in its last week; within seven days, probably fewer. Days 100–101 under the stones (ch 23). Night 101 Dane: three binding-lamps whole, five Wardens fit; bind the sleeper as it wakes; the lamps' crystal is old (weathered cuts, dust): the College "has been preparing for this for forty years". Dawn day 102 Idony breaks his oath to Dane's face ("I'll answer for it") and goes south with Maren, Orrun, Hesper, Hush. Day 103 dusk Lake Ysse: long, still, grey, mirror-calm; the Moot on the north shore (hundreds of roundhouses, hide tents, ~a hundred upturned hide boats, elk, bears, wolves, a duskcat on every roof-ridge, three rocs on a dead tree, singing). Aun: "How sure?" "Sure enough to have walked two days." The Moot decides in the morning. Echo: the three-together stir whole again.
