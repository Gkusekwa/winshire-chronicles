# Book 2, Part Three: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as `Part1_Ledger.md` and `Part2_Ledger.md`. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (28, 32, 34, 37, 40)
- Ch 28 (days 111–135): west on foot over the drained Plains; the hearth-word fails there (flint from Hesper's bag); she carries Hesper's hide bag with the last moss and the green cord. Orrun eating a little; the ash-pale band at his mane-roots stays ("It is only what it cost. It stays, so I remember"). Day 118 live ground at the High Crown foothills; Orrun turns north up a side valley: "Kin." Day 120 a high bowl under the snowline: thirty tuskers (red-brown, tusks curling back like old roots); they ring the calves; the old cow (grey-faced, one tusk broken a hand's length from the lip and worn yellow, the other a loop longer than a man) meets Orrun at ten paces; the rumble (felt, not heard); trunk to nose; she remembers where one of his kind walked with her mother's mother's mother on a road. Ten days on the herd's summer road west along the High Crown's southern flank, 3–4 leagues a day; they won't cross dead ground (a day's detour round a drained valley). Day 124 four sabrecats at dusk; Orrun opens a crevasse under one; the old cow lays her trunk across his shoulder. Day 127 the herd walks round a meadow (once water) and the old cow lays her trunk on a waist-high leaning old-realm milestone with worn hooked marks. Day 131 from the shoulder: Glasswater long and blue; Vael's slate towers and covered bridges; the Library's half-league front cut into the hilltop with tall narrow windows; the Titan lying on the lake like an island that breathes; at dusk the lower city's lamps brown and guttering, the Library blazing white, a cold off the lake. Day 133 the pass-stone above the Vael Pass (flat-topped, shoulder-high, smoothed on top); the old cow lays her trunk on it; the herd won't go down in summer; she'll be there in winter \"if I want her\"; Orrun: yes. Day 135 the lake gate (iron-rail fence, toll-house, four Wardens, sleeve-wards burning steadily): the register of wild power (columns: name, beast, gift, reach, mark); Maren gives her name, refuses Orrun (\"He's not a gift. He's not mine\"); a big short-haired Warden lets her pass; the clerk writes something small beside her name. Echo: a Saltreach salt-merchant: a fortnight ago (~day 121) a dragon farmer came down on Gallows Quay in broad day with a red and a green dragon and burned the Halloran house's warehouse to the waterline; the red knocked their drawer into the harbour with its tail; the farmer stood in the smoke with a book under his arm; the Tide Council's offering money for him. The road under the Library's cliff-front: from a high window the Old Reader: \"Maren Ashdown.\" Orrun goes very still.
- Ch 32 (days 139–140): Orrun gets up in the night of day 139 of his own choosing: "I will go up. It asked." Day 140 up through the city: the cup's edge at Candle Row felt through him in one step (thin to full); a Warden patrol with lynx stands aside at the top of Rope Street. The Library's great door (black oak on iron pins, built for book-carts) is high enough; readers back away; one old white-haired inky reader keeps writing. Half a league; Orrun slows as the hum rises ("It is under here"). The Old Reader: "Door-keeper." It knows his face from a carving on a door that isn't there any more and from a page a frightened man cut from a book. Orrun lies at ten paces: "Not long enough." "Nobody keeps anything long enough." The bird smells the black book's vellum in her coat; comes down onto the reading-desk and turns the pages with its beak; looks long at MAREN ASHDOWN. Seven stubs near the front; the fourth has the forefoot (four toes, claws drawn in) and "…kin". "He cut out the cat... He left a foot." The first archivist wrote it twice, once in the book and once in a letter to the College that sold the chains ("Your college keeps its letters"); it will tell them later; "write it down. For yourself. And for the farmer" (it knows: "three dragons chose one man in a barley field"). The lock's sentence (copied in Ada's charcoal on day 3, sitting beside the floor-disc in the lock chamber): its feathers rise and don't settle; Idony: "a binding of two... a form I've never seen." One word given: *together*. "The lock was made for two hands... So was the other thing." Keep the book in her coat; let no one read the back aloud, "Not even him. Not even me." Echo at dusk from the hill-end window (NE over the city and lake to the Iron Fells): a small green shape going north along the snow-line, then gone.
- Ch 34 (day 152; summary days 141–151): Orrun back on the shingle; Maren at the Library every second day, the bird talking of small things. Day 152 the telling, from the first archivist's letter (the College's copy, 306 years old): march-land, no kingdom; a border lord's quarrymen cut into the river hill above the bend and found a carved shrine with steps; the lord and his young clerk (the later first archivist) went down to a long dry cavern under the river and found the sleeper ("the most beautiful thing I have ever seen") and a plinth with a warm stone egg the size of a cart-wheel's hub and a stone ring as wide as two hands with six hollows. The College of Tides sent a Master and two Readers; the sleep is in the egg "as a flame is held in a lantern"; the chains were forged in the cavern over a winter while it slept; the lord broke the egg with a hammer; it woke slowly over days with the chains already on. The ring was gone afterwards: "Nobody knows where it went" (Idony doubts the bird). The lord's men took *Keep the watch below* from the arch at the bridge for their oath; the College took the arch's last line *do not ask* (a warning) and made it a muzzle. The great grey cat on the white tower's steps (people bowed; the archivist as a boy) fought them on the bridge (a step rose and crushed a man); driven off with pitch-fire on poles by a hundred men. Orrun stands; Maren feels heat, pitch, pain along his left flank under the ribs: the old ragged scar she thought was from the island hunters. "I remember the fire." Ennis, warden of the bridge, stood in the door with his old bronze; the lord's binders ("Hearthmere's first knights were binders before they were knights") smoked her three days and "took the witness out of the beast", cut him out of her bond alive, then killed him. The bronze went north; a hundred years later the Clan's first chief found her on a cliff above the Scarfell; Ragnald Holm (rockfall; she lay a year on Coldtarn's shore and didn't follow; the scar held); Sigrun (59 years); three outlived; now a fourth shared three ways, never in the Clan's songs. "Ask your farmer whether he has wondered why her. Why him." Idony's silk drawings: the faceless shapes: "The Unbound" (the seventh ring; a first-years' joke, "when you get your seventh notch"); it won't explain. Maren fills eleven pages in Kessmoor graphite (Idony's cream College paper), folds them into her coat with the black book for the farmer. The half-turn: the Titan takes relics knowing exactly what they are; nobody asks why. Echo at dusk: Idony: two dragons coming over the Fells from the south-east; "the glass says three... the third is a long way south. Still." Maren: "A barley farmer."

## Calder (30, 33, 35, 38, 41)
- Ch 30 (days 115–130): Aurveth stays at Varrhold. Wendmouth from the air (Marrow blue, Quill white, Strand green, Halloran red and gold, the most of it along Gallows Quay); the reward on the Tidesteps' rail now 500 silver over Book 1's 200. Kharrak lies up in a reedy creek in the saltings north of the city. Day 117 Brask at the Crane (thinner, yellowing black eye; home day 45; Delling paid the company off at the fish-docks, two silver a man; the company broken). The Halloran house holds the Tide Council's licence to strike the ship-and-wave; the mint is a vaulted cellar under the Halloran counting-house on Mint Lane behind Gallows Quay (three storeys, red brick, red-and-gold shutters, a ship-shaped brass knocker, a barred street grating over a chute); Brask's cousin carts the blanks every tenth day; chests go out at each new moon by barge up the Wend. Night 117 Lisska sits all night on the roof of the yellow-tower lodging-house by the fish-docks and does nothing ("It's horrible"). Night 120 Brask's chandler's bar lifts the grating; in the mint: three presses, a rack of ship/wave die-pairs and one rim-collar with six dots cut inside (Calder keeps it); the red-leather gold-spined mint-book on a lectern. Calder reads T-E-O himself. Brask reads: "The Varr boy at the ford: three hundred, for the account of the House, at the instruction of the Council's member" (Halloran holds a Tide Council seat, a hundred years). Also: wages for the drover at Varrhold; "the Halloran woman, lent, for the gate"; "The four, bound, north, for a library: the hunters' wages, a year's"; "The companies at Ashford: a chest, up the Wend, at every new moon" (the army crossed into Hearthmere in summer and holds Ashford). Rusk's under-clerk Piers Abbot (19) asleep behind the presses: Rusk is at the Tide Council in Corsa; Vael's colleges buy binding-lamps by the cartload via Halloran (crystal from the Iron Fells side of Kessmoor, shipped up the Wend and over the pass; the house takes a tenth), more than a hundred wanted. Tied, left alive. Dawn day 121: six guards and the house's new drawer (lean, grey, plain coat) in Mint Lane; his weight throws them against the wall; Kharrak comes over Gallows Quay in broad day and tail-sweeps the drawer into the harbour. Calder frees every caged beast on the quay (a hooded wild wyvern among them); leaves the phoenix feather; Kharrak burns the empty Halloran warehouse to the waterline; Calder stands in the smoke with the book, face seen. Five days up the coast to a twelve-hut fishing hamlet; the reward a thousand silver by day 124. Day 127 Brask goes to the Scarfell by the coast road with the six-rim coin as token. Day 130 north on Kharrak with Lisska toward the Iron Fells and Vael, carrying both books, the knife, the iron ring, the bill, the tin charm, the rim-collar and the stone.
- Ch 33 (days 131–150): the Iron Fells (long grey range SE from the High Crown, rust-red gullies, snow on the tops in summer; Kessmoor's mines and spoil on the south flank, goat-shielings on the Isvaren side). Calder knows Vael only from Piers Abbot and Brask ("nothing but libraries"). Five days searching from the air at dawn and dusk, lying up in snowy corries by day. Day 140 Lisska goes north along the snow-line alone at dusk and drops into a valley (seen from the Library, ch 32). Day 141 the camp: a high bowl on the Isvaren side under a north cornice, a day's flight NE of Vael; a dozen grey tents, a rope corral, a stone hearth of smouldering iron pots whose honey-smoke runs by a cut trench to the four young dragons chained to stakes (the big bronze, the black, a two-year-old red, and a yearling red hardly bigger than Lisska was); six-dot collars painted white; the two bound wyverns on a ledge above; ~twenty hunters, four binders (two by day, two by night, touching each head in turn), and a silent Saltreach drawer on a rock. Day 143 dusk Lisska's kin-call (the ledges' mother-hum) from the ridge's east end: the yearling red lifts her head and looks at the ridge with her own eyes for one breath; a binder's hand empties her again. "She'll know my voice now." Day 145 a summer snowstorm; day 146 morning Tasso (~12, goat-herd, sheepskin coat fleece-in, no hat, frost in his hair, not shivering) in a gully a quarter-league below the camp. No fuel; Calder's hands flat on the boy's chest, three turns, Ada's hearth-word ("It's not for fire, it's for warm"), Kharrak's head over his shoulder, the forge-heat down the bond: the fleece smoulders (a scorched patch two hands wide), Calder's palms blister, he holds on; the boy coughs, screams, shivers, lives. Palms wrapped in shirt-strips, raw a week. Tasso: the hunters took him a fortnight before to guide them over the cornice pass and paid his father in new silver; he fetched water and wood (three hours down, five back) and fed "the sweet pots"; a lantern owl brings papers from the city; the captain says "not yet... when the hill burns all through" they go to the library, to the old bird, "for a paper" he's been paid to fetch; the silent coast man knocked two sabrecats over backward with a flat hand. Tasso's family shieling two valleys over; Kharrak flies him home (he screams with joy); his mother gives Calder a whole goat's cheese and asks nothing. Night 149 echo: Vael's hill lit white from top to a third of the way down, the lower city almost dark ("cold fire"). Day 150 Calder goes west to watch from Starwatch, the star-tower on the highest peak (six hundred steps).

## Idony (29, 31, 36, 39)
- Ch 29 (days 121–135): Idony down the pass road at dusk day 121; the Titan has been on Glasswater since day 116, a mile out from the lower quays, between the city and the far shore; ferries go round it a quarter-mile off. The stir: lower-city lamps brown and guttering from the water up; the hill-top lamps (Library, Hall of Rings, Tides, Blue College, observatory) burning white; a line between them a third of the way up the hill (the cup's edge) coming down a street a week (Rope Street day 124, Candle Row day 131). The hum through the Long Library's floor; readers keep their feet on chair-rungs; field-glass full on the floor. The cold off the lake comes from under the Titan: a new deep glass (*deep* / *under the water*) falls and rises in a slow breathing rhythm; day 126 frost on the ferry steps at dawn, nets come up stiff. A ring of flat still water a hundred paces wide round the Titan; breath 11 in, 19 out. Nessa (35, square, sunburned, scarf) with Tib (12, a cousin's son who lives with her); every ferry now throws a handful of coarse grey salt over the prow "for the Lady" (their mother did; Nessa had stopped); the lantern owl turns on its perch to stare at the island; Mother Talley's forty-year lamp at the fish-market corner went out. Day 123 the Council in the Hall of Rings (Dane standing, both hands bandaged to the wrist): "How long will it lie there?" Idony: fifty days, the height of the stir near day 170. Judgment of his breach deferred; meanwhile he's the Council's reader for the working, to stand at its centre and call the hour. The great binding (planned in Quell's rooms days 123–130, with Dane, the masters of Blue, Tides and Scribes, and a silent very old Keeper of the Script from the Sixth-ring house): a hundred magisters (every Fifth and Fourth ring in Vael) in a ring on the Library hill, a many-voice working fed from the full stone. Day 127 Quell shows him the form: an ancient vellum roll in rust-brown ink with the College of Tides' mark, tighter than the grammar's many-voice forms, with a closing line telling the voices not to think about what they're doing; "It's ours"; she won't say from when. Day 128 the Old Reader hears everything; asks whether the walker gave back; "When she comes, bring her to me"; "What's under the hill?" "Not yet, ferryman." Day 133 echo outside Dane's door: the pass-watch reports the lion walking at the front beside the eldest cow; Dane: "Register it at the gate"; "And if she won't?" no answer. Day 135 a Library clerk: the Old Reader walked off its perch for the first time in living memory, a quarter-league to a window over the lake road, sat an hour, said one thing, walked back.
- Ch 31 (days 136–138): Orrun lies on the shingle at the water's edge past the end of the old (sinking) fish-quay below the lower city, facing the island a mile out ("as close as ten paces gets, here"); the glass lifts a finger's breadth where he lies. Maren: she'll go to the bird "when he'll leave the water. Not yet." Day 137 echo at the Library's door-end tables: a Scribe's cousin is a register clerk; the big Warden on the gate was Brannagh; "Who's going to fetch her? You?" Night 138 the bird sends for Idony and says please. A carved panel at the back of the perch's foot hides a niche; a dark-wood case bound in tarnished silver with the College of Tides' old mark (a wave over a glass). "Three hundred and four years. Nobody has asked." The contract: the College of Tides by its Master Aldwyn Sarre, Keeper of the Script, and "the lord of the river hill, in the country by the Wend". Clauses: (1) measure the sleeper beneath the river hill (a reading-table in the ancestor of Idony's notation); (2) design and cut the forms for six chains, every link bearing on its inner curve the rune of holding, which feeds on what it holds; (3) a common charm for many voices at the noon bell facing the place: *Keep the watch below* feeds the holding, *and do not ask* "so that the voices shall not doubt; doubt being the solvent of all many-voiced working"; (4) price: silver and "a tenth of the light, for so long as the light shall last" (why the College of Tides' blue lamps burned so cheaply); (5) keep the matter "outside study, in perpetuity" (Quell's ruling). Idony realises the great binding's form is the noon oath. Appendix: the rune of holding drawn stroke by stroke and its working-words; he reads them twice and keeps them in his head, unwritten. The bird: the chain-ends on the Titan's forelegs still say *hold* and still feed; whoever can speak to them "has a hand on it". "Because the door is open... and I am old." Bring the knight in two days, "Tell her I said please." He watches Maren on the shingle till dawn.
