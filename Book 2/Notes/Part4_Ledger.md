## Maren (42, 45, 48)
- Ch 42 (days 178–205): dead ground from Vael to the Fells' western saddle (Isvaren's drain); Maren's arm comes back the first evening (hot-water pain), closes her hand day 180, holds a cup day 182. Idony walks bent with Nessa's sawn-off boathook for a stick, stops hourly. Snow on the saddle from day 183 (over Orrun's knees; he breaks a channel); over the saddle day 186 in a wind; halfway down the glass lifts a hair: Kessmoor is live, thin, "like a hill farm." Orrun: "It is that way. Far. It is not lying down yet." Idony marks the draw nightly with four glasses in a ring and Kessmoor graphite (\"the one good thing Kessmoor ever made\"): wandering W and NW over the moors at the Coldrun's head, circling; "It wanders longer each time." Day 188 **Coldrun Locks**: the Coldrun canalised west between brick banks in lock-steps every half-mile, iron lift-bridges, chimneys; green alchemical lamps (a salt in water with a metal in it, lit with a spark; not the Deep; Isvaren laughs at it). Sign: SPOKEN WORKINGS FORBIDDEN BY ORDER OF THE ASSEMBLY. / BEASTS OF POWER TO BE PAPERED AT THE GUILD-HOUSE. Guild-clerk (ink cuffs, brass Bleed-gauge on a chain): Idony written *Measurer. Unringed. Isvaren* (pale band where the ring was); "If you've a gauge for the Bleed, it's to be declared"; his glasses declared "Isvaren instruments. Glass. For weather." No guild will stand for the lion (Carters for horses, Drovers for oxen, the Mines for pit-ponies and ironbacks); "nobody's beasts don't go down the canals." Day 189 **Inspector Harl Moss**, the Assembly's Office of the Cordon (forty, neat grey coat and waistcoat, clean-shaven, dry; brass gauge since he was nineteen, his father's before), by chimneyed iron steam-launch from the west, with six cordon men in oilcloth with marked crossbows. The lion stories came down the canals with the salt-barges. Three hundred gauges on the cordon read hourly for forty years; all began to climb on day 184. "We've been waiting forty years for it to wake. We'd as soon not wait alone." Moss papers Orrun himself under the Office's red seal: *A beast of the cordon. Kept on the Office's account. Not to be hindered.* Maren: "Write what you like. He won't read it." Days 189–196 nine days by an iron-sheathed horse-barge (pig-iron under tarpaulins, a big grey horse and a boy) down the locks in sleet; the lion walks the far towpath and waits for the barge; Moss's launch ahead. The country black and live-thin: chimneys, spoil-heaps, brick rows, engine-houses with turning wheels and a thud. Day 192 a four-chimneyed engine-house: Idony's glass leans toward it "like a drinking" but there's no Deep in it and the ground round isn't dying (heartstones: ch 44). Day 196 Ironhythe (brick, iron, smoke up a hill, green lamps at noon, the Hall of Measures' white-faced clock "never been wrong"); through without stopping onto **the Scald Cut** and **the Iron Road** south-east to the border. Echo, last lock out of Ironhythe: a Hearthmere envoy (crown-blue cloak, Hallowmere seal on a chain, two guards in Hearthmere grey, a ledger clerk) buying chain for "the crown's work": twelve hundredweight each from the Gannet forge and Holloway's, links bigger than ever made, "a year's work... As if we've got a year"; and a farmer in the Marches with dragons, "Three of them, they say", who burned a Saltreach mint, flies in broad day, "and the Marches folk feed him... Nobody's heard of the crown." Maren doesn't turn round. Steam wagons on iron rails beside the cut; Orrun: "They move and they are not alive... I do not like them." The ground thins to dead over three days toward a yellow-grey sky; day 201 evening all four glasses lean SE: the Titan turned S out of the moors four nights before and has lain down on the Scald. Day 204 the cordon: a grassed earth bank the height of a house with an outer ditch, huts with chimneys every few hundred paces, oilcloth men with crossbows, forty years old. Inside, **the cage**: three concentric canals of chilled running water (grey cold-haze, frost on the brick edges, fast, one direction all round) and two rings of iron pylons (each three men high, a hundred paces apart) with sagging iron cables between; four engine-houses at the quarters; "forty-one leagues of cable. And eleven thousand marks" (hooked runes struck up each pylon; "In Kessmoor we call them marks. Runes are spoken of. We don't speak of things"). **Master Engineer Agathe Pike** of the Cordon (sixty-plus, tall, heavy-shouldered, grey hair cropped short, old leather coat black at the cuffs, smoked spectacles pushed up on her forehead, soot ground into her knuckles; measures the lion like a smith a horse; "Two feet... In how long?" "A morning."). Water chilled through coils in the engine-houses, never stopped since the first cut was flooded; "Cold running water breaks it harder." "The cables hold the field. The field holds whatever's in it." Her father, a junior wright at Orrin, was in the shaft-house that night and came out sane because he was watching a gauge; began the cage; she finished it four years ago. "For whatever comes up." From the north-quarter watch-tower (iron stair in an iron frame) through a brass glass: the Scald basin, wider than Glasswater, fused black, green and brown glass, smooth or rippled like a pond frozen in wind, Orrin's streets and house-footings melted into it; white glass moths ("They ring, when they hit the glass... You'll hear them at night"). The Titan lying in the middle, head on paws, wings folded like a roof; came down three days before; the gauges climb a mark an hour; the basin's glass warm for the first time. Orrun lies on the outer bank's old grass at the north quarter facing it. Pike: "They tell me you can read when it'll go down." Idony: "A month. Perhaps less. It's quicker every time." "Good. I'd like to know when to close it... The cage. On whatever comes up."
- Ch 45 (days 230–235): day 230 Pike lets them over the three canals by narrow iron service bridges on pivots (swing aside when the field closes; "while it's open... Moss will write it down"); the pylon cables hum like rigging; the marks stay dark until the field closes. Day 231 onto the basin in boots wrapped in sacking (the glass scorches leather in a few hundred paces); **Idony rides Orrun for the first time** (behind the shoulders, hands wound in the mane, stick across his knees, the tower's brass glass on a cord): "I've never." "I know." Orrin in the glass: house-footings like knuckles under skin, a fused doorway, a sunk well-head, a street of doorways, a cart's two iron tyres melted into black rings a pace apart. Moths rise ringing at every step. Noon at the Titan: glass like a hearthstone; dead brown moss in its plate-cracks; chain-ends rusted black and red, links as thick as her body, the last link on the glass by its paw. Orrun lies at ten paces, then gets up and goes the last ten; puts his forehead to the foreleg above the band and chain-end. Through him, like weather: a want: *All of them. Awake.* ... *And one.* The runes inside every link's inner curve light white through the rust; Idony reads them leaning down: *Hold. Hold. Hold.* "It's still feeding... On what it holds." Three breaths; Orrun steps back; the light goes. The Titan never moves. Orrun: "It wants them up. All of them. My kin... And it wants... one. I do not know what one is. It knows. It is very sure." Idony mouths *hold* all the way back. Dawn 232 Idony gives Pike the day: "The thirty-third. Near noon." Echo, dusk 232 (flight 3): Lisska in from the east, thin (ribs showing), on the north-quarter tower rail; speaks to Orrun as kin within sight; Orrun relays: the farmer came down in Hallowmere's square ("the dead one, where all the lamps are glass") with the red one and Lisska; papers (Ada's writing and his, the mint-books); the council on the steps and a big crowd; a woman knight with grey hair from the Ring (Rowan) said he told the truth and she'd seen what chains do now; a man read his paper aloud; the river people (the Saltreach refugees) aren't to be driven out; the crown still wants his head. Lisska stays to carry back Maren's answer "to whatever happens here". Day 233 noon, the whole cordon on the bank: the Titan stands, looks round the ring once, puts its head down between its forepaws; the fused floor cracks outward in rings, plates lifting and sliding, the moths up in one white ringing cloud; its head down in the hole to the shoulders; something gives far down; it lifts **the egg** (grey old-realm stone, a little longer than round, carved with something long and low going round it), then turns its neck over its own shoulder and lays it between the folded wings among the roof-slate plates: the plate opens like water round a dropped stone, closes, is plate again. An old cordon man with his own brass glass: two more lumps under the plates either side (the Ring's and Vael's). It flies west low over the west quarter (cordon men flat; Maren and Orrun stand) toward the Western Deep. Orrun: "Not yet. Here first." **The surge** at dusk as the Bleed: a bright moving shimmer over the broken basin, the chilled canals smoking white, the green alchemical lamps flaring white and some cracking (Idony: it's the glass taking it, not the lamps), gauges bending on their pins; the Bleed in the ditches at the east quarter where the Iron Road comes in. Pike's forty-man crews still in the inner and outer ditches: laughing, a man digging the silt bare-handed with bleeding fingers, two lying smiling in the water, one walking singing a child's song toward the chilled canal, one trying to climb out and crying. **Tobin** comes along the bank from the east gate with rope off the gatehouse rail: thin, bones and eyes, grey coat in rags, boots tied with string, hair grown out, a thin young beard, a pale band where his ring was. Maren goes down with the rope (the Bleed in her legs like strong wine); Orrun lies along the ditch lip and heaves the ditch-floor under each man and leans the side out so he's pushed up (bond-shaping); Tobin talks nonsense to them the whole time like a voice to a frightened horse ("That's the best digging I've seen... there's better silt over here"). Eleven out of the inner ditch, twenty-six out of the outer by the white lamps on the Iron Road, half the night; three dead face-down. Thirty-four tied to the gatehouse's iron rail with their own rope; Pike: "Forty years. My father told me what it looked like." By morning some quiet, by the third day most; two never. On the bank: Tobin walked from Millbrook in summer, down the Downs and over the dead country, following the stories (Ring stakes, two places at the Moot, the wall at Vael; "Every inn. Every barge"); threw his ring in the river at the Ashford ford when the companies came over, after seeing what they did at a farm on the Downs ("Our side"); "I said to you, *when they throw you out, come and find us*... Well. I threw myself out. So I came and found you." "I'm glad." Before dawn 234 Idony: "Who's this?" Tobin: "I'm unchosen. Loud about it. And unringed now." Idony: "It makes two of us." "Five days... No. Fewer. Four, I think." "And the cage closes... On whatever comes up."
- Ch 48 (day 238; aftermath to 242): before dawn Moss explains *the Furnace*: Orrin's survivors said what came up the shafts was hot and moved, "like opening a furnace door and something looking out"; in every report for forty years, half a joke; "Is it a joke now?" "No." Stations: Pike at the north core's lever; Moss and Idony on the north tower; cordon men every hundred paces on the bank; a man at every pylon on the inner banks; the last cart of ironback plate (in at midnight) still being swung off by the north-quarter crane onto the outer bank's slope above the first canal. Dawn: the hole stops breathing; the moths go up and wait; the glass round the hole glows red, orange, runs like wax; **the Furnace**: a snout barn-wide, blunt and shovel-flat like a boar's, grey-black house-sized plates overlapping like an ironback's with red molten joints; it pushes, melting glass ahead that sets black behind; molten glass runs off its plates without marking them; it goes along under the basin floor like a mole under a lawn (Tobin). Orrun: "It is the one with its snout in the floor. On the wall... It is so hot." Pike closes the cage: a long shudder; all eleven thousand marks light dull red like coals (heartstones, not the Deep); the cables go taut with whip-cracks racing round both ways until they meet; a light-bending shimmer goes up from the inner ring higher than she can see. The Furnace stops at it; its draw on the whole Deep under the moor comes back on itself ("Like a man trying to drink from a cup and finding his own mouth"); it goes down under the edge; where it breaks the third canal's brick bed the chilled water rushes down its tunnel and a white steam column goes up higher than the pylons with a scream like every hot iron in Kessmoor quenched at once; it bursts back up inside the field. Same west, south, east. The cage holds. On the fourth (north) try all the north-quarter banks heave. **Maren's second casting**: the outer earth bank slides inward toward the first canal with the crane, cart and a dozen men; both hands flat in the earth (alive with the surge/Bleed, warm, buzzing), Orrun's earth in her arms; **the knights' stand-word** (learned overhearing Pell use it at fourteen in Greyhold's yard on a rearing grey with a student on the bridle: "You say it into the beast, not at it... It doesn't make it calm. It makes it stand. That's all. Long enough"; and his hand story: *The wyvern took it standing*), said low into the ground: the bank stops, shaking; the men scramble up, the horses are cut loose and gallop past; she lets go; the bank slides and settles; the crane, cart and stacked plates go into the canal; nobody on it. She can't feel below her waist; falls sideways. Then the three inner north-quarter banks (canal 1–2, 2–3, 3–basin) slide with the pylon crews and the bridges swung back; a weight sits on her chest; **she loses the hour**. Tobin's account: Orrun jumped from the outer bank over the first canal onto the first inner bank; the second bank broke open and a second Orrun stood up out of it; then the third; **three Orruns** (first time two shadows), heads down, forefeet braced "like they were pushing a cart up a hill"; crews hauled over at the bridge pivots on ropes, every one; two sank back "like water going into sand"; the one left lay on the first bank. Maren lay with her eyes open the whole hour counting aloud up to nineteen and back down, over and over. She wakes in the afternoon with Tobin holding her head out of the wet grass. Orrun can't shape stone for a week (day 239 a small gatehouse stone won't crack: "It is gone. For now. It will come back... I made two of me. Two, and me... It cost"); doesn't eat for four days. By afternoon the Furnace lies half-sunk in its own glass in mid-basin, joints dull red like a banked fire, inside the field: caged. In the north engine-house a man rakes grey heartstone ash out of the core into a barrow; the last cart's plates hooked dripping out of the canal and carried straight in; Pike watching the drum, hands on hips. Before dusk Maren's **first letter to Calder** (she writes Liss monthly), on the back of Idony's last cream sheet in Kessmoor graphite, plain square letters, five lines: *The cage holds. It eats beasts.* / *Corvin has the bird's roll and wants Idony to read it.* / *The three young ones you lost went south to Sarovel.* / *Orrun is well.* / *I am well.* (crossed out, then written again underneath). Tobin ties it to Lisska's foreleg in oilcloth with string from his boots. Via Orrun: "will you be here." "I don't know... Tell her I'll write." "She says that is what you always say." "Tell her it's always true." Echo: Lisska circles the cordon high for an hour, then south toward the coast.
- Ch 51 (days 265–281): snow on the moor, none on the basin (it melts in the field and steams all winter). By day 270 the envoys' camp is half gone: Lorne and Dane to Ironhythe to sign for the second cage at the Hall of Measures; Lady Aurel south to the Sun Stair; the Quill factor to the coast; Kessmoor guildmasters still visit by launch (Tobin: "They'll come back. When it does something." Pike: "It won't do anything. That's the point."). Day 266 Orrun out of the ditch: "I am hungry"; a sheep a day from Moss's stores. Day 268 the gatehouse stone splits: "It came back... I was not sure." He grows all winter: by midwinter he steps over the bank like a stile; shoulder level with Kharrak's at Vael (~16 ft). Lisska via Orrun says Kharrak is very loud. Idony walks without the stick mostly, carries it anyway; the glasses still lean to a point far down under the hole, "something it didn't take, or that isn't the kind of thing it takes"; Maren: "He won't leave. Till he's seen it." **Orrin's ruins**: the north end of the old town outside the outer ditch where the glass thins and stops: roofless stone houses forty years, doors open, snow in the rooms, a well with a cracked curb, a square chapel with a stubby bell-cote and no bell, no roof or door, a hearthstone; the cordon men call it unlucky (voices in the houses at night). Midwinter night 281 Maren, Tobin and Idony there with cordon wood, bread and a skin from a Kessmoor wright; Orrun across the street's foot like a gate. Tobin tries the hearth-word for fun (dead: the surge went out with the Furnace); flint on the third spark. Tobin: at Greyhold they'd be saying it at noon facing the wall; he was never let say it (unchosen); "Grieve'd say it if the wall fell down on him." Maren says the oath her own way (her father's oath; she heard him say it tying his boots): *I will hold the river. / I will keep the road. / I will stand between the weak and what would harm them.* / *I will keep the watch.* (no *below*, no *I will not ask what I keep*). Tobin, after her, the same (his first time ever). Idony: "I read who wrote it... The last line's a muzzle... *Keep the watch below* fed the chains"; then says it her way (asks for the third line): "That's not their oath. Theirs has a floor under it, and a gag in it. That's yours... That's a different working." "It's not a working. It's just words." "Everything's just words. Till enough people say them." Maren feels she knows what she's keeping. Dawn 281 (flight 4) Lisska over the field high, not through the shimmer ("It felt wrong. Like a mouth"), lands on a broken wall by the chapel door; Calder was two weeks at the letter and threw away eleven. **Calder's first letter**, charcoal on grey Clan tally-paper, torn in two places, lines going uphill, an S like a Z, two backwards Ds crossed out and rewritten: (1) *Maren. This is me riting. Ada is not helping, she is siting there.* (2) *My father payd the dues every year and kept his hed down and dyed in his bed. I thout that was the best a man cud do.* (3) *Yours dyed when his hound dyed. Ada told me. One life between two.* (4) *Senna dyed of the fever and I lived. There was no bond. It felt like there was.* (5) *I have one life between four now. It is not like a quarter each. It is like being a house with the doors open.* (6) *I think you know what that is like. I think you are the only one who does.* (7) *We never had a nite to say any of this. So I am saying it on paper.* (8) *Lisska says Orrun is too big. Kharrak says Lisska is a lier.* (9) *Ada says send you this: till the six are one agen?* Signed *CALDER*, the D right way round. Echo: read twice so as not to laugh, then once more for the rest. Her father (Edric Ashdown) died within the week after poachers killed his hound-beast, when she was fourteen; she has never talked of it to anyone. She heard Ada sing the whole song to Wren in the Fellwood. Idony copies line 9 into the silk margin exactly, with the question mark: "It nags." The letter goes in her coat behind the black book where the eleven pages were.

## Calder (43, 46, 49)
- Ch 43 (days 178–189): noon 178 they overtake the flood's front (brown tongue, white along its front; trees, roofs, a cart, grey ice slabs, a swimming cow) and keep a day ahead. Upper Wend (Isvaren) towns: **Wendhead** (stone bridge, mill, square round a well; the floury miller goes up the hill), **Cray's Ford**, **Lowfold** (an old woman reads it aloud: "Is that one day or one stroke?... You'd want to write it plainer"). In each Kharrak lands and Calder shouts from his back; on the biggest door he writes in charcoal (stub from the frost-fire) WATER COMING (the second W wrong at first; right by Sallow Lock) and a stroke per day of lead (Ada taught him the letters on a slate at the setters' ledge in summer). The yearling follows, falls behind, won't eat (a sheep at Lowfold); Lisska hums to her nightly: "She's going back in... In there's nothing." Day 179 out of Isvaren into the long middle Wend: Hearthmere's grey dead right bank, Saltreach's green left; **Brookend** and **Sallow Lock** (Hearthmere bank) and the Saltreach Staithes; on dead ground no word works but a warning is only words in a man's mouth. Dusk day 180 the Marches; the Eelweir (Harrow's End's landing): Fisk on the jetty with a line: "It's been here before... Everything's like this, the first time... Somebody's got to. To tell it after." Calder gets the landing, the lane and the late-barley folk up to the Fenn top field; carries Mother Gannet ("You'll want to see to that roof"; the Fenn house empty, door off). Fisk on his ridge with a blanket and a teapot. Day 181 Greyhold landing: 100+ Saltreach refugees camped under the river wall waiting to go back under the expulsion; Liss (small, dark dress with skirts pinned up, grey hair under a scarf, rope coil) has ferried them a load at a time all summer (the companies stripped the far bank too). "There's frost in your eyebrows. In autumn." The ferry: big flat square-ended boat with a rail, a sweep, an iron ring on a post running on the guide-rope to the Staithe post; forty a load; 104 across in three crossings day 181; forty left. Day 182 mid-morning the flood arrives as a fast rising, not a wall (five days and a hundred leagues spread it). Liss takes the last forty at first light, then goes back for Staithe-bank folk the water's reaching (near forty); the rope parts at the Staithe post, the ring comes off, the ferry turns downstream toward the old weir and the narrows under Greyhold's wall. **The failed casting**: Fisk's boatman's word (two old words; taught him at nine in a skiff in the reeds, "It'll bring a boat to a jetty, if the jetty wants it") said from Greyhold's jetty with Lisska's wind taken through the bond: nothing, twice (Ada: "You need both, live ground and still water, or it's only talk"). Lisska goes round downstream and low, spends all her wind in one gust (sound like a door slamming on the river; a flat grey patch on the water); the ferry is shoved across the current onto the stone end of the jetty, grinding, back stove in; everyone off, Liss last. Her emptying comes down the bond ("like the bottom going out of a bucket"); he goes down on the jetty stones; wakes later on the bank under the ferry-house wall with Lisska across his shins, wings spilled: "I'm empty... I'm all gone." "You did it." "I did. You didn't." Kharrak over both: "She can't fly. Not today. Not tomorrow... It was only a word." Echo: Liss wringing out her skirts on the jetty steps: "Have you eaten?"; Maren writes about monthly, four lines, five if something's happened: from the Wyldmarch (summer, by a trader), the Plains, Vael; "That he's grown. That she's well. That there's a man with her who reads... She never said she was well... Now she writes it every month"; the Vael letter came the day before Calder: *There's a lake. There's a bird that talks. I'm well. I'll write.* "Don't tell me. Not today." Fisk's eel-word over his oars every night forty years, never an eel: "He said it was polite." Day 183: the Eelweir lost (jetty, traps, boats, three of four houses to their wall-stumps); Fisk on his ridge in water to the eaves: "Took your time"; Kharrak lifts him by the coat-collar; the forty-year teapot lost; "Somebody's got to tell it after... I'll tell it." Days 183–186 news from refugees and a knight's boy (~14, badly healed facial cut, lame pony) from the Downs: the companies crossed by the fords above Ashford in early summer, hold Ashford, strip the Candle Downs northward village by village; Sir Rowan, back from the Ring, stripped of her lances by the council and sent to hold the Downs with a few hundred half-starved knights; steel and pikes only. The flood has drowned the fords above Ashford: the companies in Hearthmere are cut off from Saltreach for now. Day 186 the yearling to the hatching caves above Coldtarn (Lisska rides between Kharrak's shoulders, "undignified"); Gunna and Wren (Old Grey at her shoulder; Wren silent, watching); the yearling lies by the warm spring at the back with eyes half shut, gone back in (Gunna: "Some come out again. Some don't"). At dusk on the setters' ledge at the hold Calder holds up ENNIS for Aurveth: "He wrote the first one like that. With the line through it. In the dust, on the bridge, for children." That night Ada reads all eleven pages aloud by the setters' fire; the bird's question: "Well. Have you?" Calder doesn't answer; Aurveth, eyes shut: "I have." Days 187–189 at Greyhold landing: the Wend falling, flood-wrack hung higher than a man in every hedge; the ferry dragged up the bank (Liss will mend it); new hemp rope from the Staithe chandler, free; Calder rows it over with Fisk in the bow; Kharrak holds the far end on the Staithe post; made fast day 189. Fisk says the two old words over his oars mid-river: "Polite."
- Ch 46 (days 190–205): days 190–203 at Varrhold: Ada writes the account at Bel's table in the hold kitchen (door shut, dogs under the bench) on grey tally-house paper with a goose-quill she cut ("a bought pen never wrote what she meant"); a page or two a morning; "Start with the money." Mint-book lines quoted with page numbers (the Varr boy at the ford, three hundred; the chest up the Wend each new moon to Ashford; the four bound north for a library). Rook's book: every company and man, *Saltreach silver, new*. Liss says the red hand burned Upper Rush. Night 6 (day 195) Arn and Ulric at the two hearths, the chair under its grey cloth: if the crown stops driving the river folk back, the Clan sends grain and mutton down the Way to the Marches all winter, both banks ("Red and grey." "Both."). Twenty-two pages tied with a bootlace through a skewer-burned hole; Calder signs CALDER FENN himself, the D right way round for the first time. Day 205 dawn off the setters' ledge (Aurveth watches), six hours over the brown Marches, the Wend back in its banks, the bare Brackenwood and the dead grey country. Hallowmere: grey terraces down to the low grey Mere; the famous oil-less lamps black glass on iron posts in every better street; tallow smoke; the chapter-houses (grey halls end to end, steep slate roofs, carved doors) along the top terrace; **the Crown's Square** before the crown's chapter-house, steps, a dead lamp on a post at each corner. The noon bell rings twelve and nobody faces anything. Kharrak lands on the last stroke; four halberd guards in crown blue run inside; the thin hungry crowd doesn't (an old licensed worker begging with his licence pinned to his coat); Lisska on the west-corner lamp. A quarter past noon the council on the steps: **Lord Ambrose Thale**, President of the Council (named Thale, not Hesketh: Book 1 ch 32 has a Hesketh) (thin, old, careful, heavy gold chain of office), councillors in their orders' colours, clerks, crossbow guards. "Will you get down, so that we can speak to you as a man and not as a weather-vane?" Calder's speech with the three books held up in turn; "They're not the war. They're what the war did"; the Clan's offer. A councillor in blue and silver: grateful for the grain; the Halloran house a Tide Council matter; the crown is preparing a hunt for when the beast comes back, has sent to Kessmoor for chain; pardon, lance, place in the hunt for three bonded dragons. "No... I've seen what's left after... They had a hundred of their best on a hill... Half the city's drowned. I stood on the Library steps and watched them burn the bird." **Rowan** comes down from behind the guards (mended grey-white surcoat, thin, small, neat, iron-grey cropped hair, old scabbard), in Hallowmere three days waiting to beg grain for her Downs knights; stands beside the dragon; "I put a chain on it... It took the chain. It took the stakes. It took the stones... I came back from the Ring without my lances... I've seen what chains do now. Nothing"; the red hand, green boar and black ship she fights village by village are paid in that coin; holds up a six-rim coin taken off their dead: "The man who pays my enemy's wages is the man he's naming. Read the book." She never looks at Calder. **Davin** (seventeen, clerk's ink on his cuffs, cap too big; "Davin, from the Kettle, at Millbrook") reads all twenty-two pages aloud from three steps up, an hour and more; the crowd swells up the terraces (on lamp plinths, on shoulders); ends with the signature. Dusk, a brazier lit under Lisska's lamp; Rowan apart with two knights. ## Idony (44, 47, 50)
- Ch 44 (days 205–220): day 206 Pike takes Idony into the north-quarter engine-house (long brick, four chimneys, no windows, iron doors, size of the Hall of Rings). First hall: four coal-fired steam engines (pumps for the chilled water; men in leather aprons). Second hall (cold, dim, green alchemical lamps): **the core**, an iron drum three men high and as long as a ferry lying on iron trestles, ringed end to end with hand-wide iron bands of marks; arm-thick iron rods from its far end through the wall to the pylons' cables; one core per quarter. The marks: Kessmoor's own cramped hand of the hooked script (a dialect Idony has never seen but reads): *turn back* / *within*, alternating, band after band. It holds not the beast but the Deep in it: any draw inside the field comes back on itself; "It'll starve." "It'll stay where it is. Which is what a cage is for." Feed: an iron hopper the size of a cart on the core's near end, a chute to a store-room: racks of ironback plate (door-sized, hand-thick, ridged on one face, stacked like slates; the Mines' Guild breeds ironback herds in the Fells for it; mostly cut from the dead) and, at the back, straw-filled crates stencilled with Saltreach house marks and Kessmoor guilds: **heartstones**, crystallised hearts, dark red to brown, glassy, lobed, cut stumps, egg- to head-sized. Beasts: ironback, sabrecat, stonehorn, wyvern (Saltreach sells them), lynx, a few from the Wyldmarch ("The clans won't sell. Somebody does"). Burned like coal by the marks, they go grey and crumble. "It doesn't touch the Deep... It brings its own. The Titan can't take it. Dead ground can't starve it." Closed, each core burns ~150 big hearts a day: ~600 a day; stores for 40 days (Idony's sum: 24,000) and a cart a day from Ironhythe. Idony asks: "What does it cost?" Pike: ironback plate eleven silver the hundredweight at the Guild's yard, hearts forty to sixty, "It's on the Office's account... Moss does [the bills]"; "What else would it cost?" He sees himself in her (eleven years and never looked behind the panel). The stir: day 208 the basin's glass warm at the third canal's inner edge; day 215 too hot to hold a palm past ten; glass moths in clouds at dusk ringing like a tray of glasses carried downstairs, all night (he sleeps in a hut Moss gave him).
