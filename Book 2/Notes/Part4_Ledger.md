# Book 2, Part Four: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as `Part1_Ledger.md`, `Part2_Ledger.md` and `Part3_Ledger.md`. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (42, 45, 48)
- Ch 42 (days 178–205): dead ground from Vael to the Fells' western saddle (Isvaren's drain); Maren's arm comes back the first evening (hot-water pain), closes her hand day 180, holds a cup day 182. Idony walks bent with Nessa's sawn-off boathook for a stick, stops hourly. Snow on the saddle from day 183 (over Orrun's knees; he breaks a channel); over the saddle day 186 in a wind; halfway down the glass lifts a hair: Kessmoor is live, thin, "like a hill farm." Orrun: "It is that way. Far. It is not lying down yet." Idony marks the draw nightly with four glasses in a ring and Kessmoor graphite (\"the one good thing Kessmoor ever made\"): wandering W and NW over the moors at the Coldrun's head, circling; "It wanders longer each time." Day 188 **Coldrun Locks**: the Coldrun canalised west between brick banks in lock-steps every half-mile, iron lift-bridges, chimneys; green alchemical lamps (a salt in water with a metal in it, lit with a spark; not the Deep; Isvaren laughs at it). Sign: SPOKEN WORKINGS FORBIDDEN BY ORDER OF THE ASSEMBLY. / BEASTS OF POWER TO BE PAPERED AT THE GUILD-HOUSE. Guild-clerk (ink cuffs, brass Bleed-gauge on a chain): Idony written *Measurer. Unringed. Isvaren* (pale band where the ring was); "If you've a gauge for the Bleed, it's to be declared"; his glasses declared "Isvaren instruments. Glass. For weather." No guild will stand for the lion (Carters for horses, Drovers for oxen, the Mines for pit-ponies and ironbacks); "nobody's beasts don't go down the canals." Day 189 **Inspector Harl Moss**, the Assembly's Office of the Cordon (forty, neat grey coat and waistcoat, clean-shaven, dry; brass gauge since he was nineteen, his father's before), by chimneyed iron steam-launch from the west, with six cordon men in oilcloth with marked crossbows. The lion stories came down the canals with the salt-barges. Three hundred gauges on the cordon read hourly for forty years; all began to climb on day 184. "We've been waiting forty years for it to wake. We'd as soon not wait alone." Moss papers Orrun himself under the Office's red seal: *A beast of the cordon. Kept on the Office's account. Not to be hindered.* Maren: "Write what you like. He won't read it." Days 189–196 nine days by an iron-sheathed horse-barge (pig-iron under tarpaulins, a big grey horse and a boy) down the locks in sleet; the lion walks the far towpath and waits for the barge; Moss's launch ahead. The country black and live-thin: chimneys, spoil-heaps, brick rows, engine-houses with turning wheels and a thud. Day 192 a four-chimneyed engine-house: Idony's glass leans toward it "like a drinking" but there's no Deep in it and the ground round isn't dying (heartstones: ch 44). Day 196 Ironhythe (brick, iron, smoke up a hill, green lamps at noon, the Hall of Measures' white-faced clock "never been wrong"); through without stopping onto **the Scald Cut** and **the Iron Road** south-east to the border. Echo, last lock out of Ironhythe: a Hearthmere envoy (crown-blue cloak, Hallowmere seal on a chain, two guards in Hearthmere grey, a ledger clerk) buying chain for "the crown's work": twelve hundredweight each from the Gannet forge and Holloway's, links bigger than ever made, "a year's work... As if we've got a year"; and a farmer in the Marches with dragons, "Three of them, they say", who burned a Saltreach mint, flies in broad day, "and the Marches folk feed him... Nobody's heard of the crown." Maren doesn't turn round. Steam wagons on iron rails beside the cut; Orrun: "They move and they are not alive... I do not like them." The ground thins to dead over three days toward a yellow-grey sky; day 201 evening all four glasses lean SE: the Titan turned S out of the moors four nights before and has lain down on the Scald. Day 204 the cordon: a grassed earth bank the height of a house with an outer ditch, huts with chimneys every few hundred paces, oilcloth men with crossbows, forty years old. Inside, **the cage**: three concentric canals of chilled running water (grey cold-haze, frost on the brick edges, fast, one direction all round) and two rings of iron pylons (each three men high, a hundred paces apart) with sagging iron cables between; four engine-houses at the quarters; "forty-one leagues of cable. And eleven thousand marks" (hooked runes struck up each pylon; "In Kessmoor we call them marks. Runes are spoken of. We don't speak of things"). **Master Engineer Agathe Pike** of the Cordon (sixty-plus, tall, heavy-shouldered, grey hair cropped short, old leather coat black at the cuffs, smoked spectacles pushed up on her forehead, soot ground into her knuckles; measures the lion like a smith a horse; "Two feet... In how long?" "A morning."). Water chilled through coils in the engine-houses, never stopped since the first cut was flooded; "Cold running water breaks it harder." "The cables hold the field. The field holds whatever's in it." Her father, a junior wright at Orrin, was in the shaft-house that night and came out sane because he was watching a gauge; began the cage; she finished it four years ago. "For whatever comes up." From the north-quarter watch-tower (iron stair in an iron frame) through a brass glass: the Scald basin, wider than Glasswater, fused black, green and brown glass, smooth or rippled like a pond frozen in wind, Orrin's streets and house-footings melted into it; white glass moths ("They ring, when they hit the glass... You'll hear them at night"). The Titan lying in the middle, head on paws, wings folded like a roof; came down three days before; the gauges climb a mark an hour; the basin's glass warm for the first time. Orrun lies on the outer bank's old grass at the north quarter facing it. Pike: "They tell me you can read when it'll go down." Idony: "A month. Perhaps less. It's quicker every time." "Good. I'd like to know when to close it... The cage. On whatever comes up."

## Calder (43, 46, 49)
- Ch 43 (days 178–189): noon 178 they overtake the flood's front (brown tongue, white along its front; trees, roofs, a cart, grey ice slabs, a swimming cow) and keep a day ahead. Upper Wend (Isvaren) towns: **Wendhead** (stone bridge, mill, square round a well; the floury miller goes up the hill), **Cray's Ford**, **Lowfold** (an old woman reads it aloud: "Is that one day or one stroke?... You'd want to write it plainer"). In each Kharrak lands and Calder shouts from his back; on the biggest door he writes in charcoal (stub from the frost-fire) WATER COMING (the second W wrong at first; right by Sallow Lock) and a stroke per day of lead (Ada taught him the letters on a slate at the setters' ledge in summer). The yearling follows, falls behind, won't eat (a sheep at Lowfold); Lisska hums to her nightly: "She's going back in... In there's nothing." Day 179 out of Isvaren into the long middle Wend: Hearthmere's grey dead right bank, Saltreach's green left; **Brookend** and **Sallow Lock** (Hearthmere bank) and the Saltreach Staithes; on dead ground no word works but a warning is only words in a man's mouth. Dusk day 180 the Marches; the Eelweir (Harrow's End's landing): Fisk on the jetty with a line: "It's been here before... Everything's like this, the first time... Somebody's got to. To tell it after." Calder gets the landing, the lane and the late-barley folk up to the Fenn top field; carries Mother Gannet ("You'll want to see to that roof"; the Fenn house empty, door off). Fisk on his ridge with a blanket and a teapot. Day 181 Greyhold landing: 100+ Saltreach refugees camped under the river wall waiting to go back under the expulsion; Liss (small, dark dress with skirts pinned up, grey hair under a scarf, rope coil) has ferried them a load at a time all summer (the companies stripped the far bank too). "There's frost in your eyebrows. In autumn." The ferry: big flat square-ended boat with a rail, a sweep, an iron ring on a post running on the guide-rope to the Staithe post; forty a load; 104 across in three crossings day 181; forty left. Day 182 mid-morning the flood arrives as a fast rising, not a wall (five days and a hundred leagues spread it). Liss takes the last forty at first light, then goes back for Staithe-bank folk the water's reaching (near forty); the rope parts at the Staithe post, the ring comes off, the ferry turns downstream toward the old weir and the narrows under Greyhold's wall. **The failed casting**: Fisk's boatman's word (two old words; taught him at nine in a skiff in the reeds, "It'll bring a boat to a jetty, if the jetty wants it") said from Greyhold's jetty with Lisska's wind taken through the bond: nothing, twice (Ada: "You need both, live ground and still water, or it's only talk"). Lisska goes round downstream and low, spends all her wind in one gust (sound like a door slamming on the river; a flat grey patch on the water); the ferry is shoved across the current onto the stone end of the jetty, grinding, back stove in; everyone off, Liss last. Her emptying comes down the bond ("like the bottom going out of a bucket"); he goes down on the jetty stones; wakes later on the bank under the ferry-house wall with Lisska across his shins, wings spilled: "I'm empty... I'm all gone." "You did it." "I did. You didn't." Kharrak over both: "She can't fly. Not today. Not tomorrow... It was only a word." Echo: Liss wringing out her skirts on the jetty steps: "Have you eaten?"; Maren writes about monthly, four lines, five if something's happened: from the Wyldmarch (summer, by a trader), the Plains, Vael; "That he's grown. That she's well. That there's a man with her who reads... She never said she was well... Now she writes it every month"; the Vael letter came the day before Calder: *There's a lake. There's a bird that talks. I'm well. I'll write.* "Don't tell me. Not today." Fisk's eel-word over his oars every night forty years, never an eel: "He said it was polite." Day 183: the Eelweir lost (jetty, traps, boats, three of four houses to their wall-stumps); Fisk on his ridge in water to the eaves: "Took your time"; Kharrak lifts him by the coat-collar; the forty-year teapot lost; "Somebody's got to tell it after... I'll tell it." Days 183–186 news from refugees and a knight's boy (~14, badly healed facial cut, lame pony) from the Downs: the companies crossed by the fords above Ashford in early summer, hold Ashford, strip the Candle Downs northward village by village; Sir Rowan, back from the Ring, stripped of her lances by the council and sent to hold the Downs with a few hundred half-starved knights; steel and pikes only. The flood has drowned the fords above Ashford: the companies in Hearthmere are cut off from Saltreach for now. Day 186 the yearling to the hatching caves above Coldtarn (Lisska rides between Kharrak's shoulders, "undignified"); Gunna and Wren (Old Grey at her shoulder; Wren silent, watching); the yearling lies by the warm spring at the back with eyes half shut, gone back in (Gunna: "Some come out again. Some don't"). At dusk on the setters' ledge at the hold Calder holds up ENNIS for Aurveth: "He wrote the first one like that. With the line through it. In the dust, on the bridge, for children." That night Ada reads all eleven pages aloud by the setters' fire; the bird's question: "Well. Have you?" Calder doesn't answer; Aurveth, eyes shut: "I have." Days 187–189 at Greyhold landing: the Wend falling, flood-wrack hung higher than a man in every hedge; the ferry dragged up the bank (Liss will mend it); new hemp rope from the Staithe chandler, free; Calder rows it over with Fisk in the bow; Kharrak holds the far end on the Staithe post; made fast day 189. Fisk says the two old words over his oars mid-river: "Polite."

## Idony (44, 47, 50)
- Ch 44 (days 205–220): day 206 Pike takes Idony into the north-quarter engine-house (long brick, four chimneys, no windows, iron doors, size of the Hall of Rings). First hall: four coal-fired steam engines (pumps for the chilled water; men in leather aprons). Second hall (cold, dim, green alchemical lamps): **the core**, an iron drum three men high and as long as a ferry lying on iron trestles, ringed end to end with hand-wide iron bands of marks; arm-thick iron rods from its far end through the wall to the pylons' cables; one core per quarter. The marks: Kessmoor's own cramped hand of the hooked script (a dialect Idony has never seen but reads): *turn back* / *within*, alternating, band after band. It holds not the beast but the Deep in it: any draw inside the field comes back on itself; "It'll starve." "It'll stay where it is. Which is what a cage is for." Feed: an iron hopper the size of a cart on the core's near end, a chute to a store-room: racks of ironback plate (door-sized, hand-thick, ridged on one face, stacked like slates; the Mines' Guild breeds ironback herds in the Fells for it; mostly cut from the dead) and, at the back, straw-filled crates stencilled with Saltreach house marks and Kessmoor guilds: **heartstones**, crystallised hearts, dark red to brown, glassy, lobed, cut stumps, egg- to head-sized. Beasts: ironback, sabrecat, stonehorn, wyvern (Saltreach sells them), lynx, a few from the Wyldmarch ("The clans won't sell. Somebody does"). Burned like coal by the marks, they go grey and crumble. "It doesn't touch the Deep... It brings its own. The Titan can't take it. Dead ground can't starve it." Closed, each core burns ~150 big hearts a day: ~600 a day; stores for 40 days (Idony's sum: 24,000) and a cart a day from Ironhythe. Idony asks: "What does it cost?" Pike: ironback plate eleven silver the hundredweight at the Guild's yard, hearts forty to sixty, "It's on the Office's account... Moss does [the bills]"; "What else would it cost?" He sees himself in her (eleven years and never looked behind the panel). The stir: day 208 the basin's glass warm at the third canal's inner edge; day 215 too hot to hold a palm past ten; glass moths in clouds at dusk ringing like a tray of glasses carried downstairs, all night (he sleeps in a hut Moss gave him). Day 212 the Bleed in the ordinary outer ditch: bathwater-warm, an oil-shimmer, smell of a forge; grass dying in a pace-wide band; an old cordon man: "Forty years we've kept that ditch. Never been warm." Day 218 a league out on the Iron Road the glass on an iron Kessmoor milestone (number cast in it) is dead: everything drawn inward. Four glasses on the tower rail lean harder daily; the slope steeper than Vael's. Day 219 in the silk: *A month. Perhaps less. Every time, less.* (Ring three months; Vael seven weeks; here a month.) To Pike: the relic near day 233; then surge and waking, "fewer" than Vael's five days; he can give the week, not yet the day; "That's more than my father ever had... I'll want the day." Echo, day 214 at the main (west) gate where the Iron Road comes through the bank (brick gatehouse, desk under an awning): Moss's young clerk with the Office's seal on his cap and the cage's account-book (every beast inside the bank, "the Assembly's order. In case"; columns *Kind. Weight. Kept at. For.*; entries e.g. *Ironback. Forty hundredweight. North-quarter pens. Plate.* / *Sabrecat, wild. Six hundredweight. South-quarter store. Heart.*). Maren: "He's on your paper... That's enough." "This is a different book." "No... He's not going in your book." She hasn't read past the headings; Idony has and doesn't tell her; "She said no." The clerk shuts the book. Idony stands straight an hour at a time by day 215; still the stick.
