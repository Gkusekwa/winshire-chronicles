## Maren (42, 45, 48)
- Ch 42 (days 178–205): dead ground from Vael to the Fells' western saddle (Isvaren's drain); Maren's arm comes back the first evening (hot-water pain), closes her hand day 180, holds a cup day 182. Idony walks bent with Nessa's sawn-off boathook for a stick, stops hourly. Snow on the saddle from day 183 (over Orrun's knees; he breaks a channel); over the saddle day 186 in a wind; halfway down the glass lifts a hair: Kessmoor is live, thin, "like a hill farm." Orrun: "It is that way. Far. It is not lying down yet." Idony marks the draw nightly with four glasses in a ring and Kessmoor graphite (\"the one good thing Kessmoor ever made\"): wandering W and NW over the moors at the Coldrun's head, circling; "It wanders longer each time." Day 188 **Coldrun Locks**: the Coldrun canalised west between brick banks in lock-steps every half-mile, iron lift-bridges, chimneys; green alchemical lamps (a salt in water with a metal in it, lit with a spark; not the Deep; Isvaren laughs at it). Sign: SPOKEN WORKINGS FORBIDDEN BY ORDER OF THE ASSEMBLY. / BEASTS OF POWER TO BE PAPERED AT THE GUILD-HOUSE. Guild-clerk (ink cuffs, brass Bleed-gauge on a chain): Idony written *Measurer. Unringed. Isvaren* (pale band where the ring was); "If you've a gauge for the Bleed, it's to be declared"; his glasses declared "Isvaren instruments. Glass. For weather." No guild will stand for the lion (Carters for horses, Drovers for oxen, the Mines for pit-ponies and ironbacks); "nobody's beasts don't go down the canals." Day 189 **Inspector Harl Moss**, the Assembly's Office of the Cordon (forty, neat grey coat and waistcoat, clean-shaven, dry; brass gauge since he was nineteen, his father's before), by chimneyed iron steam-launch from the west, with six cordon men in oilcloth with marked crossbows. The lion stories came down the canals with the salt-barges. Three hundred gauges on the cordon read hourly for forty years; all began to climb on day 184. "We've been waiting forty years for it to wake. We'd as soon not wait alone." Moss papers Orrun himself under the Office's red seal: *A beast of the cordon. Kept on the Office's account. Not to be hindered.* Maren: "Write what you like. He won't read it." Days 189–196 nine days by an iron-sheathed horse-barge (pig-iron under tarpaulins, a big grey horse and a boy) down the locks in sleet; the lion walks the far towpath and waits for the barge; Moss's launch ahead. The country black and live-thin: chimneys, spoil-heaps, brick rows, engine-houses with turning wheels and a thud. Day 192 a four-chimneyed engine-house: Idony's glass leans toward it "like a drinking" but there's no Deep in it and the ground round isn't dying (heartstones: ch 44). Day 196 Ironhythe (brick, iron, smoke up a hill, green lamps at noon, the Hall of Measures' white-faced clock "never been wrong"); through without stopping onto **the Scald Cut** and **the Iron Road** south-east to the border. Echo, last lock out of Ironhythe: a Hearthmere envoy (crown-blue cloak, Hallowmere seal on a chain, two guards in Hearthmere grey, a ledger clerk) buying chain for "the crown's work": twelve hundredweight each from the Gannet forge and Holloway's, links bigger than ever made, "a year's work... As if we've got a year"; and a farmer in the Marches with dragons, "Three of them, they say", who burned a Saltreach mint, flies in broad day, "and the Marches folk feed him... Nobody's heard of the crown." Maren doesn't turn round. Steam wagons on iron rails beside the cut; Orrun: "They move and they are not alive... I do not like them." The ground thins to dead over three days toward a yellow-grey sky; day 201 evening all four glasses lean SE: the Titan turned S out of the moors four nights before and has lain down on the Scald. Day 204 the cordon: a grassed earth bank the height of a house with an outer ditch, huts with chimneys every few hundred paces, oilcloth men with crossbows, forty years old. Inside, **the cage**: three concentric canals of chilled running water (grey cold-haze, frost on the brick edges, fast, one direction all round) and two rings of iron pylons (each three men high, a hundred paces apart) with sagging iron cables between; four engine-houses at the quarters; "forty-one leagues of cable. And eleven thousand marks" (hooked runes struck up each pylon; "In Kessmoor we call them marks. Runes are spoken of. We don't speak of things"). **Master Engineer Agathe Pike** of the Cordon (sixty-plus, tall, heavy-shouldered, grey hair cropped short, old leather coat black at the cuffs, smoked spectacles pushed up on her forehead, soot ground into her knuckles; measures the lion like a smith a horse; "Two feet... In how long?" "A morning."). Water chilled through coils in the engine-houses, never stopped since the first cut was flooded; "Cold running water breaks it harder." "The cables hold the field. The field holds whatever's in it." Her father, a junior wright at Orrin, was in the shaft-house that night and came out sane because he was watching a gauge; began the cage; she finished it four years ago. "For whatever comes up." From the north-quarter watch-tower (iron stair in an iron frame) through a brass glass: the Scald basin, wider than Glasswater, fused black, green and brown glass, smooth or rippled like a pond frozen in wind, Orrin's streets and house-footings melted into it; white glass moths ("They ring, when they hit the glass... You'll hear them at night"). The Titan lying in the middle, head on paws, wings folded like a roof; came down three days before; the gauges climb a mark an hour; the basin's glass warm for the first time. Orrun lies on the outer bank's old grass at the north quarter facing it. Pike: "They tell me you can read when it'll go down." Idony: "A month. Perhaps less. It's quicker every time." "Good. I'd like to know when to close it... The cage. On whatever comes up."
