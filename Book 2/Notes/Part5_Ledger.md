Forty or fifty **hands** (unbonded, the knack, brown robes) do all the court's workings unseen: a hand under each room warming its floor; lighting the great hall; humming into a pipe under each garden fountain (the court says the fountains sing because the Grace loves the Bond-King); nobles' morning headaches; the gleam on the procession beasts; and at banquets a hand behind a screen drawing the fire a chosen lord pretends to raise. Day 301 Lady Aurel once, in a white gilded room over the bay; doesn't mention her secretary; he's on the household's books as *the Kessmoor measurer, a hand.* Day 302 the **royal stables** (a marble palace of their own on the cliff east of the palace, columns, gilded tile roof, house-sized stalls with gold crests, a brown hand grooming in each): pony-sized gold hounds, spotted great cats on gold chains, a white bear in a jewelled collar, coal-eyed long-legged not-horses, three olifants with gilded tusk-tips in a barn-sized stall, and at the end alone in white marble under a gilded sun the **unicorn**, ash-silver without the chalk, staring at the wall. **The collars**: gold outside (suns, crowns, the Bond-King's crest), plain iron inside with fine Sarovel court-hand script (long-tailed hooks); since the beast lay on the hill six cracked in the gold, two in the iron (the unicorn's one). The working is a **greater shaping** cut into the beast's self (the grammar's dry paragraph: *forbidden everywhere but Sarovel's court*), fed by the Grace: it takes the self out and keeps it out, "Like a breath held", nine generations. **The unmaking**: every shaping's unmaking is on the first page of the grammar's shaping chapter: the same rune read backward with the closing word turned; nobody is ever taught to say it; he reads the line at the clasp backward and turns the word in his head; says nothing, writes nothing. To Sebold: "They weren't made for this much... I'm a reader. I don't mend." Day 303 in a white gallery of nine Bond-King portraits on unicorns, **Dane** with drawings and the gold-and-white chamberlain; Dane looks, looks away. Night 307 at the unicorn's stall (he goes nightly; the crack grows): **Corvin** (tall, thin, very still, long pale face, short dark hair, grey eyes, plain dark coat with no crest or chain, no guards; the grey-bearded captain at the stable door): "Reader Vael." "Corvin." "He shot the bird." "I didn't ask him to... I'm told it died very well." He knows Idony left the binding mid-form and read the roll all night in spring from "Somebody in your college's own household. Who keeps a lantern owl... its servants keep copies." Kneels in the straw and opens the case: "I've had it eight months. I can't read a word of it"; the two Sixth rings sent his man back with the letter unopened; unfolds the appendix: "These. The words. For speaking to the iron. Teach me... You want to speak to a Titan. So do I." "What for?" "To ask it something... That's mine." Offers the roll to read and keep, the Bond-King's library, a room, people to ask him questions, a college of his own: "You were never let ask anything in Vael... Here you could ask everything." Idony wants it so much his chest hurts; thinks of the bird, the terrace, the crate of hearts: "No." Corvin: "You'll say yes. Not tonight... you won't be able to bear not knowing... You're a reader. That's what readers are"; takes the case. The stir from his bench at the north window cut through the cliff (Sebold gave it without asking): four glasses lean up toward the ridge daily harder; day 308 a lamp-globe bursts like a bell in the third room, day 309 three more; lamps taken down, the cliff-rooms dim; the garden fountains sing by themselves at night like the glass moths; ten collars cracked, the unicorn's right across the iron. Night 308 **the banquet**: white gilded hall, a hundred white lamps, the court in silk with beasts under the tables, the Bond-King at the high table with Corvin beside him; a handsome chosen lord in green with a bonded cat lifts his hand; the hand behind the screen draws, the Grace floods through him: a roaring white sheet of fire to the gilded ceiling that won't stop; the court applauds ten breaths; behind the screen a young brown-robed hand on his knees, hair going black to grey to white like Pask's; it stops; he falls forward grey, shaking, hands smoking; Sebold has him carried below; the lord in green bows to the applause. Day 309 in the silk: *Two weeks from the day it lay down. The three-hundred-and-fifteenth. Near noon. Every time, less.* (Ring three months, Vael seven weeks, the Scald a month, here two weeks.) Hands aren't let down the stair to the shrine; he'll find a way. Echo, night 311 (his twelfth night): out by the ramp in his brown robe with his stick, through the white city's lamps casting midnight shadows, to the low quarter: lions on every door (mud, ochre, soot, one gold leaf); the horse-stable with Orrun along its whole front wall, head in the door; Lisska asleep on the thatch, tail over the eaves; Maren asleep on the straw against Orrun's head, arm over his nose, coat round her, boots on, mud to the knee from the Elderwood. Tobin: "Don't wake her... She's got two letters in her coat she won't read." Idony tells Tobin: the 315th near noon, the collars, the drawer, Dane, Corvin's offer. "What did you say?" "No." "Say it twice." He doesn't; back to his bench before dawn.
- Ch 61 (days 315–318 dawn): noon 315 his four glasses stand straight up (the peak); every palace window shatters; through the vault the floor of the room above blazes in the shape of two palms (a hand warming a lady's bedchamber) and she screams; Sebold: "Out. All of you. Out. By the ramp." Idony goes the other way: through glass-rain on the cliff path, past screaming fountains and the bleeding court with howling beasts at the chapel doors, to the head of the Sun Stair; sees the Titan lift the egg out of the shrine's hole and lay it in its back ("Four"); its wing-wind knocks him down as it goes NW over the hills. He runs down the zigzags (first time since Rope Street) to the middle landing (the next flight cracked across, hanging by its iron pins) and shouts down into the white light the old-tongue word the bird called the lion and the Scald's door bore: *door-keeper*, twice. Orrun brings Maren up the stair on his back (the cracked flights hold for him: old stone under the white); "It took it. He stood in front of it." "I know. I saw." "What did you shout?" "His name. The bird's name for him." They go east along the cliff path to the low quarter. **The surge** (315–317): every lamp in Solenne white and cracking (oil, rush, tallow too); bonded nobles' gifts blaze out of control (the banquet lord's spotted cat shedding gold sparks; a lady holding her burning-gold hound, hands blistering); hands summoned back by the court burn out kneeling with palms to floors (he stops counting at eleven); the cliff cracks upward from the shrine with white light in the crack; the bay boils. Corvin told the Bond-King at dinner (via Sebold from a high-table hand): "Your Majesty's shrine is over a sleeping thing... When it wakes it will be yours, if you're ready." Dawn 316 on the broken great upper terrace (half its balustrade in the sea): the Bond-King in white and gold, crowned, on the unicorn in its cracked collar; **forty court binders** in white (chosen lords' binder-hands the court never admits to) with smouldering honey-pots on gold chains, in a ring; a cart drawn by six bonded not-horses carrying a **gate-sized hinged gold collar**, iron inside cut in the College's square hand with the rune of holding and binding-runes: **Dane's drawings**. Dane took the first ship north that morning. **The royal bond is a channel**: all forty binders pour into the king's hand on the unicorn's head through the unicorn's collar ("every binding in Sarovel running through the prisoner's collar into the king's palm"). **The fourth waking**, morning 317: the cliff below the palace breaks away from the shrine upward into the bay ("a sound like the end of the world"), rock, stair, portico and gilded barge into the sea, spray higher than the dome raining on the terrace; **the Horned Sun** rises: first a spiralled shining white-gold horn of living bone higher than palace and hills; a long narrow deer-shaped white-gold head the size of a hill with rays of light from behind the horn's base; long fine legs, house-sized cloven hooves; stands in the bay with the sea at its knees. The binders' smoke rolls out over the bay; the forty chant the court's *you're chosen now* together; the king pours; it **takes** (in a surge everything answers): its head dips, its rays go grey and in, it sways not knowing where it is (like the yearling on the Library roof). Idony walks through the ring unseen (nobody looks at hands), puts his mouth to the crack in the unicorn's collar ("as Pell had put his mouth to the grey's neck... *into* it") and says **the unmaking**. The gold and iron part and fall in two halves like dropped plates; the unicorn's self comes back fast, all at once; one breath; it rears on the broken edge, throws the king onto the top of the royal white upper stair (he slides three steps; crown into the sea), turns and drives its horn through his chest into the stone on the third step, draws it out, stands over him. The binding breaks "like a rope cut": two binders kneel burning grey; three by the edge are gone into the white sea; the rest run. The unicorn goes down the raw new cliff like a goat, wades and swims to the Horned Sun; the smoke clears with the burned-out pots; the rays come back pale, white, gold; the small silver-grey unicorn stands on a ledge of fallen cliff at its knee "like a foal at its mother's leg"; the Horned Sun puts its head down. Corvin was beside the king at the start and left along the terrace toward the harbour stair, unhurried, as soon as the binding began to take. The count: the Bond-King; two burned; three fallen: six. *My knowledge has a body count now* (said to himself twice). Night 317 the glasses come down a little toward the drain; the Horned Sun stands in the moonlit bay with the unicorn at its knee. Echo, dawn 318 looking east: the unchosen loading dozens of boats on the low quarter's shingle; the low cliff above the old fishing-stair sliding with mud-brick houses on it; three grey lions on the stair, heads down, forefeet braced, holding it off the boats.
