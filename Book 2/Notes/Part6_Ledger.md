# Book 2, Part Six: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as the earlier part ledgers. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (67, 69, 72, 74, 77)
- Ch 67 (days 323–335): NE up the drained Sun Road (vineyards and olives grey, stage-chapels' gilded suns dull, nobody sings at dusk; the gold and ochre lions still on every stage gate; keepers still kneel; the servants' gates stand open for her with unchosen grooms waiting). Orrun on three legs five days, then four with the foreleg barely touching, sound by day 331 at the border into Hearthmere's dead SW downs (ash-coloured; a dusty three-faced crossroads shrine with an empty bowl); the King's Road. **Hallowmere** day 334: the oil-less lamps black glass and dusty a year; the wells (charmed sweet two hundred years by the licensed keeping-wives' spring word) sour, brown, iron-tasting, making children sick (\"We boil it. When there's wood\"); a **bread riot** at the **corn exchange** (long grey columned building, doors barred, crown-blue halberdiers, stones at windows): no grain since the new moon (the Clan's goes to the Marches; a ship from Corsa rumoured); licensed workers begging with crown licences pinned on (*Licensed to tend the lamps of the Fourth Terrace*; a keeping-wife's *for the keeping of milk*; a bone-setter's); she gives the lamp-tender two coppers. **Reede's paper** pasted on every door, post, wall and lamp-post (cheap grey, smudged black): *THE CROWN'S CRIME. / For three hundred years the crown's lamps burned stolen light. / Under Greyhold, in a cavern beneath the river, the first knights chained a great beast. A god of the old realm. They bound it in iron and sold its light. Every lamp in Hearthmere was fed from its chains. / The crown knew. The crown hid it. / The oath you said at noon was the chain. / Now the light is gone, and the beast is loose, and the crown that stole from it would chain it again. / Ask what you kept.* (a small printed ring of six); more than half true, twisted; the press saved from Cotter's Cross; read aloud on corners. **The chapel off the corn exchange** (grey stone, slate roof, door open): six shawled old women kneel round **the hearthstone** (every Hearthmere chapel has one: a flat round grey stone in the floor before the altar where the hearth-fire was once kept), hands flat on the floor: *Mother, keep the fire. Keep it small and keep it in.* Maren kneels with them (first time since the week her father died, with Liss at Greyhold's chapel). \"Who's the mother?\" \"The hearth-mother. The grey one. Under the hearth. She keeps the fire. She always has.\" (Plant: the Grey Mother.) **Davin** (clerk's ink, too-big cap): \"You're Liss's girl\"; clerk to Hallowmere's **street tribunes** (elected in the bread riots; run the bread-lines and the wells; the council tolerates them because it must); he reads Reede's paper aloud nightly on the corn exchange steps and then tells the crowd what's true and what isn't (\"*A god.* *Ask what you kept.* They want you to ask so you'll come to them for the answer. And their answer's a ring of six\"); where the true part came from: \"Somebody told them. Somebody who knew.\" **The council**, day 334, the crown's chapter-house long hall (grey stone, dark wood, faded order banners from the beams), **Lord Thale** presiding: Maren tells them the Titan has four sleeps; the fifth is the Gullhaven reef within the month; then it comes home to the river and the cavern; beyond the first chains a vault with the ring of six hollows, the held breath; when it has all five it goes for the ring and the six become one and do its will: \"to bring it all down\"; Corvin has the roll, the reader and the chain-words and is coming. Thanked. The **second cage** is being set now in the cavern where the chains were: Kessmoor engines, the crown's chain, the College's rune of holding on every link drawn by **Dane**, at Greyhold now with Kessmoor's engineers setting anchors. \"It won't hold.\" Thale wants **Orrun as bait** in the cage \"a little way inside. So that it comes all the way in\" (mirror of Book 1 ch 59). **Rowan** (new white Knight-Captain's surcoat) to command it, lances restored; she doesn't look up. \"No.\" \"The crown doesn't ask, Ashdown.\" \"Then it can try to chain him itself.\" In the Crown's Square Orrun: \"I have been in that cavern. Three hundred years... I will not be bait.\" Rowan alone on the steps: \"I told them it wouldn't hold... They gave me my lances back and told me to command it anyway... Because if somebody else does, they'll do it worse... Come to the yard. When you're there. Not for them... For the yard.\" She leaves for Greyhold in the morning. Echo, morning 335: a Tide Council herald (blue wave on his saddle-cloth) nails a printed notice over Reede's paper on the corn exchange door; Davin reads it to the bread-line: *By order of the Tide Council of the Saltreach League. To all the Leagues and kingdoms of Winshire. Notice. / The Council has sold, to the house of Strand, a contract for the taking and holding of the thing beneath the reef called the Drowned Bell. The house of Strand has assigned its execution to the company of Captain Idris Vey. / The thing beneath the reef is the property of the Council. Let no kingdom interfere.* Maren thinks of the spit and *Keep your coast*, and goes to the street tribunes about their wells.

## Calder (68, 70, 73, 75, 78)
- Ch 68 (days 329–334): d.329 Council's answer is a summons ("Master Calder Fenn will attend the Council at Corsa and explain himself"). Night before, at the Anchor-and-Net, Calder copies the reader's numbers as a big-letter table on the last clean sheet of Clan paper (Ring: relic midsummer, woke 7 days after / Vael: relic 172, woke 5 / Scald: relic 233, woke 5 / Sun Stair: relic 315, woke 2 / EVERY TIME SOONER / THE BELL: WITHIN THE MONTH, THEN SAME DAY OR NEXT); Ada checks the arithmetic "because they will". D.330 to Corsa with Kharrak and Lisska; Aurveth stays on the spit. D.331 Council: Marrow presiding; **Master Aubrey Strand** (the soft-voiced man in green); Quill woman in white; Halloran chair empty since spring; Vey at the captains' end. Calder reads the sheet aloud himself (Marrow: "In the spring you couldn't."), then the spit, 412 roofs, 40 baskets, Kessmoor's cage on land (Pike, Moss). Marrow: "We know now, Master Fenn. As you said." Strand: contract stands; Vey's company, Kessmoor engines on barges, Gearmouth imbued harpoons; won't empty Gullhaven: oyster season, richest beds in the League, "The Lady keeps the stones" (Corsa joke). No member moves to empty it. Marrow knows Ada's words via the Clan rider's talk on the sea-steps. Vey on the steps: took the execution because nobody else would bid and the price went up; "I'll be on the reef. You'll be on the spit. One of us will be wrong... I hope it's you"; glove off for a handshake. Night 331 Kharrak sent back to Gullhaven with a word (Kharrak→Tamsin→Gunna→Arn): *They said no. Come and show them.* D.332 noon the Clan over Corsa: every Gullhaven pair + twenty more from the Scarfell, forty-odd, wheeling low over harbour and Tidehall copper roof; town on its roofs; Council on its balcony. Arn (red), Ulric (grey), Gunna (Tamsin) on the sea-steps; Arn: Clan will lift the spit with or without leave, asks the Council's word it won't hinder, and will pay Vey's wyverns from the Clan purse to take children off the salt-pan sheds. Marrow: "The Council will not hinder." Vey: "I'll take the coin. For the children. Not the reef." Night 333 Lisska west to Greyhold (**flight 6**) with two pages: the Gullhaven promise page (WHEN THE COAST IS DONE THE CLAN WILL COME TO THE RIVER) and tonight's six lines (Council won't empty the spit / oyster season / Vey on the reef with harpoons / Clan over Corsa, Council won't hinder / Vey's wyverns for the sheds, Arn paying / reader's numbers right, read them myself / Keep the door). Lisska: "There's a lion." D.334 echo: Hearthmere grain cog (crown's blue sail, Hallowmere seal) at the grain quay, bought with the crown's last gold (crown's plate sold), for Stillhaven then up the Meryl; bread riots in Hallowmere. Calder returns to the spit with Arn and the flights.
