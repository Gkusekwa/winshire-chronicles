# Book 2, Part Six: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as the earlier part ledgers. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (67, 69, 72, 74, 77)
- Ch 67 (days 323–335): NE up the drained Sun Road (vineyards and olives grey, stage-chapels' gilded suns dull, nobody sings at dusk; the gold and ochre lions still on every stage gate; keepers still kneel; the servants' gates stand open for her with unchosen grooms waiting). Orrun on three legs five days, then four with the foreleg barely touching, sound by day 331 at the border into Hearthmere's dead SW downs (ash-coloured; a dusty three-faced crossroads shrine with an empty bowl); the King's Road. **Hallowmere** day 334: the oil-less lamps black glass and dusty a year; the wells (charmed sweet two hundred years by the licensed keeping-wives' spring word) sour, brown, iron-tasting, making children sick (\"We boil it. When there's wood\"); a **bread riot** at the **corn exchange** (long grey columned building, doors barred, crown-blue halberdiers, stones at windows): no grain since the new moon (the Clan's goes to the Marches; a ship from Corsa rumoured); licensed workers begging with crown licences pinned on (*Licensed to tend the lamps of the Fourth Terrace*; a keeping-wife's *for the keeping of milk*; a bone-setter's); she gives the lamp-tender two coppers. **Reede's paper** pasted on every door, post, wall and lamp-post (cheap grey, smudged black): *THE CROWN'S CRIME. / For three hundred years the crown's lamps burned stolen light. / Under Greyhold, in a cavern beneath the river, the first knights chained a great beast. A god of the old realm. They bound it in iron and sold its light. Every lamp in Hearthmere was fed from its chains. / The crown knew. The crown hid it. / The oath you said at noon was the chain. / Now the light is gone, and the beast is loose, and the crown that stole from it would chain it again. / Ask what you kept.* (a small printed ring of six); more than half true, twisted; the press saved from Cotter's Cross; read aloud on corners. **The chapel off the corn exchange** (grey stone, slate roof, door open): six shawled old women kneel round **the hearthstone** (every Hearthmere chapel has one: a flat round grey stone in the floor before the altar where the hearth-fire was once kept), hands flat on the floor: *Mother, keep the fire. Keep it small and keep it in.* Maren kneels with them (first time since the week her father died, with Liss at Greyhold's chapel). \"Who's the mother?\" \"The hearth-mother. The grey one. Under the hearth. She keeps the fire. She always has.\" (Plant: the Grey Mother.) **Davin** (clerk's ink, too-big cap): \"You're Liss's girl\"; clerk to Hallowmere's **street tribunes** (elected in the bread riots; run the bread-lines and the wells; the council tolerates them because it must); he reads Reede's paper aloud nightly on the corn exchange steps and then tells the crowd what's true and what isn't (\"*A god.* *Ask what you kept.* They want you to ask so you'll come to them for the answer. And their answer's a ring of six\"); where the true part came from: \"Somebody told them. Somebody who knew.\" **The council**, day 334, the crown's chapter-house long hall (grey stone, dark wood, faded order banners from the beams), **Lord Thale** presiding: Maren tells them the Titan has four sleeps; the fifth is the Gullhaven reef within the month; then it comes home to the river and the cavern; beyond the first chains a vault with the ring of six hollows, the held breath; when it has all five it goes for the ring and the six become one and do its will: \"to bring it all down\"; Corvin has the roll, the reader and the chain-words and is coming. Thanked. The **second cage** is being set now in the cavern where the chains were: Kessmoor engines, the crown's chain, the College's rune of holding on every link drawn by **Dane**, at Greyhold now with Kessmoor's engineers setting anchors. \"It won't hold.\" Thale wants **Orrun as bait** in the cage \"a little way inside. So that it comes all the way in\" (mirror of Book 1 ch 59). **Rowan** (new white Knight-Captain's surcoat) to command it, lances restored; she doesn't look up. \"No.\" \"The crown doesn't ask, Ashdown.\" \"Then it can try to chain him itself.\" In the Crown's Square Orrun: \"I have been in that cavern. Three hundred years... I will not be bait.\" Rowan alone on the steps: \"I told them it wouldn't hold... They gave me my lances back and told me to command it anyway... Because if somebody else does, they'll do it worse... Come to the yard. When you're there. Not for them... For the yard.\" She leaves for Greyhold in the morning. Echo, morning 335: a Tide Council herald (blue wave on his saddle-cloth) nails a printed notice over Reede's paper on the corn exchange door; Davin reads it to the bread-line: *By order of the Tide Council of the Saltreach League. To all the Leagues and kingdoms of Winshire. Notice. / The Council has sold, to the house of Strand, a contract for the taking and holding of the thing beneath the reef called the Drowned Bell. The house of Strand has assigned its execution to the company of Captain Idris Vey. / The thing beneath the reef is the property of the Council. Let no kingdom interfere.* Maren thinks of the spit and *Keep your coast*, and goes to the street tribunes about their wells.
- Ch 69 (days 336–342): 336–337 King's Road then River Road from Millbrook; d.338 noon Greyhold: **Cassian's body still in its chains under the charter** with the board (crows); crown's blue over the gate, crown company (blue coats, crossbows) on the walls; Orrun ~16 ft and more. Yard full of new cage-chain (links a man's height, runes struck inside the curve, Kessmoor pattern). **Tam** (bigger, conscripted with every Marches smith) at the old forge under the north tower; **Odo** in a chair at the forge door, legs healed crooked, two sticks: "Cold shut. Strike it again"; "It's not the links. It's what you hang them on." Odo rebuilt the granary back wall 20 years ago (three ties; stone crumbled like stale bread). Cellars under the inner wall knocked through, drilling the old stone for the cage's iron eyes. **Isak Thurl** (new: Master Engineer Pike's man, square, quiet, grey at temples, Kessmoor iron wheel on his coat; Pike says "she remembers"): reading-rod says the stone reads empty; the first anchors in the cavern read worse; he and Pike have written to Ironhythe twice. **Dane** (very straight, gloved hands): "It held three hundred years." Thurl: "It held something asleep." Maren sleeps at the ferry house; **Liss** greyer; new rope spliced in autumn with Tam; ferry running daily, people going east on rumour; "I'll need another rope." D.339 dawn the practice yard behind the south tower: Rowan alone doing forms; Rowan: she baited the trap because she always did what she was told; turned her back on the River Road because she couldn't look at the farmer. Maren: broke Callis's arm; killed the one at the school (first time said to anyone); didn't reach Thorne. Talk till noon; the noon bell doesn't ring (rope cut in spring). Rowan: cage won't hold, written to the council twice; "I'll hold it as long as it holds. And when it doesn't, I'll let it go" (plant ch 82); won't chain Orrun, will report he couldn't be taken. **Callis** bought out of Hallowmere's cells in the autumn: fine paid in Saltreach gold by "a friend of the family"; gaoler gone too. Echo d.339 afternoon: Saltreach salt-boatman from Wendmouth: forty-odd dragons over Corsa in the Clan's colours (cousin saw it from a roof; "something about the oyster beds"). Night 339 the held breath: water-gate stair barely wide enough for Orrun; cavern as before (black still water, six rings, knight's lock lit) plus Kessmoor scaffolds and waist-wide holes for the eyes; the shut place is a plain smooth wall of old stone, warm; *It is waiting.* / *For the one who comes home. And for us.* D.340 dusk **Lisska (flight 6)** lands on the ferry-house roof, very thin; two pages; Liss: "He writes like a child." / "He writes." / "He does." Via Orrun: Calder was afraid in the hall and read every line; he says *keep the door* to Lisska every night before he sleeps. D.341 Maren's verdict on the back of the Corsa page (Odo, Tam, Liss present): *The cage won't hold. They are hanging it on the inner wall... it's empty. Odo says so. The Kessmoor engineer says so. Rowan says so. Dane says it held three hundred years. / The crown wants Orrun for bait. No. / Callis is out. Corvin bought him. / Keep your spit. Then come.* Odo: "It's a good letter." Dawn 342 Lisska leaves SE (Liss paid for a Staithe goat).
- Ch 72 (days 342–348): d.342 noon **Wynn** (hounds Ash and Sorrow) from Ferriby: a band of ~20 broken Restoration men (iron rings, no banner) with three binders took every Ferriby plough-ox, the miller's horses and two farm dogs, walked them north empty-eyed into the Brackenwood; paid the miller in Saltreach gold with **a ring of six in the rim**. Kitchen council (Liss, Odo, Wynn): Corvin's coming to the held breath and buying an army on this bank; running water stops a spoken binding and a bound beast driven into running water comes out of it itself; so **east over the Wend**, up the Drover's Way to Harrow's End and on toward Clan ground. Liss: "I'll need another rope." D.343 **Thurl gives Liss a coil of Kessmoor hawser** (hemp on an iron-wire core, thumb-thick, one of twelve for hauling cage-chain to the Saltreach anchors): "Put it down as lost in the river." "I will. And it will be." Tam and two Kessmoor men rig it jetty post to Staithe post that afternoon. Same day a council rider: every Greyhold knight to the cage, none on the River Road. D.344 Rowan sends Wynn + eight knights with hounds down the road anyway ("Scouting") and lends the crown's flat-bottomed cage barge (four knights at the sweeps, "scouting, very slowly, back and forth"). Ferry runs day and night from 344: Liss by day on the new rope, Tam at night with a bow lantern. Days 343–345 Maren and Orrun to Millbrook, Northwatch, Penfold, Ferriby, the Redwatch boathouse village: "Go over the river. Take your beasts. Don't stop on the bank. Go up the Way." Northwatch laughs; Penfold shuts its doors; **Millbrook goes** (Tam on the Kettle's mounting-block: "She's my sister"); Widow Rudd first, board and cat. Far bank: a line up the Way toward Harrow's End; the Clan will feed them. **D.346 Penfold**: twelve ringed men and two binders driving eight bound cows down the lane by the beck; a binder's word and smoke on Orrun do nothing (a bonded beast can't be bound); band and first binder flee; farmer drives the cows into the beck and each comes out of it itself, eyes filled, mooing: Maren's first sight of running water breaking a binding; second binder drops his pot and runs. **D.348 afternoon echo**: Liss, from a Saltreach salt-boatman up from Wendmouth (news off a Saltings barge): the big one went over the Saltings three days ago at noon, low, reeds flat for a league, out to sea toward Gullhaven. Maren had counted the low spring tide as today noon: "He's on it... The farmer. On his spit."

## Calder (68, 70, 73, 75, 78)
- Ch 68 (days 329–334): d.329 Council's answer is a summons ("Master Calder Fenn will attend the Council at Corsa and explain himself"). Night before, at the Anchor-and-Net, Calder copies the reader's numbers as a big-letter table on the last clean sheet of Clan paper (Ring: relic midsummer, woke 7 days after / Vael: relic 172, woke 5 / Scald: relic 233, woke 5 / Sun Stair: relic 315, woke 2 / EVERY TIME SOONER / THE BELL: WITHIN THE MONTH, THEN SAME DAY OR NEXT); Ada checks the arithmetic "because they will". D.330 to Corsa with Kharrak and Lisska; Aurveth stays on the spit. D.331 Council: Marrow presiding; **Master Aubrey Strand** (the soft-voiced man in green); Quill woman in white; Halloran chair empty since spring; Vey at the captains' end. Calder reads the sheet aloud himself (Marrow: "In the spring you couldn't."), then the spit, 412 roofs, 40 baskets, Kessmoor's cage on land (Pike, Moss). Marrow: "We know now, Master Fenn. As you said." Strand: contract stands; Vey's company, Kessmoor engines on barges, Gearmouth imbued harpoons; won't empty Gullhaven: oyster season, richest beds in the League, "The Lady keeps the stones" (Corsa joke). No member moves to empty it. Marrow knows Ada's words via the Clan rider's talk on the sea-steps. Vey on the steps: took the execution because nobody else would bid and the price went up; "I'll be on the reef. You'll be on the spit. One of us will be wrong... I hope it's you"; glove off for a handshake. Night 331 Kharrak sent back to Gullhaven with a word (Kharrak→Tamsin→Gunna→Arn): *They said no. Come and show them.* D.332 noon the Clan over Corsa: every Gullhaven pair + twenty more from the Scarfell, forty-odd, wheeling low over harbour and Tidehall copper roof; town on its roofs; Council on its balcony. Arn (red), Ulric (grey), Gunna (Tamsin) on the sea-steps; Arn: Clan will lift the spit with or without leave, asks the Council's word it won't hinder, and will pay Vey's wyverns from the Clan purse to take children off the salt-pan sheds. Marrow: "The Council will not hinder." Vey: "I'll take the coin. For the children. Not the reef." Night 333 Lisska west to Greyhold (**flight 6**) with two pages: the Gullhaven promise page (WHEN THE COAST IS DONE THE CLAN WILL COME TO THE RIVER) and tonight's six lines (Council won't empty the spit / oyster season / Vey on the reef with harpoons / Clan over Corsa, Council won't hinder / Vey's wyverns for the sheds, Arn paying / reader's numbers right, read them myself / Keep the door). Lisska: "There's a lion." D.334 echo: Hearthmere grain cog (crown's blue sail, Hallowmere seal) at the grain quay, bought with the crown's last gold (crown's plate sold), for Stillhaven then up the Meryl; bread riots in Hallowmere. Calder returns to the spit with Arn and the flights.
- Ch 70 (days 335–345): d.335 back on the spit (forty-odd pairs; a Corsa boat brought the news first). Arn under the Council notice: anyone who'll go now, today. ~200 go over days 335–338 (an 84-year-old who wants to see the hills; a woman with twins; a boy and his grandmother) to the salt-store hill and farms behind the pans; the rest stay: "The Lady keeps the spit." D.337 **Old Nan Gorrie** (blind net-mender, ~90; son drowned off the Bell twenty years back; "I'm not salt and I'm not a child"): Ulric kneels, she climbs on his back, he walks her the whole league of the spit road to a bench at the salt-store, his grey following; "You're not him. You're a good boy, though." D.338–339 **reefwyrms** in hundreds packed against the spit's inner shingle under the jetties (hiding, not feeding; came from the Bell); oyster-men can't get out; Hessa Pane: they come in before a big sea; no storm. Calder feels the breath through his own feet, quicker daily. D.341 **Strand fleet** from the west past the Saltings: eleven ships (flagship, four tow-ships with Kessmoor engine-barges, five harpoon-ships with bow bolt-throwers and rune-cut mast-long harpoons, Vey's small black single-master with hooded Sable and Hask); anchored in a line outside the breakers. D.342 Vey counts the salt-pan sheds with Arn: fourteen sheds, two wyverns, three children a lift (four if small), seven trips each "if the sheds stand that long"; every Gullhaven child over seven works the pans. D.343 the harbour-master's dozen gull-hawks won't fly out over the bay (one comes back wet and shaking); Aurveth's month up, Ivar Leck lets her fly short; she opens a crack of the deep sense: the breath very quick. D.344 noon Lisska from Greyhold via the Wend's mouth (two days): the cage, iron, drilling, the lion-woman says it won't hold; Maren's page read without his finger; his coat-lump: her answer page, the five-line letter, the eleven pages, Rook's book, Ada's account, now this. **Night 344 the stir**: every rune on every hull and oar on the spit (keel-runes steady, bow-runes true, oar-runes pull) glows white then burns and smokes; fishermen drown them; the Strand fleet's hull-runes and harpoons glow in lines out in the bay; Calder: "It's the stir... It came up like this at Vael the night before. And at the Scald." D.345 dusk: Aurveth: *A day behind the stir. It's come.* An island-shaped darkness comes in from the SW and lies down in deep water beyond the breakers, between the reef and the fleet, bigger than the reef; *It's waiting... For the tide... Three days.* (low springs 348). Town on 412 roofs in the moonlight; nobody goes in. Every boat on the spit scorched at the runes.

## Idony (71, 76)
- Ch 71 (days 318–348; prose counts days from Solenne, 318 = first day): never locked in ("Where would you go? It's the sea."). Ship: Sarovel-built, two masts, white hull smoke-black on one side, name painted out, crew twenty-odd Saltreach and Sarovel men. **Captain Mauger** (grey beard; shot the bird at Vael; knows Idony knows). Idony has his four glasses and the silk in his coat; stick lost at Solenne; a too-long spar-stick from day 319. Every morning reads the roll aloud in the stern cabin, palm on it; Corvin writes every stroke in a black book; "Eight [months]. Without anyone who could read it." D.322 Corvin wants the appendix (chain-words, lock-words): "You'll teach me. Or I'll learn them slower... and get them wrong." Taught: *hold*, *hold fast*, *turn back*, *tighten*. **D.327 the wrong stroke**: in *tighten*'s third curve Idony turns the stroke wrong (his hand decides, fourth time): it reads *let go* (the hold rune's unmaking, half-said). In the hold: four binders with pots, a **bound wyvern pair** (black-green, hooded, neck-chained in a stall: plant ch 82) and a **bound brindled Hearthmere marsh-hound** in an old iron *hold*-rune collar. **D.330** Corvin reads *tighten* aloud palm on the collar: collar loosens, somebody comes into the hound's eyes, it goes for his throat, tears his sleeve, bites his forearm; binders re-bind it. "That's not *tighten*." "No." "Is that the only one?" (no answer) "Then you'll have to be there. When it matters. To say it yourself. All of it... I'd rather you were anyway." Corvin's arm bound, then in a sling. **Night 332 Callis** comes aboard at a no-lights cove near Blackwater, rowed by two Halloran men in house grey: thin, grey-faced, right arm crooked below the elbow, left wrist thick and bent, holds a cup in both hands; Corvin holds his face: "You came." "You paid." Bought out in autumn, carried across the Marches in a cart under sacks and down the Saltreach Track; brings news of the crown's cage at Greyhold (Kessmoor iron, Kessmoor men, the Blue magister, anchors into the hill). Callis to Idony: "Is the lion bigger?" "Yes." He becomes Corvin's right hand: gives orders on deck, sleeps across Corvin's door. **Night 335 the argument**: the crown's cage is the College's chains again ("Your college sold them to Hearthmere three hundred years ago and it's selling them again"); a cage on a waking one makes it worse; "You can chain it. Or you can command it... Chains fail... So." The ring with six hollows "was made for it"; he'd ask it "Whether it remembers being asked." Idony can't find the third thing. **D.338** a silent brown pilot off a reed-boat (paid in Halloran silver) takes them two leagues into the Saltings; masts down, hidden in a ship-wide reed channel. Glasses lean SE to the Bell; lowest springs in ten days. **D.341 echo**: Idony hauled to the masthead in a sling: eleven green sails going east, four towing smoking barges, bolt-throwers on bows; Corvin: "There's the Council. Going to take it." Callis laughs; Corvin doesn't. **Night 344 the stir**: all glasses to the top at midnight; wyverns scream, hound howls, binders' pots smoke by themselves; "Within the day. Then it waits for the tide." **D.345 noon the Titan passes over the Saltings** from the NW (Sunward Hills, over the Ashlings), low; reeds flatten, birds rise in thousands; twelve iron chain-ends hanging, links taller than the masts, *hold* runes dark; **four** grown-over places in its back plates; it goes SE out to sea toward the Bell. **D.348 dawn** pilot takes them to the Saltings' mouth, bow just out of the reeds; at a little before noon the reef comes up; through the deck, the bell. Corvin closes his eyes.
