# Relic Stops Review: Book 2

*The editor's concern: the temple-and-relic journey "could feel like a sequence of similar missions; each stop should change the characters' understanding, alliances or available choices, as well as advance the Titan threat." I checked it against the 88 drafted chapters (their notes blocks and the prose), `Chapter_Plan.md` (temple table), `Magic.md` §1 and §5, and `Series_Outline.md`. The drafts are canon, so this report only makes suggestions.*

## 2. Stops that change the same thing or feel alike

**A. The taking gesture, three times.** "Between its forepaws" (20:302); "Into the hill. Between its forepaws… the way it had… into the ring of stones" (36:378); "Between its forepaws. To the glass. As it had at the Ring… As it had on the Library hill at Vael." (45:214). The prose itself flags the repeat. The framing varies (close up; from Ferry Street; the egg into the back), and 60 and 73 break the pattern, but this is where "same mission" is most likely to be felt.

**B. Ring and Scald share the same POV relay:** Idony's stir (16 / 44), Maren's taking (20 / 45), Idony's surge and prediction (22 / 47), Maren's waking (25 / 48) and temple descent (23 / 53). The content differs; the order doesn't.

**C. Shadows holding back earth: 48 and 63.** In both, three Orruns brace against sliding ground (canal banks; a cliff) while Maren says Pell's stand-word into it. The notes call 63 "two at once, as at the Scald: second time". The shadow count doesn't grow until 83. The one change, the word failing at 63, isn't followed up. 25 (lanes) and 37 (wall) are the same family but told differently enough.

**D. "Last to leave dies," three times.** Hesper in the last lane (25); Mother Ysa "rows the last boat… and doesn't come back" (63); Vey gets "the last drawer into the boat" (73). Hesper is planted and Vey has his line. Ysa first appears in the chapter she dies in, so hers reads most like a repeat.

**E. Held for a moment, then breaks free (20, 36, 73, 80).** Eyes "went blank" (20); held "one breath" (36); pulled "onto one knee" (73); "holds, for a while" (80). The methods differ, as the outline asks (Series_Outline l.103), but the beat is the same. Only 48, where the cage really holds, breaks it.

**F. The sleepers disappear once they wake.** After 25, 37 and 61 the Antlered, the Coil and the Horned Sun leave the page until Aurveth names them at 85. The only later effects are the Coil's meltflood (43) and the drains; 50's "a mountain walking" is a Western Deep rumour, not one of the five. The outline wants "one Titan loose, then two, then more" (Series_Outline l.132). On the page the build-up runs through the Last Titan's speed and the drains, and the count of loose sleepers is never felt.

**G. Not a problem: the temple rooms.** 23, 37, 53, 60 and 84 all show a domed chamber, a warm plinth and the six, but each adds exactly one new piece (six sleepers; the Coil's name; the ring and the song; the egg plus the map; the bare vault) and each is reached a different way. This repetition works.

**Continuity slip found along the way:** 84:72 reads "Like the one under the Ring that she'd heard of and never seen, where the first egg had been." In 23 Maren stood "with her hand still in the warm hollow" of that plinth.

## 3. Minimal suggestions

1. **(A) 45:214.** Keep the refrain, but make the third one mark a difference. One clause after "on the Library hill at Vael" would do it: *It didn't wait for the cup to fill this time.* The repeat then measures how much stronger the Titan is.
2. **(B)** Leave the structure alone; Sarovel and the Bell already break it.
3. **(C) 63.** Give the failed stand-word one line of consequence (*Everything she had was in them. There was nothing left to say a word with.*) and call it back at 83, where she "can't help: she's in the lions". The failure becomes a rule she pays for, not a rerun of 48.
4. **(D) Mother Ysa.** Plant her once before 63 (Tobin naming the lighthouse keeper in 58's echo), or give her death an effect: the Sorrel Isle light dark the next night, and the tribunes lighting it in the heir's name.
5. **(E)** No change needed; 48 already breaks the pattern.
6. **(F) The biggest gap.** At 50, where Idony "marks it every night", add one sentence saying he marks the woken draws too (the stag at the Worldsend, the cold one down the Wend, the Furnace in its cage) and that they are not moving together. At 75, as the Bell goes south, give Aurveth one line: *It isn't going with him. None of them are. Not yet.* That carries the count from one loose to five, gives the Bell stop the knowledge it lacks, and sets up 85.
7. **(G) Fix 84:72.** For example: *Like the one under the Ring, where she'd put her hand in the warm hollow the first egg had left.*

## 4. Verdict

The stops are already well distinguished. Each sleep-relic stop changes at least three of knowledge, alliances, choices and cost, and each has its own look: forest, city and lake, industrial basin, court, sea. Vael and Sarovel are the strongest: Vael brings the College's guilt, the bird's death and the start of the race; Sarovel loses Idony and turns Maren's mission from chasing the Titan to keeping the door. The Scald carries the turn.

The warning still applies in three places: the repeated taking gesture (20, 36, 45); the 48/63 shadow set-piece and the "last to leave dies" deaths; and the woken sleepers vanishing, so the threat builds through speed, not numbers. The Bell is thinnest for knowledge but earns its place through cost, the Clan and the marked roofs. Suggestions 1, 3 and 6 deal with most of the risk in about a dozen lines.
