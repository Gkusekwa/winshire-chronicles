*A chapter-by-chapter plan for Book 2, in the shape of `Book 1/Chapter_Plan.md`. Built on `Bible/Series_Outline.md` (the Book 2 spine), the rest of the Bible, Book 1 as drafted (chapters 41–79), the unmerged additions in `review/bible41/`, `Rulings_41-79.md`, and the magic system in `review/Magic.md` (itself all proposal unless it says canon). **Canon is marked *(canon)*. Everything else is a *(proposal)* for the author to approve, change or cut.** In the chapter table, every beat is a proposal unless it carries a *(canon)* tag.*

*Revision 2 adds a third POV, the tide-reader Idony Vael (R), and works the magic system into the plot and the action.*

*Working title (proposal): **The Hunt Beneath**. *The Six Who Sleep* would give the sleepers away before chapter 23 does.*

## Premise

The Last Titan is free, and the world finds out what Hearthmere did to it. Hearthmere's lamps are dark, its wells are sour and its ground is all but dead; the Restoration was right, and it has lost the beast it opened the seal to take. Every kingdom now wants a Titan of its own *(canon)*. Maren Ashdown, outcast, bonded to the grieving lion Orrun, follows the Titan because Orrun won't let it go. Calder Fenn, outlawed, with one dragon who can't fly, carries the truth about Teo Varr's death to a Clan that won't believe a barley farmer, because the war is still burning his village *(canon)*. And in Vael, on the night every tide-glass in his college breaks at once, a low-ranked scholar called Idony Vael watches the hole he's charted under Hearthmere for eleven years close, and something start to move across the world at the speed of a bird. The Titan goes from temple to temple, and from each it takes a relic, and behind it something that has slept since the old realm fell wakes up *(canon)*, first in a flood of wild magic and then in a famine of it. The three of them try to keep it out of every kingdom's chains, save whoever stands in the way, and race it to the next temple, until they understand what it's gathering: five Titans awake, and the sixth relic, under Hearthmere, to command them *(canon)*. A year after it flew, it comes home for that relic, and gets it *(canon)*.

## Shape

- **88 chapters in seven parts** *(proposal)*: 70 Maren-and-Calder chapters and 18 for Idony, over about a year from the morning after the flight. At Book 1's rate that's roughly **310,000–440,000 words**: an estimate, not a target.
- **Three POVs** *(proposal)*: **S** Maren, **F** Calder, **R** Idony (the researcher).
- **Calendar** *(proposal)*: Book 2 counts **days from the flight** (day 0 = sunrise in Book 1 ch. 79). Seasons are given by name.

### The rotation *(proposal)*

- **S and F keep strict alternation with each other**, as in Book 1, ignoring R chapters: the sequence of S and F chapters always runs S, F, S, F through the whole book. The book opens on S and ends on F.
- **R chapters slot in between, at a density set by the part**, placed where his reading answers what the protagonists are doing. There are never two R chapters in a row.
  - **About one in five** in the protagonists' own ground (Parts One, Two, Six, Seven).
  - **About one in three** in Part Three (Isvaren, his home) and **one in four** in Part Four (Kessmoor's engines). That's where the lore is densest and where his knowledge drives the action.
  - **One deliberate silence**: no R chapter from his capture (62) to 71, so the reader feels him missing the way Book 1 felt Calder under the beam.
- **Why this shape:** Maren and Calder stay the spine (80% of the chapters), so Book 2 still reads as their story. A fixed R slot (every third chapter, say) would force filler where Idony has nothing to do and starve him where he matters. He's tied to place and instruments, so his density follows the map. His first chapter is 3, not 1, so Book 2 opens on the reeds as Book 1 closed, and the stranger arrives once the reader is settled.

### The echo device *(proposal)*

- **S and F chapters apart:** as in Book 1, with **Lisska** flying between them. Her news reaches Maren through Orrun, who can speak with her as far kin (Rulings 5, 13).
- **S and F chapters together:** the echo is what this POV can't perceive directly: the other's bonded voices, news from the other's homeland, or **Idony's work** (a glass rising, a rune he's read, a line in his notes).
- **R chapters:** the echo is of **either protagonist**, usually seen the way Idony sees things: as a reading. Orrun's bond-shaping shows on his glasses as a small moving draw he calls **"the walker"** long before he meets the lion.

## Magic in the action *(proposal; from `Magic.md`)*

- **Hearthmere is dead ground** from chapter 1: no charms, no healing words, imbued things failing (Haverly's edge is the exception, because runes hold as long as their material, Magic §6). Fights there are steel and beasts. That's why the crown buys a heartstone cage: engines don't need the Deep.
