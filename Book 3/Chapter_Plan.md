## Premise

A year after it flew, the Last Titan has the control relic and five Titans that answer it *(canon)*. They go up together into the Worldsend, and the lights over the mountains burn like a second dawn in the wrong place. When they come down, no single kingdom can stand *(canon, Book 2's end)*, so the six kingdoms do what they have never done and stand together, every one of them paying for it *(canon: the kingdoms unite)*. One of them means to come out of the war ruling the rest *(canon)*.

**Maren Ashdown** and **Calder Fenn** still don't share a road *(canon)*. Hers goes west: Orrun remembers that the ones who set him at Hearthmere's door went "into the light", toward the sunset over the sea. To get there she has to cross **Saltreach**, the lawless league where everything has a price, and buy passage from a family of reef-pilots who have been quietly sailing to the far shore for four generations *(canon: the land beyond the sea; the people who take them there are met in Saltreach; proposal: who)*. His road is the Saltreach coast, his own people's sea, where a Titan the size of an island is coming in and the Tide Council won't empty a harbour without a contract. When the call comes for his dragons he goes over the sea too, by a different road and for his own reasons, with a Merrin pilot he meets in Blackwater, because the far shore can't be found from the air.

On the far shore they find the **elder Titans**, the gods every chapel in Winshire prays to, who were Titans once and stopped *(canon: worshipped as gods without people knowing they were Titans; proposal, `World.md`: Book 3 reveals it)*. Two of the four come back to fight. Two give what is left of themselves, to the lion and to the dragons, and go out for good *(canon)*. Orrun becomes a Titan without becoming what his kin became; the three dragons become Titan-class *(canon)*. With six on each side, and the beasts of every kingdom in the line beside the armies, the war turns. At the middle of the world, on the river where the Last Titan was chained, the alliance gets within one hand's reach of ending it *(canon: a glimpse of victory)*. Then the plotting kingdom reaches for the relic itself and breaks the line *(canon)*, and the Titans win *(canon)*: the middle of the world breaks and they rise over the field.

The six kingdoms go down. The book shows a few of the falls and lets the reader feel the rest *(canon: hint, don't show every fall; not a disguised victory)*. The lion and the dragons die protecting the few hundred people the protagonists got into the mountain *(canon)*. Their power comes back into their partners, and Maren and Calder survive as something close to Titans *(canon)*. With no Titans left, as far as anyone knows, every bond in the world goes quiet. People and beasts can't reach each other any more *(canon)*. On the last morning the remnant sees something small move by itself on the stones *(canon: the first elemental stirs)*.

## Shape

- **86 chapters in seven parts** *(proposal)*, over about eleven months, from dusk on the day the six go up from the Scarfell (Book 2 ch. 88, its day 362), in spring, to the deep snow on the Scarfell at the end of the next winter. At Book 1's rate that's roughly **300,000–430,000 words**: an estimate, not a target.
- **Calendar** *(proposal)*: days from dusk on the day the six went up from the Scarfell (Book 2 ch. 88, its day 362, = day 0), which is spring. Midsummer falls about day 90 and midwinter about day 276 (ch. 57). Part Two runs from late spring into summer, Part Three is high summer, Part Four autumn, Part Five early winter into midwinter, Part Six deep winter and Part Seven late winter. Orrun, near Aurveth's size, keeps about twenty leagues a day on the ground, day after day (a hard rider's pace, `Atlas.md`); a dragon flies eighty. The final battle is in deep winter, when Coldtarn's warm water matters most. Seasons in the prose are given by name.

Maren and Calder carry 53 of the 86 chapters (62%). That's less than Book 2's 80%, because the war is everywhere at once and the protagonists can't be. It's still their book.

### Why these four extra POVs *(proposal)*

Book 2 (revision 4) keeps all eight candidates alive and recommends Arn, Rowan, Tobin and Corvin. This plan takes **three of the four** and adds **one new Saltreach voice**:

- **Rowan (G): the war's general.** Her arc already runs from the chain (Book 1's trap; Book 2's "Better that than loose") to letting go of it for the people (Book 2 ch. 82). In Book 3 she becomes the one commander every kingdom will accept, because Hearthmere is too broken to threaten anyone. Her flaw now is her faith in a sworn word: she believes a signed Accord binds the signers. Through her the reader sees the war councils and the plan, and she's the one who signs off on the plotting kingdom's engine as a fallback. Her chapters make the betrayal personal. She dies holding the gap it opens.
- **Arn Varr (C): the dragon front, and the end of the Clan.** He gives the book the air war, which the Clan shares with the Wyldmarch's rocs, Isvaren's stormkite-riders and Saltreach's wyvern companies *(Bestiary, flying creatures)*, and the evacuations from the air, which neither protagonist can always be part of. His arc goes from claiming (the chair, the three dragons, the Clan's ownership of its dragons) to being chosen: a freed young dragon bonds him, and he opens the Clan's breeding pens for good. He survives to feel the bond go quiet, the last chief of a dragon clan whose dragons can no longer speak to it.
- **Tobin (U): inside the plotting kingdom, without knowing it.** Book 2 picked Tobin as the eye for the betrayal because it expected Sarovel to plot. This plan makes **Kessmoor** the traitor (see **The alliance and the betrayal**), and Tobin is still the right eye: he leads Sarovel's starving unchosen north to labour in Kessmoor's works as "hands" again. Kessmoor's world without bonds or Grace, where only skill counts, is the world he wanted when he joined the Restoration. He helps build the engine that breaks the line, finds out what it's for too late, and dies turning his crews to the refugee road.
- **Kit Merrin (M): Saltreach from the inside.** The author wants Saltreach explored and the crossing to begin there. Kit is a Blackwater reef-pilot whose house has sailed the drowned road to the far shore in secret for four generations. She carries Maren over; her brother Jory guides Calder. Her arc goes from "everything has a price" to carrying people for nothing. She survives into the remnant: a sea captain under a mountain.

**Not chosen** *(proposal)*:
- **Corvin**: a Corvin POV would show the reader Kessmoor's plot from the inside, and then nobody could believe in the glimpse of victory *(canon: readers should believe, for a few chapters, that the alliance will win)*. He's seen closely in Idony's, Tobin's and Maren's chapters instead.
- **Ulric**: overlaps with Arn. He dies at Corsa (19), which leaves Arn to lead alone.
- **Gunna Tarne**: rides in Arn's chapters and dies with Tamsin in the Thornwood (49).
- **Callis**: at Corvin's side. Seen through Tobin, he gets the late turn Corvin never does (71, 74).
- **Tam**: a smith at Ironhythe beside Tobin, who refuses to forge the Bridle's six-hollow ring (33) and is sent to the Marches with the wagons, then in the remnant. He makes its first fire (86).
- **Fisk** *(Book 1, Book 2)*: in the remnant, the one who "tells it after" (80, 86).

### The rotation *(proposal)*

- **S and F keep strict alternation with each other**, ignoring the other POVs, as in Books 1 and 2. The book opens on S and ends on S, so it closes on Maren as Book 1 did ("Keep the watch").
- **The other five POVs slot in between, never two in a row.** Each part gets about four to six of them, placed where that voice sees something the protagonists can't: the war council (G), the air front, shared by the Clan and every kingdom's fliers (C), Kessmoor's works (U), Saltreach and the sea (M), the Deep's readings (R).
- **Density follows the ground:** Kit is densest in Part Two (Saltreach and the crossing: three of her eight), then once or twice a part; Tobin in Parts Three to Six (the works, the engine, the battle); Rowan once or twice in every part from One to Six; Idony wherever the old magic is (the far shore, the hub, the end).
- **Echo column:** every chapter still carries something of another thread (a sighting, a rumour, a reading, a word through the bond). The reader always knows a little more than whoever's chapter it is.

## Saltreach: the lawless empire *(author's direction: a major part of Book 3; everything below is proposal unless marked)*

Saltreach is a place in Book 3, not a backdrop: Part Two is set there, Kit's eight chapters live there, and its trade supplies the betrayal's tools (sleepwood resin, heartstones, stolen knowledge). *Canon from World.md and Atlas.md: a league of port-cities ruled from Corsa by the Tide Council of trading houses and pirate captains; no king and no knights; sellswords, pirates and outlaws do the fighting; beast markets; magic sold by contract; its writ barely reaches inland, so the Marches pay their dues to the Clan.*

**Who rules.** The **Tide Council** sits at two tables in Corsa's hall. The **Houses' Table** has a seat for every house with more than a set tonnage on the water, so a house that loses ships loses its voice. The **Captains' Bench** is for the pirate and privateer captains who keep the sea lanes: their seats are won by holding a stretch of coast and lost the same way. The **Speaker**, Mistress Orla Marrow *(Book 2 ch. 49, 68: blue silk, salt-grey hair in a net, a square calm figurehead's face)*, holds the chair because both tables fear her. Votes are bought openly; buying one is a contract like any other. The Council has no army of its own: it hires one.

**The law is the contract.** Nothing binds in Saltreach except a sworn contract. A contract is sworn on **salt**: a pinch on the tongue from the same dish, the oldest custom in the League. Break one and you're **unsalted**: no one will make a contract with you, honour your debts or sell you water. It's the League's only real punishment. A Council **warrant** (a stamp on wax) guarantees a contract, a charm or a cargo; a warranted thing costs twice as much and is half as likely to be fake.

**Protection.** Every harbour mouth has a **chain**, and every ship pays **chain money** to have it lowered. Every town on the Coast Road has a **toll gate**, kept by whoever holds the town that season. Every quay has a company that will stop anyone else robbing you. Nobody keeps the peace except whoever was paid last *(Atlas: Cotter's Cross)*.

**The houses.** **Ostrey**: grain, insurance and passage; the largest house in Corsa, and the one that sells the Far Berths. **Saffery**: Wendmouth's beast trade and Gallows Quay. **Halloran**: the house that minted the Restoration's ring-of-six silver; fallen since Book 2 ch. 49, with Oswin Rusk fled. **Strand** *(Book 2 ch. 68–75)*: bought the Bell contract, lost four ships and Captain Vey on the reef, and would not empty Gullhaven in oyster season; Master Aubrey Strand. **Quill**: the house whose woman in white sat silent at the long table and left the ferry-house camp before the envoys met *(Book 2 ch. 68, 86)*. **Merrin**: a small Blackwater salvage house of reef-pilots, in debt to Ostrey. Each house paints its shutters in its own colours, so a street tells you who owns it. **Money**: house silver with the house mark on the rim, coppers for the poor; gold for the Council and the crowns.

**Saltreach's beasts.** Wyverns are the League's war-beasts: tamed, not bonded, hired by the company with their handlers *(Bestiary)*; gull-hawks carry every harbour's news; reefwyrms follow ships for scraps and sink some. In Book 3 the wyvern companies sell their sky to the Accord by contract (15, 38, 58, 64) and fly beside the Clan, the first time in memory.

**The beast markets and the honey-smoke trade.** Gallows Quay sells anything with a pulse *(Book 1)*: legal beasts, stolen beasts, beasts from kingdoms that would hang the seller. **Sleepwood resin**, cooked with honey into honey-smoke (Magic §6), is the Wyldmarch's forbidden export. The Keepers burned their groves in Book 2, so it's dearer than ever, and smuggled through the Reedmarsh and the Saltings. **Heartstone** (beast-matter) is Kessmoor's coal, and its price has trebled since Kessmoor's cage *(Book 2 ch. 49)*.

**Magic for sale.** Contract-workers sell charms by the job, warranted or not: fair winds, dry holds, a captain's luck, a mill-wheel that won't squeal *(Book 1 ch. 2: Hob's two-silver charm-tag)*. Houses keep a **drawer on retainer** the way they keep a lawyer. A drawer is useless on a deck (Magic §2a: grounding), so the houses' drawers fight on quays and reefs. **Far-stone** flakes from the Merrins' salvage are sold on Gallows Quay as "old charms", and they're the only ones that sometimes do something.

**Pirates and sea routes.** The Coast Road joins Wendmouth, Gullhaven (gone since Book 2), Corsa and Blackwater. The Saltings' channels need local pilots. The **Shattered Sea**'s reef channels, among the broken piers of the drowned road, are piloted only by Blackwater families, and the Merrins are the last who go all the way. The Bell's waking in Book 2 closed half the channels.

**Customs.** The smile arrives first *(Book 1 ch. 7: Gannet)*. Salt on the tongue for a deal. Never ask a captain where a cargo came from. The floating market at Wendmouth. The Merrins' private rule: **never sleep ashore over there.**

**Prices** *(proposal, for consistency in drafting)*: a warranted charm, 2 silver *(Book 1)*; a berth from Corsa to Wendmouth, a month's wages; a bound hound at Gallows Quay, 40 silver; an ounce of sleepwood resin, 5 silver and rising; the Book 2 contract on the lion, 10,000 *(Book 2 ch. 14)*; a **Far Berth**, 300 gold; Calder's harbour contract, one copper (15).

**The inland Marches and the coast.** The Marches farms pay dues to the Clan, not the League *(canon)*. The coast calls them "barley-folk", buys their grain cheap down the Wend at Wendmouth, and sells it dear in Corsa. Calder is both: a barley farmer, and the man who brought down a Council house. The docks love him for it and the houses don't.

**What war and the Titans do to the markets.** Titans become contracts *(Book 2: "the Bell" sold to the highest bidder)*. Insurance on drowned towns pays out in Council paper that no one honours. Refugees become cargo, and a place on a cart is bought with whatever you carried. When the far shore becomes known, the Council sells it (the **Far Berths**, 20) and sends a fleet of the rich into the Bell's water (28). After Corsa falls, the Council moves to Blackwater and sells itself to the Accord by contract: Saltreach's sellswords stand with the crowns *(Series proposal)* because the crowns are paying. Saltreach doesn't plot to rule. It sells, and what it sells (resin, heartstone, Idony's notes) helps the kingdom that does.

## The alliance and the betrayal

**The alliance** *(canon: the kingdoms unite against the Titans)*: the **Accord of Ironhythe** (12). Members: Hearthmere (the crown's council; Rowan as Marshal of the Accord), Saltreach (the Tide Council, then its remnant in Blackwater), the Clan (Arn; Ulric until 19), the Wyldmarch (Elder Tova), Isvaren (Master Quell, from a drowned Vael), Sarovel (the boy-king's regency and the unchosen tribunes, with Tobin's levy), and Kessmoor (First Guildmaster Edmund Kaye; Master Engineer Agathe Pike). Each pays the cost the Series proposes: Sarovel unbonded knights, Kessmoor magic, Saltreach crowns, the Wyldmarch the kingdoms that hunted its beasts. The protagonists hold it together *(Series proposal)* by being the only two who've been everywhere and owe nobody.

**Who plots** *(canon: one kingdom plots to be supreme and wants the control relic's power; proposal: which)*: **Kessmoor.** Why:
- It pays off Book 2: Kessmoor's heartstone cage was the only capture that held *(Book 2 ch. 48)*, and "every kingdom learns the wrong lesson from it". Kessmoor learned it best.
- After Book 2 it's the strongest kingdom left. Engines never felt a drain *(Magic §7)*, so the Accord can't fight without it. Being indispensable is what lets it plot in plain sight.
- The kingdom that banned magic after the Scald wants the oldest magic of all, so that it is never again at the mercy of what lies underneath. That's the Scald's shame turned into ambition.
- It has no bonds, so it has no stake in the beasts; and its Assembly measures everything, cost included, and then pays it.
- Alternatives: **Sarovel**, the Series' first candidate, would need a new regime first, since Book 2 kills its king and breaks the Grace; **Isvaren**, whose College sold the chain-craft twice, is too drowned to reach.

**How** *(proposal)*. The plot is hidden in plain sight as a **fallback** the Accord itself approves:
1. **The clause** (12): Titan-matter and relics go into Kessmoor's keeping after the war.
2. **The Bridle** (33, 41, 48, 62): a ring of heartstone engines and iron chain, to hold the Last Titan "if the elders fail". Rowan approves it on condition that it fires only on her word (41, 55).
3. **The knowledge**: from Saltreach's market. Gannet's copies of Idony's notes (*it answers a breath that is unbound*) go to Ostrey, which sells to whoever pays (29, 36, 44, 60). Kessmoor's engineers read "breath" as "an engine breathes too"; the reader learns it only at 69.
4. **The reader**: **Corvin**, with the oldest roll and the chain-words *(Book 2 ch. 85)*, an iron hand Kessmoor made him, and Callis beside him. The chain-ends on the Last Titan's legs still say *hold* *(Book 2 ch. 47)*: the Bridle is built to speak to them, and its central ring's inner face is cut to take the relic (48, one dim line).
5. **The smoke**: sleepwood resin bought through Ostrey (58), to open the Last Titan for binding (Magic §6). It's part of the approved fallback, on Rowan's word, so it hides nothing.
6. **The clock**: sealed orders, opened on the hour the relic is in reach (62).

**When** *(canon: the betrayal turns the near-victory into defeat)*: day two of the battle, at the moment the Lamp reaches the downed Last Titan for the relic (68–70). Kessmoor has to act before an elder holds it, because after that no one else can.

**What it breaks**: the Bridle's chains go over the Last Titan; heartstone bolts kill the Lamp; the smoke loosens every bond and every tamed beast on the river, hounds, greyharts, olifants, wyverns and dragons, so Orrun's shadows collapse and three sectors empty at once; Kessmoor's own sectors pull in to man the engine. The line opens in five places. The Bridle holds for one breath, then the Last Titan, chained again, rises past all rage and breaks the middle of the world. The Restoration's original sin, binding a Titan for power, is repeated by a kingdom that fought it *(Series proposal)*.

**Clues and reveal**: about five, spread out, see **Reveal rules**. The reader should suspect Kessmoor's appetite and still believe the Bridle is a fallback until 69. **Why nobody says anything:** Tobin does tell Rowan about Callis (33, 41), and Idony does report the Kessmoor hand (57); each time Pike has a reasonable answer, and Rowan, who needs the engines, accepts it. Her trust is part of the cost.

## Magic in the war, and the protagonists' untrained casting *(canon: in Book 3 they can use magic, but not as trained workers: strong, untaught, paying the toll in their own ways, Maren earth and stone through Orrun, Calder fire, sight and wind through his dragons. Proposal: how)*

**Two kinds of power, kept apart** *(proposal; follows Book 2 and `Magic.md` §2, §10)*:
- **Their casting is Book 2's words and runes**, said untaught with a beast's power behind them. They never learn a college's forms; they use the handful of workings they picked up on the road (Book 2's castings table), and now a Titan or three Titan-class dragons stand behind each one. The result is huge and crude: a hearth-word that fires a cave to the roof (2), a stand-word that stops a league of sliding bank (66), a herd-charm that turns a broken army north (75), a *hold* rune that keeps a hall's roof up (80). **Casting still obeys the Deep's rules**: it needs live ground (rule 5), it reaches what they touch (rule 4, though what they touch is now very large), running water breaks it, and runes need a hand that can cut them (Idony says the strokes, someone else cuts, 80).
- **Bond gifts are not casting** and need no Deep: Orrun's stone moving through Maren (1, 7, 40, 53), Kharrak's fire, Lisska's wind, Aurveth's deep sight (19, 28, 49). They work on dead ground, which is why in dead Hearthmere and on the Vale (the Last Titan's own ground) the gifts are what they have. After the gifts (37, 39) the gifts grow enormous; the words don't grow at all, only what's behind them.
- **No fine control.** Maren can drop a hillside and can't mend a cup. Calder can throw fire across a league and can't light a candle without scorching the table. Every working is the whole of what they mean, so restraint is the skill they have to learn (43, 53, 58).
- **Calder casts by talking.** He argues out loud with three dragons *(Characters.md)*, and in Book 3 the argument is the casting.
- **Maren casts by counting.** Paces, breaths, people. When she loses count (40, 77) she's in danger.
- **Three Book 2 workings come back** at the moments that matter: the stand-word at the line (66), the herd-charm on the road north (75, heard on the six roads, 77), and the *hold* rune in the hall's roof (80).

**The tolls, their own way** *(proposal)*: a bonded caster's toll falls on the beast first, then the person (Magic §2). Both of them choose to take it on themselves, to spare their beasts in battle.
- **Maren pays in feeling.** Numb hands, then feet; cold that won't leave; grey at the temple; finally she can't feel heat or cold in her hands at all (1, 7, 24, 37, 53, 59, 66). She fears becoming stone, the opposite of what her bond taught her. At the end, near-Titan, she sits on the snow all night and isn't cold (85–86): the toll has become what she is.
- **Calder pays three ways.** Fire burns his palms; sight bleeds his eyes and leaves him half-blind for hours; wind takes his breath (2, 19, 28, 49). He can't hold two at once without nosebleeds. At the gift (39) all three tolls at once nearly kill him.

**Orrun's shadows in Book 3** *(canon: one duplicate at a time by the end of Book 1; three to four by the end of Book 2; each duplicate as powerful as Orrun himself, at a toll to Orrun and Maren. Proposal: how far it grows)*:

**Everyone else's magic** *(Magic §8: magic tips fights; steel and beasts win them; at Titan scale, Titans win them)*:
- **Kessmoor's engines**: heartstone bolt-throwers, chilled chains, the Bridle. They hurt Titans, which makes them both necessary and dangerous.

## The land beyond the sea *(canon: the protagonists go there to find the other Titans; proposal: everything else)*

- **Which sea**: **the Shattered Sea**, on the drowned road. The Western Deep is Kessmoor's (Gearmouth's iron ships, the Book 2 rumour), and the far shore can be reached that way round. The way the Merrins know runs straight, from Saltreach's coast south-west over the Shatters, the broken piers of an old-realm causeway that ran past the Drowned Bell's temple *(Atlas: the Bell sank at the split)*. Its milestones count *into the light*.
- **What it's called**: Saltreach sailors say **the Lit Shore**; the light-script calls it *where the light is kept*. Neither is a kingdom's name.
- **What it is**: the land the old ones went to when they went into the light. A city of old-realm stone half under white sand; roads; no people for an age. Beasts that have never seen a person and aren't afraid. The Deep rises there in the open air as **light**, the shapes of light in every temple carving *(Book 2 ch. 23)*, and the elders are what's left of themselves inside it.
- **Its rule**: people who **sleep ashore** there don't wake. The light drinks a sleeper's breath (the toll, from the place's side). That's why it can't be a refuge, why the Far Berths are a death sentence, and why the remnant can't escape there, so the ending doesn't become a disguised victory. Idony, burned out, has nothing left to drink and is safe: a joke he enjoys. The bonded are safe while their beasts are awake beside them.
- **Who goes** *(canon rule: they don't travel together)*:
  - **Maren** by sea, on Kit Merrin's *Kittiwake*, with Orrun on far-stone ballast and Idony to read the light. Her task: find the old ones.
  - **Calder** by air, about three weeks later, over the Shatters with the Council's Berths fleet and at its pace, with Jory Merrin, met in Blackwater (26), piloting from Aurveth's back. The far shore can't be found from the air, only by the drowned road's milestones, which only a Merrin reads. His task: the fleet and his withering dragon, and his own question.
  - They meet there once, out of necessity, because the elders hear only two bonds together (34–35). Then **they come home by three roads**: Maren under the sea on Orrun's road (40), Calder over it in a day and a night, the day after the gift (42), Idony on the *Kittiwake* (44).
- **The old ones who didn't answer** (34) have gone out. Only the four are left, and by 70 only the Lady, and by 82 none.

## Corvin's death *(canon: the surviving twin dies in Book 3; proposal: how)*

- **Where he's been**: unseen from Book 2's end until 57. In the works at Ironhythe, as Kessmoor's reader, with Callis and an iron hand. Glimpsed once, hooded and unnamed (62).
- **His argument**: it's his last Book 2 position. Chains made the Titan a destroyer, so command is the only other way *(Book 2 ch. 71)*. In 57 Idony finally answers him, and Corvin isn't moved.
- **His death (74)**: on the Last Titan's back, at the relic, with Maren holding out her hand. She killed his brother, she says yes again, and she still offers. He takes the relic instead of the hand. Old magic isn't for people: it took his hand in Book 2, and now it takes all of him. He dies **for nothing**, the thing he never wanted Cassian's death to be. Callis, reaching for him, goes with him.
- **Redemption**: none (proposal). His one human moment is the hand he almost takes. **Callis** gets the late turn Corvin doesn't: he tells Tobin the orders (69) and helps him at the brake (71).
- **The Restoration ends before the world does** *(Series proposal)*: there's no Restoration army in Book 3 (it broke in Book 2, ch. 54 and 80). "One again" dies at the muster (46), when everyone learns how the one realm fell.
