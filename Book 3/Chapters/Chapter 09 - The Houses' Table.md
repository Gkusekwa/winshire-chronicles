---

The docks came out for him.

He hadn't expected it. He brought Kharrak down on the Tidehall's sea-steps at noon on the twenty-second day, as he had twice before, with Lisska on the copper roof and Ulric's grey on the step below, and the docks came out.

Not to look. To shout.

Dockers off the quays with their hooks on their shoulders. Fishwives from the market with their sleeves rolled up. Men and women off the hulks in the low harbour, in rags, wading ashore through the mud to see. They stood along the quays and on the bollards and on the roofs of the counting-houses and shouted his name.

*Fenn. Fenn. The farmer.*

He stood on the sea-steps and didn't know what to do with his hands.

---

"They love you," said Ulric.

Beside him on the step. Grinning.

"Why?"

"You brought down a house," said Ulric. "The Hallorans. You stood in that hall half a year ago with a mint-book and let the clerks read out who paid for the war, and the Hallorans fell. Then two months ago you stood in it again and told the Council the sea was coming, and the Council let four thousand drown on a spit rather than lose its oysters, and then a barley farmer flew three thousand five hundred of them off their roofs anyway." He looked at the quays. "The docks have never seen anyone do that to the houses. Not in their lives."

"I didn't do it to the houses."

"The docks think you did," said Ulric. "That's what counts on a dock."

---

A house clerk spat on the sea-steps as they went up.

A thin man in a good coat with a house's colours on his cuff, coming down the steps from the Tidehall's door. He stopped two steps above Calder and looked at him, and at the dragon on the step below, and spat. On the step. At Calder's feet.

Then he went on down.

The docks saw it. The shouting went up a note.

"And that's the houses," said Ulric.

---

He sent Lisska north the next morning.

From the copper roof. To look. At the six, wherever they were now. She went without arguing, which wasn't like her.

*I'll be four days,* she said. *Five.*

"Don't go close."

*I never go close,* said Lisska. *You're the one who goes close.*

---

The Council sat on the twenty-fourth day.

The great room. He knew it. The long black table under the smoke-darkened painted ceiling of ships, the tall windows over the harbour, the light coming off the water onto the ceiling so the painted ships seemed to move.

He knew it better now. He'd had Ulric tell him how it worked.

---

The long table was the Houses' Table.

A seat for every house with more than a set weight of ships on the water. The house with the most ships sat nearest the speaker's chair. The house with the least, furthest. A house that lost ships lost places. A house that lost too many lost its seat.

The Strand house's chair was three places further down the table than it had been two months ago. Four ships on the reef.

The Halloran chair was gone altogether. Taken out of the Tidehall after the fall, Ulric said, by dockers, and burned on the quay. Nobody had stopped them.

---

And along the wall by the door, a bench.

The Captains' Bench. A long oak bench, battered, with the names of dead captains cut into its back. The pirates and the privateers who held the sea-lanes and the channels sat there. Not at the table. On the bench. A captain won his seat by holding a stretch of coast, and lost it the same way.

They didn't sit still. They lounged on the bench and put their boots up on the table's end and talked to each other while the houses talked, and laughed, and spat.

And at the head of the table, in the speaker's chair, Mistress Orla Marrow.

---

She hadn't changed.

Blue silk. Salt-grey hair in its net. The square calm face like a ship's figurehead, giving nothing away.

She'd read his page aloud to this table. *The Council was told. I told it myself. It was oyster season.* In her own voice. In this room.

She watched him come up the length of the table and didn't move at all.

---

He laid the notice in front of her.

The Gaffer's. Torn off the post at Sallow Bar. Folded once. He unfolded it on the black wood in front of the speaker's chair, with the blue wave at its head and his name in the middle of it, and stood back.

"Here I am," he said.

The table looked at him.

"And there they are." He pointed. Through the tall window. At the sea-steps, where Kharrak lay with his chin on the bottom step and his tail in the harbour. "One of them. The green one's gone north. The old one's at my farm." He looked down the table. "Take them."

---

Nobody moved.

He hadn't thought they would. He stood there and let them not move. Down the length of the Houses' Table. Along the Bench.

Someone on the Bench laughed.

---

Aubrey Strand didn't laugh.

The soft-voiced man in green. Three places further down than he'd been. His voice was still soft. The face over it had gone hard since the reef, and stayed hard.

"The contract stands," he said. "The house of Strand is owed. Four ships. An execution paid for and not delivered. A captain." He didn't look at Calder. He looked at Marrow. "The Council put the contract up on the house's petition. It stands until it's met."

"Then meet it," said Calder.

Strand looked at him then.

"I intend to," he said.

---

"Not with the Bench, you won't."

A big voice. From the bench by the door. A big man with it, lounging, with his boots up on the table's end and a gold ring in one ear and a laugh in his face. Calder didn't know him.

"Hal Rennick," said Ulric, under his breath. "Holds the Saltings' channels. Nobody gets through the Saltings without his say."

"Nobody on the Bench will take that," said Rennick. Pleasantly. Pointing his chin at the notice on the table. "Ten thousand? Against forty dragons and three of them his?" He laughed. "Strand, you're cheap. Make it a hundred thousand and I'll think about it. I'll think about it for a long time. From very far away."

The Bench laughed.

Strand didn't.

---

"I've not come about the contract," said Calder.

The table went quiet.

"I've come about the low harbour."

---

He'd seen it from the air.

Inside the chain. Inside the two round towers at the harbour's mouth, behind the deep-water quays where the house ships tied up. The low harbour. Where the mud was. Where the old ships went to die.

Hulks. Dozens of them. Ships too old to sail, dragged up onto the mud at the harbour's back and left there on their sides, and people living in them. Whole families in a ship's hold. Washing on the rigging that was left. Smoke from fires on the decks. At low tide you could walk between them on the mud. At high tide they floated, a little, and leaned against each other.

Corsa's poor. Thousands of them. On the mud. Inside the chain.

---

"The one with the bell on its back is coming home," he said.

He told them how he knew. Plainly. Aurveth. The old one. Who'd felt it lying in the light in the mountains with its head turned toward its sea. Who'd felt it since.

"When the six come down out of the mountains," he said, "it'll come to the sea. To this sea. It slept off Gullhaven for longer than Corsa's been a city. It'll come back to the water." He looked down the table. "I don't know where. I don't know when. But when it comes, it comes with a wave, and you've all seen what that does. Or heard."

He looked at Marrow.

"Empty the low harbour," he said. "Before it comes. Get them off the mud. Get them inland."

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

Then a house at the top of the table did. A woman in grey with the Ostrey house's mark on her collar. Calm.

"It's herring season," she said.

---

He'd known she would say it.

He'd known somebody would. He'd almost said it for them, in his head, walking up the table. Two months ago it was oysters. This year it was herring.

"The low harbour's where the herring's gutted," said the Ostrey woman. Reasonably. "The hulk-folk are the gutters. Two thousand of them. The season's six weeks. If you empty the low harbour in herring season, you lose the season, and the houses lose the season's price, and the hulk-folk lose the season's wages, which is the only money most of them see in a year." She looked at him. "Who pays that, Master Fenn?"

"Who pays the wave?"

"No one," said the Ostrey woman. "It's not on any contract."

---

Marrow asked, in the end.

As she had two months ago. Calmly. *Does any member move that the Council order the low harbour emptied before the herring's in?*

She looked down the table. At the houses. At the Bench.

Nobody moved.

The houses didn't look at each other. They didn't need to. They'd closed ranks since the Hallorans fell. Ulric had told him that too. Anything that touched one house's money touched all of them, and they voted as one, and this touched all of them.

The Bench didn't move either. Rennick looked at his boots on the table's end and grinned at nothing.

"Then the Council does not so order," said Marrow.

---

She caught him afterwards.

Not on the sea-steps. In a passage at the back of the Tidehall, behind the great room, where the clerks went in and out with their papers. She was standing by a window in her blue silk looking at the harbour when he came through, and she didn't look round.

"Master Fenn."

He stopped.

"I read your page," said Marrow. "Aloud. At that table. All of it."

"I know. Thank you."

"Don't thank me," said Marrow. "It cost me two votes at the next sitting and a window in my house." She turned her head and looked at him. "Somebody put a brick through it. With your name on the brick."

---

"Then empty the low harbour," said Calder.

"I can't."

"You're the speaker."

"I'm the speaker because both ends of that table are afraid of me," said Marrow. "They're afraid of me because I've never asked them for anything they wouldn't give." She looked back at the harbour. At the hulks on the mud, leaning. "The day I ask them to give up a herring season for a barley farmer's dragon's feeling, they stop being afraid of me."

"Then they drown."

Marrow was quiet.

"Bring me a contract," she said. "A contract I can pass. Something that costs the houses less than the season. Then I'll put it to the table."

She looked at him once more.

"And don't make me read another of your pages," she said. "I haven't got another window."

---

The gull-hawks came in all day.

He watched them from the quay on the twenty-fifth. Grey and white and hook-beaked, all appetite, coming in over the harbour mouth from every direction to the harbour-master's perch on his roof. Each with a tube on its leg.

The Council's news. From every harbour on the coast.

The harbour-master's boy took the tubes off as they landed and ran them up to the Tidehall.

---

He asked the boy where they came from.

The boy pointed, one by one, at the birds on the perch. That one, Blackwater. That one, Wendmouth. That one, a ship out on the Saltings, Captain Rennick's. That one, a ship out of Gearmouth, round the Western capes, a month at sea.

"How does it find its way?"

The boy looked at him as if he were simple.

"It knows the ship," he said. "A gull-hawk always knows the ship. That's the only thing it knows." He looked at the bird. "Lose your gull-hawks and nobody can find you."

Calder watched one go up off the perch with a tube on its leg and turn out over the harbour mouth and go straight, east, along the coast, without circling. As if it could see where it was going from here.

---

Nell Tallis was on the wyvern quay.

He found her on the twenty-sixth. Ulric took him. A short quay at the harbour's eastern end, away from the house ships, where the sellsword companies tied up and kept their beasts. Iron rings in the stone. Straw. The smell.

Five wyverns. Hooded. On chains to the rings. Black-green, barbed tails curled round their feet, their heads under the hoods turning at every sound.

And a board on a post.

*For hire. Any contract. Ask for Tallis.*

---

She was on a barrel by the board.

Lean. Freckled. The top of one ear burned off long ago. In a wyvern-rider's leather coat with the company's mark on the shoulder, a grey glove on a black field. She was mending a strap with an awl and didn't look up until he was standing in front of her.

Then she did.

"Master Fenn," she said.

"Nell Tallis."

"The same." She went back to the strap. "I gave your page to Marrow myself. Into her hand. She read it out the next sitting." She pulled the awl through. "I heard it. From the Bench. I'd never heard the houses so quiet."

---

There was a man on the quay already.

When Calder came. A house factor, in green, with the Strand house's mark on his cuff. Calder had seen him at the edge of the quay, talking to her, with a paper. He stood there now, a few paces off, looking at Calder.

"Master Strand's factor," said Nell. Without looking up. "With a contract. For your three." She held up the strap to the light. "The house wants the company to take it. Ten thousand. The company's the only one on the coast with wyverns that's flown against dragons." She lowered the strap. "Or near them."

"And?" said Calder.

---

"And no," said Nell Tallis.

She said it to the factor. Not to Calder.

"I told him already. I'll tell him again, since you're here, so you can both hear it." She put the awl down. "The company took the Clan's coin. At Gullhaven. For the children on the sheds. Arn Varr paid it, out of the Clan's purse, into the captain's hand." She looked at the factor. "The captain's rule. The company doesn't take a contract against the coin's payer. Not while the coin's still warm."

"The captain's dead," said the factor.

"The rule isn't," said Nell.

---

The factor went away.

Nell watched him go up the quay. Then she picked up the strap again.

"I'll fly for anyone else," she said. "Anyone who can pay. That's the company. That's what it's for." She looked at Calder. "Can you pay?"

"No."

"No," said Nell. "That's your trouble, Master Fenn. Everyone on this coast loves you, and nobody on this coast can sell you anything."

---

Lisska came back at dusk on the twenty-eighth day.

Thin. Tired. Out of the north, over the hills behind the city, low, onto the copper roof of the Tidehall where she'd gone up from. She lay there on the green copper with her wings out and her sides going.

He went up on the roof.

He sat down beside her and put his hand on her neck where the plates met.

---

She gave it to him.

The Plains. From high up. Grey, dead. And the black band of the burning across them, wider than before, further west. Near the Wyldmarch's western edge now. Near the river.

And on it, five shapes. Together. Moving west, slowly. Slower than before.

And one more.

Apart. Far apart. Already leagues off from the others, south and west of them, alone. Dragging itself. A back like a reef with the temple on it. Across the dead country. South-west. Away from the others.

Toward the sea.

---

*They've split,* said Lisska.

He opened his eyes. They didn't bleed this time. She'd not gone as close.

*The one with the bell has left the others. It turned away from them three days ago, Aurveth says. I asked her as I came over. It's going south.* She lifted her head off the copper and looked at him. *To its water. Like she said.*

"Where?"

*I don't know,* said Lisska. *The sea's a long way from where it is. It's slow on land. Slower than you'd think.* She put her head down again. *But it's coming.*

---

He sat on the Tidehall's roof with his hand on her neck.

Below him, in the dusk, the low harbour. The hulks on the mud. The fires on their decks going one by one. Two thousand people in the herring season. Inside the chain.

The quays were empty. The docks had gone home.

On the wyvern quay at the harbour's end, a woman on a barrel was mending a strap by a lantern, and beside her a board on a post said *For hire. Any contract*.

He looked at it for a long time.
