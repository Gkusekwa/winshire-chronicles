---

She fell asleep on the hundred-and-twenty-seventh.

She didn't know she had. She was sitting against the milestone at the road's edge with her hand on the stone, in the dawn, after four nights awake, watching the great grey shape on the hill-top watch her lion. And then the sun was high and hot on her face, and she was lying on her side in the grass, and it was noon.

She sat up so fast her head swam.

She was breathing. She put her hand on her chest, the right side, where she could feel it. Her heart going.

Alive.

---

Orrun was lying where he'd been. Ten paces from the great paws. His head up. Looking at her.

*You slept,* he said.

"I didn't mean to."

*I know.* He didn't look away. *I didn't sleep. I watched you. The whole time.*

She looked at him.

*The light came,* said Orrun. *In the dawn. Round you. Close. I felt it come.* He was quiet a moment. *It looked at you. And at me, awake. And went away.*

---

She sat in the grass a long time.

*If you sleep ashore over there, I don't know what happens.* Kit, on the beach. *They left him with his eyes open.*

She'd slept ashore. Four hours, five. With the light round her. And woken.

Because he'd been awake. Beside her. Watching.

She didn't know why that should matter to the light. She knew it did. She'd felt it, through him, as he told her: the light coming close, and finding her not alone in herself, but tied to something that was looking back at it. Awake. And going.

---

After that, she slept in watches.

An hour. Two. In the grass by the milestone, with her back against it, while he kept awake ten paces from his mother. Then she'd wake, and sit up, and keep awake herself, and he'd put his head down on his paws and close his eyes. Not for long. He'd never needed much sleep. An hour, and he'd lift his head again.

They didn't talk about it. They kept the watch for each other.

---

Her name came on the hundred-and-twenty-ninth.

Not a word. The great grey shape didn't use words. She gave Orrun things, slowly, over days, and Orrun passed them on: a feeling, a picture, a weight. And one of them, on the fourth day, came through into Maren's head as a name.

The shape had another name. Maren knew that. That name was light and stone and older than speech, and nothing in her could hold it. What Orrun gave her was its shape. And the shape came out in her own tongue, in her own head, as a name she'd heard before.

*The Grey Mother.*

---

Hesper had said it. At the Hollow Ring, a lifetime ago, Keeper Hesper with her duskcat, telling Maren what the Wyldmarch's clans called the thing they prayed to when the ground shook. Tova had said it, at the Ysse fords, under her breath, when the ridge came up out of the turf. Every chapel in Hearthmere had a hearthstone at its heart that the old women called by it, on their knees, when the lamps still burned.

The Grey Mother.

Maren sat by the milestone and looked at the shape on the hill-top. Grey as the Ruin's arch. As the milestones. As Orrun.

*She's my mother,* said Orrun.

He didn't say it as news. He said it as a thing he'd always known and had only now remembered.

---

She showed them on the hundred-and-thirtieth.

At noon. Maren knew it was coming. She felt it coming through Orrun all morning, a gathering, as weather gathers over the downs before the rain. The great shape on the hill-top stirred. The light round her rose. And she lifted her head from her paws.

And lowered it.

Slowly. Down. Across the ten paces. Until her vast grey face, the size of a house's gable, was close above Orrun's head, and her gold eyes, the size of cartwheels, were looking down at him from no distance at all.

Orrun lifted his head and put it against her paw.

Maren put her hand on his shoulder.

And the Grey Mother showed them.

---

*A land. Green to its edges.*

She was in it. Not seeing it. In it. As you are in a dream you know is a dream, and are in it anyway.

Green hills. Green valleys. A sky without smoke. And across all of it, from the edge of sight to the edge of sight, roads. Grey roads, of the same grey stone, straight, with milestones every league, crossing rivers on bridges of grey stone that stood up out of the water on piers.

And on the roads, people.

And beasts. Walking with them. Among them. Not on chains. Not on leads. Walking. A woman on a road with a great grey cat padding beside her, its shoulder at her shoulder, the two of them talking. A boy on a bridge with a bird on his wrist that wasn't hooded. Lions on the steps of a tower, and people going up the steps past them and bowing, and the lions bowing back.

She'd seen the lions. Aurveth had shown the farmer, and he'd said so in a letter. She'd never seen them bow back.

---

*And the old ones.*

Shapes too big to see whole. Of every kind. Grey, and gold, and pale, and dark. Walking the land among the people. Lying on the hills. And the people coming to them, and sitting with them.

Being taught.

She saw it. An old one lying on a hillside, vast, and a ring of people in front of it, and between them, on the air, marks. Hooked marks. Square and spare, with little hooks at the ends of the strokes. Drawn in light. And the people copying them into the dust with sticks. Into clay. Into stone.

*So we could talk,* came through Orrun. Not words. A meaning, that came out as words in her head. *So the small ones could talk with the beasts. With us. We gave them our tongue. Set down. So they could keep it.*

The hooked script.

The milestones. The arch. The chains.

---

*And then iron.*

The picture changed. Darkened. A forge. A man at the forge, with a hammer, and on the anvil, iron, and on the iron, the hooked marks. Not cut into stone. Struck into iron. Into a ring of iron.

A collar.

On a beast. A great grey cat, like the one on the road. Standing very still. With a collar round its neck with marks on it, and its eyes empty. Nobody behind them.

Then another. Then many. Beasts on the roads with collars on, walking behind people, not beside them. Silent.

Then chain.

---

She'd known it would be chain.

Link after link. Each as long as a man. Struck on the inside of the curve with the same word, over and over, where the thing inside the chain would never see it.

*Hold.*

And inside the chain, a Titan.

Under a hill. In the dark. Chained. Its breath going out of it, through the chain, up through the hill into a city on the hill, where lamps burned without oil, and wells ran sweet, and people said words and the words worked, and nobody asked where it came from.

Maren had trained under that hill. She'd sworn the oath at noon facing its wall.

---

*And then they rose.*

Fire. She couldn't see it all. Nobody could have. The chained one, and the others, the ones who'd felt it chained, rising. The land breaking. Green going black. A road cracking from end to end and the sea coming in over it. Piers standing up out of water where there'd been land. Ice. A bell, ringing under the sea.

The split.

It went on a long time. She was in it. She couldn't get out. Orrun's shoulder under her hand was shaking.

---

*And then the sleeping.*

The old ones. Coming together. Many of them, in light, on a plain of black ground under a black sky. Gathering round the six, one at a time. Laying them down. Under hills. Under the sea. Under a lake, a mountain, a stair, a ring of stones. Six. And with each, a stone, glowing, set into the ground over it, to hold its sleep.

And a seventh stone, over them all.

And with each one laid down, an old one went thin.

---

She saw it happen. An old one, vast, gold, giving: bending over the sleeping shape under the stone, pouring itself into it. And going thin as it poured. Pale. Light coming through it. Until it wasn't there. Only light, rising off the black ground, going up.

Into the light.

Then the next. Then the next.

Six laid down. And the plain behind them, where the old ones had gathered, many, so many she couldn't count them, was nearly empty. A few left. Thin. Pale. Going away west.

*It cost us,* came through Orrun. *Our bodies. We had nothing else to give. We gave them.*

---

*And then a door.*

A river. She knew the river before she knew she knew it. The Wend. Wide, brown, slow, with an island in it. And on the island, a door. Grey stone. An arch.

And in front of the arch, set down on the stones by something vast and grey and thin, already going pale, a lion.

Young. Half-grown. Thin. Grey. With a mane only beginning, and too-big paws, and eyes looking up at the vast grey thing that had set him there. Not afraid. Waiting to be told.

And she told him.

Not in pictures. The one thing in all of it that came in words. Because Orrun had it in words. He'd always had it. He'd kept it three hundred years. It came up out of him through Maren's hand on his shoulder in her own tongue, as the verse under the arch had it, as the knights had said it every noon of their lives facing the wall.

***Keep the watch.***

---

The picture went.

She was on the hill-top. In the sun. Her hand on Orrun's shoulder, shaking. Her face wet on the side she could feel.

The Grey Mother's great head was still lowered over him. Her eyes on him. Orrun's head against her paw. His eyes shut.

That had been him. On the island. The young lion with the too-big paws.

His mother had set him there.

And gone into the light, and he'd kept the watch three hundred years, alone, until a student with a practice sword came across the river.

---

She understood the rest before anyone told her.

She sat on the grey rock with her hand on him and understood it.

The one realm hadn't fallen because the Titans were wicked. It had fallen because its people had taken the tongue they'd been given to talk with, and struck it into iron, and put it round the necks of the beasts they'd been taught to talk to. Then round a Titan's. Then lit their lamps on what bled out of it.

That was what the Restoration did. What Corvin did. What the crown had done, under Greyhold, for three hundred years, and called it the gods' gift.

The one realm had fallen for doing exactly that.

And it was being done again.

---

She asked.

She didn't know how. She thought it at Orrun, as hard as she could, and he passed it on. *Will you come back? Will you put them to sleep again? There are six of them awake. Will you come?*

The Grey Mother's eyes moved. Off Orrun. Onto her.

For the first time.

Gold. Old. Very tired. They looked at her a long time.

The answer came through Orrun in words. His words for it. Slow.

***We closed the door once.***

A pause, as long as the light's breath.

***We have nothing left but light.***

---

She looked at Orrun for four days.

The Grey Mother did. Maren watched her do it. She'd lowered her head again after the showing, onto her paws, but not all the way. Close to Orrun. And she looked at him. All day. All night. With the light rising round them both.

On the hundred-and-thirty-second she licked him.

Once. Along his whole length. A great grey tongue, slow, from his nose to the end of his tail, that went over him as a wind goes over stone, flattening his mane, and Orrun lay under it with his eyes shut and didn't move.

---

Maren felt it.

Through the bond. She couldn't not. What he felt, lying against his mother's paw with his eyes shut, being looked at. Being licked. Being a cub.

Being somebody's.

He'd been hers since the island. She'd been his. But he'd been alone three hundred years before that, keeping a door, and she'd never once felt in him what she felt now. A thing so warm and so old and so whole that she couldn't stand in it.

She had to look away.

She got up and walked to the edge of the hill-top and stood there with her back to them, looking at the green hills and the grey road going down them toward the sea, until it passed.

---

On the hundred-and-thirty-second she found the white.

In the morning. She'd woken from an hour's sleep against the milestone, with Orrun awake, and pushed her hair out of her face with her right hand. And there was a lock of it, over her left ear, that came into her fingers white.

Not grey. White. Like an old woman's.

She looked at it a long time. It was still hers. She could feel it pull at her scalp when she tugged it.

She left it.

---

The old man came on the hundred-and-thirty-fifth.

At dusk. Up the road. She saw him from the hill-top, a long way down, a small grey horse coming up the paving between the green hills with a small grey man on its back, wrapped in a blanket, slowly, as the light began to rise out of the grass round him.

She went down to meet him.

He saw her coming and his face lit.

"Maren!"

"Kit let you come?"

"Kit," said Idony, "had no say in it. I'm a witness." He looked past her. Up the hill. At the vast grey shape lying on its top in the rising light, with the smaller grey shape against its paw. He stopped. The horse stopped under him.

He looked for a long time.

"I've come to read," he said at last. Very quietly. "Is there anything to read?"

"Yes," said Maren.
