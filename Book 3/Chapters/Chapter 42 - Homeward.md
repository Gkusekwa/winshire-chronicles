---

He could stand on the hundred-and-forty-ninth.

Not well. He stood up off the paving at the crossroads at dawn with Jory's arm under his and the scars on his chest pulling like a shirt sewn on too tight, and stood there, and didn't fall over.

That would do.

"Home," he said. Cracked. To the three of them.

*Yes,* said Aurveth. *Now.*

---

Kharrak's neck was wider than a road.

He hadn't thought about how he'd get up. He'd climbed Kharrak's foreleg since the mill, a step and a pull and a swing. Now the foreleg was a cliff.

Kharrak put his head down on the paving.

Calder walked up it. Up his nose, between his eyes, onto his neck, as you'd walk up a hillside. And lay down on the neck behind the head, where he'd always lain, and found it was like lying on a warm red hill.

*Hold on,* said Kharrak.

"To what?"

*Me,* said Kharrak. *You always have.*

---

Jory went on Aurveth.

He walked up her foreleg too, slowly, looking at the bronze under his boots as if it might crack, and lay along her neck behind her head with his long brown hands flat on her plates.

"She's warm," he said.

"She always was."

"Not like this," said Jory.

---

They went up off the crossroads at dawn.

All three at once. Lisska first, out of the square in a single beat that flattened the grass for a quarter-league, up into the morning. Then Kharrak, heavy, the paving cracking under him as he went. Then Aurveth.

Both wings. Straight. Spread over the green country like a roof.

He looked back once at the crossroads. Four roads. Three milestones standing, one a pool of grey slag. His copper on the south one.

Then they were too high to see it.

---

They went straight.

North-east. Over the far shore's green hills. Over the hall's ridge, a grey line of columns, empty. Over the lagoon. Over the shelf and the Needle and the last pier, standing whole, with its birds.

And out over the Shatters.

---

Lisska had said it couldn't be found from the air.

*It's not there. Anything. From up here.* Every way the same, she'd said. The stones sticking up far apart like birds' rocks. No shore. No light by day.

It was there now.

Aurveth could see it. Through the sea. He saw it through her, lying on Kharrak's neck: down through the blue-black water, the drowned road, running north-east under the waves in a long grey line, straight, with the piers standing up out of it a day apart. Each one a cold grey shape going up through the water to the air. Like the posts of a fence across a flooded field.

They didn't need to find it. They could see it.

---

Jory called the stones anyway.

He couldn't help it. Calder could hear him across the air, from Aurveth's neck, thin, shouting the numbers into the wind as each pier came up out of the sea below them and went under.

"*One!*" A pier, whole, at the shelf's edge.

"*Two!*"

"*Three!*"

Counting up. Toward home.

"*Eleven!*" And then, quieter, Calder thought: "*Kit's mark.*"

---

*Forty-one* came at noon.

The first pier. The last, from here. Standing up out of the sea at the Shatters' edge, broken at twice a mast's height, white with birds.

A month's sailing for the *Kittiwake*. Half a day for them.

Jory stopped calling. Calder looked across at him. He was lying along Aurveth's neck with his face on her bronze, and his shoulders were going.

Calder looked away.

---

The *Kittiwake* had been below them in the early morning.

Early. Between the third pier and the fourth. A day out from the Needle. Low in the water. So low he thought at first she was sinking. Two boats astern on lines. And her deck black with people.

The ninety-three.

---

Lisska went down first.

Of course she did. Out of the sky, in a long curve, low, over the ketch's mast. Ninety-three people on the deck looked up and saw a green dragon the size of a hall coming down out of the sky at them, and screamed.

He heard it from the air.

---

Kit didn't scream.

He could see her. At the tiller. In the stern. A small figure in a sailor's coat, with her hand on the tiller and her face turned up, watching the green dragon go over her mast. She didn't move. She didn't let go of the tiller.

When Kharrak came over, lower, slower, the size of a hill, she raised one hand.

---

And at the mainmast, waving both arms, an old man in a blanket.

Idony. Jumping. He was jumping, on the deck, in his blanket, with both arms over his head, waving them, as a boy waves at a fair.

He was shouting something. Calder couldn't hear what.

He couldn't shout back. He had no voice to shout with.

---

*Tell him,* he said to Lisska.

*Tell him what?*

*That we're going home. That the lion's coming under the sea. That I'll see him at the river.*

Lisska went down again. Low over the deck, slowly this time, so that the ninety-three didn't scream as much, and looked at the old man as she went over.

She couldn't tell him. Not in words. Nobody heard her but Calder, and the beasts. The old man had no beast.

She told him anyway. Calder felt her do it. Looking at him, all of it in her face.

The old man stopped jumping. He stood at the mainmast with his arms down and looked up at her, and then he nodded. As if he'd heard.

---

Gannet was at the foot of the mainmast.

Beside the old man. Coiling a line. Looking up.

He wasn't smiling.

Calder didn't know the man. He'd seen him in the hall, a thick Wendmouth face between the columns with a board on his knee. He saw him now from the air, looking up at a dragon the size of a hill going over the mast, with no smile on his face at all, and then looking down again at the line in his hands, and going on coiling it.

---

Aurveth looked back at noon.

From *forty-one*. Back the way they'd come, a long way, to the first piers under the shelf. He felt her look.

Through the sea. Through the sea-bed. Into the stone.

*There,* she said.

---

He saw it through her.

Under the piers. Under the sea-bed. In the stone, down there in the dark, along the line of the drowned road.

Something warm.

Grey. The size of a hill. Walking. Slowly. North-east. Inside the stone, along the road's line, under the piers, with the stone in front of it opening and behind it closing, as a seam opens and is stitched shut behind the needle. A grey warmth, going home through the bottom of the sea.

With something small on its back.

*The lion,* said Aurveth. *On his road home.*

---

And far off, out in the black water beyond the stone, something pale.

*Something,* said Aurveth. *Gone.*

It might have been the Lady. He watched the lion through her for a long time instead, walking home under the sea.

The knight on his back, small, asleep.

---

He didn't sit up most of the flight.

He couldn't. The scars pulled. He lay along Kharrak's neck on his side with his face on the warm red plates and his hand under his shirt on the three ridges over his heart. His nose bled twice. He let it. His breath was short. He breathed what he could.

*You're bleeding on me,* said Kharrak.

"Sorry."

*Don't be,* said Kharrak. *I've had worse on me.*

---

The coast came up in the afternoon.

Saltreach. Long and low and grey-green. He knew it from the air now better than he knew his own fields.

Empty.

He saw that before he saw anything else. The Saltings' salt-villages with no smoke. The salt-flats with nobody on them. A spit going out into the sea, where something had happened, he could see it, the grass flattened and wreckage on the shingle above the tide-line. He didn't know what.

Corsa.

The harbour brown. The towers broken. The low harbour bare mud. And on the hill behind the city, on the ropewalk terrace, forty-one ships on their cradles.

Empty. Every one. No washing. No smoke.

---

He looked at it a long time.

*They've gone,* said Lisska. *Inland. Somebody took them.*

"Arn," said Calder. "Arn did."

He hoped it was Arn. He thought it was. He looked west, along the coast, to where the Wend came down past Wendmouth into the sea. A long way up it, on the river's bank, smoke. A great deal of smoke. A camp, the size of a city.

He didn't go there.

Home first. Wren first.

---

They crossed the Marches at dusk.

High. Over the Saltreach bank, over the Way, over farms he knew. East of the camp. A long way east. He saw its fires come on along the river as the light went, thousands, going away up the bank.

And something else. From the camp, faintly, a long way off, so faint he only heard it through Lisska's ears.

Hounds. Howling.

---

The Scarfell came up in the dark.

The moor. The tarns, black. And at the end of everything, the black water of Coldtarn in its bowl of rock, and below its rim, a little way down, a light.

A lantern. At a cave mouth.

---

They were too big for the yard.

He'd known. He hadn't thought about it. The hatching caves' yard was a flat of trodden turf before the cave mouth with the burn running across it and the hatching bowls inside, and the old one had always had to lie half out of it with her tail on the moor.

Kharrak came down on the moor above the cave mouth.

His tail lay across the burn. His chin lay on the crag above the cave. Aurveth came down on the tarn's shore, on the black stone where they'd poured, and folded her straight wings. Lisska lay on the crag's top, the size of the crag.

None of them could have got into the yard if it had been ten times the size.

---

Ada was at the cave mouth.

With the lantern. In her shawl. Looking up.

Up. At a red hill with its chin on the crag above her. At a bronze one on the shore of the tarn. At a green one on the crag's top, looking down at her sideways.

She didn't say anything.

She held the lantern up. As if to see better. As if it would help.

---

And past her, running, a girl.

Out of the cave. Past Ada. In her nightgown, with her plait coming undone, barefoot on the cold turf. Across the yard. To the foot of the red hill. Where a man was coming down off its nose, slowly, one hand on the plates, walking down a dragon's face as if it were a hillside.

She stopped at the bottom.

He stopped at the bottom.

---

"Papa," said Wren.

He went down on his knees. It hurt. The scars. He did it anyway.

She came into his arms. He held her. She was bigger. She'd grown. He'd been gone fifty days, since Coldtarn, and she'd grown.

"You're bleeding," said Wren. Into his coat.

"A bit."

"Your voice is wrong."

"Yes."

"Say something," said Wren. "Say it twice."

---

He told her everything.

That night. In the cave, by the warm spring, with the hatching bowls round them and Ada in her place by the fire and Bel in hers. Jory asleep already by the spring, curled up, as if he'd never sleep anywhere else. And three dragons outside, the size of hills, with their heads at the cave mouth looking in.

He told it in his cracked voice. All of it.

And she made him say everything twice.

---

The far shore.

"Say it again."

"A shore. At the end of the sea. With white sand. And a city under the sand. And deer that walk up to you."

"Say it *again*."

---

The light on the hills at night. The milestones with no numbers. The road under the sea.

The lion.

"As big as a hill," he said.

"Say it again."

"As big as a hill. Bigger."

She looked out of the cave mouth at Kharrak's chin on the crag.

"As big as *him*?"

"Yes," said Calder.

She thought about that.

---

The gods.

He didn't know how to say that one. He said it anyway. Twice.

"The gods are what's left of Titans that stopped."

"Say it again."

"The gods are what's left of Titans that stopped."

Ada, by the fire, looked up.

She didn't say anything. She'd sung a song all his life about the old ones going up into the light. She looked at him a long time. Then she looked back at the fire.

---

The storm.

That one Lisska told, through him, putting her head in at the cave mouth: *I made a storm. Out of nothing. On a clear morning. And I threw it.*

"Say it again," said Wren. To Lisska.

*I made a storm,* said Lisska. Delighted. *And I threw it.*

---

He gave her the present last.

He'd nearly forgotten. He put his hand into his coat for it, past the letter in charcoal with the D the right way round, past Marrow's copper with the ship-and-wave worn almost off, and found it.

He held it out.

---

A stone. The size of her fist.

Grey. Glassy on one side, as if it had been melted and poured and set. Swirled. And on the swirled face, if you looked, if you knew, the shapes of letters run together. Hooked. Square and spare. Gone soft at the edges, like letters written on a frosted window and breathed on.

Faintly warm.

Humming, a little, if you held it. She held it.

---

"It's from the end of the world," said Calder.

Wren looked at it. Then at him.

"What is it?"

"It was a milestone. At a crossroads. On the far shore. Kharrak melted it." He touched the swirled face with his scarred finger. "It said *into the light*."

"Say it twice."

"*Into the light,*" said Calder. "Into the light."

---

She held it in both hands.

On her knees, in her nightgown, by the warm spring, with her plait undone.

"It's warm," she said.

"Yes."

"It's humming."

"Yes."

She held it against her chest. She didn't say anything else for a long time. Then she said, "You brought it," as if he'd done something nobody had ever done, and he supposed he had.

---

Ada put her hand on his chest.

Later. When Wren was asleep with the stone in her fist and Bel had gone to her bed. Ada got up from her place by the fire and came and stood in front of him where he sat by the spring, and put her hand flat on his shirt.

Over the scars.

She'd never done that. Not since he was a child. She held her hand there, and he felt her feel them, the three ridges over his heart, bronze and rust and green.

She didn't say anything.

She took her hand away and went back to the fire.

She didn't sing. He hadn't thought she would.

---

Outside, Aurveth was awake.

He felt her. On the black stone at the tarn's edge. Lying with her straight wings folded and her new eyes open, looking down through the dark. Through the moor. Through the mountain. Through the whole of Winshire under her, all the way to the sea.

*He's still walking,* she said. *Under. The lion.*

*Good,* said Calder.

*And west,* said Aurveth. *Across the river. In the Mere. Something burning.*

He didn't ask her what. He was too tired.

He slept by the spring, with his hand on his chest.
