---

Tova had said eighteen days. The clans walked it in seventeen.

He didn't. He waited at the Wend with the air. Rowan wanted it that way. The pairs could make the Thornwood in a day of flying. The clans couldn't make it in less than seventeen, with the stonehorns and the old people and the carts. So the clans went first, on the two-hundred-and-eighth, at dawn, along the east road, a column three leagues long.

And the lion went with them.

He'd watched it from the ridge, sitting on Kharrak's foreleg with Wren beside him. A grey hill, walking east along the road at the column's head. Slowly. A man's pace. Putting its great feet down one after another with the care of a big dog walking among puppies.

*He's walking short*, said Aurveth.

*Yes.*

*So they can keep up.*

*Yes.*

Wren had watched it out of sight.

---

The air went on the two-hundred-and-twenty-fourth.

He kissed Wren on the ridge in the dark and gave her to Ada and climbed up Kharrak's nose. Arn's forty pairs went up off the camp's western edge round them, and the four rocs off the eastern ridge, and the stormkites like thrown grey threads, and Nell's wyverns on their chains, hooded, behind the riders.

The three could have done it in a morning. They flew at the pairs' pace. All day. Eastward, over the Marches, past the Scarfell's southern end, over Saltreach's empty scrub and moor, high, and people on the roads below ducked.

They came down at dusk.

---

The clans' camp was on the open downs a league west of the Thornwood.

Fires. Ten thousand people. Stonehorns lying in the grass with their plated heads down. The Keepers' wolves in a ring round the Keepers' fires, and the bears, which he hadn't known the Keepers had, sitting up among them like old men.

And at the camp's eastern edge, the lion. Lying down. Facing the wood.

He didn't go to her.

He sent the clerk. A boy called Hobb, Davin's second, sixteen and pimpled, whom Rowan had sent with the air to carry words between the two ends of the line so that the two ends of it needn't talk. Hobb ran through the camp in the dark and came back.

"She says she's ready," said Hobb. "She says dawn."

"Tell her yes," said Calder.

Hobb ran.

---

He looked at the Thornwood.

He'd heard about it all his life. Everybody in the Marches had. The great wood. Oak and thorn and ash for twenty leagues, so thick at its heart that the Keepers said the light at noon there was green. The Wyldmarch's heart. Older than the kingdoms.

It was grey.

All of it. As far as he could see in the dusk, from the downs to the edge of the sky. Grey sticks. Bare. Dead, and standing. Every tree in it, dead on its feet, grey as bone.

And at its heart, leagues in, a thing that moved.

---

*There*, said Aurveth.

He looked through her.

A crown. That was what he saw first. Rising out of the grey wood a long way in. Branches. A forest on top of the forest, bigger, wider, a quarter of a league across from tip to tip. He thought it was trees.

Then it turned its head.

---

It was a stag.

A stag the size of a hill. Lying in the wood with its legs folded under it, grey-brown, mossed down its flanks as an old stone is mossed. Its head up. And on its head, the antlers. Branching and branching. Each tine as long as a tower, and from each tine more. A crown like a wood.

The Antlered.

---

And round it, the trees walked.

He saw that last. He didn't believe it until he'd seen it a long time. All round the Antlered, for a league on every side, the dead trees were moving. Slowly. Pulled up by their roots, standing on their roots as on legs, grey, and walking. Round it. As cattle walk round a bull.

Oak. Thorn. Ash. A league deep of them.

*They hurt*, said Aurveth.

He knew she was right. He didn't know how she knew.

---

They went up at dawn.

The stormkites first. Aud Hallin's six. He'd thought they'd be Wardens, on great birds. They weren't. Two girls of fifteen and four slight old women, light as children, in grey felt, on long thin grey birds with wings like a heron's and tails like a swallow's, and they went up off the downs before the sun like smoke going up a chimney.

They didn't fly at the wood. They flew over it. High. Circling. Slowly. As kites circle over a field.

And they felt it.

---

Aud Hallin had told him at the fire. In her short way. *The kites feel weather. They feel the Deep's tides the same. A day off, sometimes. That* (the stag) *is weather. When it's going to turn, they'll feel it before it does.*

They did.

He was on Kharrak's neck a hundred breaths into the morning, over the wood's western edge, when he heard the first horn. A bone horn. One note, short, from a stormkite high over the wood.

And a hundred breaths later the Antlered turned its head left.

Two notes for right. One long for *coming*. The kite-riders blew them down to the clans on the downs all day, and the clans moved before the wood did.

---

Then they burned it.

---

*Now?* said Kharrak.

*Now.*

He felt it come up through the red. Through the bond. Out of the mark on his chest that was a scar now. Up through the great neck under him. He put his scarred palms flat on the plates. He didn't need to. He did it anyway.

Kharrak opened his mouth.

---

White.

Not red. Not gold. He'd never get used to it. White, as the sun's white when you look at it by mistake. A river of it, a quarter-league long, going down out of Kharrak's mouth onto the walking edge of the wood.

Dead wood burns like paper.

It went up. All of it. A line of fire the length of a town along the wood's western edge, and the heat came back up at them so hard that Kharrak went up a hundred feet on it without moving his wings.

*Oh*, said Kharrak. *Oh, yes.*

---

And the trees kept walking.

He saw that. Through the smoke. The burning trees. Every one of them on fire from root to crown, and still walking. Out of the wood. Onto the downs. Toward the clans.

Burning. Walking. A wall of burning trees coming west at a walking pace across the open grass, toward ten thousand people and their stonehorns.

---

*Lisska*, he said.

She was already there.

---

He felt her go. Off to the north, small and green against the smoke, and then not small. She went along the burning line low and fast, and her wind came off her wings, and the fire turned.

Like a crowd turns. All at once. The flames that had been leaning west toward the downs leaned east. Back into the wood. The burning trees leaned with them. The wind pushed them over, back, east, into the trees behind them, and those caught.

*There*, said Lisska. *Easy.*

---

It wasn't easy.

It went on all day. Kharrak burned a line and the burning trees walked out of it, and Lisska turned the fire back into the wood, and more trees walked out, and Lisska turned it again. Over and over. All morning. All afternoon.

The heat came up through Kharrak's neck. Into his hands. His palms had been scarred pink for a year. By noon they were blistered again. By the afternoon the blisters had broken and he could feel the plates under the skin, hot, wet.

He kept them there.

---

The rocs did the rest.

He hadn't known what Yeva meant at the fire, when she said the rocs would bring stones. He saw now. Four great rust-and-white birds coming in from the east, from the Worldsend's scree, a day's flight, with rocks in their feet the size of carts.

They let them go over the wood.

He watched one go down. Turning a little. Into the walking trees, where fire hadn't stopped them. An oak fifty feet tall, burning at the crown and still walking, and the rock came down on it, and it went to splinters. And the one behind it. And the one behind that.

The rocs went back east for more.

---

Below, on the ground, the Keepers went in.

He saw them from the air. Little. Green. Thirty Keepers, and their wolves and their bears, going into the wood's edge where it had burned, between the black stumps, at a run. Driving things out.

There were beasts in the Thornwood still. Maddened. Boar. Elk. Bears gone wild. Wolves gone wrong, grey, thin, with their eyes white. Everything that had lived in the wood and hadn't died when it died, and couldn't get out from among the walking trees.

The Keepers drove them out. Onto the downs. Where the clans' stonehorns took them and turned them north, away.

That was hers. Her part. Maren's.

---

He looked for her once.

He didn't mean to. He looked down at the wood's western edge, through the smoke, and the lion was there. A grey hill. Lying. Facing the wood. Not moving.

And the clans going round it. Ten thousand people and their beasts, moving left at one horn and right at two, streaming round the lion as water streams round a rock in a river.

He looked away.

---

*Farmer*, said Aurveth, on the second day. *Come up.*

He came up. On Kharrak. High. Higher than the kites. Into thin cold air, with the wood a grey map underneath and the smoke going off it east in a long brown banner.

Aurveth was over the Antlered.

Circling. Slowly. Old bronze wings full out. Her head down. Looking.

*I'm looking for it*, she said.

---

He looked with her.

Through her. Down. Into the stag. As he'd looked into the Last Titan from the Scarfell. As she'd shown him the coal in its back.

Grey-brown. Moss. The great flanks. The legs folded. Inside them, the bones, like a hill's ribs, grey. Inside those, slow red shapes. The lungs. The gut. All of it hot. All of it drinking.

He looked for the heart in its chest.

It wasn't there.

---

*No*, said Aurveth. *Higher.*

Up. Up the neck. Into the head. Into the bone of the skull, thick as a castle wall.

And there. Where the antlers came out of the skull. Where the two great roots of the crown grew up out of the bone between its ears, each root as broad as a hall.

Between them. Deep. In the bone.

Red. Slow. Hot. The size of a barn. Beating.

*There*, said Aurveth. *Under the crown. It's hiding it in the antlers.*

---

He held his breath.

The crown. A quarter-league of branches. Each tine as long as a tower. You couldn't get fire through that. You couldn't get a rock through it. You couldn't get a wolf through it, or a bear, or a stonehorn, or a man.

*How does she get to it?* he said.

*I don't know*, said Aurveth. *I only see.*

---

He sent the word down.

By Lisska. She was the one who could.

She went down off the smoke to the wood's western edge. Low. Over the clans. Over the lion. Into his sight. And the lion lifted its head.

Kin-speech. Only in sight. Lisska couldn't talk to Orrun from the clouds. She had to be where he could see her.

He felt her do it. Through the green. A long low call, with words in it, that he heard only half of.

*Under the crown. Where it grows. In the bone. She saw it.*

And the lion's voice, deep, slow, stone settling, that he heard less than half of.

*Yes.*

Lisska came back up.

*He'll tell her*, she said.

He didn't ask what she'd say.

---

The Antlered got up on the second afternoon.

The long horn, from every kite at once. *Coming.*

He was on Kharrak over the wood's southern side. He saw it. The crown rising. Higher. Higher. Out of the grey wood, the antlers going up into the smoke like a forest lifting off the ground. Then the head. Then the neck. Then the stag standing, the size of a hill, a hill standing up, and the ground shaking so hard that he saw the clans on the downs a league off go down on their knees.

It turned west.

And walked.

---

And the wood came with it.

All of it. A league of walking trees on every side, coming west with it at a walking pace, out of the burned edge, across the black stumps, onto the downs. Toward the clans. Toward the fires. Toward the lion.

*Burn it*, he said.

Kharrak burned a line across its front a league long.

---

It didn't stop. It walked into the fire. Its legs, mossed and grey, went into the white fire and came out black and kept walking. The trees round it went up like torches and kept walking. Lisska turned the fire onto them, back, east, into the trees behind, and they burned, and kept walking.

The rocs dropped their rocks into its crown. They broke tines. Great branches of antler as long as towers came down out of the crown, turning slowly, into the wood. The crown didn't care. There was so much of it.

The clans fell back. A league. The lion fell back with them. Walking backwards. Facing it.

At dusk the Antlered lay down again on the downs' eastern edge, a league closer than it had been, with its burned wood round it, and its crown smoking.

---

They lost Bryn Tolle on the first day.

He hadn't known him. A Clan rider. Young. Red-thread. On a young green no bigger than a horse. He'd gone low over the walking edge after Kharrak's first burn, to look, and a walking oak had reached up.

It didn't do it fast. That was what he couldn't stop seeing. It reached up slowly, as a man reaches for an apple, and its grey branches closed round the green and the rider together, and pulled them down into the wood.

They didn't come up.

---

A woman's brown on the third day. The woman lived. The brown didn't. They found her on the downs at dusk, sitting in the grass, alone, with her hand on her chest where the mark was, not crying.

---

The third day was the Keepers.

---

It was dusk. The Antlered lying down again, the wood round it. The Keepers had gone in at noon on the south side, deep, further than they'd ever gone, because there were beasts in there still. Elk. Boar. A she-bear with two cubs. Things that couldn't get out.

The Keepers went in after them.

They drove them out. Most of them. Out through the black stumps onto the downs, the elk and the boar and the bear with her cubs running in front of the wolves.

And behind the Keepers, the wood closed.

---

He saw it from Kharrak. The trees walking in behind them. Slowly. Grey. From both sides, as a door closes. Between the Keepers and the downs.

Thirty Keepers. Their wolves. Their bears. In a long green line, running back west toward the downs between the burning stumps.

And the trees leaning in over them.

---

*Lisska*, he said.

*I know.*

He didn't wait for Kharrak to put him down. He went across. From the red to the green, in the air. He'd never done it. He did it. Down Kharrak's shoulder and off it, onto Lisska's neck, as she came up under him. A hill-sized green neck, coming up under his feet like the deck of a ship.

He held on.

*Now*, he said.

---

She went down.

Over the Keepers. Low. Along the line of their running. Her wings full out, from one side of the running green line to the other, and her wind coming off them.

And he held it.

---

He'd never done it before.

She'd made wind. She always had. He'd felt it, through her, all his life with her. This was different. This was him. Through her. Holding it. Putting it up over the running line like a roof, and keeping it there.

A wall of wind. Standing. Over the Keepers.

And the trees leaned on it.

---

They couldn't come down. That was what it did. The walking trees, grey, leaning in from both sides, their branches reaching down for the running green line, and the wind between, holding them up. Like a man holding a door shut with his back.

He felt them lean. Every one. On him.

He held it for the length of a breath.

Then another.

---

The toll came on the second breath.

His breath. It was always his breath, with Lisska. Since the Shatters. Since the sea. Every time a little more. He'd thought he knew it.

He didn't.

It went in. Into his chest. Not like a hand, this time. Like sand. As if somebody had poured sand into him. Into a sack. Filling. Down at the bottom first, and up, and up.

He couldn't breathe out.

---

*Let go*, said Lisska. *Let go now.*

He held.

The Keepers ran.

A third breath. A fourth. The sand at the top of his chest. In his throat.

He held.

---

They came out.

The first of them. Out from under the wind, onto the open downs. Running. Wolves first. Then Keepers. Then bears, lumbering, and Keepers on the bears. Coming out of the end of the wind-wall into the open grass.

Twenty. Twenty-two. Twenty-five.

And then not.

---

At the back of the line, eleven. Under the wind still.

Walking. Slowly. Two of them carrying a third between them. An old woman, in green, her legs gone. And the others with them. Waiting. Not leaving her.

Eleven. Under the wind. In the dark, with the trees leaning.

And he couldn't hold it much longer.

---

Tamsin went past him.

---

He didn't see her come. He heard her. Through Lisska. An old voice. Old green-grey, three hundred and twelve years old.

*I've done this before*, said Tamsin.

And she went down under the wind.

---

Gunna Tarne on her neck. Grey hair flying. No cloth over her mouth this time. Tamsin's grey-green wings with the burn-scars on them from the resin at the Roosts, white in the firelight. Under the wind-wall. Down. Among the leaning trees. To the eleven.

She landed among them.

He saw it. Through Lisska, under him. The old dragon putting her shoulder down among the Keepers, in the dark, under the trees, and the Keepers climbing up. All of them. The old woman lifted up by the other two.

Eleven on her back.

Again.

---

She got up.

Slowly. She was old. She'd been burned. She got up off the ground with eleven people on her back and Gunna on her neck and went up under his wind, and up, and out over the top of the wind-wall, over the trees leaning on it.

And down. Onto the open downs. Low. Her shoulder almost on the grass.

And let them go.

Eleven Keepers rolling off her back onto the downs, into the grass, and the old woman with them, and the clans running out from the fires to them.

---

Then she turned back.

He didn't know why. He'd never know. For the wolves, he thought, after. There were wolves still under the wind. Four of them. That had stayed with the old woman.

Tamsin turned back over the wind-wall for the wolves.

And he couldn't hold it.

---

It went out of him slowly, as sand runs out of a torn sack. The wall of wind thinned. The trees leaned.

And came down.

---

On Tamsin.

On the old grey-green dragon going down over the wind-wall for four wolves. The grey branches closing over her, slowly. Her wings. Her neck. Gunna on her neck.

Gunna didn't get off. He saw that. She could have. Tamsin had put her shoulder down. Gunna put her hands on Tamsin's neck instead, flat, and her head down on them, and stayed.

The trees closed.

---

He felt Tamsin go.

Through Lisska. Like a lamp blown out in the next room. The light under the door, and then not.

One life between two.

Both.

---

On the downs, a long way off, a dragon screamed.

Red-gold. Small. Ildra. On the grass by the Clan's fires, with Arn on her neck, screaming, at the wood, and Arn with his face in her neck.

---

He didn't remember Lisska bringing him down.

He remembered the grass. On his back. A blanket, somebody's, under him, and the stars, and the smoke going over them. He remembered trying to breathe out, and not being able to, and Lisska's head beside him in the dark the size of a barn, and her breath on him, warm.

*Little*, said Lisska. *Little at a time.*

He breathed. A little. Out. In. A little.

As if his chest were full of sand.

---

It was full of sand for a day.

He lay on the blanket on the downs. He couldn't speak. Not even cracked. Nothing. His palms were open and somebody wrapped them in wet cloth. His nose had bled once on the second day, from the deep sight, and somebody had wiped it. Hobb sat by him and gave him water a sip at a time.

Arn came once. At midnight. He stood over the blanket with Ildra behind him. He didn't say anything.

Calder couldn't.

Arn stood there a long time. Then he put his hand on Calder's shoulder, once, and went away.

---

Four Keepers dead. Eleven wolves. Two bears. A wyvern. Bryn Tolle and his green. A brown.

Gunna Tarne and Tamsin.

He counted them on the blanket in the dark. He'd never counted things before.

---

He was awake at dawn.

He couldn't sleep. He lay with his head turned to the east, on the blanket, breathing a little at a time, and watched the wood.

The Antlered was still there. Lying. A league in. With its crown smoking against the grey of the morning.

And at the wood's edge, the lion.

---

He watched it get up.

Slowly. The grey hill rising. Standing at the wood's edge with the clans drawn back behind it on the downs, all of them, ten thousand, in silence.

Then it lay down again.

And sank.

---

Into the ground.

He didn't understand it at first. The lion lying down at the wood's edge. And going down. As a stone goes down into a pond. Slowly. The earth closing over its flanks. Its back. Its shoulders. Its mane. The ground closing over it like water, without a sound.

And it was gone.

There was grass where it had been.

---

He lay on the blanket and stared.

Then, a league in, among the walking trees, under the Antlered's feet, the forest floor moved.

It heaved. Up. As a sea heaves. The burned black ground and the roots of the walking trees and the stumps, all lifting together, in a long swell, and the trees on it tipping over. And splitting. A long crack in the ground opening in the middle of the swell, black, and widening.

And out of it, grey. Mane-first.

Rising up out of the ground among the walking trees, under the Antlered, with the earth falling off his back like water, the lion.
