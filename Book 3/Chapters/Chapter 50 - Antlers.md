---

For three days she sat on his head and listened.

The horns. One for left. Two for right. A long one for *coming*. They came down out of the smoke all day from the grey birds high over the wood, and she heard the clans move round her before she saw them. Ten thousand people and their beasts, and the stonehorns, going left at one note and right at two, round Orrun where he lay at the wood's edge, as a flock parts round a cairn.

She didn't move. Neither did he.

It wasn't time yet.

---

She watched the fire.

White. A river of it out of the red one's mouth, a quarter-league long, onto the walking edge. The trees going up like paper and walking on, burning, toward her. And the green one's wind coming along the line and turning them back into the wood.

All day. Over and over.

She watched the rocs bring their rocks. She watched the Keepers go in on foot between the black stumps and come out driving things. She watched a young green no bigger than a horse go down over the wood to look, and a walking oak reach up for it, slowly, and pull it down.

She didn't feel any of it.

She watched.

---

On the second day the green one came down.

Low. Over the clans. Out of the smoke, the size of a crag, and straight at them, and the clans ducked, and Orrun lifted his head.

The green one's voice came through him. Long. Low. With words in it that she heard only through Orrun, as she heard everything now.

*Under the crown. Where it grows. In the bone. She saw it.*

*Yes*, said Orrun.

She thought about it. The crown. A quarter-league of antlers, each tine a tower. The heart under it. Between the roots, in the bone.

*Tell her*, she said. *Then we'll pull its head down.*

Orrun told her. The green one went back up into the smoke.

---

On the third day, at dusk, she watched the old dragon go under the wind.

She saw it from the edge. The green one low over the wood with the farmer on her neck, and a wall of wind over the Keepers' line, and the trees leaning on it. And an old grey-green dragon going down under the wind among the trees, and coming up again with people on her back, and putting them down on the downs.

And going back.

And the trees closing.

---

*The old green one's gone*, said Orrun. Very quietly. *And her girl.*

She knew who it was. Gunna Tarne. She'd handed the Fenns to her once, at the Scarfell's edge, a long time ago. Tamsin, who'd called Orrun *the cat on the steps*.

She didn't feel it.

She knew she should. She sat on his head in the dusk with her good hand in his mane and knew exactly what she should feel, and where, and how much.

Nothing came.

---

They planned it at the fire that night.

Tova. Keeper Rhun, broad and grey, with a wolf at each knee, who had known Hesper. Tova's two grandsons, who'd take the stonehorns in. Hobb, the clerk's boy, sitting a little apart, writing it down for the farmer, who was lying on a blanket on the far side of the camp and couldn't speak.

Maren didn't ask how he was. Hobb told her anyway.

"He can't breathe out," said Hobb. "Not properly. He can't say anything. He wrote it on my board." He held the board out. *Like sand.*

She nodded.

---

"There are beasts under it still," said Rhun. "In the wood. Round its feet. Elk. Boar. Bears that went wrong. A white hart, my wolves say." She put her hand on the wolf at her right knee. "If you open the ground under it, they go down with it."

"Then get them out first," said Maren.

"At dawn," said Rhun. "My bears. The wolves. Tova's stonehorns behind, to turn them."

Tova nodded slowly.

"And then?" said Tova.

"Then he goes under," said Maren.

---

"Under?"

"Under the ground," said Maren. "He can. It's all stone, under. It moves for him." She looked at the fire. "He'll go down here, at the edge, and come up under it. In the middle of the walking wood. Where nothing can get at him from the side."

"And you?"

"I'll be here."

Tova looked at her.

"On the ground," said Maren. "At the edge. With my hands on it." She looked at her one good hand on her knee. "I don't need to be on him. I'll be in him. And the others."

"The others," said Tova.

"Three," said Maren. "Four of him. One for each side of its head."

---

Tova didn't ask anything else.

She put a hand on Maren's shoulder, on the cold side, and left it there a moment, and Maren saw her take it away again and look at her palm.

---

The drive went in at first light.

She watched from the edge. On her knees, already, in the burned grass, with her good hand flat on the ground and her dead one in her coat.

Rhun's bears first. Eight of them, great brown shapes going in along the burned south side at a lumbering run. Then the wolves, grey, low, a hundred, fanning out among the stumps. Then the stonehorns, two hundred, with Tova's grandsons on the leaders, their plated heads down, their hooves cracking the black ground.

Into the walking wood.

---

The horns came down from the sky. One. Two. One.

She heard the beasts before she saw them. Something crashing. Then out of the trees at a run, a herd of elk, thirty, forty, their eyes white, their antlers small and thin and ordinary. Boar, after them, squealing. Three bears, not the Keepers', gone wrong, grey in the coat, thin, coming out on their hind legs and dropping and running.

The Keepers' wolves at their heels. The stonehorns turning them north, away, across the downs.

And last, at the third hour, alone, out of the grey trees into the burned grass, white as milk, a hart.

It stopped at the edge. It looked at her. At the woman kneeling in the grass with her hand on the ground.

Then it ran.

---

"That's the last," said Rhun. Behind her. "My wolves say."

"Yes," said Maren.

She looked up at Orrun.

---

*Now?*

*Now*, she said. *Go under. Come up under it.*

---

He lay down.

She felt him do it. Through the ground under her hand. The great weight of him settling. His chin on his paws. And then going down.

Into the ground. As a man goes down into deep snow. Slowly. The earth taking him. Opening under him without a crack, without a sound, as water opens, and closing over his flanks, his back, his shoulders. His mane. His eyes, last, pale, looking at her.

Then grass.

---

And she went with him.

---

She didn't close her eyes. She didn't need to. She was kneeling in the burned grass at the wood's edge with her hand on the ground and Tova standing over her and Rhun's wolves lying down round her in a ring. She could see all that.

And she was in the stone.

With him. Going east. Through the ground under the Wyldmarch. Through it, as a fish goes through water. Rock and root and old stone, and all of it moving for him, opening ahead and closing behind, and the weight of the wood above.

And the others.

---

Three.

She felt them come. As she'd felt them come on the far shore. Out of him, as breath comes out of a body. Three more of him, each as big as he, in the stone round him.

Four lions. Under the wood. In the dark.

And she was in all four.

She could feel each one. Separate. Four weights. Four bodies going through the stone. Four sets of eyes, seeing nothing. And her own, at the edge, seeing grass.

Four. She let the number go. She was them.

---

She felt it above.

The Antlered. Lying in the walking wood a league in. Its weight on the ground. Hot. Drinking. Every root round it drinking for it. The walking trees on their roots round it, each one hurting.

*It hurts*, said Orrun. Four voices. One.

*I know.*

*Here.*

*Yes.*

---

They came up.

---

She saw it four ways at once.

From the north. From the south. From the east and the west. The forest floor heaving up round the Antlered in four great swells, as a sea heaves. The walking trees on it tipping over, sliding. The ground splitting. And out of each split, mane-first, grey, rising up into the morning with the earth running off them like water:

Four lions. Each the size of a hill. Round it.

And from the edge, a league off, through her own eyes: four grey hills standing up out of the wood where there'd been none.

---

The Antlered got up.

Fast. Faster than she'd known it could. The crown going up off the ground, the head, the neck, the whole hill of it rising, and the wood round it surging in.

The walking trees came at the lions. All of them. From every side. Grey branches reaching. Slowly.

The lions didn't fight them.

They went for the crown.

---

Not the throat. Not the legs. The crown.

She'd thought it through at the fire, all night. You couldn't get at the heart through the antlers. No fire through them. No rock. No jaw.

So you used them.

---

Each lion took a tine.

The great roots of the crown. Where the antlers came out of the skull, two on each side, broad as halls, and the first tines off them, each as thick as a tower. Four lions. Four jaws. Each closing on a tine near its root. Stone teeth on antler.

And pulled.

---

Down.

As you'd pull down a stag caught in a thicket by its horns. As she'd seen it done once, as a girl, in the Brackenwood, with her father and two foresters and a rope, a stag caught in the brambles by its antlers, wild-eyed, and three men pulling its head down to the ground so they could cut it free.

Four lions, the size of hills, pulling the Antlered's head down by its own crown.

---

It fought.

She'd known it would. She felt it in all four jaws at once. The neck like a hill's spine, bending against them. The crown tangled in itself, tines locking on tines, the whole quarter-league of it a thicket now, caught. The head coming down.

Slowly.

It pulled back. All four lions slid on the ground toward it. She dug in. Four sets of claws. Into the stone. The stone held them. It was hers. It was his.

The head came down.

---

Its forefoot came up.

She saw it from the south lion's eyes, and from Orrun's. The great foreleg, mossed and burned black from Kharrak's fire, lifting. And coming down.

On Orrun's foreleg.

---

She felt it.

She felt him feel it. Through the bond. The weight of a hill coming down on his leg. The bone of it in his shoulder, groaning. Stone grinding on stone.

*Hold*, she said.

*Holding.*

He didn't let go of the tine.

---

The head came down to the ground.

All the way. The great skull on the burned forest floor, with the crown tangled above it and four lions dragging on it and the neck bent like a bow.

Down. Held.

*Now*, said Orrun. Four voices. *Maren. Now.*

---

She opened the earth.

From the edge. A league off. On her knees in the burned grass, with her hand flat on the ground. She didn't do anything different. She'd done it all her life with him. Under Greyhold. At the Ruin. At the Saltings, with a cliff.

She pulled.

Not small. Not a wall.

Everything.

---

The ground under its head opened.

A split. Under the skull. Black. A quarter-league long, from one side of the crown to the other, opening in the forest floor as a mouth opens.

And the head went down into it.

The four lions pulled it down. Into the split. The skull. The great bone of the brow. Down, and down, into the ground, until the split was full of skull, up to the antlers' roots, and the crown lay across the top of it like a fallen wood.

There. Under the crown. In the bone.

---

Then she closed it.

---

The stone of the Wyldmarch closed.

From both sides. On the skull. On the bone between the antler roots. Where the heart was.

She felt it close. Through her hand. Through four lions. Through the ground for a league round. The stone coming together, slowly, as a hand closes. Patient. The whole weight of the country.

On the heart.

---

It didn't scream.

She'd thought it would. She'd braced for it in four bodies. It didn't.

It stopped.

---

Everything stopped.

The great body on the forest floor went still. The neck. The legs. The forefoot on Orrun's leg went slack, and heavy, and he pulled his leg out from under it.

And the walking trees fell down.

All of them. At once. Every tree in the walking wood, for a league round, on every side. As a crowd sits when the king sits. They didn't topple. They didn't crash. They lay down. Every oak and thorn and ash, all together, onto the ground, with a long soft sound like a sigh going out of a forest.

The Thornwood lay flat.

---

And she felt it go.

---

*Don't let me feel it go*, he'd said.

She'd said she'd try.

She felt it coming. Out of the Antlered. Through the ground. Through the four lions' jaws on the crown. Its going. Its life, or whatever it had in place of life, going out of it.

Into him.

He'd feel it. All of it. He was kin. He'd feel his kin go.

---

So she took it.

She didn't know how. She did it anyway. She pulled it through the bond. Through him. Past him. Into her. Because she couldn't feel, and he could.

It went into her.

Like cold into cold.

She felt it come in, and lie down in her, somewhere under her ribs on the grey side, and go still. She didn't feel what it was. She felt only that it was there. As you feel a stone in your pocket.

---

*That was mine*, said Orrun.

Very quietly. Four voices. Then three. Then two. Then one.

*I know.*

*You took it.*

*Some.*

He was quiet.

*Not all*, he said. *I felt some.*

*I'm sorry.*

*No*, said Orrun. *It's all right. It's stopped. It wanted to stop.*

---

And under her hand, she felt the other thing.

---

The breath.

Going out of the dead Titan into the ground. She felt it go. Through her hand, through the stone. A warmth, leaving the great still body under the fallen crown.

Down. Into the ground. Into the Deep.

And then up. Rising in the Deep, a long way down. As warm water rises.

And then running.

West.

Like water finding a slope. Away under the Wyldmarch. Under Saltreach's empty moor. Under the Marches, the Wend. Under the burned Downs, the whole country. Running west through the Deep, a long warm thread of it, faster than a horse.

Toward Hearthmere.

---

She didn't understand it.

She knelt on the ground and felt it go west under her hand, and didn't understand it at all.

*Where's it going?* she said.

*Home*, said Orrun. Slowly. *I think. Down. Home.*

She thought so too.

---

The cheering started.

She didn't hear it at first. She was still in four bodies, in a fallen wood, with a dead Titan's crown across them. And one body on its knees at the edge, with a hand on the ground.

Then the shadows went.

Back into him. One. Two. Three. Like breath. And she was in two bodies. Then one.

Her own.

And she heard it.

---

Ten thousand people on the downs.

Shouting. All of them. The clans, the herd-men, the old people on the carts. The Keepers, on their knees in the burned grass with their wolves howling round them. The riders, on the downs and in the air, Arn's pairs going up off the grass and round and round over the wood, the dragons roaring.

On and on. It didn't stop. It went on and on, over the downs and the flat grey fallen wood, under the smoke.

The first Titan killed.

---

She knelt in it.

With her hand still flat on the ground.

---

"Knight."

Tova's voice. Tova's hand on her shoulder. On the cold side. And then off it, quickly, as if it had touched iron in frost.

"Knight. You can stop."

---

She looked up.

The sky was red.

---

It was dusk.

She didn't understand that either. She looked at the sky. Red in the west, going down behind the downs. The smoke going up into it. The first stars in the east, over the fallen wood.

It had been the third hour. The white hart. Rhun's voice. *That's the last.* The third hour of the morning. A moment ago.

It was dusk.

---

"How long," she said.

"All day," said Tova. Quietly. "Since the third hour. You've knelt there all day, knight. It fell at noon." She looked at Maren's hand on the ground. "We cheered all afternoon. You didn't move."

---

She tried to count it.

The day. The hours of it. She couldn't. There was nothing to count. The third hour, and then dusk, and nothing between. As if someone had cut the day out of her with a knife and pressed the edges together.

She'd lost it.

A whole day. Gone. She'd never have it.

She took her hand off the ground at last. The grass under it was white.

---

Orrun came back along the ground in the dusk.

Out of the fallen wood. Walking. Slowly. On three legs, mostly. The great left foreleg held up a little, and put down carefully, and lifted again.

He lay down beside her at the edge. His chin on his paws.

*It's broken?* she said.

*No. Hurt.* He put his nose down near her. *A week. Maybe.*

She put her good hand on his nose.

---

The dragons came at dark.

Not Arn's. The three. Off the downs where they'd lain all afternoon with the farmer on his blanket between Kharrak's forelegs. Up. Over the fallen wood. Red. Bronze. Green. Out over the dead Titan where it lay under its crown.

Circling. Low. Slowly. Round and round.

And crying.

---

She'd never heard it.

Not from them. Not from any dragon. A long high call. All three. Over and over. Thinner than a roar, and higher than the sound they made over a pour. Like wind in a split tree.

Round and round over the dead Antlered in the dark, crying.

---

*Why are they doing that?* she said.

Orrun lifted his head. He watched them a long time.

*They don't know why*, he said.

She looked at him.

*Do you?*

He didn't answer.

He put his chin back on his paws and closed his pale eyes, and she sat beside him in the dark at the edge of a fallen wood with a stone she couldn't feel lying under her ribs, and listened to three dragons cry over the first dead Titan, and tried to count the stars, and kept losing them.
