---

Rowan wanted the river watched.

Not the Vale. The Vale was the line, and the line had ninety-eight thousand people on it, and the knight's walls, and the lion lying on the eastern one like a hill. The river below the line was nobody's. Ninety leagues of the lower Wend between the hub and the sea, with the Saltreach bank on one side and nothing on the other but dead Hearthmere fields.

The Bell was at the mouth. Everybody knew that. Kit's gull-hawk had said so on the two-hundred-and-thirty-ninth. *Whole sea's white.*

So from the two-hundred-and-seventy-eighth he flew the river.

---

On Lisska, mostly. She liked it. Down the Wend in the morning, low, over the grey water, to the mouth and back. Ninety leagues each way. Kharrak and Aurveth on the ridge above the hub, resting. Lisska didn't need resting.

*I like rivers*, said Lisska. *You can see where they're going.*

---

On the two-hundred-and-eighty-first, at dawn, she smelled it.

---

*Honey*, said Lisska.

He didn't smell anything. Frost. River. The cold.

*Burnt honey*, said Lisska. Her voice had gone strange. Low. *Like the Roosts.*

He went cold.

---

The barge was under the Saltreach bank, a few leagues below the hub.

Poling upriver. Slowly. A flat grain-barge, broad, low in the water, the kind that came up from Wendmouth with Saltreach wheat when there'd been Saltreach wheat. Grain-sacks piled on its deck under tarred canvas. Four men on the poles. No pennant on its mast. There'd been one; he could see the pale place on the mast where the ties had been.

Lisska came down on the river beside it.

The four men dropped their poles.

---

The bargee was a fat man called Ambry Coyle.

From Wendmouth. Frightened. Honest, Calder thought, after a while: frightened as honest men are, who don't know what they've done. He stood on his deck with a dragon's head the size of a barn beside him and his hands up and said everything at once.

"It's paid for. It's papered. I've a waybill. Kessmoor's. I'm only the carrier, master. Ostrey hired the barge. I'm only the carrier."

"Show me," said Calder.

---

Under the grain-sacks, casks.

Forty. Squat, iron-hooped, the bungs sealed with pitch. The smell came up out of the hold when Coyle's men pulled the canvas back. Even he could smell it then. Sweet. Thick. Burnt. Like honey left too long on a fire.

He'd smelled it at the Roosts. In the smoke over the ledges. Tamsin's head low, her eyes half gone, a wet cloth over her nostrils.

---

The waybill was Kessmoor's.

Printed. A form. With the blanks filled in, in a clerk's hand. He couldn't read all of it. He could read enough.

*Resin, sleepwood. Forty casks.*

*For the hub batteries.*

And a line he read twice, slowly, with his finger, because he wanted to be sure.

*To open the Titan for the fallback.*

At the bottom, a mark. Square. Red ink. *G. Tolley, for the Assembly.*

---

"Where'd it come from?"

"Ostrey's yard," said Coyle. "In Wendmouth. They load, I carry. A silver a cask." He swallowed. "It's papered, master. It's for the Accord. It says so."

It did say so.

He sat on the casks with the waybill in his scarred hand and the honey-smell coming up round him, and didn't know what to do.

---

He took it to the hub.

The barge and Coyle and the forty casks, up the river under Lisska's eye, two days at a pole's pace. And the waybill, folded in his coat.

---

The council sat on the two-hundred-and-eighty-third.

In the hay-barn. He stood at the back, by the ladder, as he always did. When Rowan looked at him he came forward to the door's end and put the waybill on it.

"Forty casks," he said. "Sleepwood. Off an Ostrey barge on the river. Bound for the batteries."

---

Pike picked it up.

She read it with her spectacles on her nose. All of it. Slowly. Then she put it down.

"Yes," she said.

---

"The Accord's warrant says sixty," said Rowan. "Bought through Blackwater. In the autumn. Delivered." She looked at Davin. Davin nodded; he had the warrant in his case; he had everything in his case. "It doesn't say a hundred."

"It's the same order, Marshal," said Pike. "A second delivery. We judged sixty wouldn't be enough to cover a Titan of that size if the wind was wrong. So the Assembly ordered forty more, through Ostrey's yard, by barge, because the Blackwater road is very slow in winter." She took her spectacles off. "Engineer Tolley was hasty. He should have brought it to you for the warrant first. I'll see the warrant is amended."

"He should have," said Rowan.

"He should," said Pike. "I'm sorry."

---

Calder spoke.

He hadn't meant to. His voice came out of him cracked, at the door's end, louder than he wanted.

"It's not the Titan," he said.

They looked at him.

---

"The smoke," he said. "It won't open the Titan. Not only the Titan." He put his scarred hands on the door. "It opens everything. Every beast it reaches. On the wind."

He'd seen it. They hadn't. Some of them had heard. He said it anyway.

"At the Roosts," he said, "the binders burned it on the ledges to open the young ones. It went up the cliff on the wind. It opened every dragon on every ledge. Not just the ones they wanted. All of them. Tamsin went down into it with a wet sheet over her nose and it opened her anyway. Lisska came loose from me." He looked down the table. "Like a boat off its rope."

---

Nobody said anything.

"If that goes up on the Vale," he said, "it doesn't stop at the eddy. It goes where the wind goes. Every hound on the line. Every greyhart. The olifants. Nell's wyverns. The Clan's dragons." He looked at Arn. "Mine."

He didn't look at the knight. She wasn't there. She was on the eastern wall with the lion.

"Hers," he said.

---

"The wet cloths work," said Arn. Slowly. "For a dragon. Over the nostrils. Gunna showed us." He looked at the table. "For a wyvern, maybe."

"Nobody can cloth a thousand hounds in a minute," said Calder. "Or a greyhart. Or an olifant." He breathed. "Or him."

Nobody asked who.

---

Rowan was quiet a long time.

"The smoke goes up on my word," she said. "Only. It's minuted. It always was."

"I know," said Calder.

"Then here's more." She looked at Pike. "Every cask. Sealed. My seal, on the bung, over the pitch. The hundred. Kept in the batteries' magazine on the Ferry Flat. Under a Hearthmere guard, as well as Kessmoor's. Day and night." She turned to Davin. "Write it."

Davin wrote it.

"Agreed," said Pike.

---

He went out of the barn into the cold.

He didn't feel any better. He didn't know why. Rowan had said everything he'd wanted her to say. Pike had agreed to it. The casks would sit in the magazine with Rowan's seal on them and two guards at the door, one grey and one Hearthmere, and nobody would light them unless Rowan said, and Rowan wouldn't say.

He stood in Liss's yard and looked down at the Ferry Flat. The twelve iron beds in the frost. The six posts on the eddy's edge. The batteries' crews round their braziers.

*You smell of it*, said Lisska, from the ridge.

*I know.*

*I don't like it on you.*

---

The Bell came up on the two-hundred-and-eighty-fifth.

---

Kit's gull-hawk came first. Before dawn. To the hay-barn's roof. A grey-and-white bird with a slip on its leg in Kit's quick bad hand.

*Sea's going white. All along the mouth. It's moving.*

He was on Lisska before the light.

---

He saw it from far up the river.

The mouth of the Wend. Where the river went into the Shattered Sea between two low headlands, and Wendmouth stood on the eastern one on its stilts. Beyond it, the sea.

White.

From the headlands out. Three leagues of it. White water, as if a storm were standing on the sea in a clear dawn. And in the middle of it, a league off the mouth, a shape. Long. Low. A reef where there was no reef. Coming in.

And under the water, through Lisska's feet, through his own, through everything, the bell.

---

Wendmouth.

He'd been here once. Long ago. Before the dragons were the size of hills. He'd walked in off the Wendwood at dawn with nothing but a coat and a capstan bar and walked out again the next night with a price on his head and one of Joss's greys on a halter.

It hadn't changed.

---

The painted warehouses. That was what he remembered. Along the whole waterfront, a league of them, on stilts, over the water. Red and blue and yellow and green, each house's colour, four houses that hated one another. Doors at the water level for the barges and cranes at the top for the ships.

And in the lee of the eastern headland, where the river met the sea and the water was slow, the floating market.

---

Barges. Hulks. Rafts. Two hundred of them, perhaps. Lashed together, side by side, end to end, with planks across and ropes between, so that you could walk from one to the next across a quarter-league of water. Stalls on them. Tents. Smoke from cook-fires. A town, on the water, that rose and fell with the tide.

Where everything still sold. Even now. Even this winter. Fish. Salt. Rope. Rumour. People.

Six thousand of them still there, Kit had said. The four houses wouldn't leave their stock. And the people wouldn't leave the houses' bread.

---

The first wave came at noon.

He saw it come. A long swell. Up the mouth from the sea. Not breaking. Lifting. Like the whole sea taking a breath in. Up the river's mouth past the headlands, a hill of grey water a league wide, moving in.

Under the stilts.

---

The warehouses went one after another.

He watched them go. All along the waterfront, a league of them. The swell going in under the stilts and lifting, and the stilts breaking, and the painted fronts folding forward into the water. Red. Then blue. Then a whole row of yellow, slowly, as a row of men kneels. The cranes going down with them. The noise of it came up to him a breath late, a long splintering roar that went on and on.

And the floating market heaved.

---

All of it. Two hundred barges, lifted on the swell, together, and the lashings parting. He could see them go. Ropes snapping. Planks going into the water. Barges sliding apart. People on the planks. People in the water.

*Now*, he said.

*I know*, said Lisska.

---

They lifted it.

He'd never be able to say, after, how. Not in order. It was too big for order. He'd done Gullhaven with white crosses on roofs and a hundred dragons. He'd done Corsa's hulks. This was neither. This was a town coming apart on the water while the sea breathed in under it.

He did what he'd always done. He shouted.

---

Arn came at the first hour after noon. Sixty pairs. Kit's bird had come to the hay-barn before dawn, and Rowan had sent them down after him, and they'd flown hard. Red and grey and brown, coming down out of the north over the river, low, with baskets.

And behind them, wyverns.

Nell Tallis's. Black and grey, hoods off for once, their riders bent low. Black Ruth's, behind, a dozen. The Crane brothers' two, huge and patched. Coming down the river beside the Clan in the same long uneven flight.

Saltreach's wyverns, over Saltreach's own city. And the Clan beside them.

---

And on the water, Kit.

He saw the Merrin barges come out of the river's side channel where they'd been lying since the autumn. Four of them. And a dozen more behind, free captains' barges, the last on the coast. Poled, rowed, sculled, out into the heaving water among the floating market's broken pieces.

Kit at the tiller of the first. In her coat, with her crooked nose and her hair tied back. Standing up. Shouting.

---

The barges took the barges.

That was how it went. Kit's people threw lines to the floating market's barges as they drifted apart, and made them fast, and towed. Upriver. Away from the mouth. Two, three at a time behind each Merrin barge, with the people still on them.

And the hulks that couldn't be towed, the old dead ships at the market's heart that had been lashed there for years and had no rudders and no masts and wouldn't float far, the dragons and the wyverns took the people off them.

In baskets. On their backs. One at a time, if they had to.

---

And the count went by bird.

He saw it start and didn't understand it at first. Grey-and-white birds, everywhere, over the water. Gull-hawks. Wendmouth's message birds; every house kept them, and every barge-master. Going from barge to barge. From hulk to barge. From barge to the bank.

With slips on their legs.

*Forty off the Saffery hulk.*

*Ninety on the Merrin second.*

*Twelve in the water off Gallows Quay. Two dead.*

---

Kit had them.

He saw her catch one out of the air at the tiller without looking, take the slip, read it, shout a number to the barge behind her, and throw the bird up again. Then the next. Then the next.

She was counting the whole floating market. On birds. From the tiller of a barge in a heaving river.

He'd seen the knight count. He'd never seen anyone count like that.

---

Gallows Quay was the last.

At the floating market's eastern end, where the market met the city. A stone quay going out into the water from the waterfront, with the cage-rows along it and a stone building at its end.

The Halloran beast-office. Rusk's. Empty since the house fell.

Forty people on its roof. The swell had taken the quay's root and the building stood on its own now in the water, with the sea breaking round it.

---

He put Lisska down on the quay's end.

On the stones, in the spray. Her great green bulk taking up all of it, her tail in the water. The forty came down off the roof onto her back, children first, sliding.

He stood on the quay while they did it. And looked at the cages.

---

They were still there.

Iron. Rows of them, along the quay. Rusted now. Empty. Every one.

And the doors hanging open.

He'd broken them. With a capstan bar stolen from a chandler's yard, in the dark, on a night like this but warmer, a lifetime ago. Every bolt on the quay. He'd let everything out. A wyvern, screaming up into the dark. Hounds. Martens. A white ox.

A unicorn colt. Lean. Silver-black. With a stub of horn. That had stopped at the end of the quay and looked back at him with eyes like something remembering.

And gone.

---

Nobody had shut the doors.

Nobody had ever shut them again. He stood on the quay in the spray with the sea breathing in round the beast-office and looked at the rusted doors hanging open on their hinges, and thought: that's the only thing I ever did on purpose.

Then he looked at Rusk's window.

The glass case was still there. On its stand. Empty. Where a feather had lain on a velvet cushion, red-gold, that never cooled.

Somebody had taken it. Long ago.

Rusk, maybe. Him and his black ship, out past Corsa's towers on the morning tide, and nobody Calder knew had seen either since.

---

*Farmer*, said Lisska. *They're on. All of them. Come on.*

He got on.

---

Kharrak went to the Bell.

He'd wanted to all day. He'd lain on the eastern headland with his chin on the rocks and his gold eyes on the white water, and Calder had said *wait*, and *wait*, and *not yet*, until the market was off the water.

At the third hour after noon, with the last of the barges going upriver and the hulks empty, he said *now*.

---

Kharrak went out over the sea.

Low. Over the white water. A hill flying, red, with his shadow going over the waves under him like a cloud. To the shape a league off the mouth. The long low back, where there was no reef. The water piling white along it.

He came round it once.

Then he opened his mouth.

---

White fire. Into the sea.

Not on the Bell. Round it. In a ring. Kharrak flying round the Bell's back a league round and putting his fire down onto the water as he went, a quarter-league of it at a breath, round and round.

The sea boiled.

---

Steam. A wall of it. Going up white off the water in a ring a league round, with the Bell's long back in the middle of it. The water inside the ring heaving. Bubbling. The noise of it like every kettle in the world.

And the bell. Under the water. Through everything.

Once. Long.

Then again. Different. Lower. Further.

---

It went back.

He saw it go. Through the steam, through Aurveth's sight, which she gave him from the ridge ninety leagues off because she'd been watching. The long back sinking. Turning. Going out. Away from the mouth. A league. Two. Into the open sea.

And stopping.

Lying there. Two leagues off the mouth, in the white water. Waiting.

---

*More*, said Kharrak.

He was still going round. The fire still coming. The ring of steam getting wider.

*More. I can get it. I can boil it.*

---

"Small fire," said Calder.

---

Kharrak didn't stop.

*Small?*

"Small."

*That was small?*

"Smaller than you wanted," said Calder. "Stop now."

---

Kharrak flew round the steam once more with his mouth shut.

Then he came back to the headland and lay down on the rocks with his chin on them and the steam going up off the sea behind him, and was quiet for a long time.

*It was smaller than I wanted*, he said at last.

*I know.*

*I wanted it all.*

*I know you did.*

*I didn't*, said Kharrak. As if he'd only just noticed. *I didn't take it all.*

---

Five thousand two hundred.

Kit had it. On slips, in a box, at her tiller. She read it to him on the bank two days later, with the floating market strung out up the river behind her under tow. Eight days yet to the Staithe, where they'd lash it again, a smaller town on a quieter river.

Five thousand two hundred, off the water and out of Wendmouth.

About eight hundred not.

---

He flew home on the two-hundred-and-ninetieth.

Up the Wend. On Kharrak, high, with Lisska and Aurveth beside. The river grey under them. The Hearthmere bank dead and white with frost on the left. The Saltreach bank on the right, brown scrub, and the Drover's Way going north through it.

And ahead, the Vale.

---

He saw it from a long way off.

Two lines. Grey. Across the land. One on each side of the river. Straight. From the water to the high ground. As if someone had ruled them on the country with a pencil, as Davin ruled the old man's glass-logs.

New. They hadn't been there in the summer. Nothing in the land had ever been that straight.

The knight's walls.

---

And on the eastern one, at the river end, a hill.

He knew it was her. He didn't look closer. He didn't ask Aurveth to.

He flew over the line, and down onto the ridge above the hub, and got off Kharrak, and stood for a while in the cold with his hand on the red one's nose.

*Small fire*, said Kharrak.

*Yes.*

*Don't tell anyone.*

*I won't.*
