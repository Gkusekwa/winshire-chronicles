---

Rowan said it at the end of the council.

At dusk. In the hay-barn, with the door open because the barn was full and hot with people, and the cold coming in round them. The line settled. Every sector. Every kingdom's beasts in their places. Every sky.

Then she put her hands flat on the door and looked at the two ends of it.

"The river," she said.

---

Calder was standing at the back. By the ladder. The knight was at the door's foot, sitting, because Davin had put a stool there and she'd sat on it without seeing it.

"Everything on this line is a kingdom's," said Rowan. "Except four things. Two of them are gods, and they'll go where they go. The other two are yours."

She looked at the knight. Then at him.

"Nobody at this table can tell a lion where to stand," said Rowan. "Or three dragons where to fly. I can't. Thale can't. Pike can't." She didn't look at Pike. "You'll settle it between you."

"When?" said the knight.

"Tonight," said Rowan. "I'll not have you settle it tomorrow, with them on the field."

---

He went down to the ferry at the second hour.

Liss had said she'd take them. Middle of the eddy. Not one bank or the other. "Neither of you'll come to the other's bank," Liss had said, "so you'll come to my water." She'd said it as if it were a joke. He didn't think it was.

---

The night was hard. Clear. Frost on everything, and the stars thick, and no moon yet.

He stood on the landing below the ferry house and looked at the Vale.

---

Two lines of fire. On the walls. End to end. As there'd been at midwinter, and every night since. The camp's fires behind them, thousands, all the way back up both banks.

And on the Ferry Flat, below him, the Bridle.

---

It had come down the Iron Road six days ago. He'd watched it come in. A line of green lamps a league long coming down the road out of the west in the dark, on wagons and sledges, with Sarovel's hands walking beside it. The engines. The chain in its lengths. The ring.

Now it sat on its beds.

Twelve engines, each the size of a barn, black, on the twelve iron beds in a ring a quarter-mile across. Their fires banked. He could see the glow at their fireboxes, red, low. Warm. Green lamps on poles round them. The chain laid out from each engine in to the middle, along the cords, black, the links as long as a man. And in the middle, in its cradle, the ring. Two men tall. With an iron stage on its top that had a rail round it and a stand at its front like a lectern.

And at the eddy's edge, the six posts. With their jibs. And the chain-ends hanging from the jibs now. Not practice lengths. The Bridle's.

He looked at it a long time.

---

*It's warm*, said Kharrak. From the ridge. *All of it. I can feel it from here.*

*Yes.*

*I don't like it.*

*No.*

---

The knight came down the bank at the second hour.

From the ferry house. She'd been in the kitchen with Liss. She came down the steps to the landing slowly, as she did everything now, in a blue coat too big for her, a Warden's. Her right hand bandaged. Her left in her coat.

She got onto the ferry. He got on after her. Liss got on last, and went to the windlass, and put her back to them, and hauled.

---

The scow went out onto the eddy.

On the new rope. Pale hemp, creaking. Out over the grey water, turning slowly under them, the right way. The bank going away behind. The other bank coming no nearer.

In the middle Liss stopped hauling.

She made the windlass fast and stayed at it. With her back to them. Her shawl over her head. Looking upriver.

---

And on the water a little way off, a head.

Long. Pale. Like a heron's, on a neck like a river. Lying on the eddy, still. With eyes the colour of deep water.

The Lady. Watching them.

He'd never been so close to her. He'd seen her from the air, and from the bank. Not like this. Thirty paces. He could see the frost forming on the water round her and melting again.

The knight didn't look at her. She'd been closer.

---

They stood on the ferry's deck, a pace apart.

As they'd sat by the pool on the far shore. A pace apart. Not touching.

---

"You came when I wasn't there," he said.

To the hall. Under the tarn. He'd had her letter from Lisska's teeth.

"Yes," said the knight.

"Lisska said you'd be cross."

"I was."

"She said you'd be glad too."

The knight looked at the water.

"I was that too," she said.

---

He looked at her hand.

The bandage. Round the palm, round and round. A smith's bandage, neat, tied off with a smith's knot.

"Tam," she said, before he asked. "I put it on a pot. I didn't know."

"You didn't feel it."

"I don't feel anything," said the knight. "In my hands. Not hot. Not cold. I watch them." She looked at her bandaged hand on the ferry's rail. "I watch everything."

---

He couldn't think of anything to say to that. She didn't seem to want anything.

"Your voice," she said, after a while.

"It's worse in the cold."

"It's worse," she agreed.

---

They did the river.

It didn't take long. They'd both thought about it. He thought she'd probably thought about it longer.

---

"He stands on the river," she said. "Here. At the eddy's lower lip." She nodded downstream. Where the eddy's grey turning water went out into the Wend's run again, below the ferry. "Between the batteries. The bed's his. He can raise it where he wants. He'll stand on it."

"Where the Last one comes."

"From the west. Out of the Mere. Into the river. Here." She looked at the water. "Where it was chained."

"And the shadows?"

"Three," said the knight. "In the three sectors where the line's thinnest. The Hearthmere bend. The Wyldmarch's river end. Saltreach's scrub end." She said it as she'd said everything at the council. Flat. Counted. "The Accord counts him as four."

"Can he?"

She was quiet.

"Yes," she said. "For a day."

---

"The dragons," she said.

"Over the river," he said. "And the gap. Between the banks, where the wards don't cross." He thought of the old man's chalk: two wards, one each side, the river between, nothing over it. "That's ours. Kharrak goes south-west if the Sun comes up the Sun Road side. Lisska over the people. Aurveth high. Seeing."

"For the coal."

"For the coal," he said.

---

Then the other thing.

Neither of them said it for a while. The ferry turned slowly on its rope. The Lady lay on the water and watched. Liss stood at the windlass with her back to them.

---

"North," he said at last.

"North."

"The Drover's Way. Tam's wagons. Kit's barges up the river as far as they'll go, then off and walk. Through Harrow's End and up past the Fellwood. Over the Scarfell's lip. To Coldtarn." He breathed. "The hall."

"Five hundred," said the knight.

"Five hundred."

"And the rest?"

"The caves. The hold at Varrhold. The ledges. The byres." He looked at the fires on the walls, thousands, all the way back up both banks into the dark. "Anywhere with a roof."

Neither of them said how many that was.

---

"I'll be at the door," said the knight. "Counting."

"I know. You said."

"Did I?"

"In the letter. *Don't tell anyone.* And Lisska said: *She says she'll count them in.*"

The right side of her mouth went up. The left didn't move. It hadn't in months.

---

"Wren asked about you," he said.

The knight looked at him.

"What?"

"Whether you'd say it twice."

"Say what?"

"Anything," he said.

---

He put his hand in his coat.

He'd been carrying them since the spring. Since the last night before he flew up to the caves, when everyone left Liss's table and he'd gone back in for his coat and found them there. On the table, among the cups. Where she'd put them down and not picked them up again.

He took them out.

---

The five links.

The first chain. From the ruts at Upper Rush, the night of the raid, in the autumn of the first year. A length of it as long as his forearm, the last link cut clean through, dark iron, heavier than it should have been. And on every link the ring of six, small, stamped. And inside each ring two little crossed hammers.

Wrapped in the strip of his shirt. The same strip. Torn from the hem, the night before the island. With TWO burned on it with a charred stick from his own field.

The black had faded. Two years in a coat. He could still read it. It was one of the first words he'd ever written.

---

He held it out.

She looked at it. She didn't take it for a moment.

He'd emptied them out of his own coat onto that same table, the spring before, and she'd picked them up and carried them a year. Then she'd put them down again.

"You left them on Liss's table," he said. "At the end."

"Yes."

"I didn't know if you meant to."

She looked at the bundle.

"Neither did I," said the knight.

---

She took them.

In her bandaged right hand. He watched her take them. And he saw her look at her hand afterwards, at the bundle in it, to see if she was holding it. She couldn't feel it. She had to look.

She was holding it.

She put it in her coat.

---

"It won't come to that," said the knight.

North. Coldtarn. The hall. Five hundred.

She said it to the water. Flat. As if she believed it. He didn't know if she did. He didn't think she knew either.

---

"Say it twice," he said.

---

She looked at him.

For a long time. On the ferry, in the middle of the eddy, with the Lady watching and Liss's back turned and the frost on the rail. Her grey face and her burned one. Her eyes, which were the only part of her that still moved as they used to.

She didn't say it.

She didn't say anything.

---

He nodded.

That was all right. That was Wren's rule, not his. If you said a thing twice it was true. If you couldn't, it wasn't, or you didn't know. Either way you didn't say it.

She hadn't said it.

He found he was glad.

---

Liss turned the windlass.

Without being asked. She'd heard. She'd heard everything; she always did. She took the windlass off its catch and hauled, and the ferry went back across the eddy on the creaking rope toward the Hearthmere bank.

Neither of them said anything else.

---

He looked up at the ferry house as they came in.

The window. The back room. Lit.

---

The old man was at his shelf.

He could see him through the glass. In his blanket, standing, which he didn't do much. Davin beside him with a lamp held up. Both of them leaning close to the shelf. To the glasses.

Eight of them, in a row. He'd seen them before. Little sealed things, the length of a finger, with a drop of quicksilver in each under a mark.

Every one was climbing.

He could see it from the ferry. The silver in them, bright in Davin's lamp, high up the glasses. Higher than he'd ever seen them. And five of the eight higher than the rest. Near the tops. Still going.

---

The old man's shadow was on the wall behind him. Huge. Leaning over the shelf.

He saw the old man put out a shaking hand toward the glasses and not touch them.

---

*Five*, said Aurveth. From the ridge. Very quietly. *Coming. From five ways. All to here.*

*I know.*

*Tomorrow*, said Aurveth. *Dawn. Perhaps before.*

---

The ferry bumped the landing.

The knight got off first. She didn't look back. She went up the steps to the ferry house with her bandaged hand in her coat on the five links, slowly, and in at the kitchen door past the two knights and the hound.

He stood on the landing.

Liss made the scow fast. She came up beside him with her shawl over her head and stood there too, looking at the old man's lit window.

"She didn't say it," said Liss.

"No."

"Good," said Liss. "I'd not have believed her. Go and get warm."

She went up the steps.

He stood on the landing a while longer. Then he went up the bank to the ridge, where the three lay waiting in the frost, warm, and lay down between Kharrak's forelegs, and didn't sleep.
