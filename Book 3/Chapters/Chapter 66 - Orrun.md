---

The Saltreach bank was alive.

She'd felt it come. In the last three days, through Orrun's feet on his bed in the river, through the stone of the eastern wall when she lay against him at night. A hum. Faint. Then not so faint. Coming up under the Saltreach bank as the five came in.

The old man had seen it in his glasses. Climbing. Five of them near the top. She'd thought it was the five, coming. It was the five, and what came with them: the knot, filling.

---

Under the eddy. The middle of the world. Where every kingdom's tide-lines met in the old College's brown ink. As the five came in from five horizons, the Deep came with them. Pulled. Running up the lines toward the middle, as water runs downhill. Filling the knot.

And coming up.

On the Hearthmere bank, nothing. He'd drunk it for three hundred years in his chains. It was dead and it stayed dead.

On the Saltreach bank, it hummed.

---

He came at dusk.

---

All day he'd walked along the Hearthmere ward with his head on it. She'd watched him from Orrun's head. The white going along the ward with him. The voices under it. The olifants standing. The Furnace coming up under the batteries, and the Lady, and the steam a league high, and him lifting his head when it died.

At dusk he came to the end.

---

The river.

The Hearthmere ward came down to the Wend's west bank and stopped. A hand's breadth from the water. A pale edge in the air.

He put his head round it.

As a dog puts its head round a door. She saw him do it. The great grey head on its long neck, coming round the end of the ward, over the water, where there was nothing. Looking.

Then he stepped down into the river.

---

Off the bank. Into the Wend. Between the wards. In the gap.

The river came up to his shoulders. Grey. Frozen at the edges. It parted round him and he walked in it, slowly, downstream. Toward the eddy. The chain-ends on his legs dragging in the water, iron, as long as towers, turning in the current behind him.

Toward the eddy's lower lip.

Toward Orrun.

---

*He's coming*, said Orrun.

*I know.*

*Stay on*, said Orrun. *Hold on.*

She put her hand in his mane. The right. She watched it go in. She couldn't feel it. She watched it close.

---

They stood face to face in the river.

---

Two hills. In the grey water, in the dusk, at the eddy's lower lip, with the wards lit faintly on either bank and the river dark between. The same grey. Stone and something like stone. The same long heads. The same pale eyes.

She'd never seen it so clearly. Kin. Him and the thing under Greyhold. The same, as the Lamp and the Horned Sun were the same. As the Lady and the Coil. One that had stopped and one that hadn't. Only this time she didn't know which was which by looking. Only by the wings, folded on the other's back. And the chains.

---

*Brother*, said Orrun.

---

She heard it. In him. Low. Slow. Stone settling.

The other looked at him.

She waited for it to answer. She remembered the cavern. The end of the second spring. The relic in the dark. Its voice, once, in Orrun, that she'd heard through him: *You kept it well, little brother. It is not yours to keep any more.*

It had known him then.

---

It didn't answer.

It looked at him. With its pale eyes. Across thirty paces of grey water. For a long time.

As you look at a stranger on a road.

---

*It doesn't know me*, said Orrun.

Very quietly.

*It knew me. At the door. Under the hill. It called me little brother.* He was still. *It doesn't know me now.*

*Why?*

*I'm not little*, said Orrun.

---

Then it came at him.

---

She'd been on his back when he fought before. At the Ring. At Vael. Under Greyhold. On the far shore. On the Antlered's crown in four bodies. She'd never been on him when he fought his own kind.

It came at his shoulder. As stags come at each other. Head down. And he met it.

---

The river went up.

Both sides. In sheets. White. Freezing in the air as it went up and coming down as ice on her, on her face, on her hands in his mane. She didn't feel it. She saw it, on her sleeves, white.

Two hills shoulder to shoulder in the river, pushing.

---

The chain-ends swung.

She hadn't thought of the chains. The twelve ends on the Last Titan's legs, dragging. As it turned, they turned. In the water. Like flails. Iron. As long as towers. One came round out of the river in a long black arc, shedding water, and across Orrun's flank.

She felt it.

Him. She felt him feel it. A line of fire down his side, under her, through the bond. The iron's *hold* on its inside, cold, burning.

He didn't move.

---

*Hold*, she said.

*Holding.*

---

And the others held.

She was in all four. She'd been in all four all day. The shadow at the Hearthmere bend among the knights, with its paws pressed flat on the heaving ground, holding the wall with the stone lions. The shadow at the Wyldmarch's river end, lying among the stonehorns. The shadow at Saltreach's scrub end, where the Sun had pressed all day on Kharrak's fire.

Four weights. Four bodies. And her own.

She felt each one. She felt them press on her. She'd never felt it like this. Four hills on her, at once, and the fifth in the river.

---

Then the bank went.

---

She felt it before she saw it. Through Orrun. Through the bed under his feet. Through the ground on the Saltreach side, alive, humming.

Sliding.

---

The Last Titan was drawing.

Not on Orrun. On the ground. She felt it pull. Under the river. Under the Saltreach bank. The Deep that had come up into the knot in the last three days, humming in the earth of the Saltreach bank, and the Last Titan in the river drawing on it, as he'd drawn on the Hearthmere ground for three hundred years.

And the earth followed it.

Toward the river.

---

She looked.

The Saltreach bank. Where the Wyldmarch's line came down to the water. Where Kessmoor's second battery stood on the bank's edge with its bolt-throwers. Where the eastern wall's river end met the Wend, and she'd lain all night against Orrun.

All of it. Moving.

Slowly. As wet sand slides. A league of it. The earth of the bank, with the clans standing on it and the stonehorns grazing on it and the battery's engines on it and the wall's end on it, sliding toward the river. A pace. Another. The frozen turf cracking in long lines parallel to the water. The battery's bolt-throwers tipping. People falling. The stonehorns lifting their heads and bellowing.

Toward the Last Titan, standing in the river, drawing.

---

*Put me down*, she said.

*Maren.*

*Put me down. On the bank. Now.*

---

He did.

He put his shoulder into his kin and held it there, a hill leaning on a hill, and with the same breath bent his head down to the Saltreach bank. Down. Until his chin was on the sliding earth at the water's edge.

She went down his face.

On her backside, as she always did now. Down his brow, down his nose, sliding. She couldn't feel her hands on his stone. She watched them. She watched them all the way. Onto the bank.

Onto the earth.

It was sliding under her.

---

She knelt.

And put her hands on it.

Both.

The right, numb to the wrist, that she could move and not feel. The left, dead to the shoulder, that she couldn't move at all; she took it out of her coat with the right hand and put it down on the earth beside the other, flat, and left it there.

She watched them both. On the sliding earth.

---

It hummed.

Under her hands. She couldn't feel her hands. She could feel the hum. Through her knees. Through her wrists. Alive. As at Coldtarn. As at the Scald, in the surge, with the bank sliding into the chilled canal and Pell's word in her mouth.

Live ground.

And behind her, in the river, with his shoulder in his kin's, him.

---

She knew a word.

---

Pell had taught it. Not to her. In the yard at Greyhold, to the older students, the year she was fourteen. A horse gone wild, a big grey, rearing, and Pell walking up to it, not fast, and putting his hand flat on its neck and saying a word into it. Low. Into the neck, under the mane.

*It doesn't make it calm*, Pell had said. *It makes it stand. That's all. Long enough.*

She'd said it once in her life. At the Scald. Into a bank.

She said it again.

---

Into the ground.

Low. Bent down over her hands. Her mouth almost on the earth. Into it.

With him behind it.

---

The bank stopped.

---

All of it. At once.

A league.

She felt it stop. Under her hands, under her knees, out from her in every direction. The whole slope of the Saltreach bank, from the water's edge back to the clans' line and beyond it, the wall's end, the battery, the stonehorns' grazing. Sliding. And then not. Nailed. As the grey had stopped in the yard, all at once, shaking. A league of earth, standing still, shaking.

And further.

She hadn't meant further. It didn't know how to stop where she meant. It went out from her into the ground and the ground stood, and it went on into the river, and the river stood.

At the bank's edge. For a breath. The grey water of the Wend, running past the eddy's lip, stopping. Standing up against the bank like a wall of glass. Still.

Then running again.

---

Huge. Crude. Like everything she did now.

But the bank stood.

---

It went out of her arms.

She felt it go. She'd thought there was nothing left in her hands to go; there wasn't. It went higher. Up from her wrists. Up her right forearm, the feeling going out of it as water goes out of a torn sack. To the elbow.

She looked at her right arm, flat on the earth to the elbow. She couldn't feel any of it. She watched it.

Her left was dead to the shoulder already. There'd been nothing there to take.

---

She didn't stop.

She'd learned that much in the Thornwood, and at the Saltings, and under the sea. You didn't stop when the toll came. You stopped when it was done.

It wasn't done.

---

The bank stood. It wouldn't stand for ever. The stand-word made a thing stand. Long enough. Not for ever. The Last Titan was still in the river, drawing. She could feel it pulling on the earth under her hands, steadily, patiently, as it had pulled on the Hearthmere ground for three hundred years.

When the word let go, the bank would slide again.

So she shaped it.

---

With Orrun's earth. Through her. Still kneeling, still with both dead arms flat on the ground.

Ridges.

Up. Out of the stopped bank. Behind the water's edge. Along the clans' line. Three long banks of earth, rising out of the frozen ground, the turf splitting along them and the soil coming up underneath, black, steaming in the cold. Waist-high. Then man-high. A quarter-league long, each, one behind the other, between the river and the clans.

Something for the clans to stand behind. Something for the earth to slide against.

---

And open.

In front of them. At the water's edge. The earth opening. A trench. Long. Deep. Between the river and the first ridge, a quarter-league of it, black, the soil going down into it in clods as it opened.

So that what slid, when it slid, went in. Into the trench. Not over the ridges. Not onto the people.

---

She didn't know how she knew to do that.

She'd never dug a trench. She'd never raised a bank. She'd seen the Greyhold engineers do it once, on the river, in the first year. She'd watched them from the wall, with their spades and their lines, for a week.

She did it in a breath. Crude. Enormous. Three ridges and a trench, a quarter-league, out of the stopped bank, through her dead hands.

---

And went cold to the heart.

---

She felt it go in.

Not into her hands. Into her middle. Up from her arms, through her shoulders, into her chest. Down. To the place under her ribs on the grey side, where the stone from the Thornwood lay. The going of the Antlered, that she'd taken for him.

The cold went through it. Through the stone. Into the middle of her.

Into her heart.

She felt it slow.

---

She knelt on the bank with her dead arms on the earth and her heart going slow and cold in her chest, and didn't move.

---

Something lay down next to her.

A wolf. Grey. Then another. Then a ring of them. Rhun's. Lying down on the earth round her, close, their sides against her, as they'd lain round her at the Thornwood's edge.

And a cloak. Over her shoulders. Heavy. Green.

"Knight," said Rhun. Somewhere above her. "Your lips are blue."

"Are they?"

"Very."

"I can't feel them."

"I know," said Rhun. And sat down on the earth beside her, with her wolves, and didn't go away.

---

In the river, he drove it back.

---

She watched it from the bank, through Rhun's wolves. Through him. Both.

He was alone. Three of him were in the sectors, holding the line. One of him was in the river, with his kin.

He put his whole weight into its shoulder.

All of it. A hill's weight. A Titan's. The weight the Mother had given him on the far shore, and everything he'd grown into since. Into the Last Titan's shoulder, in the river, in the dark.

And it went back.

---

A pace.

The river going up white round them. The chain-ends swinging, and one across his flank again, and he didn't move from it.

Another pace.

Back. Up the river. Away from the Saltreach bank. Away from the clans and the ridges and the trench and the woman kneeling on the earth with the wolves round her.

Into the eddy.

---

He drove it into the middle.

Where the island had been. Where the eddy turned slowest, a quarter-league across, grey, nothing growing, no birds. The middle of the world. Where it had been chained under the river, in the dark, for three hundred years, with six chains and a hill on top of it.

It went down.

Into the deep water there. To its back. Its wings folded under the grey surface. Its chains trailing out round it in the turning eddy like weed. Its long neck along the water. Its head on the water.

And lay there.

---

Its pale eyes open.

Not beaten. She could see it wasn't beaten. It lay in the eddy's middle as a man lies down in his own bed. Where it had wanted to go. Where it had always been coming.

*Home*, said Orrun.

He stood at the eddy's lower lip, on his raised bed, with his flank burned twice by its chains and his breath going in and out of him like a bellows.

*It's lying where its chain was.*

---

The bank held.

Behind the ridges. The word letting go of it slowly, in the dark, and the earth beginning to slide again, a little, a hand's breadth, into the trench. And stopping there.

The clans behind the ridges.

---

Aurveth came over in the night.

---

She was very high. Higher than she'd ever been. Over the eddy, circling. Bronze in the light of the wards and the fires, a long way up.

And Lisska below her. Lower. Over the river. In sight.

Of Orrun.

---

*Look*, said Lisska.

Through Orrun. To Maren. Kin-speech. Only in sight. Lisska over the river, where Orrun could see her, and Aurveth over Lisska, where Lisska could see her, and the farmer on the ridge between Kharrak's forelegs, where none of them could see him, seeing through all of them.

*She says look*, said Lisska.

---

Orrun looked.

And she looked through him.

---

Down. Through the water. Into the eddy. Into the great grey shape lying there with its chains round it. Through the stone and leather of its back. Through the plates.

Five dark shapes. In its back. Grown into the plates. Egg-shaped. Cart-sized. Dark. Cold. The sleep relics, five of them, that should have kept the six asleep, grown into the back of the one that had woken them.

And among them, one that wasn't dark.

---

A coal.

Burning. Red. Bright. Brighter than she'd ever imagined, from the farmer's telling, from the old man's. Like a coal that somebody's blown on. Twice. Behind the left shoulder. A hall's breadth below the ridge of the spine. Between the second dark egg and the third.

The relic. The stone the old ones made. That answered a breath that was unbound.

Burning.

---

*There*, said Lisska. *That's where. She says remember.*

*I'll remember*, said Orrun.

*She says you too*, said Lisska. *The knight.*

*I'll remember*, said Maren.

---

Then the coal was gone, and the eddy was grey water, and the great shape lay in it with its pale eyes open.

---

They stayed like that all night.

Orrun at the eddy's lower lip, on his bed, his forefoot on the raised stone. His kin in the eddy's middle a quarter-league off, lying on the water where its chains had been. The two of them looking at each other, across the turning water, in the dark. Neither moving.

The three shadows in their sectors. Lying among the people. The lion counting as four.

And her, on the Saltreach bank, behind the first ridge, in Rhun's green cloak with the wolves round her, with her heart going slow and cold in her chest, counting it.

She counted his heartbeats. Through the bond.

They were slower than hers.

She hadn't known anything could be.
