---

They got up at the same breath again.

He was awake for it this time. He woke between Aurveth's forelegs with her head curled round him, and his hands in their bandages under his chin, and lay there in the grey and felt it. All five. At once. Getting up.

*Again*, said Aurveth.

*Yes.*

*You slept*, she said. *Good.*

---

The words went up off the line at dawn.

*Keep the watch.* A hundred and six thousand. Fewer. He said it late. And the wards lit, pale gold and pale blue, over both banks, the river dark between.

He got up onto Kharrak's neck. His hands were raw under the bandages. He put them on the plates anyway.

*My face hurts*, said Kharrak.

*I know.*

*I'll do it anyway.*

*I know you will.*

---

The Sun came off the hills at sunrise.

Not at the scrub end. He saw it from the air. It didn't come down onto the Saltreach wall's east end where it had pressed all yesterday on Kharrak's fire. It came down further west. Along the scrub. Along the wall's outside. Toward the river.

*It's going for the gap*, said Aurveth. From high above. *Where the wards stop. It wants the river.*

*Then it can have it*, said Calder.

---

He took Kharrak down.

Along its front again. White fire. A league. But not to stop it this time. To turn it. Kharrak flying along the Sun's eastern side, and the white wall going up between the Sun and the Saltreach ward, and the Sun turning from the heat. West. Toward the water.

Where it wanted to go.

And again. And again. Driving it. Down. Toward the Wend. Toward the shallows below the eddy.

---

The shallows were a league below the lower lip.

Gravel. Wide. Where the Wend spread out after the eddy and ran thin and fast over a long bar of stones, a quarter-league across, knee-deep to a man. The camp had called them the Kingfisher Shallows in the autumn, because there'd been a kingfisher there, once, before the frost.

He drove the Sun into them.

---

It went in.

Off the scrub. Down the Saltreach bank. Into the river. Its half-league of day coming down onto the water.

And the water hissed.

Steam. All along the shallows. White. The river boiling on the gravel where the light touched it.

And the light dimmed.

He saw it dim. In the water. The white-gold going grey-gold. As a lamp dims in a fog. The Horned Sun standing in the Wend's shallows to its knees, its light going into the water and the water taking it.

---

And the Lamp came down the river.

---

From the eddy's head, where it had stood two days on the water. Walking. On the light. Down the river, past the eddy, past the lion at the lower lip, to the shallows.

To its kin.

---

They didn't fight.

Not as he'd seen anything fight. The Lamp came to the Horned Sun in the shallows and put its head down. Its horn to the Sun's horn. Light to light.

And pressed.

Down.

---

The Sun went down.

Into the water. Slowly. Its forelegs folding. Its head coming down under the Lamp's, into the shallows, until its horn was in the river and the river was boiling round it.

Grey-gold. Dim. Steaming. Held.

Down.

---

*It's down*, said Kharrak.

He couldn't believe it either. He hung over the shallows on his great red wings with his blistered face and looked at the thing he'd burned on the horn and the face for a whole day, lying in the river under its kin's light, dim.

*It's down*, said Kharrak. *It's down.*

---

North, the Lady had the Coil.

He saw it through Aurveth. She'd been in the channel above the line all night, winding, in the black ice and the steam. At dawn she'd wound it down. Down under the ice. Onto the river-bed.

And the river-bed had come up to meet it.

One of the lion's shadows. The one that had held the bend all day with its paws flat on the heaving ground. It had gone at first light up the Hearthmere bank to the channel and into the river, and raised the bed there, as the lion had raised his. A bar of stone across the channel.

The Lady pinned the Coil against it.

Its pale light under the ice, coiling. Slower. Slower. Pinned, between the Lady's coils and the lion's stone.

---

South, the Bell.

---

He didn't see it at first. It had gone back down the river in the night. Back to the white water off the mouth, as it had after Kharrak's fire, and at sunrise it came on again. Ninety leagues off. Then Aurveth looked, and he looked with her.

Another shadow. The one from the scrub end. Free now, with the Sun in the shallows. Going south. Down the Saltreach bank. At a Titan's run, a hill running, a league in a breath, along the river toward the sea.

To the mouth.

Into it.

Into the shallows at the Wend's mouth, between the two headlands, where Wendmouth's stilts were broken stumps in the water. And standing there. Across the channel. A grey hill with a mane, in the sea, chest-deep.

And when the Bell's long back came up out of the white water toward the mouth, it put its shoulder into it.

And held it off.

---

The bell rang. Under the sea. Through the shadow. Through the lion. Through everything.

Over and over.

Held.

---

And the Last Titan.

---

In the eddy. Where it had lain all night, on the water, where its chains had been. It rose at sunrise. Up off the water. Its wings unfolding, for the first time since it had walked off the downs. Stone and leather, opening, wide as the eddy.

To go up.

Out of the river. Into the sky. Over the line. Over the wards, which ended at the water and had nothing over the river.

---

Orrun went at it.

From his bed at the lower lip. Through the eddy's turning water, a hill going through a pool, the water going up white round him. And from the Saltreach side, the last shadow, the one from the Wyldmarch's river end, coming down the bank into the eddy at a run.

Two lions.

They hit it as it rose.

---

He saw it from the air. Two grey hills going up out of the water at a grey hill with its wings half open. One at its shoulder. One at its flank. And their weight.

It came down.

---

Into the eddy. Into the middle. On its side. Its wings folding under it into the water. The eddy going up in a wave that ran out across the whole quarter-league of it and broke on both banks, and on the Ferry Flat, and round the Bridle's jibs.

And Orrun stood over it.

---

His forefoot on its neck.

---

Behind the head. Where the neck met the skull. A lion's foot, as big as a house, grey, pressing down. Holding the great head down on the water. Its pale eyes open, looking up.

No chain. No iron. No *hold* on the inside of a link.

A foot. On its neck. Pressing.

The reverse of the chains.

---

The line saw it.

He heard it. Both banks. A hundred and six thousand people, under the wards, seeing it. A sound like a breath taken in.

The Sun down in the shallows under its kin's light. The Coil pinned in the north. The Bell held off at the mouth. The Last Titan down in its old prison with the lion's foot on its neck.

All five.

Held.

---

*Now*, said Aurveth.

From high above him.

*Now, farmer. It's under the plates. The Lamp can't see it. I can.*

---

He knew.

They'd said it all on the far shore, in the Hall of Light, with the old man reading the letters off the air. *Only one of us can hold it.* *They hear two together.* *Two hands.* They'd said it again at Rowan's table in the summer, and in the barn in the autumn, and on Liss's ferry three nights ago.

He'd known since the far shore that it would come to this. To him and her, on the river.

He'd thought he'd mind more.

---

He went across.

From Kharrak to Aurveth. In the air. Over the shallows, with the Sun lying in them dim under the Lamp's light. As he'd gone from Kharrak to Lisska in the Thornwood. He was getting better at it. Kharrak came up under Aurveth, close, closer than they ever flew, and he went down Kharrak's shoulder and onto her bronze neck as she came level.

*Stay over the Sun*, he told Kharrak.

*I'll stay*, said Kharrak. *Go on.*

---

He brought her down.

Aurveth. Slowly. As she flew everything. Down out of the high air, where she'd been seeing for two days, onto the river. Into the eddy.

Beside the lion.

On the bed he'd raised, at the edge of the eddy's middle. Along Orrun's flank. Her great bronze body lying in the grey water beside his great grey one, and her head on the water by his shoulder.

---

He climbed across.

From her neck to Orrun's shoulder. Grey stone. Warm. It hummed. Up the shoulder. Onto the neck. Up the mane, which was stone too, and singed black on one side.

To the head.

---

She was there.

Between his ears. Where she always was. Sitting. In a blue coat too big for her and a green cloak over it that wasn't hers. Her face grey on one side and burned on the other. Her lips blue.

She looked at him.

---

"You came," she said.

"You knew I would."

"Yes."

---

He held out his hand.

---

She looked at it. His right hand. Bandaged. Raw under the cloth from two days on Kharrak's plates.

Then she held out hers.

Her right. Bandaged too. Tam's knot. She held it out slowly, and he saw her look at it as she did, to know where it was.

He took it.

---

She couldn't feel it. He knew that. She'd told him on the ferry. He held her hand in his and she looked down at their two bandaged hands together, to see that he had it.

He had it.

*Two hands*, said the old man's voice, in his memory, reading the light. *When two hands open what one hand shut.*

---

*Now*, said Aurveth.

---

He looked through her.

Down. Into the Last Titan. Through the stone and leather of its back, under the lion's foot, through the plates. As he'd looked from the Scarfell across a hundred leagues. As she'd shown Orrun in the night.

Five dark eggs. In its back. Grown into the plates. Cold.

And the coal.

---

Red.

Burning. Brighter than at the Scarfell. Brighter than in the night. Two breaths in it now: the Antlered's and the Furnace's. Behind the left shoulder. A hall's breadth below the spine. Between the second egg and the third.

He saw it.

And he felt her hold.

---

Through her hand. He couldn't feel her hand; she couldn't feel his. He felt her anyway. Through it. Orrun's hold. The weight. The lion's foot on the neck. The patience of stone, pressing down, not moving, holding a hill on the water as it would hold a hill for a thousand years if she asked it.

Her. Holding. And him. Seeing.

Two.

---

The Lamp came up the river.

---

He saw it come. Out of the shallows. Leaving its kin lying there under the water in its own dimmed light, held, and walking up the river. On the light on the water. Past the lower lip. Into the eddy.

To them.

Too bright to look at. He looked anyway. He didn't care about his eyes any more.

It came to the Last Titan's side, on the water. And stopped. And lowered its head.

Listening.

---

*They hear two together*, the old man had read.

---

It heard them.

He felt it hear. Something touched the bond. Both bonds. His and hers. At once. Not in his ears; in him. As the Wayfarer had touched him at the crossroads. Light. Feeling for something.

Finding it.

*Two*, it said. He didn't hear it. He knew it.

---

It walked onto the Last Titan's back.

On the light. Up the great grey flank as up a hill. Slowly. Onto the back. Among the plates. Among the five dark eggs grown into them. Too bright. Its hooves not touching the stone.

He showed it.

---

He didn't know how. He looked. Through Aurveth. At the coal under the plates. And the knowing went out of him, through his hand, into hers, into Orrun, into the stone under the Lamp's light.

*Left.*

The Lamp went left.

*Down. Toward the shoulder.*

It went down.

*Under the second plate. There. Between the second egg and the third.*

---

Hand over hand.

That was what it was like. As you guide a blind man along a rail. His sight, going through her hold, into the Lamp. A step. A step. Her hold keeping the Last Titan still under it. His sight showing it where.

He'd never felt anything like it. He didn't think anyone had.

Two bonds. One thing.

---

The Lamp stopped.

Behind the left shoulder. A hall's breadth below the spine. Between the second dark egg and the third.

Over the coal.

---

It lowered its horn.

---

Slowly. The long horn, like a spear of noon. Down. Toward the plates. Toward the place he was seeing through Aurveth, the red under the grey.

The tip touched the plates.

And the plates parted.

---

Round the horn's tip. As ice parts round a hot iron. The great grey plates of the Last Titan's back, that the knight had cut at with Haverly's edge once and rung like an anvil, parting. Opening. A crack. A hand's breadth. Two.

And out of the crack, red light.

The coal. Coming up through the plates. Its light on the Lamp's horn. On the Lamp's face.

---

The Last Titan didn't move.

Under the lion's foot. Its head on the water. Its pale eyes open. Looking up at the sky. Not fighting. Not moving.

Held.

---

"We've got it," said Calder.

---

He said it out loud. He hadn't meant to. It came out of him, cracked, in his ruined voice, on the lion's head, with her hand in his.

"We've got it."

---

"Yes," said the knight.

She didn't say it twice.

He didn't ask her to.

He didn't need to. He could see it. The Lamp's horn going down into the parting plates a finger's breadth at a time, and the red light coming up, and the Last Titan held under the lion's foot, and the Sun down in the shallows, and the Coil pinned, and the Bell held off at the mouth.

They had it.

He believed it. All of him. As he'd never believed anything.

---

Above them, Lisska.

---

Over the eddy. Low. Circling. Green, the size of a crag, her wings full out, her shadow going round and round over the water and the lion and the bronze dragon lying in the river and the light on the Last Titan's back.

Watching the Lamp go down to the coal.

She didn't say anything.

---

He waited for her to. She always did. *There. Easy. Did you see that?* She'd talked all his life, all of hers. Through every fight. Through the Thornwood. Through the sea.

She didn't say anything.

She went round and round over the eddy and watched, and said nothing at all.

He'd remember that.

---

He didn't look at the Ferry Flat.

He was looking at the coal. At the Lamp's horn, going down. At her bandaged hand in his.

He didn't see the battery-masters on the Flat across the eddy, at their engines and their bolt-throwers and the six jibs at the water's edge. He didn't see them take their brass watches out of their coats.

All at once. Eight of them.

He didn't see them open them.

He didn't see them look.
