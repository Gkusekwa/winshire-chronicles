---

He held them by talking.

That was all he had. He stood on the lion's head in the smoke with his arms out and his ruined hands open and talked. Not loud. He couldn't be loud. Into the bond. Three bonds. Into each of them.

*Here. I'm here. I'm here.*

---

They were slack.

All three. He could feel them. The green, the bronze, the rust. Three ropes going out from his chest where the three scars were, and on the end of each one a dragon, and the ropes gone slack. As ropes go slack when the boats on the end of them slip their moorings. Still tied. Drifting.

He didn't pull.

He'd pulled at the Roosts, the first time. On Lisska. Hard. Until his nose bled. He'd thought that was how you held.

It wasn't. You couldn't pull a boat in that's drifting in a current. The rope breaks.

You talked to it.

---

*Hold on to me*, he said. *I'm here. You know me. I'm here.*

Over and over. In his voice that was a crack in a pot.

*Hold on.*

---

The smoke was low.

He saw that, after a while. Through the yellow-grey. It lay on the water and on the ground like fog in a valley. Thick. Heavy. Sleepwood smoke was heavy; Gunna had said so, at the Roosts, tying wet sheets over noses. It rolled along the ground. It didn't go up.

He looked up.

Above the smoke, the sky. Grey. Clean. Not far. A few hundred feet.

And going up into it, out of the smoke, dragons.

---

The Clan's. Arn's. Red and grey. With cloths over their noses. Wet. Tied round their muzzles with straps, as Gunna had shown them. Going up. Out of it. Arn on Ildra at the front, small, red-gold, with a cloth over her nose too, looking back and shouting.

Arn had been ready.

Arn had stood on the ridge all night watching men with watches.

---

*Up*, he said. *We have to go up.*

---

He went across.

From the lion's head. To Aurveth's neck, in the water beside the lion's flank, where she lay with her head up, shaking. She'd stopped screaming. She was making a sound now he'd never heard. Low. Like an old woman crying in another room.

He put his hands on her neck.

*Here*, he said. *Old one. It's me. I'm here.*

*Farmer*, said Aurveth.

Faint. From the end of the rope.

*Up*, he said. *Come up. With me.*

---

She came up.

Slowly. As she did everything. Out of the water. Her wings opening. Shedding the eddy off them in sheets. Up. Through the smoke. Slowly, because she was old and the smoke was in her and her wings were shaking.

He talked to her all the way.

*Up. That's it. Up. I'm here. I'm here.*

---

Lisska came after his voice.

He felt her come. Out of the smoke, somewhere below. Following the sound of him down the slack rope. Green. Up. Her wings going wrong and then less wrong.

And far off, over the shallows, Kharrak. Turning. Turning. Then up.

---

They came out of it at four hundred feet.

---

Into clean air. Cold. Grey sky. Aurveth's wings beating slow and heavy in it. The smoke below them, lying on the Vale.

And the ropes came back.

---

Not right. He felt them come taut again, one after another. The bronze. Then the green. Then, a long way off, the rust. As a rope that has been slack comes taut when the boat is pulled back in. With a jerk. With everything that had drifted out on the current still in it.

But taut.

---

*I was gone*, said Lisska.

Beside him. Level with Aurveth, green, shaking.

*I was gone. I was gone. Where was I?*

"Here," said Calder. Out loud. "With me. You were with me."

*I couldn't hear you.*

"You did, though. You came."

She was quiet. She hadn't been quiet since the morning on the eddy.

*I came*, she said.

---

He looked down.

---

The Vale lay under a sea of smoke.

Yellow-grey. From the downs to the scrub. Over both banks. Over the river. Lying. Rolling a little in the wind off the west. And things sticking up out of it.

The six jibs at the eddy's edge, black, with the chains going out from them. The chains themselves, taut, across the eddy, glowing white along every link. The Bridle's ring, on the Flat, with its twelve engines roaring and their chimneys going up through the smoke. And on the ring's top, the stage. And a man on it.

And in the eddy, a grey hill. Sliding.

---

He looked at the line.

What he could see of it. The walls, grey lines through the smoke on both banks. The wards over them. Flickering. The Hearthmere one dark in long stretches. The Saltreach one thin, pale blue, the Lady's colour only, the gold gone out of it.

And the gaps.

---

Five.

He counted them. He didn't count things. He counted these.

The bend. Where the shadow had been. The Wyldmarch's river end. Where the shadow had been. Saltreach's scrub end. Where the shadow had been. And both batteries, either side of the hub, where Kessmoor's crews had stood all day and weren't standing now.

Five places where there was nothing.

---

And through them, two.

---

The Coil. Coming down the channel from the north. Past where the bar had been, across the river, which was nothing now. Pale. Fast. Under the ice, and then out of it. Into the Hearthmere bend's sector.

Where the shadow had been. Where the knights had been, before their hounds turned on them.

And behind the bend, the Hearthmere camp.

---

He saw the rime go across it.

White. Racing. Over the tents. Over the fires. Over the carts. A quarter-league in a breath. Feathering. And people running out of it. Into the smoke. Into the cold. Children. He could see them from four hundred feet. Small dark shapes in the white, running.

---

And the Sun.

---

Coming up the river. Out of the shallows. Its half-league of day flaring white again, the river boiling round it.

Up the Wend. Past the lower lip. Past the eddy.

And out. Onto the Saltreach bank. At the Wyldmarch's river end. Where the knight had raised three ridges and a trench yesterday out of the sliding earth, and the clans had stood behind them.

Into the Wyldmarch's line.

Into the clans' camp behind it.

---

He saw the tents burn.

As the scrub had burned. The Sun walking up the bank out of the river onto the camp, and the tents going up white in front of it, a row at a time. The carts. The stonehorns' fodder. The clans running. North. Away from it. Into the smoke.

North. Toward the scrub. Toward the pontoon's end and the Drover's Way.

Across a league of open ground.

And the Sun turning after them.

---

*The coal*, said Aurveth.

---

Beside him. Under him. She was looking. She always was.

*It's still there. Under the chains. I can see it. In its back. Between the second egg and the third.* She was quiet. *Burning.*

He looked.

Through her. Down. Through the smoke. Into the eddy. Into the grey shape under the chains, rising. The plates. The five dark eggs. And the coal.

Red. Bright.

Still there. Nobody had it. Not the Lamp; the Lamp was in the water, dimming. Not Kessmoor; the chains were on it, but the coal was in the back, under the plates.

Nobody.

---

He could go.

He could take Aurveth down. Into the eddy. To the coal. He didn't know what he'd do there. He didn't know what anyone could do there, without the Lamp. But he could go.

He looked at the coal.

Then he looked at the clans' camp burning, and the people running north across the open ground in front of the Sun.

And the Hearthmere camp, white, with children in it.

---

He went to the people.

---

He didn't decide. He'd never decided anything. He looked at the coal and then at the camps, and something in him turned, as it had turned at the Roosts and at Gullhaven and at Corsa, and he was already going.

*Kharrak*, he said.

*Here.*

*The Sun. The clans. The open ground. North of the river end.*

*I see it.*

*Don't kill it*, said Calder. *Hold the road.*

---

Kharrak came over the smoke from the shallows.

Red. Huge. His blistered face. Out of the south, low over the yellow-grey, his wings going strong again now that the rope was taut. Over the Saltreach bank. Over the burning camp. To the open ground where the clans were running.

And put himself across the Sun's road.

---

White fire.

Not at it. He'd learned. *Small fire.* He'd learned at the Wend's mouth with the Bell, and all day yesterday at the scrub end, holding.

Across it.

A wall. From the river's edge, where the Sun had come up out of the water, east. Along the ground. A league. Between the Sun and the open ground where the clans were running.

White. Standing. A league of it.

---

And behind it, a road.

---

Clear ground. Between the fire and the scrub. A league long. Open. North. To the pontoon's end and the Drover's Way.

The clans on it. Running. Then walking, because the fire was between them and the Sun now, and they could walk. Stonehorns among them, that had scattered at noon and were coming back to their people out of the thinning smoke. Carts. The old ones on the carts. Rhun's wolves.

Going north. Behind the fire.

---

*Hold the road*, he said.

*I can't kill it anyway*, said Kharrak. *It's too bright.*

*Then hold.*

*I'm holding.*

He went across to the red one at the fire's end, in the air, as he'd gone across a hundred times.

---

Lisska went to the Hearthmere camp.

He sent her. Across the river. Over the white. Where the Coil's rime was racing over the tents and the children were running in it.

She went low. Under the cold. As she'd gone under the Sun's heat yesterday over the Saltreach camp. And put her wind under it.

Up.

He saw the rime lift. Off the tents. In a long white sheet, going up into the clean air above the smoke. Up, and away, east, over the river.

*There*, said Lisska. *There.*

She didn't say *easy*.

---

Aurveth saw for him.

High. In the clean air. With her head down. Looking into the smoke, through it, where nobody else could see.

*There*, she said. *By the Hearthmere wall's foot. Under the bend. Two hundred. Under the smoke. They can't see where to go.*

*Arn*, he said.

He didn't know if Arn could hear him. Arn wasn't his. He shouted it anyway, in his ruined voice, at the red-gold shape over the centre. And pointed.

Arn saw him point.

Arn took his pairs down. Into the smoke. With the cloths over their dragons' noses. With baskets.

---

And he shouted at the people.

Down. From Kharrak's neck, when Kharrak came round at the end of the fire's line. At the clans on the open ground, at the stragglers, at anyone who'd look up.

*North.*

His voice. Nothing. A crack in a pot. They couldn't hear it from three hundred feet.

He shouted anyway.

*North. The Drover's Way. North.*

---

He could feel the bank under them humming.

Alive. The Saltreach bank. Where the knight had said the stand-word yesterday into the sliding earth. He could feel it through Kharrak, through the fire going into the ground. The Deep. Come up. Live.

He didn't think anything of it.

He didn't think of anything but the road, and the fire, and the people on it.

---

In the afternoon there was a rent in the smoke.

---

Over the eddy. The wind shifted, and the yellow-grey tore open for a breath, and he looked down through the hole.

---

The eddy. The chains, twelve of them, taut, from the jibs across the grey water to the great shape in the middle. Glowing. The great shape rising. Its back out of the water now, the plates, the five dark eggs.

The lion. Beside it. Sliding. Off his bed. Sideways. His great grey head low.

And between the lion and the Last Titan, on a chain, something small.

---

Climbing.

---

On one of the Bridle's chains. Taut. Black. Glowing. Going from a jib across the water and down onto the Last Titan's back.

A person.

On it. Hand over hand. With one hand. And her knees. Going up the chain from where it came over the lion's shoulder, toward the Last Titan's back. Her left arm hanging. A blue coat. A green cloak, gone.

He knew who it was.

---

She couldn't feel the iron. She'd told him. On the ferry. *I can't feel anything. In my hands. I watch them.*

He watched her watch her hand. Go onto the chain. Close. Watch it close. Then her knees. Then the hand again.

She couldn't feel the iron.

She was climbing it anyway.

---

The smoke closed.

He didn't see her again.

---

*How long?* said Kharrak.

At the end of the fire's line. Turning. His breath short. Calder could feel it. The red one's great chest heaving. Fire coming out of him for an hour. Two. More. A league of it, against a sun.

*How long?*

*As long as it takes*, said Calder.

*That's not an answer.*

*It's the only one I've got.*

Kharrak flew along the fire again, low, and put more of it down, white, across the Sun's road, between it and the people going north.

*It'll do*, said Kharrak.
