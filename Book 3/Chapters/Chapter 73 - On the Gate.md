---

She saw it all from the wall.

The wax. She didn't hear it; she saw it, across the Flat, below her, at the jibs and the bolt-throwers. Eight men in grey taking packets out of their coats at once, and breaking the seals, and reading.

At noon. On the hour. Without her.

---

She saw the jibs swing. She saw the chains go over the Last Titan. She saw the bolt-throwers turn from the line on both banks, the line she'd put them on, and fire into the light on the Last Titan's back.

She saw the Lamp's horn come up out of the plates.

She saw the magazine's doors open. She saw the Hearthmere guard on the ground in front of it. She saw a cask go past a brazier with a broken seal on its bung.

Hers.

---

She didn't move.

She sat on her greyhart on the wall's top at its river end, with two swords at her belt, and watched Kessmoor take her line apart, and didn't move, for the space of three breaths.

Three breaths. She'd remember that. If there was anything to remember with.

---

The falcon knight came at the fourth breath.

Up the wall's face, by the steps at its foot, running, gasping. The young woman who'd guarded the old man since midwinter. With her falcon on her shoulder, flapping.

"Marshal. Master Vael says. The batteries open their orders on the hour without your word. The Bridle means to take it. Tobin said. And Callis."

She'd run the long way round. By the wall's foot. Rowan could see it in her face.

"I know," said Rowan. "I can see it."

---

Then the smoke reached the wall.

---

Yellow-grey. Rolling. Over the wall's top. Sweet. Burnt. Into her mouth.

And the greyhart went.

---

Under her. She felt it go. Not a shy. Something going out of it. Its head coming up, and round, and its eye on her, and nothing in its eye.

It reared.

She went off. Backwards. Over its quarters. Onto the wall's top, onto the stone, on her back, hard. And the greyhart came down on its forefeet where her head had been, and screamed, and was gone. Along the wall. Into the smoke. At a gallop.

The white blaze, going.

---

She got up.

She had both swords. She'd kept both swords. She'd been a knight thirty years; you kept your sword when you fell, if you kept nothing else.

Haverly's edge on her left hip. Her own on her right. She stood up on the wall's top in the smoke with her back aching and looked at the Vale.

---

The hounds had turned.

She could hear it. Along the wall. West, toward the bend. Screaming. Not the hounds. The knights. And the hounds. Snarling, and screaming, and the sound a hound makes when its knight kills it.

The greyharts bolting through the militia. The olifants trumpeting, breaking, through Sarovel's people. She could hear all of it and see none of it in the smoke.

---

"Runners," she said.

They were there. Her four. The corn-exchange porters. They'd come up the wall after the falcon knight, because that was where she was. And the signaller from the river watch, with his bag of flags, white-faced.

---

"Every camp," said Rowan.

She'd thought about this. Not today. In the autumn. In the barn. On her knees in the frost at the empty cradle. She'd thought: if it comes, what's the one thing I'll say.

"Run north. Every camp. Now. The Drover's Way. The River Road. Any road north." She looked at them. "By the Marshal's word."

---

It was the only word she had left that anybody would obey.

She'd given her word on the Bridle. On the smoke. On the batteries holding their sectors. On the clause. Her word, sealed, minuted, read aloud. Kessmoor had agreed to every one of them, as fast as she'd said them.

Her word was nothing now. At the centre.

At the camps it was still the Marshal's.

---

They went.

The four runners down the wall, two each way, at a run, shouting it. The signaller up onto the wall's parapet with his flags.

She watched the flags go. *North. North. All camps.* And along the wall, a league off, another signaller's flags answering. And across the river, on the Saltreach wall, through the smoke, faint, another.

*North.*

---

Then she looked at the gap.

---

Below her. East. Between the wall's river end and the Ferry Flat. The Hearthmere battery's sector. Where Kessmoor's crews had stood with their bolt-throwers since the autumn, facing west.

Empty.

They'd gone in. To the Bridle. All of them. As the orders said.

And behind the gap, the camp.

---

The Hearthmere camp's eastern end. Tents and carts and fires, a half-league of them, from the wall back toward the downs. And through the middle of it, a road. The camp's own road, trodden since the autumn, going north-west across the Vale to the River Road and the upper Wend.

North.

People on it. Already. The flags had reached them; or they hadn't needed flags. Thousands. Going north-west up the camp's road with what they could carry, out of the smoke.

And at the gap's river edge, cold.

---

She felt it come.

Before she saw it. The air going hard. The smoke going white. Rime coming over the wall's edge from the river. Feathering on the stone.

The Coil.

---

It had come down the channel. Past where the lion's bar had been. Along the river's edge behind the wall, under the Hearthmere bank, in the water, pale.

And now it was coming out of the river.

Into the gap.

---

"Knights," said Rowan.

---

She didn't know how many she had.

She went down off the wall by the steps at its foot and stood in the gap, in the empty battery's sector, with the camp's road behind her and the river in front, and the rime coming over the frozen grass toward her, and shouted it.

"*Knights. To me.*"

---

They came.

Out of the smoke. From the bend. From the hub. From wherever they'd been when their beasts turned.

On foot. All of them.

---

Wynn first.

His left forearm bound up in a strip of his own surcoat, soaked dark. Where he'd got it up between his throat and Ash's teeth. And at his knee, a hound.

Sorrow. The other one. Grey, shaking, with its eyes back in it. Come back through the smoke to its knight.

Ash hadn't.

"I had to," said Wynn. He didn't say what. "Sorrow came back."

---

The woman with the falcon. Her bird gone up out of the smoke, somewhere, wild.

The knights from the bend. Twenty. Thirty. Bonded and unbonded. She couldn't tell which, now. The bonded had nothing at their knees. Their hounds were dead, or gone, or worse. Their greyharts were running somewhere in the smoke with their eyes white.

The unbonded had never had anything at their knees.

They looked the same.

---

Militia. With pikes. Farm-men and porters and a forge's apprentices. Fifty. Who'd seen the knights going to the Marshal and come too.

A dozen of Mattias's unchosen. On foot. Their white steeds gone. In Sarovel's grey.

Ninety, she thought. Perhaps ninety.

---

She looked at them.

In the gap. In the rime coming off the river. Ninety people with swords and pikes and nothing at their knees. Bonded and unbonded. Hearthmere and Sarovel. Knights and porters.

---

"On the gate," said Rowan, "there is no bond."

---

They'd all been first-years once. Some of them. At Greyhold, at Millbrook, at the river schools. They'd learned it before they learned to hold a spear. The oldest rule. The proudest.

*On the gate, there is no bond.*

When a knight went up onto the river gate, bonded or not, they stood the watch alone, and were counted the same as every other knight on the wall. One sword. One pair of eyes.

Equals.

---

The ones who knew it straightened. She saw them. The ones who didn't know it looked at the ones who did, and straightened too.

---

"Here," she said.

She walked out into the gap. To its middle. Between the river and the camp's road. And drew.

---

Both swords.

Her own in her left. Plain. Hearthmere steel, thirty years in her hand.

And Haverly's edge in her right.

She looked at it.

---

Dark.

The strokes along its flat. The quickened letters Haverly's smith had cut, that woke on live ground and burned white along the edge. Dark. Every one. This was the Last Titan's ground. He'd drunk it for three hundred years. There was nothing in it to wake a blade.

The knight had said so. In the yard. *It'll be dark on the day.*

*It's still steel.*

---

It was still steel.

That was what she had. Not a chain. Not a ward; the ward was going out over her head, in long dark stretches. Not a word; her word was nothing at the centre.

Steel.

---

"A line," she said. "Across the gap. Shoulder to shoulder."

They made it.

Ninety. Across the empty battery's sector. From the wall's foot to the Flat's western edge. Knights and porters and unchosen. Shoulder to shoulder. Facing the river.

Not a chain.

People.

With the road behind them. With the people on it going north.

---

The Coil came out of the river.

---

Its head first. Out of the water at the gap's edge. Out of the rime. Long. Pale. Pike-shaped. The size of a barn. Its eyes like ice over deep water. Frost on every scale.

And the cold.

---

The front rank froze.

Not all of them. Some. Where they stood. She saw a porter at the line's river end, a young man with a pike, go white. All at once. As a window frosts. His pike white. His face white. Standing. Not moving.

The cold came off the Coil like a wall. A spear's length of it. Anything inside it, stopped.

---

She went forward.

Out of the line. Into the cold. A pace. Two. Toward the head.

She couldn't feel her hands. She didn't need to. She'd watched the knight not feel hers all winter. You looked. You watched your hand.

She watched her right hand bring Haverly's edge up.

And cut.

---

At the jaw. Where the pale scales met under the long head. Steel on frost-scale.

It rang.

Not as a blade rings on a Titan's plate; the knight had told her that, once, about the cavern. *Like a hammer on an anvil.* This was higher. Like a blade on ice.

And left a mark. A long white scar, across the scales, where the edge had gone.

---

The Coil reared.

Up. Its head going up out of the rime. Away from her. Its body coming up out of the river behind it, pale, coiling.

She cut again. At the throat, as it went up. Another scar.

And the pikes came past her. The militia. Shoulder to shoulder behind her, a dozen pikes going up at the pale throat together, as a hedge goes up.

---

It came round.

Its body. Out of the river. Through the gap's river edge. A long pale wall of it, coiling, coming round through the line's end like a wall falling. Slowly.

Men went under it.

She heard them.

---

Then it stopped.

---

She saw it stop. The great pale head, up, over the line. And turning. Away from her. Toward the river. Toward the eddy.

Listening, as a hound listens when it hears its name.

Something was calling it.

---

She looked where it looked.

---

East. Over the Flat. Over the eddy. Through the smoke, where the wind had torn it for a breath.

The chains. Twelve of them, taut, glowing, from the jibs to the great grey shape rising in the eddy's middle. Its back out of the water. The plates. The five dark eggs.

And on the chains, climbing, a man.

---

From the jib nearest the stage. Up the chain. Toward the Last Titan's back. Tall. In grey. Climbing well. Fast. With his left hand and his right.

And his right hand flashed.

Iron. In the grey light. On the black chain. Gripping. A made hand. Kessmoor's.

Climbing toward the coal.

---

And below him, on another chain, small, slow, one-handed, a woman.

She knew who.

---

The Coil turned its head back.

To her.

Whatever had called it, hadn't finished calling. It looked down at the woman with two swords in the gap and the ninety behind her and the road behind them.

And came on.

---

She held.

---

She didn't know how long. An hour. More. The Coil coming on, and the line holding, and the cold. The front rank going white. The second rank coming up into it. Pikes. Swords. Haverly's edge, dark, ringing on frost-scale, again and again, leaving white scars.

Behind her, the road.

She looked back at it once. Through the smoke. Thinning. The camp's road going away north-west across the Vale. People on it. Fewer. Fewer. The flags still going along the wall: *north*.

Emptying.

---

Wynn beside her. With his torn arm. With Sorrow at his knee, shaking, growling at the cold.

"Marshal."

"Hold."

"Marshal, the road's nearly clear."

"Then hold till it is."

---

She thought about the clause.

She didn't know why. In the cold, with Haverly's edge going up and down in her right hand, that she was watching because she couldn't feel it.

*I'll not be the first*, she'd said. To the farmer. At the table, on the water-meadow, with the Wyldmarch walking away. *If I break it the day it costs us, every kingdom at this table breaks the next one the day it costs them.*

She hadn't been the first.

---

*Agreed*, Pike had said. *Agreed. Of course, Marshal.*

As fast as she'd said them.

She'd been glad.

---

The Coil's body came over the line's middle.

Where she stood.

---

A wall of it. Pale. Coming down. Coiling. Over the line. Over her.

And the cold.

---

It was very quiet.

She'd thought it would hurt. It didn't. It came over her and everything went white, and still, and very quiet. Like snow falling at night on Greyhold's yard when she was a first-year and stood the gate alone for the first time.

One sword. One pair of eyes.

---

She stood in it.

With both swords up.

---

Wynn saw her.

After. When the Coil had gone past. On, toward the river, toward whatever was calling it in the eddy, its body sliding back through the gap into the water. Wynn on his knees in the rime with Sorrow beside him and the road behind him empty.

He saw the Marshal.

---

Standing. In the middle of the gap. White.

Frozen standing, as the porter had frozen at the line's end. In the rime. Her mended surcoat white. Her face white. Her eyes open, looking east, over the eddy.

Her own sword in her left hand.

Haverly's edge in her right. Raised. White with frost along the flat.

And under the frost, the letters.

Dark.

---

Behind her, the road was empty.

Everyone who'd been on it had gone north.
