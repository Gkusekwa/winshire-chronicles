---

She took the fourth chain.

It came over Orrun's shoulder. Black. Taut. From the jib at the eddy's edge, down across the water, over the lion's shoulder where he'd stood beside his kin, and on, up, onto the Last Titan's back behind its left shoulder.

The one nearest the coal.

She put her hand on it.

---

She couldn't feel it.

She looked. Her right hand on the black iron. Bandaged. Tam's knot. She watched her fingers go round the link. She watched them close.

Then her knees. Round the chain. Then the hand again. Higher.

The link under her hand was as long as a man and as thick as her waist. On its inside, where she couldn't see, the *hold*. She could see its light coming through the iron's edges. White. Thin.

She climbed.

---

She'd climbed the far shore's cliff with one arm. She'd climbed down a lion's face a hundred times. She'd never climbed a chain.

It was easier than she'd thought. And harder. You didn't need to feel. You needed to look, and to put your hand where you looked, and to trust that it closed. And to do it again. And not look down.

She looked down once.

Orrun. Below her. In the water. Sliding. His great grey head turned up toward her. His pale eyes. At the end of a slack rope.

*Hold on to me*, she'd said.

She didn't know if he had.

---

The chain moved under her.

Moving. Along. Toward the Flat. She felt it through her knees. The Bridle. Hauling. Twelve engines, breathing, on twelve chains, pulling the Last Titan across the eddy toward the bank.

She looked up the chain.

The great grey back coming toward the Flat's edge. Toward the jib-posts. Its flank coming up against the bank by the posts, in the water, with a grinding sound.

She climbed.

---

She came onto its back behind the left shoulder.

---

Plates.

She'd seen them through Aurveth's eyes, through the farmer's. She'd never stood on them. Grey. Like a hillside of slates. Each one as big as a barn door, overlapping the next, going up to the ridge of the spine and down to the water. Cold. Hard. Ridged.

And grown into them, five dark shapes.

---

Egg-shaped. Cart-sized. Dark grey, almost black. Set into the plates as stones are set into a wall that has grown round them over a long time. Cold. She could feel the cold off them on her face, the right side. The sleep relics. That the old ones had made to keep the six asleep. Grown into the back of the one that had woken them.

She walked between them.

Between the second and the third.

---

The crack was there.

Where the Lamp's horn had been. A hand's breadth. Two. A split in the plates, ragged, going down. And out of it, red light. Coming up. Lighting the underside of the plates round it red. Warm on her face.

The coal.

---

And at the crack, a man.

---

Kneeling.

Tall. In a grey drover's coat. His back to her. A long case lying on the plates beside him, open, empty; the roll was on the stage, she thought, on the stand. He'd left it there.

He was looking down into the crack.

He'd come before her. Up a chain from the jib nearest the stage, faster than she could climb, while the Bridle hauled. The Titan's flank lay against the Flat's edge now, by the jib-posts, and the posts had a platform round their feet, iron, for the crews. You could step from the platform onto the plates.

---

He heard her.

He turned his head.

---

The long pale face. The short dark hair. The grey eyes.

His brother's. She'd seen it on his brother, at the lock, under the school, with her sword in him. She'd seen it on him in the cavern, at the relic, with his hand turning to stone.

He didn't get up.

"Ashdown," said Corvin.

---

His right hand was in his lap.

Iron. Black. Jointed. Pinned. The brass tendons. She'd heard of it from the old man; she'd never seen it. He moved the fingers. One after another. *Click.* As if counting.

---

"You killed my brother," said Corvin.

He said it as he said everything. Without heat. As a man reads out a figure.

"Yes," said Maren.

---

He nodded.

As at a figure. As if she'd confirmed a number he'd had written down for years.

He looked back at the crack.

---

"It held," he said. "The Bridle. You'll have felt it." He nodded at the chains, glowing, going over the back to the jibs. "Twelve engines, breathing. Unbound. The old man's line, read properly." He almost smiled. "By engineers."

She didn't say anything.

"So," said Corvin. "Now I lift it out. And carry it to the ring. There's a hollow cut for it." He held up the iron hand. "This. Not flesh. It took my flesh last time. This is iron. Kessmoor's. Made by no one. Worked by nothing. A made thing." He moved the fingers. "An engine breathes. An iron hand holds."

---

She looked at the hand.

Then she held out hers.

---

Her right. Bandaged. She watched it go out. She couldn't feel it go. She watched it. It went out, toward him, over the red light coming up out of the crack, open. The bandage. Tam's knot.

"Give it to me," said Maren.

---

He looked at her hand.

---

"Not to them," she said. "Not to Kessmoor. Not to the ring. Not to anyone." She didn't take her eyes off him. "Not to me, either. I don't want it. I'll give it to the Lamp, if the Lamp's alive. I'll throw it in the river. I'll put it back. I don't care."

She held her hand out.

"Give it," she said. "Don't take it. Give."

---

He looked at her hand.

---

She saw him look. His grey eyes on her bandaged hand, out over the crack. She saw something go across his face. She didn't know what it was. She'd never seen it on him. She'd never seen it on his brother.

His left hand moved.

His own hand. Flesh. The forearm stiff where a hound had bitten it, on a ship, a year ago.

It moved. An inch. Toward hers.

---

Then it stopped.

---

He looked away from her hand. Back. Into the crack. At the red light.

And she saw him decide. She'd seen people decide before. She'd decided, often enough. It went across his face and was gone and his face was as it had always been. Still. Patient.

He turned to the crack.

---

And knelt.

---

Properly. Not as he'd been kneeling, on one knee, looking. Both knees. On the plates. His back straight. His head bowed a little. His hands in front of him.

As a man kneels at a wayside shrine.

She'd seen that too. Under the school. At the lock. Him, kneeling at the old door in the first winter, with his head bowed, while his brother talked. The same.

---

He put his iron hand into the crack.

Down. Into the red light. Onto the coal.

---

The iron went white.

She saw it. From the fingers. White-hot, then white, not hot, white as the *hold* on the chains, all at once, the whole hand. Then grey.

Then stone.

Up to the cuff. A hand of grey stone, on the coal, in the red light, the brass tendons gone grey with it, the pins grey, all one.

---

He looked at it.

"Oh," said Corvin.

Quietly. As a man says *oh* when he's added a column and found it doesn't come out.

---

The stone hand didn't lift.

He tried. She saw his shoulder move. The stone hand lay on the coal and didn't lift. It was part of the coal now. Part of the plates.

He looked at it.

Then he put his own hand on it.

---

His left. Flesh. Into the crack. Down. Over the stone hand. Onto the coal beside it.

To lift it with both.

---

It took him.

---

She watched it go.

Grey. Out of the crack. Up his left hand. Up the wrist, under the sleeve. She saw it come out at the collar. On his neck. Grey. Up. His jaw. His mouth. His cheek.

He didn't make a sound.

His eyes were open. Grey too, now. Looking down at the coal. Still.

---

Stone.

Kneeling. At the crack. A man of grey stone, both hands in the red light, his head bowed, his back straight. As a man kneels at a wayside shrine.

For a breath.

---

Then the stone went to sand.

Grey sand. All at once. As a sandcastle goes when the tide reaches it. The kneeling shape slumping. Running. Down. Into the crack. Through the plates. Into the red light. Like sand through fingers.

Gone.

The coal brighter.

---

Old magic wasn't for people.

The old man had said that. On the far shore. In the Hall. *It took his hand at the relic. It isn't for us. It never was.*

It had taken his hand. Now it had taken all of him.

For nothing.

He'd always said, in the old man's telling, that he didn't want his brother's death to be for nothing.

---

Callis was there.

She didn't see him come. Not up a chain; he couldn't have. From the jib-posts' platform onto the plates. Walking. With his crooked arms at his sides. As he'd walked the gate.

He was behind where Corvin had knelt.

He'd reached for him. At the end. She saw it after, in her head, when she tried to remember. Callis coming up behind the kneeling man and putting both his bad arms round him, from behind, to pull him off the coal.

---

The grey went into him too.

Through his arms. Round Corvin. Into his chest.

He looked at her. Over the place where Corvin's shoulder had been. His grey face going greyer.

"It's all right," said Callis.

To Corvin, she thought. Or to her. Or to nobody.

He went with him.

Sand. Down the crack. Into the red light.

---

She stood on the plates alone.

With her bandaged hand still out.

She took it back.

---

Then the chains went.

---

She felt it under her feet first. Through the plates. Through the stone. Through the Titan.

The *hold*.

---

On every link. All twelve chains. Over the back, across the eddy, to the jibs, to the ring. Glowing. White. Brighter. All at once. As a lamp flares before it goes out.

She felt it close.

On the Last Titan. As it had closed for three hundred years under Greyhold. The *hold*, cut on the inside of every link. Closing. Tight. Feeding.

For one breath.

---

Then she felt it open.

---

As a hand opens. Not torn. Opening. *Let go*, the word on the links said, instead of *tighten*. She didn't know it. She'd know it later, from the old man, sitting in the snow: a stroke turned the wrong way in the third curve, on a ship, a year ago. And Corvin's own wrong mending of it in the fourth.

The chain-words he'd read from the stage had carried it.

The *hold* let go.

---

The light went out of the links.

Link by link. From the Titan's back, outward. Along every chain. Across the eddy. To the jibs. To the ring. White, then grey, then dark. As candles go out along a table when someone walks down it.

The chains went slack.

All twelve. At once. Falling. Across the plates, across the water, with a sound like a forge's whole stock of bars dropped on a stone floor.

---

And the Last Titan rose.

---

She felt it rise. Under her. The whole hill of it. Getting up out of the eddy. The chains sliding off it. Its wings coming up. Its head.

And she felt what was in it.

---

Nothing.

---

She'd felt it before. Under Greyhold, through Orrun, at the end of its chains. Rage. Three hundred years of it. On the downs, yesterday, walking along the ward: hunger, and something like patience. In the cavern: that one line, to Orrun. *Little brother.*

Now, nothing.

It had been chained again. For one breath. And let go. And something in it had gone past rage, past hunger, past anything. Into a stillness.

Worse than rage.

---

It used the relic.

---

She felt the coal flare under her feet.

Through the plates. Red. White. All of it at once. The relic. The stone the old ones had made so that one of them could hold it and call the six into the light.

Held. By a Titan. Who wasn't one of them.

Calling.

---

She heard it.

In the stone. In everything. Through her feet, through the plates, through the Titan, through the ground under the eddy, through the knot where every kingdom's tide-lines met. Out. In every direction. A sound under sound.

A call.

---

And on the horizons, they answered.

---

She felt them answer. East, on the Saltreach bank, behind the farmer's fire: the Sun, flaring white. Near, in the river by the Flat: the Coil, lifting its head. Far south, at the mouth: the Bell.

And in the coal under her feet, the two that had gone into it. The Antlered. The Furnace. Their breaths. Answering too. From inside it.

---

The jibs went.

She saw them go. The six iron posts at the eddy's edge, with their chains, torn off. The Titan rising, and the slack chains still on the jibs, and the jibs coming off the posts as a man pulls weeds out of a wall.

And the chains went back across the Flat to the ring.

And pulled.

---

Three engines went into the eddy.

On their own chains. Off their beds. Across the Flat's turf, dragged, the size of barns, their fireboxes spilling coals, and over the edge into the water. Steam. The stage going over. The ring tipping in its cradle.

On the Flat, people. Crews in grey. Running. Not fast enough.

And by the ring, small, with a book in her hand, a woman with spectacles.

---

She saw Pike look up.

At the Titan rising over her. At the engines going into the water. At the ring tipping.

Pike didn't run. She looked up at it with her spectacles pushed up on her forehead, as she'd looked at everything. As if she were measuring it.

The ring came over on her.

---

*Maren.*

---

Him.

Taut. The rope. All at once. She felt it come taut, as a mooring rope comes taut when the boat on the end swings back on the tide. A jerk. In her chest. In her middle, where the cold was.

*Maren.*

---

The smoke had thinned on the water. She didn't know when. He'd come back. Up the slack rope. Out of wherever he'd drifted. To her.

She felt him reach.

---

His jaws closed on her coat.

---

Behind her. On her back. The Warden's blue coat that was too big for her. His great grey jaws, from below, from the water, reaching up the rising flank of his kin, and closing on the coat between her shoulders. Gently. As a cat takes a kitten by the scruff.

As he'd dragged her out of the Wend by her coat once, under the ice, in the first winter, before she knew what he was.

He lifted her.

Off the plates. Off the rising back. Out over the water.

And down.

---

Into the eddy. Into the grey water. With her coat in his jaws. Holding her up. Her head out. The water going over her and she didn't feel it.

She hung from his jaws in the eddy and looked up at the Last Titan.

Standing. Over the eddy. Its wings open. Its chains gone. The coal in its back blazing through the plates.

Calling.

---

*I've got you*, said Orrun.

Round the coat. Through his teeth.

*I've got you. I've got you.*

---

She looked past him. South. Down the river. Farther than eyes go: through him, through the long reach of his sense in the stone of the river-bed, a hundred leagues of it.

To the mouth.

---

The Lady.

At the Wend's mouth. Between the headlands. In the white water where the shadow had stood all morning, and wasn't standing now.

Wound round the Bell.

Coil on coil. Pale on the long dark back of it. As she'd wound round the Furnace, and the Coil. Round and round. Holding it. Out in the sea. Out of the river. So that it couldn't come up the Wend into the camps.

The bell rang. Under the sea. Over and over.

She held it.
