---

He carried her out of the eddy in his jaws.

By her coat. As a cat carries a kitten. Out of the water that was going down into the ground round them, up the Saltreach bank's broken lip, which he'd told to hold, and it held.

Up. After the herd.

She hung from his teeth and watched the Vale go away under her. The four on the field. The hole with the river going into it. The light on every horizon.

At midnight he put her down and she climbed onto his head.

She didn't remember climbing. She was on his head, between his ears, where she always was.

---

They went north all night.

Behind the herd. Behind the farmer's word.

---

She could feel it.

In the ground. Under Orrun's feet. Under the herd's. Under everything. The live ground of the Saltreach bank, roaring with the surge, and in it, running north through it, a word.

Short. Sharp. In the old tongue.

His father's word. For turning cattle.

---

It was still going.

He'd said it hours ago, with his hand on a tusker cow and his other hand on the ground and three dragons behind him. And it had gone into the ground and out through the ground and turned the whole broken army north.

And it hadn't stopped.

It was running north under them. Through the earth. Under every foot. Turning them. Keeping them turned. Keeping them walking.

As driven cattle walk. All night. Without stopping. Because the word behind them won't let them stop.

---

She heard it as his voice.

He had none. She'd seen him try to speak, on Kharrak's neck, over the column, in the dusk, and nothing come. He pointed now. He'd been pointing all night.

But in the ground she heard it. His voice. Ruined, cracked, a crack in a pot. Saying one word over and over, under everything, all the way north.

---

They came to Harrow's End at dawn.

---

Below the village. Where the Vale's scrub ended and the land went up. Past the roofs, past the farmer's top field and the dark of the Fellwood above it, north and east, in a long brown-white swell, to the Scarfell's edge a day off. White on its tops. The snow.

And the roads.

---

Six of them.

---

She saw them from his head as the light came up. Six lines coming up out of the Vale toward the high ground. Six streams of people on them.

The Drover's Way. The broad one. Rutted. The farmer's road. With the most on it. The broken army's main stream, and her brother's wagons, and the Staithe's four hundred that had come up from the south in the night.

East of it, the scrub track. Narrower. Through the last of the gorse before the moor. The clans. With their carts. With their stonehorns.

West of it, the towpath. Along the riverbed. The river's old bed, empty, mud and stones and pools, going north up the Vale. Sarovel's people walking beside it. And grey shapes among them. Olifants.

Further west, across the drained river, the River Road. The Hearthmere bank's road, north to Eskby. Rowan's people. The camp's. The knights'.

North-west, a straight line. Old paving under the turf, she could see it in the dawn. The Oneroad. The road of the one realm, that went out to every kingdom from the middle of the world. The tuskers on it, in front, as the cow had found it in the dark. Isvaren's people behind.

And the moor track. Straight up from Cotter's Cross. Everyone else.

Six.

---

Too many people.

She could see that too. Too many, on six roads, going up into the Scarfell in deep winter, a day from its edge, with the four on the Vale behind them. Too slow. Too strung out. The tails of the columns still on the Vale, in the scrub, at the riverbed's edge, a league back. More.

And behind them, the light.

---

She waited.

All morning, below the village, with him lying under her, while the columns went up past them. She counted them going past. She lost count. She waited because he was so tired, and because she hoped.

At the second hour after noon the light on the Vale moved.

She saw it from his head. Far off. Over the hole. The white light that had stood there since yesterday, blazing. Moving.

And the tails of the columns were still in the scrub.

---

She looked down at him.

At his head under her. His mane, scorched black on one side, frosted on the other. His flank, burned by the chains three times, and the shadows' wounds in it.

*Orrun*, she said.

*Yes.*

---

*Six*, she said.

He was quiet.

*Six?*

*One for each road.*

He was quiet a long time. She felt him think about it, slowly, as stone thinks.

*That's most of me*, said Orrun.

*I know.*

---

He didn't argue.

He never had. Not once. Not on the island, not at the Ring, not on the far shore when she'd said yes to the Mother. He'd said *I know* and *yes* and *all right*.

He lay down.

Below Harrow's End. Across the ends of the six roads, where they came up out of the Vale. Facing south. His chin on his paws.

And made them.

---

Six.

---

Out of him. As breath comes out. One. Then another. Then another. Rising out of the ground round him, out of the ground, grey, stone, each one as big as he. Each one a hill.

Four. Five.

Six.

---

She'd never seen six.

Three, in the line. Four, under Greyhold, once. Never six. Six Titans. Six hills with manes. Rising out of the ground round a seventh lying across the roads, in the afternoon light.

They went.

One to each road.

---

And she went with them.

---

She hadn't known. She'd thought she knew. She'd been in four bodies on the Antlered's crown. In four on the line. She'd thought four was a great deal.

This was seven.

---

She was on the Drover's Way, walking beside her brother's wagons. She was on the scrub track, beside the clans. She was on the towpath, beside the olifants. She was on the River Road, at Eskby's ford. She was on the Oneroad, beside the tuskers. She was on the moor track, beside everyone else.

She was below Harrow's End, lying across the roads' ends, facing south.

And she was on his head. Between his ears. In her own body. Which she couldn't feel.

---

Seven.

She was spread across them. As butter is spread too thin on bread. As a voice is spread across a valley until it's only a whisper at the far side.

She was in all of them, and in none of them very much.

---

She stopped breathing.

---

She knew it. From far off. From seven places at once. The small body on the lion's head, that she was hardly in at all, sitting between his ears with its hands in his mane. Not breathing.

She noticed it as you notice a door's been left open in another part of the house. She didn't go and shut it.

There wasn't enough of her to go.

---

Her heart stopped.

She knew that too.

From six roads away.

---

She looked through the shadows' eyes.

All six. At once. She'd never seen so much.

---

The Drover's Way.

Her brother's wagons. Forty of them. The long flat Hearthmere wagons with canvas tilts and dray-horses, on the rutted road across the Vale's scrub, full. The old. The hurt. The children too small to walk. And on the last wagon's box, Tam. In his leather apron, with his coat over it. With Odo's tools in their roll under the seat. With the reins in his black-knuckled hands.

Looking up at the shadow walking beside his wagons.

Looking at it as he'd looked at Orrun on the water-meadow. *He's only big.*

He raised his hand to it.

She couldn't raise one back. She was too thin.

---

The Oneroad.

The tuskers. Eighteen and the old cow. Walking the old realm's highway as if they'd walked it every winter of their lives, and perhaps they had. Sledges behind them on their rope traces. Children on their backs. In their shaggy red hair, holding on. Quell walking at the old cow's head, in her too-big coat. The Wardens behind, with their lynx. The sabrecats behind the Wardens, padding.

The shadow walking beside them. And the old cow turning her head to it, and rumbling, and the shadow rumbling back.

---

The towpath.

The olifants. Eleven. Sarovel's. Walking along the empty riverbed, grey, enormous, with the old people and the children on their backs. Not in traces. Free. Walking because they'd been asked, in the low-city words. A thin strong woman walking at the first one's trunk with her hand on it.

Sarovel's people behind them. On foot. Thousands. Singing. She could hear them. Something low, in their own tongue, that she didn't know.

---

The scrub track.

The stonehorns. In front. In a wedge. As they'd turned into the beast-flood. Their great plated heads down. Going through the last of the gorse at a walk, and the gorse breaking under them. Breaking a road. Flattening it. Behind them, a road where there'd been none, wide enough for carts.

The clans' carts on it. Behind the stonehorns. The old ones on the carts. Rhun's wolves running beside, low, grey.

Rhun walking. With a wolf at each knee.

---

The River Road. Eskby's ford.

The river was gone. The ford was mud. The Hearthmere bank's people coming down to it and crossing it on foot, over the mud and stones of the drained Wend, where it had run black with ice a month before.

And at the ford's edge, standing in the mud, a knight. With a bandaged arm. With a hound at her knee.

Wynn. And Sorrow.

Carrying a child on her back. Waving the others across.

---

The moor track.

Everyone else. Kessmoor's crews who'd run. Sellswords. Militia. People she didn't know. Going up the straight track from Cotter's Cross with nothing in their hands.

And a long way south of them all, far down the Drover's Way where it came up off the Vale's edge, small, two people and a dog. Walking. Too far off to be anyone.

She thought she knew who.

---

The herd-charm under all of it.

Under every road. In the ground. Turning them north. Keeping them walking.

His voice.

---

She stayed spread across seven for an hour.

There wasn't enough of her to count with. She knew it was an hour because the sun moved.

Up the moor, the heads of the columns reaching the high ground. Strung out behind. Going up. Into the Scarfell.

---

At the hour's end he let them go.

---

One by one.

The Oneroad's first. The shadow beside the tuskers stopped, and turned, and walked back down the road toward him, and lay down beside him, and went into him, as breath goes in.

Then the towpath's. Then the scrub track's. The moor track's. The River Road's.

The Drover's Way's last. It walked beside Tam's wagons as long as it could. Then it came back.

---

And she came back.

---

Into herself. Out of six. Out of the seventh, almost. Into the small body on the lion's head, between his ears, that she'd hardly been in for an hour.

It wasn't breathing.

She breathed.

---

It hurt. She noticed that. She couldn't feel her hands, or her arms, or her face, or her feet. But she felt the breath go into her. Like cold water. Into her chest. And out.

And her heart.

She felt it start.

Once. Hard. Like a fist on a door. Then again. Then again, slow, cold, going.

---

Something touched her face.

She didn't feel it. She saw it. Huge. Grey. Rough. His tongue. On her face. As a cat licks a kitten that's been out in the snow.

She couldn't feel it.

She let him.

---

*You stopped*, said Orrun.

*I know.*

*Your heart. It stopped. I felt it.*

*I know.*

*Don't*, said Orrun.

She put her hand on his head. The right. She watched it go flat on his stone.

*I won't*, she said.

---

He got up at dusk.

---

Slowly. Slower than she'd ever felt him. As an old man gets up off a bench at the end of a day. The six he'd made were in him again. And the wounds they'd taken on the roads. And what they'd cost.

*That was most of me*, he said.

He wasn't complaining. He was telling her.

*I know.*

---

He turned.

Below the village. With the roads behind him going up into the Scarfell, and the people on them going up, and the dark coming down. He turned round. Away from the roads.

West.

Toward the Vale.

---

She looked where he looked.

Over the scrub. Down the Vale, in the dusk, lit. Every horizon still burning. And in the middle of it, over the hole, a white light.

Moving.

Getting up off the hole. Rising. On wings.

Coming north.

---

*He's coming*, said Orrun.

*Yes.*

*My kin*, said Orrun. Very quietly. *He's coming after them.*

He lay down again. Across the roads' ends. Below the village. Facing the light. His chin on his paws.

The last thing between the Vale and the roads.

She sat on his head and watched it come, and counted her own heartbeats, slow and cold, to be sure they went on.
