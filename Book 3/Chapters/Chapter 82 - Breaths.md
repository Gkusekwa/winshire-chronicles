---

Davin had picked them up.

Out of the frozen grass on Liss's slope, after he'd fallen on the bank. The three glasses that had gone off the board when he ran. Davin had come back for them, before the river went, and put them in his case in their wool.

The other five had been on the shelf in the ferry house.

The ferry house was on the mud now. Or in the hole.

---

Three.

Davin set them out on the floor of the hall. By the third pillar. Under the rune. In a triangle, on a flat stone, as Idony told him. One toward the door, which faced east over the tarn. One toward the hall's dark back. One against the southern wall.

"If one falls before the others," said Idony, "it tells you where."

Davin knew that. Davin had read his logs on a barn wall. He set them out anyway, exactly, and put Liss's brass lamp beside them.

---

They were at the tops.

All three. They'd been at the tops since the hall was opened. The quicksilver in each, pressed up against the sealed glass at the top of its throat. Shaking. As if it wanted out.

He'd never seen that. Not for this long. Not in all his life. Not at Vael in the surge. Not at the College the night every glass broke.

Off the scale.

---

"Everywhere," he said.

Davin looked at him.

"The surge. It's everywhere. Under everything. It's not coming from any one place." He put his shaking hand down flat on the hall's floor beside his hurdle. "Feel."

Davin put his hand down.

The floor was warm. It had been warm when they came in. It was warmer now. Like a hearthstone with a fire on it.

"The Deep," said Idony. "Coming up. Here. Everywhere."

---

Above them, in the roof, the rune was lit.

Faint. White. The lines that Tam had cut, glowing along their edges. Holding.

He looked at it.

He'd said the strokes. A smith who couldn't read had cut them. A knight who couldn't feel her hands had held the stone. A lion lying across a slope had put his earth through her.

It had answered.

He didn't know if he'd ever been as proud of anything.

---

The hall was full.

Five hundred and thirty-three. The knight had counted. In the niches, three high, along both walls. On the floor between the pillars. Against the walls, standing, some of them. Lying close. Children asleep on people they didn't know.

Breathing. He could hear five hundred and thirty-three people breathing, in the warm dark, and the storm outside the door.

---

The first went out at the second hour.

---

He saw it.

The door glass. The one toward the east. Its quicksilver, pressed at the top. Dropping. All at once. A finger's breadth. As if someone had let go of it.

Then climbing back.

And the other two, a breath after. Dropping. A little. Less. Climbing back.

---

"East," he said.

Davin had his pencil.

"East. Along the Scarfell's edge. Toward the Worldsend." He looked at the glass. He knew it. He'd read it for a year. He didn't know how he knew. He'd felt each one's breath on the glasses for a year, as you know a voice in the next room.

"The Sun," said Idony. "The Horned Sun. It's gone out."

---

"Gone out?"

"Burned through." He looked at the glass climbing back up to the top. "It made the surge. It broke the middle and it stood in the surge and it blazed. It went east blazing. And it burned through. As a lamp burns white before it fails." He breathed. "It's gone out. Write it."

Davin wrote.

*The Horned Sun. East. Second hour.*

---

Across the hall, the beasts lifted their heads.

---

He saw it. He didn't know what he was seeing. The hound, by the door, at the knight's feet: grey, lying with its chin on its paws. Its head coming up. The wolf by the western wall with the Keeper boy. The two greys by the fourth pillar. The stonehorn calf. The lynx.

All their heads. Coming up. At once. As the three on the ridge had lifted their heads and looked west, once, at dawn.

Looking round.

As if someone had called them. By name. From a long way off.

---

Then they put their heads down again.

---

The second went out an hour later.

The back glass. The one toward the hall's dark back, west. Dropping. Close. Hard. A finger. Two. Then climbing.

Close. Very close.

"West," he said. "Under the beck. The Coil."

---

At the door, the farmer turned his head.

He was on the step. Beside the knight. In the doorway, with the storm behind him. He'd been standing there since he brought the last ones up. He turned his head and looked at Idony, across the hall. And nodded.

His eyes were strange. Idony had seen them when he came in. Fixed on something past the storm.

He'd seen it go.

---

"The Coil," said Idony. "The Pale Coil. Gone out. Write it."

*The Pale Coil. West. Under the beck. Third hour.*

---

The beasts lifted their heads again.

All of them. Looking round. Longer this time. The hound got up. It stood by the knight's feet and looked out of the door into the storm and whined. The knight put her hand on its head. She looked at her hand to see it was there.

"She's shaking," said Wynn. From the floor by the door.

---

He looked at the hound.

At the beasts. At the hall full of people breathing.

At the glasses at the top.

And something came into his head.

---

The Deep was their breath.

He'd known that a long time. He'd told Gannet so, on the far shore, by the columns. *Breath = the Deep. The sleepers' breathing.* The six, breathing under the temples for a thousand years. Everything that worked, worked on it. Charms. Lamps. Drawers. Wards.

And the bond.

He'd never asked what the bond worked on. Nobody had. The bond wasn't a working. It was a beast choosing you. It was older than the colleges. It was just there.

Like the Deep was just there.

---

He looked at the hound with its head up.

And didn't finish the thought.

He didn't want to.

---

The third went out near the fifth hour.

---

South. Far. The south glass, and the door glass after it, and he knew the way of it.

Not a drop. A fall. Long. Slow. The quicksilver going down its throat a long way. Further than the others. As a sigh goes on. Then climbing, slowly, back.

He knew it.

He'd known it all his life. Before he could read. On the ferry on Glasswater, with his grandmother's hand on his shoulder, and her prayer.

---

"Ysse," said Idony.

Davin looked at him.

"The Lady," said Idony. "Under the water." He watched the glass climb. "Under the sea. Far south." He breathed. "She's gone out."

---

He heard someone move on the floor by the wall.

---

Kit Merrin.

She'd been lying against the wall by the second pillar with her knees up and a one-eyed dog asleep on her feet. She sat up. She was looking at him.

She didn't say anything.

---

"Write it," said Idony. "*Ysse. The Lady. South. Under the sea.*"

Davin wrote.

---

And a breath after, the fourth.

The same two. South. Far. A fall. Not as long. Close after the first. As if it had been waiting for her.

---

"And the Bell," said Idony.

He looked at Kit.

"With her," he said. "Under the sea. A breath after." He looked at the glass. "Together."

---

Kit looked at him a long time.

She'd seen them go down. He knew that. She'd said so on the step in the morning, to Jory, between counts, and Davin had heard her, and told him.

*They went down. Together. I can't say more than I saw.*

---

She looked at the glass, and neither of them spoke.

She lay back down against the wall. She put her hand on the dog.

She didn't cry. He didn't think Saltreach captains did.

---

*The Bell. South. Under the sea. With her.*

Davin wrote it.

---

The beasts lifted their heads.

All of them. And this time they didn't put them down. They stood. The hound. The wolf, on its bad legs. The two greys, their ears back. The calf, bleating. The lynx, low, its tail going. Standing in the warm dark among the people, looking round.

Old Grey put his head down to the girl with the plait, where she lay against his foreleg, and blew on her hair.

---

"Four," said Davin.

"Four," said Idony.

---

The glasses climbed back to the tops.

All three. And stayed there. Shaking.

The surge was still going.

---

Four were out. And the surge they'd made was still going. Along the lines. Under every kingdom. He could feel it in the floor, warmer. In the glasses. In the rune overhead.

He knew where the lines went.

He'd drawn them. As a young man. At the College. On the founding chart, in brown ink, that was in the hole in the Vale now, with the ferry house.

---

"Write this," he said.

Davin wrote.

---

"Vael," said Idony.

He didn't know why he was saying it. He said it.

"Under ice already. The surge under the ice now. The lake. Glasswater. The Library hill."

Davin wrote.

*The ferry. Nessa's ferry, on the ice.* He didn't say it. Davin didn't write it.

"Ironhythe. The Coldrun. The Low Yards. The Hall of Measures." He breathed. "And its clock."

Davin wrote.

"Solenne. Sorrel Isle. The lighthouse." He thought of a boy-king on an island. "Corsa's terrace. Blackwater. The chain. Hallowmere."

---

He stopped.

---

He looked at the glasses.

At the tops. Shaking.

He could go on. He knew every line. Every town on every line. Every city. Every chapel with a hearthstone. Every crossroads with a three-faced god. He could name every place in Winshire the surge was going through tonight.

He'd been a reader all his life. It was what he was for.

---

"No," he said.

Davin looked up.

"Write: *the surge went along the lines*," said Idony. "That's all. Don't write the names."

---

Davin looked at his page.

Six names. In his clerk's hand. Vael. Ironhythe. Solenne. Corsa. Blackwater. Hallowmere.

He put his pencil on the first, to cross it out.

Then he didn't.

He looked at the six names a long time. Then he wrote under them, *the surge went along the lines*, and closed the case.

Idony didn't make him cross them out.

Somebody should have them.

---

"One," said Idony.

---

He was looking at the glasses.

At the tops. All three. Still shaking. Still pressed against the glass.

But differently.

---

He hadn't seen it, at first, with the others going out. Now he saw it. With four gone, the surge still everywhere, the glasses still at the top, he saw that they were all pressing the same way.

The quicksilver in each, at the top of its throat, leaning. A little. All three. In the same direction.

South.

Toward the south wall.

Close.

---

"One," he said. "One left."

---

"Where?" said Davin.

Idony didn't answer.

He looked at the door.

---

The hill shook.

---

Under them. Round them. The whole hall. The floor going up and down under his hurdle as a ferry goes on a swell. Grit coming down from the roof. People sitting up in the niches. A child screaming.

The rune in the roof flared. White. Bright. Holding.

The grit stopped.

---

And in the doorway, against the storm's flickering green and gold, the knight and the farmer stood side by side on the step, looking out.

Past them. Outside. Below the step. Something moving.

---

Huge.

White.

Filling the doorway's dark with light. Coming up to the slope's foot.

And between it and the door, rising, getting up off the slope where it had lain all evening with its chin on the step, grey, a hill, with a mane, standing up.

The lion.
