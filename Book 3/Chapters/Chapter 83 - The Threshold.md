---

The Clan came down out of the storm before it did.

Twenty pairs. Small dragons, young ones, red and grey and brown, the size of horses, of carts. Coming down out of the flickering dark onto the shore below the slope, one after another, with their riders bent low and wet cloths still tied over some of their noses.

Arn at the front. On Ildra.

He came up the slope. Over Orrun's foreleg, which the lion put out for him without being asked. With Ildra behind him, climbing, her claws on the lion's stone.

He stopped on the step.

---

"Our families are in," said Arn. "Some of them. The rest went down to Varrhold. The big ones took them. Thirty pairs. They'd never get through a door." He looked at the door. "These will go through it. One at a time. If you'll have them."

She looked at the twenty on the shore. Small enough. One at a time.

"Twenty," said Maren. She counted the riders. "And twenty."

---

They came in.

Riders first. Then the dragons, one by one, through the door, folding their wings, ducking their heads under the lintel with its words. Into the warm dark. Along the hall to the back, past the pillars, past the people in the niches, who pressed themselves back into the stone to let them by.

They lay down along the hall's back. Among the pillars. Close. As the freed had sat round the three on the crags.

Ildra last. Behind Arn. She stopped in the doorway and looked back at the lion.

Then she went in.

---

And it came down to the slope.

---

Along the western shore. In the storm. White. Blazing. All of it, through the plates, the surge and the coal both, so bright she could see the snow falling through its light as through a lamp's.

On foot. Its wings folded on its back. Its long neck low.

Past Aurveth.

Past the great white shape lying across the beck's mouth, frosted from end to end. It didn't look at her. It went past her as the Sun had gone past Kharrak on the top field. As past a stone.

And stopped.

At the slope's foot.

Where Orrun stood.

---

He'd got up.

She'd felt him get up, under her hand on the jamb, through the stone, when Idony said *one left*. Off the slope. Off his belly. Onto his feet. A hill, standing. Grey. At the foot of the slope below the door, between the door and the shore.

Between the door and his kin.

---

*The last great one lay down to sleep below the stone,* she thought.

She didn't know why. It came into her head as the old man's words came into his. The verse. From the arch at Greyhold. From the first winter. From the slab under the tower that the farmer had rubbed with a charred stick and sent her in a bundle with the five links.

*And its own kin kept the door.*

---

They'd kept it once.

Under Greyhold. For three hundred years. A young lion on the steps of a door in the dark, that people bowed to, that the Grey Mother had set there. Keeping it. A cat on the steps.

And then they'd lost it. In the cavern. At the end of the second spring. The relic. The door. The chains going out. She'd stood with her back to the wall and her palm on the gold rings and Haverly's edge dark, and the door hadn't been kept.

---

*Brother*, said Orrun.

---

To it. Below her. Facing it. Two hills on the shore of a black tarn in a storm.

It didn't answer.

It didn't look at him. It looked past him. Up. Up the slope. At the step.

At the door.

---

She knew what it wanted.

She'd felt it on the eddy. When it got up under the slack chains. Nothing in it. No rage. No hunger. Past them. A stillness.

It wanted everything down.

Everything. Every temple open. Every kingdom under the sea. Every breath gone out.

And in the hall behind her, five hundred and fifty-three people breathing.

And twenty dragons.

---

*Make them*, she said.

Through the bond. Down to him.

*Make them. Three. Hold it.*

---

*No*, said Orrun.

---

*No more of me out there.*

He didn't turn his head. He was looking at his kin.

*No more shadows. Not one. All of me here.* She felt him breathe. *One.*

---

She felt him do it.

---

All of it. The part of him he'd always kept for making them. That had made two on the Greyhold hill, and one at a time at the fords, and two on the drowned road. Three on the line. Three on the Antlered's crown. Six on six roads, that had cost most of him. What was left of it. The share.

He drew it in.

Into himself. All of it. To be one thing.

---

One.

---

She saw him change.

He couldn't grow; he was as big as anything there was. He was heavier. Denser. As stone is denser than earth. His grey going darker. His edges sharper against the storm's light. A hill that had been a hill and a little more, and was now only a hill, entirely, all of it him, nothing kept back.

One lion. At the foot of a mountain.

---

*Maren*, said Orrun.

*Yes.*

---

*Let me go.*

---

She stood on the step with her hand on the jamb.

She hadn't known she was holding. She'd held him so long she'd forgotten it was holding. Since the first winter. Since the binding at Hollin Knowe, with the collar and the smoke, when she'd given into it so that he'd live. Since the far shore, on the hill, when the Mother asked and she said yes. Since the eddy, when he'd come loose like a boat off its rope and she'd held the rope.

She was holding it now.

---

*I have to keep the door*, said Orrun. *I can't keep it if you're holding me.*

*I know.*

*You know you're holding.*

*I do now*, she said.

---

*You kept the watch*, she said.

Down to him. Through her hand. Through the stone.

*Keep the door.*

---

*I kept it before*, said Orrun. *Under the hill. For three hundred years.* She felt him almost laugh. Stone settling. *I was small.*

*You're not small.*

*No*, said Orrun. *Not now.*

---

*Count them for me*, he said.

*I have. Five hundred and fifty-three. And twenty dragons.* She breathed. *And a hound. And a wolf. And two greys. And a calf. And a lynx.*

*That's a lot.*

*Yes.*

*Good*, said Orrun.

---

She let go.

---

Not of the bond. She couldn't let go of that; it wasn't hers to let go. Of her hold. On him. The holding. The hand on the rope.

She took her hand off the jamb.

She watched it come off. She couldn't feel it come off. She watched her bandaged hand come away from the stone and hang at her side.

And she felt him go free.

Free. As a man walks out of a door into the morning.

He was still there. All of him. In the bond. Where he'd always been.

She wasn't holding him.

---

The farmer was beside her.

On the step. In the doorway. She didn't know when he'd come. He'd been there since he brought the last of the sixty up; he'd stayed. He stood beside her with his scarred hands at his sides, looking down at the two hills on the shore.

He didn't say anything.

He didn't touch her.

He was there.

---

The Last Titan came up the slope.

---

At Orrun. At the door behind him. Up the raw slope of stone and earth below the step, white, blazing, its head low, coming. As it had come at him on the eddy. As stags come.

And Orrun turned his back on it.

---

She saw him do it.

He turned. Away from his kin. Toward the crag. Toward the rock beside the door, where the crag came down to the slope, grey, steep, with the Scarfell's whole shoulder going up above it into the storm.

He put his forefeet on the slope.

He put his head against the crag.

---

Against the stone. His great grey brow. On the crag's foot, beside the door, a little below the step, where she could have reached down and touched his mane.

She didn't.

---

The ground was alive.

She could feel it from the step without her hand on anything. Coldtarn's ground. The Deep warm under it. The surge in it, coming up from the broken middle of the world. The whole mountain over them humming. Roaring, under the hum.

He spoke to it.

---

Not a question.

She'd heard him ask, once. On the island, at the end of the first winter, the roar that went out over the Vale. *What are you?*

He'd given an order on the Saltreach bank. *Hold.*

This wasn't *hold*.

---

*Come down*, said Orrun.

To the mountain.

---

The Last Titan reached him.

On the slope. Below the step. Its head into his flank, where the chains had burned him three times. Its weight. All of it. A hill against a hill on a slope under a door.

He didn't turn round.

He kept his head on the crag.

---

And the mountain came down.

---

She felt it start. Under her feet. In the stone of the step. In the jamb. In the lintel over her head with its words. In the roof of the hall behind her, where the rune flared white.

Above. The crag. The shoulder of the Scarfell over Coldtarn's western side. All of it. A hillside of stone, the whole height of it, going up into the storm.

Coming down.

---

Coming down. As he'd asked. As the scree had moved for him, once, like water. The whole crag over the door bowing. Leaning out. Over the slope. Over the two hills on it. Over the beck's mouth and the white shape lying across it.

Slowly.

Then all at once.

---

*Back*, said the farmer.

His hand on her arm. She saw it there.

They stepped back.

Into the doorway. Into the warm dark. Side by side.

---

And watched.

---

From inside. Through the door. As they'd watched from the shore at sunrise once, side by side, with the lion and three dragons, the empty sky.

The mountain coming down outside the door.

Grey. Stone. A hillside of it, out of the storm's flicker, down over the slope. Over the Last Titan's white blazing back. Over Orrun, with his head against the crag.

She saw him last. His mane. Scorched black on one side. Frosted on the other. His head down against the stone. Not moving. Keeping the door.

Then stone.

---

It filled the doorway.

From the bottom. The falling stone coming down over the step and piling against the door's frame. Grey. Rising. Up the doorway. Knee. Waist. The storm's light going out of the door from the bottom up as a window darkens when snow drifts against it.

Shoulder. Head.

The lintel.

---

Dark.

---

The door was stone.

---

Dust. In the hall. In Liss's lamp. Grit coming down. People screaming. Then not.

The roof held. The rune in it white. Bright. Holding.

The door held. Stone. All the way up. Cold. A wall where the doorway had been, with the lintel's words over it, that she couldn't read, white with dust.

Kept.

---

*the last great one lay down below the stone*

---

It came into her.

---

Him.

Through the bond. As the stone closed over him. She felt him come.

---

Not as cold. She'd thought it would be cold. She'd been cold so long.

A weight. All of him. Coming up the bond into her. Stone. Patience. The long slow patience of stone, that holds a hill for a thousand years if you ask it. The earth. His earth, that had gone through her since the first winter, through her hands, through her arms, out into walls and banks and hills.

Coming in. Not going out.

All of it.

---

And his power with it.

---

She felt the mountain.

The one that had come down. Outside the door. On him. On his kin. She felt it in her. Every stone of it. Alive. Many-shaped. Each stone its own, and all of them one, and moving, a little, as stones move over a long time, settling.

Hers.

She could have asked it to move. She knew she could. She could feel it waiting to be asked.

She didn't.

---

And the cold went out of her.

---

She didn't feel warm. She didn't feel her hands. She didn't feel her face.

She simply wasn't cold.

The cold that had been in her since the Thornwood. Under her ribs, on the grey side, with the stone she'd taken for him. The cold to the heart from the Saltreach bank. The cold from the roof's rune. All of it.

Gone. Not into anything. Not out of her.

It had stopped being cold. It had become what she was.

---

She lived.

She stood in the dark of the doorway with the farmer beside her and the door stone in front of her and lived.

One life between two. And one of them was under a mountain.

She lived anyway.

He'd given it.

---

"*Idony*," said Davin.

---

Behind her. In the hall. By the third pillar.

She turned.

---

The old man was on his hurdle. Up on one elbow. Looking at the glasses on the flat stone, in Liss's lamplight, with the dust coming down through it.

Three. At the tops, still. Pressing. All three, leaning, toward the door. Toward the stone where the door had been.

And then the door glass fell.

---

Long. All the way. The quicksilver going down its throat from the top to the bottom, slowly, as a sigh goes, as the Lady's had gone.

And the other two after it.

All the way down.

And stayed there.

---

Flat.

---

The old man looked at them a long time.

"Nothing," said Idony.

Very quietly. In the dust.

"Nothing anywhere is breathing."

---

Somebody coughed in the niches.

A child. In the dust. Then another. Five hundred and fifty-three people, breathing, in the dark, round him.

He looked round at them.

He didn't correct himself. He'd meant what he'd said. He meant the Titans. She knew it. He knew she knew.

He lay back on his hurdle and shut his eyes.

---

At the hall's back, among the pillars, the dragons had been making a sound.

She'd heard it under everything. Since the lion stood up off the slope. Low. The twenty of them. Ildra among them. The sound the Clan's dragons made at the tarn's edge when the ash went down into the black water. Not a roar. Not crying.

It stopped.

---

All at once.

Every dragon in the hall. At the same breath.

Silent.

---

She stood in the doorway with the farmer beside her and listened to the silence at the back of the hall.

She'd never heard twenty dragons silent.

She didn't know what it meant.

She thought she did.
