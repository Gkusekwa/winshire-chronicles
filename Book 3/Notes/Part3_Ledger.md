# Book 3, Part Three: The Far Shore: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as Book 2's part ledgers. Before writing a scene that touches another POV's days or people, read this file. After deciding any fact another chapter could contradict (who is where on which day, what someone said, a name, a wound, a number, a beast), add one line under your name. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (30, 34, 37, 40)
- Ch 30 (days 130–135; 126–129 told back): **Dawn 127 Maren falls asleep against the milestone after four nights awake; wakes at noon alive**: Orrun stayed awake (*I watched you. The whole time... The light came... It looked at you. And at me, awake. And went away*). **The bonded are safe while their beast keeps awake beside them**; she sleeps in watches of an hour or two while he wakes, and he (needing little) while she does: "They kept the watch for each other." **D.129 her name** comes through Orrun as a shape, rendered in Maren's tongue: **the Grey Mother** (Hesper said it at the Hollow Ring; Tova under her breath at the fords; Hearthmere's chapel hearthstones); Orrun: *She's my mother* (as a thing always known, just remembered). She doesn't use words; she gives Orrun pictures and feelings. **D.130 noon the showing**: she lowers her gable-sized head across the ten paces; Orrun's head against her paw; Maren's hand on his shoulder: **(1) the one realm**, green to its edges, grey roads and milestones and piered bridges everywhere, people and beasts walking side by side unchained, **lions on tower steps bowing back**; **the old ones** (shapes too big to see whole, of every kind) **teaching the hooked script in light on the air**, people copying it into dust, clay, stone (*So we could talk... We gave them our tongue. Set down*); **(2) iron**: the marks struck into iron, **a collar** on a great grey cat with empty eyes, then many, then **chain** (*hold* inside every link), then **a chained Titan under a hill bleeding its breath into a city's lamps and wells**; **(3) the rising and the split**: the road cracking, the sea over it, ice, a bell under the sea; **(4) the sleeping**: on a black plain the old ones lay the six down under hills, sea, a lake, a mountain, a stair, a ring of stones, each with a glowing stone to hold its sleep **and a seventh over them all**; **each old one that gives goes thin, pale, into light; the plain nearly empty after, a few left going west** (*It cost us. Our bodies. We had nothing else to give*); **(5) the door**: the Wend, the island, the grey arch, **a young half-grown grey lion with too-big paws set down there by a vast thin grey shape**: ***Keep the watch*** (the one thing that comes in words: Orrun has kept it in words three hundred years). **Maren understands: the realm fell for doing what the Restoration does** (and the crown under Greyhold). **She asks if they'll come back and sleep the six again**: the Mother's eyes on Maren for the first time: ***We closed the door once... We have nothing left but light.*** **Echo, days 131–134**: the Mother looks at Orrun as a cub is looked at; **d.132 she licks him once from nose to tail-tip "as a wind goes over stone"**; through the bond Maren feels him being somebody's and **has to walk to the hill's edge and look away**. **D.132 a lock of her hair over the left ear gone white** (plant for 37). **Dusk 135 Idony up the road on the grey horse**: "Kit had no say in it. I'm a witness... I've come to read. Is there anything to read?" "Yes."
- Ch 34 (day 142; 137–141 told back): **Dawn 137 the Grey Mother stands for the first time** and walks south-west along the road (the paving sings under her; deer watch; birds rise and settle); Orrun at her shoulder unasked, Maren on him; Idony on the grey horse; Gannet on foot. **The hall** on the long ridge where the road crosses it: **a long double row of tall square grey columns, roofless; a paved floor clean as if swept; a long narrow pool down the middle a man's height deep, spring-fed at the south end, draining under the floor at the north; a great arch over the north doorway.** The Mother lies along the hall's east side outside the columns with her head on the floor between two of them by the pool; Orrun by her head. **The light writes the lock's sentence over the arch and it stays**: ***One hand shut it. / Two hands open it together. / What is opened together is kept together, or it is not kept.*** Idony goes very still: "**They wrote the lock... The same hand. *They* did. Not the crown. Not the first knights**"; silent an hour. **Dawn 140 the Lamp walks up the ridge on the sunrise**: white-gold, tall as the columns, horse-like and not, a mane of light streaming back in no wind, **one long straight horn "like a spear of noon"**; stands at the south end by the spring; the pool lies at its feet "like a sword"; Idony: Sarovel's priests "are very bad singers." **Dusk 140 the Lady up the spring's channel into the pool**: pale moon-on-frost light coiling the pool's length and further down than its bottom; **a heron-like head on a neck "like a river", eyes the colour of deep water**; **Idony kneels and can't get up for a long time** ("My grandmother prayed to her every night. On the ferry... A hundred years of us... I never did"). **D.141 the Mother writes: *We called. / Others did not answer. / They have gone out.*** Four. **Noon 142 Calder** (Kharrak huge, Lisska on a broken column-top, Aurveth dull and thin but flying straight) lands outside the western columns and walks in; **last met at Ironhythe's yard (d.65)**; he sees her grey neck, white lock, still mouth-corner; she sees **his palms scarred pink and shiny to the wrists and his voice gone to a crack**. "You came." "The old one was dying." Neither says *song*. **Nothing happens while apart**; the arch's letters flare when he enters; **the four lights gather over the pool into a cart-sized brightness that won't make words**; Idony: "It's waiting... *Two hands*... it wants you to stand together." **Calder holds out his scarred right hand across the pool's corner; Maren goes round and takes it with her right** (her left is dead). **The light forms; both feel it through their beasts**: ***Lion's bond. Dragons' bond. / Together. / Ask.*** **The Wayfarer is in the light without coming: "It speaks through them... It's in them" (Orrun); Calder's free hand goes flat to his chest each time it speaks (his marks go hot).** **Gannet** between two western columns since dusk 137 with his board, copying the arch and the pool's light as Idony reads. They keep holding hands "like two children at a fair". "Well." "You ask. You've got the voice."
- Ch 37 (day 146; 145 told back; echo dawn 147): **Dawn 145 Calder and his three go back south-west to the crossroads**; at the western columns: "Two hands." "I know." **Dusk 145 the Lamp walks off the ridge's east end into the last light and the Lady sinks away down the pool's channel "like a tide", "to take a shape".** **Dawn 146 the Mother comes out onto the ridge and lies down facing Orrun**; grey script (Idony reads aloud so Maren has the words): ***Lion's knight. / He is yours. You are his. / One life. / What I give him goes through you. / Will you let it?*** **Maren thinks of Hollin Knowe** (Book 1 ch. 73: she walked into the bond-forcers' binding and offered her life in his place): "A binding took. It didn't ask... This asked." **"Yes." / *Yes.* / *Are you afraid?* / *Yes.* / *So am I.*** (truth this time). **The gift**: the Mother's jaw on the ground before his paws; light into the ridge, into Orrun, **through the bond into Maren as cold: into all her bones, even those she can't feel** ("Like the stone a long way down where nothing had ever been warm"); **stone flows up into Orrun from the ridge, the paving, the hall's columns, "as water goes up a wick"**; **he grows: barn, house, the hall's height, a hill (the size of the Mother's hill)**; **grey as old stone; mane gone to dust/mist/smoke like hers; eyes gone from gold to pale ("the colour of the sky at dawn"); no wings** (the Last Titan has stone-and-leather ones); **the far shore's stone flows round his paws "as water flows round a stone in a river"; it comes without pulling**. **The Mother thins (mane, body, paws, eyes last on Orrun) and goes out "like a lamp"; the grass flattened in her shape; the hall's hum stops "as a bell stops when someone puts a hand on it"; only Orrun hums now.** **He's himself: *Maren... I'm here. I'm still here*** (same voice, deeper, slower, "like stone settling... at the bottom of a well"; no rage); she weeps and **can't feel her face on either side now (the cold)**; *I'm not now.* **Noon: three shadows, each hill-sized and exactly as big as he: *They're me. All of them. Now.*** She doesn't count them. **Idony cries at the arch ("There wasn't anything to read"); Gannet's board blank all morning; his smile doesn't come.** **Afternoon in the empty pool's reflection: her white lock spread to a band of iron-grey over the left ear and temple "like Rowan's"; cold for the rest of her life.** **Echo, dawn 147**: Orrun now feels the whole far shore's stone "as she'd once felt the floor of a single room"; **through it, far south-west, three dragons screaming (one deep, one high, one old)**: *The farmer's.* *The other gift.* until they stop.

## Calder (28, 32, 35, 39, 42)
- Ch 28 (days 125–128; 118–124 told back; the tow 128–138 summarized): **The fleet sails day and night** on Hal's charts. **Dusk 119 at *forty-one*** (the pilots sail straight past it): **Aurveth comes down on the sea beside Ostrey's flagship; Jory jumps from the rail onto her shoulder**; Rennick's two guards go back to their dice; Rennick laughs. **Jory rides Aurveth's neck from then on, calling each pier by its broken shape** ("*Forty. Bear west a point...*"); Calder shouts it down; nobody listens. **D.121 at the fifth pier Rennick's three black ships heave to and next morning turn back north-east**, signalling *Turn back. Danger*: "I don't sail at night where I can't see the bottom, farmer!... Good luck to you!" (**Rennick survives.**) **Night 123 the *White Heron*** (Quill, white pennants) **strikes a fallen pier in the dark; sixty drown; the dragons lift eleven off her rigging** by Kharrak's fire. **Celia Ostrey: "The Merrins sail this. So can we. We have paid."** **Night 127 near pier twelve the Bell rises under the four leading ships**, lifting them on its back, rings *down*; they go over; its ring-wave flings six onto the reefs either side of the road. **Jory: "Twelve to eleven! The road's whole!"**: **half a league of unbroken flat causeway a fathom and a half down, the only such stretch on the road** ("It's the shallowest thing on the road that won't break you"). **Kharrak and Lisska herd the flagship's longboat and jolly-boat onto it with fire**; they ground on the stone. **The second wave**: Calder's hand on **Lisska's** neck: *I know*; **her wind, never asked for, comes up through him** ("as if the sky itself were breathing into him through her") and goes out of her **as a wall of wind across the sea, tearing the wave's top off**; it passes under the boats, spent. **Toll: breath**: nothing comes in; Lisska frightened for the first time he's heard; **Kharrak lands on pier twelve's broken top and lies flat as a bed**; a thread of air at a time till dawn; **speechless till d.129; voice cracked and thin for weeks; never gets his breath back all the way (always short of it on a hill after)**. **Losses**: **nine of eleven ships** (four off the Bell's back, five on the reefs; two found drifting empty); the Bell goes back down under the road. **Survivors: 63 in the two boats** (house servants, mostly children, a few rich in salt-white coats, **Master Loy** the pilot, grey-faced with his chart) **plus 31 lifted off wreckage: 94**. **Celia Ostrey goes down at her flagship's rail, calm; Aubrey Strand goes down with his ship.** **The tow, d.128–138**: boats rowed by turns, towed by Kharrak and Lisska on war-harness lines; Aurveth ahead with Jory; nights tied to piers (people sleep in the boats, dragons on pier-tops); water from the longboat's breaker and two rains caught in sails; **nobody dies on the tow**. **Echo, d.128 at pier eleven: *Into the light, eleven* and, knife-scratched under it, Kit's mark: a small ship with a K for a mast**: "She's been past. Days ago." **D.129 Calder's warning** to all ninety-four: "Nobody sleeps ashore. Over there... If you sleep ashore over there, you don't wake up... That's the price. Of the berth you paid for."
- Ch 32 (days 135–140): **Dusk 137 the last pier**; **the Bell lies silent at the shelf's edge west of the Needle**; Jory won't take two laden boats past it; **Abram's page near the book's end: *South way. Small boats.***: half a day south along the shelf's outer edge to **a shallow cut** (too shallow for the *Kittiwake*; a longboat at the top of the flood) into **a small blue bay on the shore's south coast**, where **a second paved road** comes down out of the hills into the sea; tide-line milestone *into the light* (Jory's first sight of it). **D.138 landfall of all 94**: deer, a hare, birds on shoulders; **some lie down on the warm sand at once; Calder (voice cracked) pulls them up**: "Nobody lies down... Don't sleep ashore." **Night: everyone in the anchored boats, sitting up, sleeping in shifts on each other's shoulders; Kharrak and Lisska awake in the shallows alongside; Calder awake on the beach against Aurveth** (he keeps the rule; doesn't know the bonded's exemption). **Piers Calloway** (new: young, handsome Corsa wine-merchant's son; "I paid six hundred gold to come to a land without Titans... a reef-pilot told a sellsword a tale") **sleeps in the dunes with a blanket; dawn: on his back in the marram grass, eyes open, smiling, dead**; a deer smells his face. **Buried by the beach's numberless milestone; Loy says something; the 93 believe.** Fish come to their hands in the shallows. **D.139 inland half a day on the southern road to the crossroads** where it meets the western (lagoon) road running north-east: **a grey paved square with a milestone at each corner; in the middle a shape of pale gold light taller than a tower: a long body, wings folded "as a heron folds them", three long necks, three heads looking down three roads**: **the three-faced god of the Harrow's End crossroads shrine** (dog-kennel-sized, a bowl for coppers, the carving worn by rain and hands). **Aurveth breathes properly for the first time since Coldtarn**: long, deep, slow, sides filling (bronze still dull, plates still proud): *Oh.* **Calder's two coppers: Marrow's (kept) and his own last, a Hearthmere copper with a lamp on it, which he sets on the south-corner milestone's flat top** out of habit. **The Wayfarer speaks in him through all three bonds at once, three voices that are one; his three bond-marks on his chest (rust-red, green, bronze) go hot**: ***Farmer.*** **"Why me?... I was cutting barley... There were riders on the Scarfell who'd waited their whole lives... And three of them came to me. In a mill. Dying. Why?"** ***Because you have room.*** Nothing more; the dragons understand (Kharrak's low chest-sound, Lisska's tilted head); he doesn't. **"Will you come?... and fight?"** ***No. I have only this left. It is for them.*** (He's afraid he understands.) **Dusk, through Aurveth: *The others are gathering. In the hall. Where the road crosses the ridge. North and east... The other bond is coming. Come. Two hands.*** He thinks of the lock under Greyhold. **Echo, dusk 140 from the north-east corner: on the northern hills a long grey shape lying in the light and a smaller grey one beside it**: "The lion." He sits awake against Aurveth all night, feeling her breathe.
- Ch 35 (days 142–143): **Calder asks four gods "Can you put them back to sleep?"**; **the Lamp's script** (white-gold, sharp-edged, "cut out of noon"; Idony shades his eyes): ***No. / The houses are broken.*** (the temples) ***/ The stones are in the last one's back.*** (the relics: six, five dark, one like a coal) ***/ The sleeping cost us what we were. We have nothing left to pay with.*** **Dusk 142**: ***Two of us will take a shape again, and stand with you. / Two of us will give what is left, and go out. / To the lion. To the three.*** Calder feels it go into Aurveth "like a stone into a well" (not fear); Maren feels Orrun hear it. **They let go at dusk; the light goes out "like a lamp when the door opens"; they sit on the pool's edge a pace apart.** **The talk** (Idony asleep an hour by the arch for once; Gannet a dark shape at the columns): **she read his midwinter letter at Orrin three times, "Twice so as not to laugh at the spelling... For the rest of it"**; **his father paid the Clan's dues every year, kept his head down, died in his bed ("I thought so till I was thirty")**; "None of them were keeping my head down"; **her father: poachers killed his hound in the Brackenwood; he sat in his chair by the fire a week and died; she was fourteen on the floor by the chair**: "I grew up knowing exactly what a bond costs. And I went and got one anyway"; **Senna died of fever six years ago, ill nine days; Wren was four**; "It felt like half of me went with her"; **his line: one life between four "like being a house with the doors open"; hers: "One life between two is a house with one door. And somebody always standing in it."** Her mouth: "It's paid" (he doesn't ask by whom). **Her fear: the Mother's gift will make Orrun a Titan, "The thing under Greyhold"** ("I saw it go out of its chains at the Ruin... at the Ring... at the shut place"). **His fear: "The old one's going to die"**: she's waited her whole life for something (Sigrun said so) "and I think it's the last thing she was waiting for." **Neither comforts the other.** **Morning 143 the plan** (hands joined again; the light returns at once): ***There is one stone over the six.*** (Idony: "The seventh. The one from Hearthmere's vault. The coal in its back") ***/ We made it. Only one of us can hold it. / If one of us takes it out of the last one's back, the six will hear. / They will come into the light with the one who holds it. / They will stop, as we stopped.*** ***I will carry it.*** (the Lamp). Maren: "Someone has to get it there... Two of them fight. Two of them give. To us. So we can get it there." **Echo: Gannet between the western columns two days, a pencil stub worn to a thumb's width, writing every line Idony reads.**
- Ch 39 (day 147; 143–146 told back; echo dusk 148): **D.143–144 Jory leads the 93 up the western road to the lagoon** (sleeping sitting up in turns, Kharrak and Lisska awake at either end; nobody dies); **Kit takes all 93 aboard the *Kittiwake*** (hold, deck, the two boats towed astern): "*in the boats, two at a time*"; **she charges nothing** (Jory finds that the strangest thing in the world). **D.145 Jory back to the crossroads on Aurveth with Calder.** **Dawn 147 the gift**: the Wayfarer's three faces turn from the roads to the dragons; marks hot; ***Farmer. / Hold them. / What I am goes into them. Through you. / Three ways. One gift.*** "Yes." The light comes down "like rain coming off a roof all at once when the wind takes the gutter" into him and out three ways (the mill, the other way: "Not three deaths. Three lives"). **Kharrak's fire comes out white; the square's south-east milestone melts and pools like wax and cools to slag**: *Oh, I like that.* **Aurveth's deep sight**: through the hills to the ridge (the lion a grey hill-sized warmth with something small between its paws: *He's changed*); down through the sea-bed to the drowned road's piers, "each one a cold grey standing shape"; **far east over the sea a burning like a coal among five dark shapes** (she doesn't tell him). **Lisska's wind: she goes up and clouds come out of a clear sky and wheel round her in a ring a league wide; she carries the storm and throws it west over the sea, laughing**: *Did you see that?* **Aurveth's holding wing cracks "like a green branch" and straightens at the root; both wings spread full across two roads; the bronze comes back bright.** **The toll, through all three bonds at once**: fever from the marks out; **blood from his nose, then his eyes**; **his three bond-marks (bronze, rust, green) burned into raised hard ridged scars on his chest, "as if somebody had pressed three hot seals into his skin"** (for life); the dragons can't come near (Kharrak's heat cracks the paving; Lisska's wind flattens Jory; Aurveth sees his heart stutter); **Jory holds his head and feels his heart stop under his hand for a count of three, then start**; *Hold on.* **The fever breaks at noon**: "Here." **The Wayfarer thins; its middle head looks down at him at the last; it goes out like the Mother; the square's hum stops. His Hearthmere copper is still on the south milestone.** **Dusk 147 their size**: **Kharrak the size of a hill, deep forge-red, gold-eyed, white shimmer over his jaws**; **Aurveth larger, bronze shining, eyes the size of cartwheels now**; **Lisska still smallest, the size of the hall, pale-green-eyed, chin on a milestone "the size of a stool to her"**; all still themselves: *Bigger.* / *Did you see my storm?* / Aurveth: ***You nearly went... Don't. Not yet. I've only just stopped waiting.*** **Echo, dusk 148** (he slept a day lying against Aurveth, awake above him): **through her sight the grey hill walks off the ridge to the lagoon and into the sea with something small on its back; the sea-bed stone opens before it "as water opens round a boat's bow" and closes behind, along the drowned road under the piers**: *The lion. Going home. Under.*

## Idony (31, 36)
- Ch 31 (day 136): **Gannet arrives at the hill's foot at dusk 135, an hour behind Idony**, bag half full of milestone flakes: "Kit sent me. To see you didn't fall off... That's not a rock"; **he stays awake all night by the fire** (thorn branches, dry grass) chipping flakes and whistling. Idony confirms Maren's count (sixty in, a hold, sixty out). **Dawn 136 the light gathers on the air in front of the Grey Mother a man's height off the rock into lines of hooked script in pale gold, each letter hand-sized, holding a long breath, fading, the next coming**; Maren feels the meaning through Orrun but can't read the words; **Idony reads aloud, Maren writes one-armed in her square hand**: ***We were as they are. / We woke, and drank. / We stopped. / We put down what we were, and walked without it. / The small ones saw us walk, and gave us names. / They made us houses. They lit lamps to us. / They said we walked unbound. / We went into the light. / They kept the names.*** Asked (via Maren to Orrun) what she is: ***What is left.*** Idony sits down. **The reveal**: the chapel hearthstone (the Grey Mother), the Lamp on the Sun Stair, the Lady under the water (his grandmother's prayer on Glasswater), **the three-faced crossroads god** with its market-day copper: **Titans who woke and drank and stopped, put down their bodies and the drinking, and went on as light**. **The seventh ring**: the College's rings are notches on a worker's staff (first a hedge-charm; fourth a tide-reader, once his; sixth the Keepers of the Script); **the seventh, *the Unbound*, "old magic: working the Deep with nothing between," was never a person's: "They *were* the Deep, with nothing between... the College had been drawing a notch on a staff for a god."** "I don't believe in gods... Measure. Not prayer... Write it down anyway": ***The gods are what is left of Titans who stopped.*** He laughs till his eyes run, then sits long without laughing. **Noon: *Others of us are near. / Come to the hall. / Bring the other bond.*** Maren's face. **A pale gold line drawn on the air south-west along the road to where it crosses a far ridge, and one word: *Hall***: "On the road. Where it crosses that ridge. A day, south-west." (The Hall of Light lies between the Mother's hill and the Wayfarer's crossroads.) **His hands steady all day** (an old man's), reading without his glass; he doesn't tell Maren (nor she him about the white lock). **Echo, night 136**: Gannet asks for the pages "to know what the stone's worth"; Idony: "Let him. It's only true"; **Gannet copies them by firelight; asks "What's *ring*? In the old tongue... *The seventh ring*"**; Idony explains; **Gannet writes: *Ring. Notch on a staff. Seventh: theirs.*** "Thank you, master." Idony very flattered.
- Ch 36 (day 144): **Morning, the Lamp (hands joined)**: ***We made the stone when we laid them down. / A safeguard. If they woke, one of us could hold it, and call them into the light. / The small ones found it. They used it to hold the last one in its chains.*** (the crown's vault under Greyhold, feeding the chains) ***/ It answers a breath that is unbound.*** **Idony said exactly that at Blackwater (d.82–83) and Maren wrote it**: he must have read it years ago off some stone or book at Vael without knowing; frightened "of a sentence"; "Well. I've found out." **His explanation at noon**: the relic only answers a breath with **nothing between it and the Deep**: theirs, the Unbound's; **not a person's, not a bound beast's, not a chained Titan's** (in the Last Titan's back it can only "sit there, under the plates, like a coal"); **"That's why Hearthmere never used it... It never answered them once."** "And the Lamp can." "If it can get to it." **Gannet asks from the columns: "What's *breath* mean?... I've got it down. I don't know what it's worth."** Idony sits by him on the grass: **the Deep (the Bleed, the crown's gift, "the Mother's milk" in the Wyldmarch) is the six's breath, sleeping a thousand years under the temples**; **Gannet writes in a squarer hand than expected: *breath = the Deep. The sleepers' breathing.* / *unbound = nothing holding it.*** "Thank you, master." (Idony touched; misreading comes at 69.) **Afternoon, the Mother's grey script to him alone** (hands apart; Maren: "It's for you"): ***Small one. / Your hands. / There is a little left in me. I can mend what you burned.*** He thinks for one breath of writing his own notes again and words that work; looks at the lion; **"No. Thank you... Every scrap of you goes to him... I can read with my hands shaking. I've done it a year. I'm quite good at it now."** Maren: "She's glad." He doesn't believe in gods; is glad she's glad. **Dusk: the little grey horse** (with him since the beach, grazing outside the columns) **comes in, hooves *tock* on the paving, and puts its head against his chest: a choosing beginning**; **"No. Go back to your herd... I've seen what it costs... I'm too old to be anyone's door"**; it goes, looking back. **He stays unbonded by choice.** **Echo, night**: Maren kneeling by the Mother's head with Orrun's head in her one arm: **"I'm not afraid... Whatever she gives you. Whatever you are after"**, three times; Idony knows she's lying; so does the lion ("how still the lion lay").

A long way out. Moving... It doesn't mean anything.* To be poured for at Coldtarn without ashes. **Night 146 the chair offered** at the Ashlings-edge camp: Bjarke (changed since the pens) for red and grey: "Sit in it." **Arn: "The chair's for whoever pours." "You poured." "Two of us poured."** (with Ulric for Haldor; with Calder for Ulric); **Gunna laughs like a fox: "Oh, that's Teo's brother. To the bone."** Taken as a no. **Echo, an hour after dawn 147: every dragon on the coast (a hundred and more) turns south-west at once and cries, high and torn**; rocs scream back; wyverns pull at chains; **Ildra: *It hurts. It's not ours... His three. The farmer's. Far... Something's going into them. Something too big.*** It stops all at once. **D.150 the coast empty to the Wend** (the terrace's carts a day short of it; roc-carried children running about the Accord's camp); **Wendmouth and Blackwater won't come**. Arn's dusk flight: *Empty. Like the pens.* "No. The pens were full. With things that couldn't go. This is empty because they went." *Better.*

## Tobin (33)
- Ch 33 (days 130–140): **Pike walks the mile of yards every morning at the clock's six with Strake** (back from the Downs, hands burned worse, her crew dead). **The Low Yards**: a mile of iron sheds, slipways, hissing rail-cranes, steam-hammers that shiver the canal, green lamps, noise all night. **The Ring Yard** (a quarter-mile cinder field at the southern end where the canal turns): **twelve barn-sized heartstone engines on iron sledges in a circle, joined by great black Kessmoor-iron chain (links longer than a man and thicker) round the ring and in to its middle like spokes**; **the hands call it the Bridle; Kessmoor calls it *the Accord's fallback*** (*If the elders fail. If the line breaks*). **Twenty chain-forges** along the south side in an open iron shed; **Tobin strikes at the fifth; Tam Ashdown smiths at the fourth**. **Tam and Odo came up the Iron Road in spring with forty Hearthmere smiths the Accord hired**; **Odo Marl in a wooden wheeled chair Tam built** (legs broken under the beam at the Millbrook forge), drives it by its wheels. **D.132 Odo wheels along a spoke and finds seven bad links** ("Cold-shut... Poured too fast... It doesn't ring. It thuds"); **Strake's press parts two at the welds "like bread"**; Pike thanks him through her spectacles; **d.133 Odo moved off the works to the smiths' barracks across the canal "for his health"**; Strake: "He frightens the hands." Tam: "No he doesn't." **Echo, d.134: Tam to the forges: "Anybody heard from my sister? Maren... I missed her by two days"**; a Sarovel hand: she went to sea; **"She hates boats."** **D.135 the order** (Strake's drawing, a brass half-egg template): **the central ring, two men tall, waist-thick, to sit at the Bridle's heart; inner face cut with six egg-shaped hollows in a circle, each a hand's breadth deep**. "What goes in the hollows?" "That's not the forge's business." **Tam (who made binders' collars at Millbrook): "I'll make chain... I'll not make that. I can't see what it's for... I'll not do it twice."** **Pike comes down at three (unheard of), listens: "Of course."** **D.136 Tam off the works with twenty Kessmoor wagons to the Accord's camp behind the Wend**, paid to the day; a thin quick Kessmoor smith takes the fourth forge and the ring; Odo sees Tam off from the towpath without laughing. **Nobody asks why.** **Heartstone feed: sealed iron crates by night barge, opened only inside the engines' sides with lids toward the engine; "old stock"; nobody sees inside.** **Night 137 Callis** at **the seventh engine's firebox**, night shift: thin, grey, in a too-big Kessmoor engine-hand's leather apron; **right forearm knit crooked halfway, left wrist thick and stiff** (Maren broke both); "**Not *sir*. I'm nobody here... I feed the boxes. They pay in bread... Grip died in the winter. In the snow. On the road... Please.**" Tobin says nothing and goes back for coal. **D.138 the letter by Moss's courier** (down the Iron Road every third day to the Marshal; "She'll have it in four days"): ***Marshal. Callis is in the works. Corvin's man. The one who opened the river gate. He's feeding the heartstone engines in the Ring Yard. Seventh engine. Night shift. I don't know who hired him. Tobin.*** **He tells nobody else: Pike holds the wages and the bread; Moss writes every name.** **D.140 at six**: the Bridle on the cinders; **the ring in a timber cradle at its heart, three hollows cut, three to go.**

## Kit (29)
- Ch 29 (days 126–135): **The custom kept** (ashore by day in pairs, back by dusk, the boat never left, nobody lies down even at noon); **Hal's longest stay was six days**; the crew restless, Tansy stops singing; **the fish swim to Tansy's shins and lie in her hands looking at her**; she lets the first go, cooks the second. **Dusk 128 Sim** (17, the elder brother) on boat-watch with Nye: "Just up the road. A little way. To see it close"; Nye shouts once and doesn't leave the boat ("The boat never leaves. You said"). **Dawn 129** found **a quarter-league up the road on his back in the grass at the paving's edge, one hand on the stone, eyes open to the sky, smiling a little, warm, no mark, not breathing**. Kit had told him the rule, never why (Hal told her Abram's brother's story once, at the tiller). **Idony**: "**It took his breath. The light. It drinks. Like the six drink... Only gently... It doesn't know it's doing it.**" "He wasn't asleep long." "**He didn't need to be.**" He closes the boy's eyes, slowly. **Sim buried on the beach above the tide-line beside the numberless milestone**; Nye digs it alone with the ketch's spade; Ferris says the words (as for Hal at sea without a body); **Kit pours all the salt from the chipped dish onto his chest**. **Gannet chips far-stone daily among the column-tops** (*tap, tap*); **night 130 Kit sees through the forehatch grating Gannet in the hold copying a page of Maren's square hand onto Merrin paper with a pencil stub**: "For the news... I keep a log... I'm copying the bits about the stone. So I'll know what I've got when I sell it... Can't sell a thing if you don't know what it is." "Don't let him see you. The old man. He's touchy about his notes." "He'd be flattered." **She doesn't look closer** (thinks about that afterwards for a long time). **D.131 Kit wants to leave**: **sixteen days at anchor, the longest by ten**; **Corrie won't go ashore at all now** (sits on the forehatch); Nye does the casks alone. Ferris: "You swore... Your father never swore salt to a knight of Greyhold with a lion on his deck." **She refills the dish from Tansy's sack and stays.** **D.133 Idony goes inland**: "I haven't slept more than an hour a night since the cellars... **I'm burned out... There's nothing left in me to drink. I'm the one man on this whole shore who's safe**" (a joke he enjoys); **a small wild grey horse comes down the beach, noses his shoulder, and lets him mount from Tansy's hands**; Kit: "You're not party to the contract." **"No. I'm a witness."** Tansy: "Witnesses can taste." **D.134 dawn Gannet goes up the road after Idony on foot** ("To see he didn't fall off the horse... I never sleep. Ask anyone"), canvas bag on his shoulder; Kit lets him. **Echo, d.135 from the platform by glass**: on the far hill's top the long grey shape in faint light; before it a grey lion lying; **beside him a woman sitting, very small, back against something**; **at the hill's foot a grey horse with an old man on it, going up**; Kit goes back to the ketch and waits "as she'd sworn to".
