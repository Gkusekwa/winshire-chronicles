# Book 3, Part Four: The Old Ones Come: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as Book 2's part ledgers. Before writing a scene that touches another POV's days or people, read this file. After deciding any fact another chapter could contradict (who is where on which day, what someone said, a name, a wound, a number, a beast), add one line under your name. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Kit (44)
- Ch 44 (days 185–195; 172–184 told back): **D.172 home**: twenty-four days by day-sailing; **nobody died**. **Blackwater's chain mended in iron with a toll-hut and grille on the western headland**; **the clerk won't take an unsalted hull's chain money** ("It's not a contract. It can't be"); she lies off a day. **Fosse in a four-oared boat: the 93 are the House of Ostrey's passengers; Ostrey pays their chain money, so the chain goes down for her ketch.** Loy ashore first without looking back; the rich to Ostrey's counting-house; servants and children anywhere; a little girl looks back. **Unsalted**: **the eastern-cliff spring's keeper (tally-stick; sold Hal water thirty years) turns his back; Tansy fills the casks at midnight a cupful at a time from a bitter peat-seep under the north shore by the boathouse**; nobody buys far-stone from her; the Merrin house still under Council wax; she lives aboard ("It wasn't funny any more"). **D.186 Fosse: the remnant Council (the houses that didn't sail, in the boathouse) finds the *Kittiwake* forfeit for breach of the d.92 requisition and assigns the claim to Ostrey; a Council marshal to take her d.196.** **D.187 Lisska brings Jory home**: the size of a hall now, she settles on the roofless boathouse and **its walls crack**; the whole inlet watches; Jory slides down her foreleg, rows across, runs; **Kit holds him on the walkway**: "The farmer brought me... He told Lisska. *Bring him home.*" Lisska looks sideways, pleased; her wingbeat rocks every boat. **That night the spring's keeper looks at the ground and lets Tansy fill the casks.** **D.189 Kit sees Gannet come out of Ostrey's counting-house putting a heavy purse in his coat; he raises his hand; she thinks he sold Ostrey far-stone flakes and doesn't ask** (he's crew, not house, so not unsalted; she thinks about it later for a long time). *(Off the page: he sells Fosse his copies of Idony's notes; Fosse sells on: ch. 60.)* **D.192 a Kessmoor courier brings Rowan's answer to Idony's letter** (said on d.173 in the stern cabin, **written by Corrie in her round hand**): ***The house of Merrin carries for the Accord, under the Accord's warrant, by the war's necessary costs. / Its ketch and its people are the Accord's carriers, and no other claim lies against them while the war lasts. / I. Rowan. Marshal.*** (brass seal). **Fosse: the Tide Council signed the Accord; "The house of Ostrey withdraws its claim... For the duration of the war... Wars end."** **At cost: no profit, no percentage, no chain-money; "There was no word for it in the League"; she takes it.** **Two old salt-white Saltings salt-barges bought on the Accord's paper** (the bargemaster spits and takes it), rigged by Ferris and Lorn in two days. **D.194 Idony goes up the Wend on the first barge** with Nye and Corrie and a hold of Saltings salt fish, sea-birds on his shoulders: "I'll tell them. What I read... Not at first. Then they will"; **the chain-hut takes the Accord's paper and lowers the chain.** **Echo, dawn 195 from the cliff-top: east under the mountains' end, out of the sun's road of light on the sea, the Lamp walks up onto the black rocks and inland north-west over the mountains' foot toward the Marches**; **all Blackwater comes down its ladders onto the walkways to look** (Fosse at his door in his nightshirt, spectacles in hand); nobody speaks. "At cost."
