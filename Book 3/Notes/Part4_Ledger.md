# Book 3, Part Four: The Old Ones Come: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as Book 2's part ledgers. Before writing a scene that touches another POV's days or people, read this file. After deciding any fact another chapter could contradict (who is where on which day, what someone said, a name, a wound, a number, a beast), add one line under your name. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (43, 47, 50, 53)
- Ch 43 (days 181–185): **D.181 low tide off the Drowned Bell's reef across a league of shallows to the coast below Gullhaven's empty hill**; **Saul Trent** (new: the fisherman of ch. 40) rows ahead looking back every third stroke. **Maren rides on Orrun's head now, between his ears in the mist of his mane** (the only place she can see from). **The Coast Road hums for him; he feels all Saltreach's stone** (*It's all stone. Under. All of it*). **Redshank** (new: a dozen stilt-huts at the Saltings' western edge; a handful of fisher-folk came back for the fish): **they run inland across the flats leaving pots on the fires; a dog stays.** **D.182 Brede** (white beard, old blue dragon) **and a red-thread girl of twenty with a cut lip on coast-patrol from the camp**; they land a quarter-league off; Orrun lies down; Brede walks up: "Knight." **News at a shingle fire**: the camp (ninety thousand; Rowan Marshal; the Downs; Hallowmere broken; the thing in the Mere); Corsa fallen; Marrow dead; **"And Ulric... On the Tidehall's steps... A clerk's boy, last. Sigge took the boy. The sea took Ulric... Arn and the farmer poured him, at Coldtarn. Together"**; **Maren understands Lisska's cry at dawn 95 was for Ulric**; **the farmer home three days back on hill-sized dragons; Brede: "I never thought I'd see the old one fly straight. I've known her sixty years."** **D.183 the wall**: a small walnut-brown old woman: "Then he can put my wall back" (her dead husband's man-length sea-wall knocked flat by the Bell's surf d.145); **Maren's smallest pull raises the stones and makes the low cliff above Redshank creak end to end and crack: a black line top to bottom, a man's width at the top, dust in the wind; she stops; the stones fall; the crack stays.** Orrun: *Too much... I'm too big. You're too big. Everything comes.* **"She couldn't mend a wall. She could drop a cliff."** Old woman: "Well. That's no good." **They rebuild it by hand all day** (Maren one-handed, unfeeling; the red-thread girl unasked; the villagers one by one): "That'll do." **Her rule, to Orrun: *Nothing small. Not with it... Anything small, I do with my hands... When there's nobody under the cliff.*** *That's hard... Harder than the big things.* *Yes. That's why.* **D.184 counting again aloud**: huts, boats, nets, gulls (losing them and starting again), his half-league steps, "The hundred-and-eighty-fourth day" (unsure; counts anyway); **Orrun counts with her: *Forty... Forty-one. Forty-two.* "Forty-three."** **Echo, dusk 185 on a rise above the Saltings** (Wend's mouth a day ahead; Brede's pairs settling): **Orrun lifts his head north-west through a hundred leagues of stone to the Mere: his kin lying in the water, head on the bottom, awake, bigger than he, a coal among five dark shapes on its back**: *It's my kin... Alone. Burning.* **He doesn't go; turns north and walks on**; she counts his steps aloud until she sleeps; he stays awake.

## Calder (45, 49, 51, 54)
- Ch 45 (days 185–190; 150–184 told back): **The three are too big for the yard** (the cave yard "a doorstep" for Kharrak's forefoot); **the high-moor herdsmen call them *the three hills***. **Calder recovering ten days on Ada's pallet by the warm spring** (scars pull; cough; short breath; stops twice climbing to the rim); Wren with her milestone stone; Ada's broth, no singing; Bel up from Varrhold with eggs and gossip ("the most restful thing in a year"). **They eat a flock a day**; herdsmen's tally-sticks ("put it on the account... Gorm's"); **Kharrak hunts the wild moor cattle and Plains stonehorns off the Scarfell's eastern edge**; **Lisska fishes Coldtarn and the other tarns empty**; **Aurveth eats a sheep in two days and looks** (east mostly). **About a hundred freed dragons sit on the crags round the bowl "like children round a fire"**, not too close. **D.160 Kharrak tries Teo's (third) ledge at the Roosts and it cracks; a piece of its lip falls into the gorge**: *It's small. Everything's small.* Riders won't come near; the ledges' young ones are frightened. **D.185 Arn on Ildra** (at Varrhold since returning from the coast): "They'd eat the Scarfell in a year... The riders won't go near them... They're not dragons any more." "They're dragons. They're just big." **D.186 on Sigrun's rock** (Aurveth on the pouring-stone, Kharrak a red wall along the rim, Lisska on the crag pretending not to listen, Ildra between Arn's feet): **"You never wanted them." "No... They're not the Clan's. And they're not mine."**; Gorm still has them in the books; the banner request "I didn't understand it... Yes" (now); Arn tells the pens (the red's head in the straw; *no*); "She chose me because I held a dying thing's head in the straw"; Teo stood once and laughed down the stair (Bel told Calder years ago); **the chair: "Two of us poured... For Ulric, you and me... Gunna laughed for an hour." "I'm not Clan." "No. That's why it's funny."**; **after: "They'll not come back to the ledges." "No." "Good... Gorm can write *gone*... Those three chose you... Not the Clan. Not even you, really. They chose."** **The pour for Ivo Brack and Sunniva without ashes was at Coldtarn d.160** (Calder asleep in the cave; Arn didn't ask). **Night 188 through Aurveth's deep sight, a hundred leagues (the Wend a cold line; the Downs black): the Last Titan lying on its side on the Mere's bottom, head on the mud, stone-and-leather wings folded, chain-ends on its legs; five cart-sized dark egg-shaped sleep relics grown into its back-plates (Ring, Vael's, Scald, Sun Stair, Bell) and among them the control relic burning "like one coal left at the back of a grate": exactly *behind the left shoulder, a hall's breadth below the ridge of the spine, between the second dark egg and the third*.** ***Only me. And you, through me.*** (Orrun feels it, can't see where.) He fixes the place in memory (plant for 68). **Echo (shown d.186): the grey lion-hill walking north up the Coast Road along the Saltings, the knight on its head, "Her mouth moves. All the time"** (counting). **D.190 a young grey-thread rider on a lathered horse** (no Clan rider will bring a dragon near the three hills) **with Rowan's paper: *The Accord musters at the camp behind the Wend on the two-hundredth day. / The old ones are coming. The Lamp has been seen at sea, walking. / Come. / I. Rowan.*** Wren: "Say it again." "We'll go." *Yes. Now.*

## Rowan (46)
- Ch 46 (days 200–205; 190–199 told back): **D.191 the lion** comes up the Wend's west bank from its mouth and lies down on the water-meadows below the camp's southern edge (nine thousand run, then stop when Maren stands on its head). **D.196 the three hills** come over the moor's north edge at dusk and lie along the long ridge above the camp's western side; Calder walks down, stopping twice. **D.196 Idony** arrives on the first Merrin barge (a salt-white Saltings barge, two Blackwater sailors, Nye and Corrie); asks Rowan for a table, paper and Corrie; **Corrie writes for him three days** (Davin stays Rowan's clerk). **The muster**: Hearthmere's knights (fewer than three hundred; 290 at the kneeling) with hounds; Tova's clans (ten thousand), stonehorns, Keepers and wolves, **four roc-riders under Yeva** on the eastern ridge; Sarovel's unchosen under Mattias on **the white bond-steeds their knights left** and **forty olifants** (up the Sun Road from Solenne, now hauling Kessmoor's wagons); **Isvaren: thirty Wardens with snow lynx under Warden Aud Hallin** (new: one eye lost to frost; won't talk of Vael) **and six stormkite-riders** (long thin grey birds that feel weather a day off); Rennick's sellswords; Nell Tallis's, Black Ruth's and the Crane brothers' wyverns; Arn's hundred pairs and the freed round the three hills; Pike's engines, Strake. **Night 199 the Lady**: the whole Wend goes pale and cold from the pontoon past the barn; at midnight a heron-like head on a river-long pale neck rises by the pontoon and lies on the water (stays there). **Dawn 200 the Lamp** comes over the eastern (Hearthmere-bank) downs on the sunrise and **walks across the Wend on the sun's light on the water** (the river doesn't part) into the camp's eastern meadow; too bright to look at. **Every beast in the camp turns its head to it at once** (incl. Ash and Sorrow at Wynn's knee, the stonehorns, hooded wyverns, rocs, the Clan's dragons, the three hills, the lion). **The forty olifants kneel in their traces on the camp road.** **Eleven wild unicorns** (from the Sunward Hills, Mattias says) run down through the western lines and stand in a ring round the Lamp. **Kneeling order**: Sarovel's unchosen first (some weeping), the clans (Tova off her bay mare), crown men, militia, sellswords last; ninety thousand. **Rowan doesn't, then dismounts and kneels for her knights, crown men and Agnes Wick's porters, so none kneels alone. Davin doesn't kneel; he counts.** **D.201 council in the barn** (Thale, Pike, Tova, Arn, Mattias, Rennick, Aud Hallin; Maren and Calder at the table's two ends; Idony on a stool): **Idony reads the far-shore script and says it: *The gods are what is left of Titans who stopped*** (the Grey Mother, the Lamp, the Lady, the three-faced god); **the collapse's cause read out** (script struck into iron; collars; chain with *hold*; a chained Titan under a city): "The one realm fell for doing exactly that." **Thale goes white, hand flat on the table; Tova: "*One again*... They wanted *that* again." Idony: "They didn't know what it was." Mattias puts his face in his hands.** **By dusk the whole camp has it; nobody says *one again* after.** **The plan**: the Lamp carries the relic; the six hear and come into the light and stop; **Calder's first words: "We take it there... The Accord holds the six. Together. In one place. Long enough. And the Lamp goes in and takes it." Maren nods.** **Pike (hint 3 of 5)**: "And afterwards?... Does the stone go too? Or does it remain? For keeping?" Idony: "I'd think it goes. It's theirs. They made it." Pike: "Of course. I ask only for the record. The clause... Into the Assembly's keeping"; writes in a small book. Rowan: Kessmoor being Kessmoor ("She'd think of it later"). **Echo d.203: Kit at the tiller of the second Merrin barge brings thirty Keepers in green from the Staithe** (the dead Thornwood's western edge) at the Accord's cost; **their wolves (thirty, forty) come off first, walk through the camp and lie down round the Lamp**. **D.205 banners on the water-meadows**: Hearthmere grey and gold; Sarovel white and gold (Mattias); Isvaren blue; Kessmoor black and grey; Saltreach a dozen captains' pennants on one pole; **the Wyldmarch's first-ever banner, a Keeper's cloak on a stonehorn's pole**; the Clan's red and grey thread plaited on one staff; **the Marshal's plain grey, no device, from a crown-man's cloak**. **Maren at the line's south end before the lion; Calder at the north end below the three hills; they don't look at each other.** The Lady stays in the river by the pontoon.

## Kit (44)
- Ch 44 (days 185–195; 172–184 told back): **D.172 home**: twenty-four days by day-sailing; **nobody died**. **Blackwater's chain mended in iron with a toll-hut and grille on the western headland**; **the clerk won't take an unsalted hull's chain money** ("It's not a contract. It can't be"); she lies off a day. **Fosse in a four-oared boat: the 93 are the House of Ostrey's passengers; Ostrey pays their chain money, so the chain goes down for her ketch.** Loy ashore first without looking back; the rich to Ostrey's counting-house; servants and children anywhere; a little girl looks back. **Unsalted**: **the eastern-cliff spring's keeper (tally-stick; sold Hal water thirty years) turns his back; Tansy fills the casks at midnight a cupful at a time from a bitter peat-seep under the north shore by the boathouse**; nobody buys far-stone from her; the Merrin house still under Council wax; she lives aboard ("It wasn't funny any more"). **D.186 Fosse: the remnant Council (the houses that didn't sail, in the boathouse) finds the *Kittiwake* forfeit for breach of the d.92 requisition and assigns the claim to Ostrey; a Council marshal to take her d.196.** **D.187 Lisska brings Jory home**: the size of a hall now, she settles on the roofless boathouse and **its walls crack**; the whole inlet watches; Jory slides down her foreleg, rows across, runs; **Kit holds him on the walkway**: "The farmer brought me... He told Lisska. *Bring him home.*" Lisska looks sideways, pleased; her wingbeat rocks every boat. **That night the spring's keeper looks at the ground and lets Tansy fill the casks.** **D.189 Kit sees Gannet come out of Ostrey's counting-house putting a heavy purse in his coat; he raises his hand; she thinks he sold Ostrey far-stone flakes and doesn't ask** (he's crew, not house, so not unsalted; she thinks about it later for a long time). *(Off the page: he sells Fosse his copies of Idony's notes; Fosse sells on: ch. 60.)* **D.192 a Kessmoor courier brings Rowan's answer to Idony's letter** (said on d.173 in the stern cabin, **written by Corrie in her round hand**): ***The house of Merrin carries for the Accord, under the Accord's warrant, by the war's necessary costs. / Its ketch and its people are the Accord's carriers, and no other claim lies against them while the war lasts. / I. Rowan. Marshal.*** (brass seal). **Fosse: the Tide Council signed the Accord; "The house of Ostrey withdraws its claim... For the duration of the war... Wars end."** **At cost: no profit, no percentage, no chain-money; "There was no word for it in the League"; she takes it.** **Two old salt-white Saltings salt-barges bought on the Accord's paper** (the bargemaster spits and takes it), rigged by Ferris and Lorn in two days. **D.194 Idony goes up the Wend on the first barge** with Nye and Corrie and a hold of Saltings salt fish, sea-birds on his shoulders: "I'll tell them. What I read... Not at first. Then they will"; **the chain-hut takes the Accord's paper and lowers the chain.** **Echo, dawn 195 from the cliff-top: east under the mountains' end, out of the sun's road of light on the sea, the Lamp walks up onto the black rocks and inland north-west over the mountains' foot toward the Marches**; **all Blackwater comes down its ladders onto the walkways to look** (Fosse at his door in his nightshirt, spectacles in hand); nobody speaks. "At cost."
