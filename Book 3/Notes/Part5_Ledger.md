## Maren (56, 59, 63)
- Ch 56 (days 272–275): **Dawn 272 a fifteen-year-old stormkite girl lands on the eastern rampart, ice on her bird's wings: the Wend froze in the night from the Isvaren border south to Eskby, black ice, clear, a yard thick ("You can see the bottom").** **Upper Wend villages** (new): **Eskby** (Hearthmere-bank ferry village with a stone landing and a chapel, twelve leagues above the hub), **Otterholt** (Saltreach bank, eel-weirs), **Trimley** (Hearthmere bank, a mill), a dozen farms: **two thousand people**. Maren remembers the first freeze (crossing to the lion at dusk in a punt; the family on the ice; the rope parting; something dragging her out). **Noon 273 from the high ground: the river black as glass to the border, fish frozen mid-swim, rime a quarter-league inland, thick as fur on Eskby's roofs and chapel bell-cote.** **The reeve (old man) and two hundred in a chapel built for sixty, pews burning: every well frozen to the bottom; beasts frozen standing in the byres; "Is it coming back?" "Yes."** **Dusk 273 Quell's column down the middle of the ice from the north: nineteen tuskers (oldest cow grey round the eyes, tusks yellowed, one broken short) and four hundred of Vael's last people on foot and sledges; the tuskers walk the river because the old road came down the valley (*They remember. They walk where it was*); the cow's rumble through Maren's boots; Orrun answers (*Kin. Far kin... They know me*).** **Master Beatrix Quell** (grey, thin, a too-big Warden's blue coat over a magister's black): **"Vael's under ice. To the tops of the towers. The lower city went in the summer. The upper in the autumn... The herd agreed to bring us. Isvaren thinks it hires them. It doesn't. It asks"; the Coil a day behind, going south, "to the middle".** **Night 273 loading sixty sledges (Eskby, Otterholt, Trimley); d.274 morning plaited rope traces round the tuskers' chests, three sledges each, four for the oldest cow; drovers carry no goads; south down the ice at a man's pace; Orrun on the bank (too heavy for the ice); Maren walks the ice at the back, watching it because she can't feel it.** **Noon 274 the Coil: a killing cold with no wind; rime racing inland; a crow falls frozen; a quarter-league pale coiling light under the ice from the north, the black ice going white "like old marble" behind it.** **Orrun goes onto the river; the ice breaks under him; the river-bed rises under his feet (bond gift); waist-deep, he stamps a quarter-league of ice open bank to bank so it must come up; it rises: a barn-sized pike-like pale head, ice-over-deep-water eyes, frosted scales; he takes it behind the jaw and holds its head down on the raised bed all day and night while it thrashes; ice cracks a league north; spray freezes and falls as hail.** **The cold comes up the bond into Maren's middle, "under her ribs, on the grey side, where the stone from the Thornwood lay. Cold going into cold." *It's my kin too. All of them are. Every one... It's so cold. It's always been this cold.*** **The thrashing cracks the ice under the column's tail: the last sledge (Trimley's miller's) goes in; Maren runs a league on the ice, goes flat as the river watch teaches, the miller's wife ties the rope round her; she can't feel her hand in the water; goes in; can't feel the cold (knows that's what will kill her); gets the girl's blanket, watching her hand; the rope goes slack and she loses the hole; something huge and pale lifts them both out onto the ice: this time she sees it: the Lady.** **The girl: Hessa** (new, nine, the miller's daughter), screaming, alive. **Quell wraps Maren in the Warden's coat: "You're shaking... Very badly... Can you not feel it?" "No."** **Night 274–275 the Lady comes twelve leagues up from the eddy under the ice, leaving a grey open running channel down the river's middle; dawn 275 she winds round the Coil in Orrun's open water; steam and frost in a white wall; the ice breaks up for leagues; (*Let go*, as a shape of light, heard through Orrun); the Coil flees north under the remaining ice; the Lady drives it two leagues "as a dog drives a sheep", turns back, the ice going to water behind her. The first time an elder fights.** **Orrun's jaw and forelegs frosted white to the shoulder; *It'll come again. When he calls it.*** **Echo, night 274: a low red glow far north-east beyond the Saltreach-bank hills, thirty leagues and more: Calder burning the ice off the Drover's Way fords at Cotter's Cross and Hollow Beck to keep the north road open (Lisska told Orrun on the wall two days before); too far to help; she hadn't wanted him to see her.** **D.275 south on the bank toward the hub: two thousand, the four hundred, sixty sledges, nineteen tuskers; Hessa asleep on her mother's back; Maren on Orrun's head in Quell's coat. Quell: "The Coil will come again." "Yes." "When?" "When it's called."**

## Idony (57)
- Ch 57 (days 276–277): **Midwinter night 276: the camp keeps the Hearthmere long night with fires along the tops of both ramparts end to end, two leagues of fire across the Vale (clans, Sarovel, even Kessmoor's crews with a brazier on the Ferry Flat).** **Idony in his old back room off Liss's kitchen (shelf, cot, window on the eddy), his eight glasses on the shelf; Davin asleep in the loft; Liss at the clans' fire on the Saltreach wall because Tova asked.** **Third hour: Corvin through the kitchen door: hood down, plain grey Kessmoor drover's coat, long pale face, short dark hair, grey eyes, thinner; the long case (the oldest roll) under his left arm; the right hand iron, black, pinned at every knuckle, brass wire tendons to a cuff strapped above the stone-sealed wrist; on its back near the cuff a stamp half filed off: a ring with a line across it like a rule.** **"You turned the third curve. In the word for *tighten*... The hound told me... I've worked at it a year"; he draws the chain-word in spilled water with an iron finger: curves one and two right, the third still turned as Idony taught it (wrong: *let go*), and the fourth, which was right, turned the other way with great care. "Tell me if it's right." Idony says nothing.** Corvin's case again (chains made it; chain it or command it; chains fail twice; "I'm offering it a hand on its neck instead"). **Idony's answer at last: "You never asked it what it wanted... Did you? Ever?... Nobody asked... There aren't any better hands. There are only hands... The old ones took their hands off... *They walked unbound.*"** **"No. I won't tell you." Corvin: "Then you'll have to be there to say it. All of it. Yourself." Idony: "I'll be there. I won't say it." "Where have you been? This year." "Measuring."** (He never says *Kessmoor*.) **Fourth hour: Rowan with twenty knights and Wynn's hounds, sword out (Davin ran to the hay-barn in his shirt); the camp searched all night (ferry, landing, a knight at each rope post, the pontoon of chained barges below the eddy, both walls, the batteries, tents, horse-lines): nothing; every third man in a drover's coat, half Kessmoor's crews in grey.** **Council d.277 in the hay-barn: Idony: "It's Kessmoor work, Master Engineer... I've mended Kessmoor glasses for forty years. The knuckles. The brass... a stamp... A ring and a rule." Pike at once, kindly: "Of course it is, Master Vael. We make the best hands in Winshire... Since the Scald... We sell them. On Gallows Quay, through the Bench's factors... Half the captains on the Bench have one of ours under their glove"** (Rennick wiggles a gloved left hand) **"A one-handed outlaw with Hallorans' silver would buy the best. And file the stamp... I'm sorry he frightened you." Rowan looks at Pike a long time, nods; orders a double watch on the ferry house (two knights and a hound at the kitchen door; a young woman knight with a falcon on a block). Idony doesn't argue.** (Hint 4 of 5, explained away.) **Echo, noon 277: Kessmoor's crews (two hundred, both banks, grey) at the six jib-posts on the eddy's edge with barn-long practice chain-lengths; crew-masters with open brass watches set by the Hall of Measures' clock; on the stroke of noon all six chains swing out over the eddy as one, hang over the old place, swing back: "Like the hands of a clock."** The water-word has dried to a pale cup-ring on the table.

## Rowan (55)
- Ch 55 (day 270; 249–269 told back): **The camp moved three leagues up the Wend to the eddy in one day, d.249** (Davin counted the lost boots). **Davin's count d.268: ninety-eight thousand** (the Sun Road column, the coast's last people, the last Marches farms). **The council now sits in Liss's hay-barn above the ferry house on the Hearthmere bank, looking down on the eddy** (Liss's goats moved to the next field, complaining; the door on trestles came up by cart). **Noon d.270 Rowan's chalk map of the Vale on the swept barn flags** (up half the night on her knees); all stand round it: Thale (on a stick now), Pike, Mattias, Rennick, Aud Hallin, Arn, Idony in his chair at the map's north edge, Maren at its foot (her right hand in her coat now too); **Keeper Rhun in the doorway with two wolves: "We'll stand. On your line. Where you put us. Not at your table."** **The line west to east: Hearthmere bank from the downs' foot: Hearthmere's knights (three hundred, with ones in from the north) and militia, greyharts carrying knights along the bend back to the river, hounds; then Sarovel: the sixty olifants as walls ("For their own. Not for a goad"), the unchosen on the white ex-bond-steeds behind them ("where they've something big to bolt behind"); then Kessmoor's batteries both banks round the hub ("Where the relic will be." "Agreed"); Saltreach bank from the river: Kessmoor's second battery, the Wyldmarch next to the river (stonehorns, Keepers' bears and wolves, clans), Isvaren (Aud Hallin's thirty Wardens with snow lynx and twelve unbonded, untamed sabrecats from the College's pens at Vael, down the Iron Fells with the last Wardens d.261, following the Wardens who fed them), Saltreach at the east end where the wall meets the scrub (Rennick's sellswords, Black Ruth's and the Cranes' wyverns on the ground till wanted; "Paid. By the Accord's chest. On my seal").** **Air: each over its own (the Clan wherever Arn says over centre and both banks; rocs over the Wyldmarch; stormkites over Isvaren calling turns for all; Nell Tallis's wyverns over Saltreach's end); "Nobody owns the sky. You share it." River: the Lamp, the Lady in the water, the lion, the three.** **Idony on the wards: *Keep the watch* is the old College jailer's charm *Keep the watch below... do not ask* with the chains taken out; said at noon by every voice on the line; two wards, one per bank, never across the Wend; the Vale is the deadest ground in Winshire, so the Lamp's and the Lady's light ("the Deep in the open air") feeds them "like oil into a lamp... While the voices don't doubt. And while the light holds."** **The Bridle minute of d.147 read aloud by Davin. Pike: the Bridle finished on its flats at Ironhythe, footings laid; Ostrey's sleepwood resin, bought through Blackwater on the Accord's warrant, in sealed casks; both only on the Marshal's word, only if the Lamp fails; the batteries hold their sectors and don't leave the line for the Bridle unless Rowan says; nothing taken off the field before the end. Pike: "Agreed... Of course, Marshal... Agreed." Rowan notes she agreed to everything, fast, and is glad ("She'd have been worried if Pike had argued").** **Calder flew in from the north that dawn; Davin has set a stool with Liss's cushion for him at the map's Saltreach end for the first time: "I'll stand"; he stands by the loft ladder; Davin takes the cushion away.** **Dusk, the yard, the trough: Maren gives Rowan Haverly's edge one-handed (right, watching her own grip): "It's no use to me. I don't fight with it. I fight with him... I've carried it two years. I've drawn it four times. It's been dark for three of them... He'd want it on a gate. Not in a lion's mane." Rowan draws a hand's breadth: the quickened strokes dark. "It's dark." "It's dead ground. It'll be dark on the day... It's still steel." Rowan wears it beside her own; it pulls her a little to the left.** **The footings (Kessmoor surveyors, thirty, d.255, ten days' work): on the Ferry Flat (the water-meadow on the Hearthmere bank between the rampart's river end and the eddy) twelve black iron beds flush with the turf in a ring a quarter-mile across, white cords pegged as spokes to an empty timber cradle at the middle, and six tall black iron jib-posts along the eddy's bank to swing the chains out over the water.** **Midnight 270, moon two days off full, frost: Rowan stands alone in the empty cradle; thinks of her Book 2 cage (six black chains in curves over the river below Greyhold, white lines in every link; *Better that than loose*), the first year's trap with Thorne's boar in it, and her summer words *Chains on a Titan are what started this*; tells herself it's a fallback that will be taken home in the spring unfired; believes it; sleeps.**
