# Book 3, Part Seven: Coldtarn: shared drafting ledger

*Same rules as Book 2's part ledgers. Before writing a scene that touches another POV's days or people, read this file. After deciding any fact another chapter could contradict (who is where on which day, what someone said, a name, a wound, a number, a beast), add one line under your name. The plan wins over the ledger if they clash; note the clash.*

## Maren (80, 83, 86)
- Ch 80 (day 322, afternoon to dusk): **Orrun walked north from the Scarfell's foot after the last wagon (d.321), his kin following at a distance and stopping when he stopped; at the second hour after noon 322 he comes over the bowl's southern rim in the surge-storm, round the shore through the crowds, and lies down across the foot of the new raw slope (Gannet's slide) with his chin on the door's step "As a dog lies across a doorway"; he puts out a foreleg on the stones as a stair; Maren climbs down his face onto the step.** **Kit: "You said you'd be at the door." "I'm here." "You're late." "Yes." "Two hundred and ninety... Two hundred and ninety-one. The girl in the red coat. I caught her... Gannet threw her."** **Maren's right hand flat on the humming live jamb; she counts on from "Two hundred and ninety-two" as people come up Orrun's foreleg, shoulder and head onto the step.** **A Wyldmarch woman's stonehorn calf (small, plated, bleating) counted.** **Ada's lantern round the shore from the caves; Ada, Bel, Pip and Nan; Ada: "Is this the place?" "Yes." "Then I'll come up"; Maren holds her lantern for the last pace: "Three hundred and five." "That's me, is it." "Yes." "Well."** **Wren (nearly twelve, plait tight) with Old Grey on a halter and the young mare Sorrel on a rope behind: Old Grey won't go into the dark; "Talk to him." "I do. He doesn't understand. Papa says... He just likes me." "Talk to him anyway." Wren's hand flat on his neck under the mane where the big vein runs (as Pell's on the grey in Greyhold's yard), talking low into him; he comes up over Orrun's foreleg and in; Maren counts the two greys.** **Wren: "Papa's on the moor. With Lisska and the old one. Bringing the last ones up... Kharrak's gone. Lisska told me. In the night... He said to tell me it was small."** **Fourth hour: Liss up the Way twenty leagues on foot with a sack and Fisk: "The river's gone... The ferry's on the mud... The windlass-pin. I brought the pin... There's no rope." (Three hundred and forty-one.) Fisk: "Somebody's got to. To tell it after."** **Wynn with Sorrow ("Does she count?" "She counts"). Eleven Kessmoor crews from the hub battery's twenty west-facing throwers who refused the orders and kept firing "Till the bank went" (Maren felt a man in a brake at the end, through the stone; didn't know who) (three hundred and sixty-eight). A Sorrel Isle olifant-boy with no olifant; two Wardens, one with a lame lynx; three burned sellswords; the Gullhaven woman captain with knots on a string; Osk and the wolf (already in: "I salted the wolf"; Maren takes one off).** **Dusk: "And you." "And me." Kit four hundred and seventy-two; Jory four hundred and seventy-three; plus a hound, a wolf, two greys, a stonehorn calf and a lynx. Niches full, floor filling; Tam: "Thirty more. If they stand."** **The roof cracks between the third and fourth pillars near the door (heard through the jamb "like a branch going in frost"); grit and a fist-sized stone fall; the roof-stone sags a hand's breadth; people start for the door.** **Idony from his hurdle by the third pillar: "*Hold*... I cut it once. At Vael... I can't hold a chisel." Tam with Odo's roll: "I can. I can't read." "I'll say the strokes. You cut them." Wren holds Old Grey still under the crack, talking into his neck; Tam stands on the dray-horse's back with Odo's chisel and hammer.** **Idony: the crag, door and roof are one stone; Maren's hand on the jamb with Orrun's earth through her from his chin on the step: "The ground's alive... The Deep's under us. Warm. That's why it's warm... It'll answer. If it's cut right."** **She holds the tired sagging roof through the stone; Idony says, Tam cuts (*tick*): *Down. / Then the hook. Left. / Then the cross. High. From the right. / The tail. Short. Down and in... That's the last.*** **The rune answers: the cut lines light thin white at their edges; the roof-stone lifts its hand's breadth back; the crack closes to a hair; the grit stops; the roof holds. Tam: "Is that right?" Idony (crying, she thinks): "Yes. It's right."** **Toll: colder than ever (colder than the Saltreach bank, the sea, the black ice), into her middle with the Thornwood stone; she shakes (Kit says so).** **Echo through Orrun, chin on the step, eyes south: his kin, white, blazing, on foot with wings folded, coming over the lip at Harrow's End past the grey shape on the top field without stopping, onto the high moor, up the Way toward Coldtarn, slowly: *He's coming up. He's slow... He's in no hurry.***

## Calder (78, 81, 84)
- Ch 78 (day 321): **Calder's voice back at dawn as a thread "like a door that hasn't been opened all winter"; *There you are.*** **The six roads run together above the Scarfell's foot into the Drover's Way, up through Harrow's End and over the Scarfell's lip toward Varrhold and Coldtarn; heads over the lip in the night; tails on the Way all morning; Tam's forty wagons last, Tam on the last box.** **Harrow's End empty (his mother's roofless house by the beck; the Eelweir below a ditch of mud where the river was; Fisk's house by the weir, door hanging); the Fenn top field on the slope between the village and the lip beside the Way: his grandfather's chest-high dry-stone walls that his father mended every spring, gone to weed, snowed (where he turned the elk).** **Noon: below the column's tail the snow goes off the moor in a line, heather smoking: the Horned Sun on the Way a league below Harrow's End, now two leagues of white day with the surge; it went round east of the lion in the night (the Last Titan rose off the hole at dusk 320, came north, and stopped two leagues west of the Scarfell's foot facing Orrun) and came up from Cotter's Cross onto the Way behind the tail.** **Kharrak: *I'll go. Back. To the field.* *I'm coming.* *No.* He lands on the lip and puts his head down: *Get off... Stand here. On the edge. Count them over. Like her... Somebody has to count them over... Farmer. Get off.* Calder gets off (the first time he's ever not come). *It was small. At the mouth. Wasn't it.* *It was small.* *Good.*** **Kharrak lands across the Way on the top field, wings out, tail over the wall, face to the light, and puts all his fire across the Way and the slope, a white wall a league long from the beck at the village's foot to the moor's shoulder: not at the Sun; between; the Sun presses; the field's dry-stone walls run to glowing slag "like candle-wax"; the snow goes off the slope; the Eelweir boils.** **Through the rust scar Calder feels everything go out of Kharrak: *Small?* *No. Not small. Not this time.*** **Calder on the lip counts the wagons over ("Thirty-one... Thirty-seven"); Kharrak's red going dark; Tam's last wagon over the lip at about the third hour after noon: "Forty"; Tam lifts one black-knuckled hand off the reins and goes on toward Coldtarn. *That's all of them, Kharrak. That's the last.*** **The fire-wall parts from the middle "as a curtain parts"; the Sun's light comes over Kharrak lying across the road on the field, red going grey, face on the running snow-melt: *I held it. I didn't kill it. I held it.* *You held it.* *I'm good at it.* *You are. You're the best at it.* *Tell the little one. The one with the ribbon. Wren. Tell her it was small.* *I'll tell her.* *Tell her twice.* Kharrak dies "as you feel a fire go out in a grate you're sitting by".** **The power comes back up the bond through the rust scar "As a river comes back into a dry bed"; for a breath Calder's pink-scarred palms glow white-gold from inside; he lives (unlike the mill, something came back: given). One of three.** **The Sun goes over Kharrak "as the sun goes over a stone", doesn't turn up the Way after the column, and goes east along the Scarfell's southern edge toward the Worldsend, burning, smaller.** "On that field Kharrak had held a sun." **Echo from the lip looking west: two leagues off at the Scarfell's foot the lion lying across the roads' end facing south, the size of a stone from here, and coming up toward it out of the Vale's light, white, on wings, slowly, his kin: the last thing between the Last Titan and the road.** Lisska lands beside Calder and puts her head against his back and says nothing.
- Ch 81 (day 322, dusk into night): **Since noon Calder on Lisska has brought the tail of the tail across the high moor: sixty (the hurt, the old who'd walked twenty leagues, a few of Arn's riders on foot whose dragons died on the Vale); he counts now ("She'd taught him, without trying"); Aurveth very high, seeing.** **Dusk: the surge comes over the Scarfell from the south as weather: a sky-high black wall of wind, snow and hail flickering green, white, gold and blue "like lightning in a summer cloud, but all the colours", roaring like the Shatters; the sixty on the bowl's southern rim with the shore path a quarter-league below, the storm a quarter-league behind.** **Lisska: *Go on. Get them down. I've got it*; she goes up into the storm's face and holds a roof of wind over the rim and the path herself (not through him, unlike the Thornwood); hail drumming on it; Wren's faded blue ribbon (tied on in the first winter; on her horn when she carried the five links to Maren) streaming from her horn. *Lisska, come down.* *When they're down. Not before.* He gets all sixty down to the shore: *They're down.* *Good.* Her wind goes out all at once "As a breath goes out of you when you've held it too long"; the storm drops onto the bowl; she comes down slowly "as a leaf comes down in still air" into the tarn's black open middle, which closes over her; the ribbon floats a moment blue on black and goes down. Lisska dies.** **The green scar hot, then nothing; two of three; the wind comes back into him and his lungs open fully for the first time in a year.** **Aurveth: *Under the ice*: through her, the spent Pale Coil (thin rime-light burning out in the surge, "a lamp burning white before it failed", not out) coming up Hollow Beck's frozen bed from the Varrhold side toward where the beck comes out of the rock under the door's slope; nobody else can see it; Orrun is looking south.** **Aurveth: *I'll go.* *No... Not you too.* *I'm old... I've been waiting a long time for something... Before the girl. Before Ragnald... This is it. I think this is it.* She comes down, enormous and slow, and lies across the beck's frozen mouth between the tarn's ice and the slope's foot beside Orrun, chin on the snow; the Coil comes up under her and pushes; the ice cracks; her bronze frosts white; she holds: *Get them up. The sixty... Go on.*** **Calder carries them up himself over Orrun's foreleg one at a time (an old Sun Road man light as a child; a rider with both legs burned whose dragon died at the hub; a woman with a baby); Maren on the step, hand on the jamb, counting without looking at him: "Four hundred and seventy-four. Four hundred and seventy-five."** **"Why me?" answered: *You could always carry more than one... One life between four. A house with the doors open. Most people are a house with one door... We knew. All three of us. The first time we saw you. Before you did.* *I'd have carried you.* *You did. All of us. All this way.*** **Her last: *He had your hands... The first one. My first. Or I've always thought so. Big. Square. Not clever. Kind... Ennis.* Aurveth dies; the bronze scar hot, then nothing; three of three.** **The Coil stops under her, lies a long breath, then sinks back down the beck west under the ice toward Varrhold, its light thinning, and doesn't come up.** **The sight comes back into him: through the storm, the crag, the Scarfell: the moor, the lip, the top field, the burning Vale and its hole, the flat grey mouth, the sea, dead Hearthmere, the Mere, Isvaren's lit snows, Sarovel's hills, the Worldsend, the Western Deep: to the edge of the world. Fire in his palms (Kharrak's), wind in his chest (Lisska's), sight in his eyes (Aurveth's): at the mill three died and took; now three died and gave; he lives.** **"Four hundred and ninety." He brings them all up; the last at full dark a ten-year-old Hearthmere boy with nobody: "Five hundred and thirty-three." "That's all... That's the last."** **Echo: on the step beside Maren in the doorway: the lion across the slope's foot, chin on the step, eyes south; over the bowl's southern rim in the storm, coming down the shore path on foot, wings folded, long neck low, white and blazing, toward the slope and the door: his kin.**

## Kit (79)
- Ch 79 (days 321–322): **Kit, Gannet and the one-eyed dog (under Gannet's coat, feet bleeding on the ruts) come over the lip at Harrow's End at the second hour of night 321 and pass the top field by starlight: walls glassy black slag, snow gone, ground steaming, a grey winged hill across the road; nobody on the road stops.** **Dawn 322 Coldtarn down the beck's valley path: black steaming middle, white ice ring; the freed gone from the crags in the night, nobody knows where; the hall's door open in the western crag's foot with a line of unreadable script above it; a steep forty-pace slope of loose scree and frozen turf from the shore to the step, the only way up; nobody at the door.** **On the shore: Tam's forty wagons (Tam with his head in his hands: "She's at the Scarfell's foot. With the lion. With the other one coming"); a block of a few hundred Clan families in red and grey from the ledges and Varrhold, waiting, not going in (Arn's *in the order they come*); thousands coming over the bowl's rim.** **Kit climbs to the step in the warm air from the door: "In the order you came. The knight said... Five hundred... The rest to the caves. The hold at Varrhold. The ledges. The byres... The wagons came first." Tam starts lifting the old out. Jory (came in the night with the Staithe's four hundred): "I'll count." Davin, with Idony carried up the Way on a hurdle by four men, sits on the step with a pencil: "Names. Someone should have them."** **The salt: the last of the *Kittiwake*'s galley dish (coarse grey Blackwater pan-salt) tipped into her coat pocket at the Staithe; a pinch on every tongue at the step: "Salt between us. Nothing owed."** (Instead of *What's sworn is kept, or neither of us is fit to drink with*: a contract with no price; "The salt meant *I won't*.") **Tam: "What's this?" "Salt." "What for?" "So you know you don't owe me anything."** **Then the Clan's families (a girl with a basket of eggs she won't let go); Kessmoor runaways in grey; Osk's lame once-broken-backed wolf from Varrhold's pens licks the salt off her fingers. Jory: "Two hundred."** **The overflow goes down the beck to Varrhold's hold and pens, the caves across the bowl (an old woman in a shawl at the cave mouth with a lantern lit in daylight waving them in: Ada), the ledges, led by Arn and his riders and Tam.** **Noon 322 the ground shakes like a deck taking a sea; the tarn's middle heaves; a hard south wind with sideways snow over the rim and the sky darkening; Idony on his hurdle: "It's the surge. It's coming up the Scarfell. From the middle... It'll be all weather."** **The scree slope slides toward the tarn under the climbing line; Kit, Jory and Davin pull people onto the step; a Wendmouth girl of five in a patched red floating-market coat sliding alone, crying; Gannet goes down the moving scree "as a sailor keeps his feet on a deck in a sea", picks her up and climbs back, grinning for the first time since the far shore; four paces from the step a water-cask-sized boulder falls from the crag above the door into his legs; he throws the girl up "as you'd throw a line to a dock": "Kit. *Catch.*" She catches; he goes down with the scree into the shallows. Gannet dies.** **They bring him up on ropes; on his back in the snow, eyes open, still grinning a little (as when he taught her to splice as her father's bosun); Kit puts the last pinch on his tongue: "Salt between us. Nothing owed"; she leaves the purse of Ostrey silver in his inside pocket and closes his coat over it.** **The sky over the tarn held against the storm: about fifty Clan pairs circling low; Yeva's last two rocs over the slope; a black-green wyvern with no rider (Black Ruth's or Nell Tallis's, nobody knows) flying among the dragons, unchained.** **Echo: high above all, Aurveth, crag-sized, circling with her head down, staring at the tarn's frozen western edge where Hollow Beck comes out from under the scree, at something under the ice Kit can't see.**
