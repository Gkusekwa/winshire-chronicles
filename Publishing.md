# Winshire — Publishing Plan

**Established:** the Winshire books will be self-published through **Amazon KDP** and **IngramSpark**, **one edition only**: the same complete text in ebook and print *(author, Oct 2026: no split, no simplified or abridged second edition)*. Self-publishing keeps control of the books and of all other rights, including game and screen rights.

*Everything below is a working plan. Platform terms change, so check each service's current rules before relying on them. This is not legal advice.*

## One edition *(established, Oct 2026)*

- **Every book is published complete, in one volume, never split**, with all the chapter-opener art and every chapter-end map. The ebook and the print book carry the same text.
- **Every chapter** *(established)*: it opens with its abstract picture and closes with the updated map. The bare map opens the book.
- **No simplified or abridged print text.** This replaces the earlier plan of a full ebook and a shorter print edition. An outside review (Oct 2026) warned that two texts risk reader backlash and double the continuity work across three books.
- **Print length is the constraint to solve.** At 6 × 9 inches and about 300 words a page, Book 1's draft (about 305,000 words) runs past 1,000 pages. That is above the usual print-on-demand paperback limits, so check the current maximums on KDP and IngramSpark for paperback and hardcover. The options:
  - a larger trim size or a denser page;
  - a hardcover or a large paperback, if IngramSpark allows the page count;
  - a later revision trim of the one text. The review suggested about 240,000–250,000 words for Book 1. Not yet: the author has said "no trimming just yet".

## Where the books go

- **Amazon KDP** *(established)*: the ebook in the Kindle store, and the print edition on Amazon.
  - **Ebook choice:** **KDP Select** (exclusive to Amazon in 90-day terms, enrolled in **Kindle Unlimited**, which pays by pages read and suits long fantasy series) or **wide** (also sold on Kobo, Apple Books, Google Play and others).
- **IngramSpark** *(established)*: the print edition for bookshops and libraries beyond Amazon, and ebook distribution to other stores if the ebook goes wide.
- **ISBNs:** the print edition needs one, and an ebook sold outside Amazon usually does too. Get your own from your country's ISBN agency so every edition lists your imprint.

## Ebook practicalities

- **Images:** about 160 images in Book 1 (79 openers and 79 maps, plus front matter). Keep them lean, greyscale PNG or JPEG about 1,600 px wide, because many readers use black-and-white e-ink screens.
- **Delivery fee:** under KDP's 70% royalty option, Amazon charges a delivery fee per megabyte of the ebook file, so compress every image and check the file size before upload. (Check current rates.)
- **Preview** on an e-ink Kindle, a phone and a tablet before release.

## From draft to published book

1. ~~**Finish the draft** of Book 1, working from the series outline.~~ *(Done: first draft, 79 chapters, about 311,000 words.)*
2. **Revise it yourself,** then share it with a few **beta readers** who read epic fantasy.
3. **Developmental edit:** a professional editor checks structure, pacing, characters, and the two-POV balance. This is the most valuable paid step.
4. **Copyedit, then proofread:** line-level corrections, then a final typo pass on the formatted book.
5. **Cover:** a professional fantasy cover designer, as covers sell genre fiction. Commission one cover style that can extend across all three books.
6. **Formatting:** the ebook and the print interior from the same text, with the opener art and chapter-end maps placed as images. Kindle Create is free; Vellum and Atticus are paid.
7. **Metadata:** title, subtitle ("Winshire, Book 1"), description, categories, and keywords.
8. **Upload** the ebook to KDP (and the wide stores if going wide), and the print edition to KDP and IngramSpark. Preview the ebook on several devices and order printed proofs of the print edition before release.

## Rights and the Winshire name

- **Keep all rights.** Don't sign anything that grants exclusive rights to the story, characters, or world. Editors, designers, and formatting services should work for hire, with you owning the result.
- **Copyright** arises automatically when you write the work in countries that follow the Berne Convention, including Tanzania. Registering it with your national copyright office (in Tanzania, COSOTA) is optional but gives you dated evidence.
- **The name "Winshire"** will span books and games. Consider a trademark search and filing before the first launch.
- **Contracts with editors, designers, and translators** should state that you own the finished work.

## Launch and readers

- **Build a reader list before launch:** a simple website and a mailing list, so readers of Book 1 hear about Book 2.
- **Advance readers:** send review copies to early readers so reviews exist on launch day.
- **Release timing:** fantasy series often sell best when the next books follow quickly. Consider writing well into Book 2 before Book 1 launches.
- **Serializing first** (for example, on Royal Road) can build an audience, but check that it doesn't conflict with KDP Select exclusivity if you choose that route.
- **Winshire Tales** *(settled by the author, Oct 2026; `Tales/Plan.md`)*: a within-Book-1 tale and a before-Book-1 tale go out free with Book 1 as reader magnets (mailing-list sign-up, KDP free days); after each book its tales are collected and sold as *Winshire Tales, Volume N*, ebook first, then print. First four: Old Grey, The Keeper of the Library, The Bridge Warden, The Drowned Road.
- **Keep the world consistent:** artwork, names, and canon stay consistent across the books and anything adapted from them later.
