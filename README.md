## Core canon

Winshire is the name of the world and the series. The six kingdoms occupy a land once united as the realm of Winshire; that older name is mostly forgotten. It is a medieval fantasy with learned magic and bonds between people and magical beasts.

The story follows two protagonists on a shared timeline, in alternating chapters, and their paths meet at the end of Book 1. One protagonist is an unbonded student training to become a knight, a woman aged 26–30. The other is a farmer aged 28–36 with a young daughter, living outside the Dragon Clan, who becomes accidentally bonded with all three dragons, almost simultaneously, when the Clan’s three bonded warriors die during the civil war. He is the first person in known Winshire history to bond with more than one beast.

The antagonist is the Restoration (working name), a faction that wants to restore the old realm by overthrowing the current regimes, capturing beasts through raids, roadblocks, and camps, and finding an ancient, powerful beast to aid its cause. It is led by twin brothers whose identity is revealed right before the battle at the end of Book 1, as the Restoration's first open war against the kingdoms breaks out; until then, people speak of its leader as one person who can be in two places at once. It is after an ancient Titan, known in Book 1 as the Last Titan, locked in the catacombs of Hearthmere, a kingdom rich in magic. Its events center on the border region that Hearthmere and Saltreach share, where both stories begin.

The story is told first as a **three-book series**, self-published through Amazon KDP and IngramSpark (the complete edition as an ebook only; a simplified version in print), with any games adapted from the books later. Book 1 ends with the Titan freed. Book 2 reveals, while everyone hunts it, that other Titans exist: the Titan travels between temples gathering relics; each sleep relic it takes wakes one of five Titans put to sleep ages ago, and the sixth relic gives it control over them. Some Titans prove to be world destroyers. From Book 2, the other kingdoms are introduced while Hearthmere and Saltreach keep growing, with parallel, intertwined events. Book 3 is the Titan War, in which the kingdoms unite (though one plots to be supreme), the ancient lion becomes a Titan, the three dragons become Titan-class, and the elder Titans join or give their power. Victory seems close, but the Titans win, and everything is destroyed. A remnant the protagonists saved survives; the lion and the dragons, now Titans, die protecting people; with no Titans left, the bond between beasts and people breaks; and the **Age of Elementals** begins. The student and the farmer survive as near-Titans and become the Council of Immortals in a next series, which reveals that some Titans survived.

**Settled after Book 1:** all magic is borrowed from the Titans (the Deep; six disciplines, not counting bond gifts or old magic; see `Bible/Magic.md`). Book 1 has few magic users; Maren and Calder don't cast in Book 1, begin limited casting in Book 2, and are untrained but capable in Book 3. From Book 2 a third POV joins: **Idony Vael**, a man, a tide-reader of the College of Tides at Vael in Isvaren, who survives the series. After Book 1, Maren and Calder don't travel together; they meet only out of necessity, each with a separate task. Orrun multiplies: glimpses in Book 1, one duplicate at a time, three or four by the end of Book 2, each as strong as he is. Some Book 1 warriors and leaders get POV chapters in Book 3 (the Book 3 plan proposes Rowan, Arn and Tobin, plus Kit Merrin, a new Blackwater reef-pilot). Book 3 explores Saltreach, where they meet the people who take Maren and Calder to the land beyond the sea, where other Titans are found. Book 1 runs from the harvest fair at the end of summer to early spring (day 0 to sunrise on day 144); see the Calendar in `Bible/World.md`. Books 2 and 3 are provisional until the author settles their open questions (`Bible/Series_Outline.md`, **Open questions for the author**).

## Continuity rules

- These are **beast books, not dragon books**: dragons are one kind of beast among many, the Dragon Clan's. Every kingdom's beasts matter on the page, and the sky is shared.
- Spell the land and the series **Winshire**, never "WinShire".
- A bond is what lets a person and a beast communicate. Some beasts are sentient before and after bonding, and they still exist after the fall. When the bond goes quiet at the end of Book 3, people and beasts can no longer understand each other.
- Mountain-sized beasts are called **Titans** in the story's world. Some are world destroyers. The elder Titans are Titans who transcended the physical: gods, truly divine, spoken of with reverence and belief.
- The border region is **the Marches**; a river marks the border, and the Old Ruin stands on an island or ford in it. The seal's two locks lie beneath the student's school and at the Old Ruin; the catacombs run beneath the border between them, and the protagonists meet underground there.
- There is one temple in each kingdom. Hearthmere's held its Titan's sleep relic, which Hearthmere broke to wake and chain it, and the control relic, hidden by old magic in the deepest catacomb vault; Hearthmere never knew it was there. The Titan returns there for it at the end of Book 2.
- Bonded partners feel each other's emotions and pain, speak mind to mind, and carry a visible bond mark; the farmer feels three minds at once. A bond shares speech and feeling, not every thought: a partner can keep a thought private, though strong feeling leaks through.
- The farmer's want is to know why the dragons came to him ("why me?"), which leads him into the old realm's history.
- A bond shares one life between two, in both directions: when either partner dies, the other usually dies too. The three dragons survived their partners' deaths because the farmer took the pull of all three at once.
- The books set the canon and come first; any games adapt them later (game scope to be decided).
- The collapse was an earlier rising of the Titans that nearly destroyed the old realm, until the elder Titans put them to sleep with old magic. The old realm split when the Titans were put to sleep. The split is unrelated to bonding; bonds existed before and after it.
- Exceptions to bond death (a partner surviving the other's death) are extremely rare.
- Book 1 ends with the Titan beneath Hearthmere freed.
- The series ends in defeat: the Titans win and everything is destroyed. "Everything" means the six kingdoms and the old world (their cities, crowns, laws, and the age of Titans and bonds), not every living thing. Don't soften it into a hidden victory. What survives is a remnant of people, the two protagonists (now near-Titans), and the sentient beasts; the kingdoms, the lion, and the dragons do not.
- When a bonded partner dies, their beast usually withers and dies. When every bond goes quiet at the end of Book 3, the beasts don't die.
- Everything about the Council of Immortals belongs to the later books. Books 1–3 don't reveal or name it, and don't hint that anyone can still hear beasts after the bond goes quiet.
- At the end of Book 3, no Titans appear to remain and beast–human bonding no longer exists; Book 3 only hints at the full destruction. The new age is the Age of Elementals. Don't reveal in Books 1–3 that some Titans survived; that belongs to the next series.
- In the story's world, only ancient beasts, old songs, and a few scholars use the name Winshire; most people speak of their own kingdom or "the Six Kingdoms."
- Saltreach is a confirmed name. The other kingdom names (Kessmoor, Sarovel, Hearthmere, Isvaren, the Wyldmarch) are working names until confirmed.
- Knights are protectors. A bond is not required for knighthood, except in the kingdom that makes it a requirement (Sarovel).
- Magic can be learned without a bond. Knight schools and magic schools are separate.
- The student begins unbonded. The lion-like beast's bond is gradual and mutual, formed only through respect and sacrifice, and the beast stays violent by nature afterward.
- The farmer never seeks, finds, or captures his dragons. The three bonds form accidentally and almost at once, as the dragons are drawn to him while their warriors die, and they feel like a curse before they can feel like a gift. He is the first person in known Winshire history to bond with more than one beast.
- When either bonded partner dies, the other usually dies too (one life between two, both ways). The farmer's threefold bond is a singular exception, not a repeatable method.
- The twins are **Cassian and Corvin Vale**. Maren kills Cassian, the public face; Corvin, the strategist, survives Book 1 and is killed in Book 3. One is killed at the conclusion of Book 1 by the student, as his lock gives way (the moment the lion learns its kin is now a world destroyer), while his brother, Corvin, turns the other lock under the Old Ruin at the same moment. The other continues through Book 2 and is killed in Book 3.
- Don't reveal in Book 1 that other Titans exist; in legend, the Last Titan is the last of its kind. Book 2's first clue is carvings of six Titans in a temple the Titan has just left.
- Hearthmere discovered where its Titan slept, freed it from its sleep, chained it, and harnessed its power; that is the source of Hearthmere's magic. Ages in chains made the Titan a destroyer. Only Hearthmere's rulers and the inner circle of the knightly orders know this secret; most of Hearthmere's history is built on it. The Restoration exposes it as part of its case against the regimes. The full history of when and how it happened is revealed in Book 2, through books, scrolls, and an intelligent beast that learned to speak the human tongue. That beast is killed in Book 2 by the surviving twin's hunters, so none is left who can speak with people when the bond goes quiet.
- There are six temples, one per kingdom. There were once seven relics (six sleep relics and the control relic); Hearthmere broke the Last Titan's sleep relic, so six remain: five sleep relics and the control relic. Only sleep relics wake Titans. For each sleep relic the Titan acquires, its Titan awakes and starts causing havoc, sending the kingdoms further into chaos. By the end of Book 2 the five are awake and the Titan takes the sixth relic, gaining control over them. The elder Titans put the Titans to sleep with old magic, and the temples were built at that time to hold them asleep. The sixth relic grants control over other Titans; the elder Titans made it as a safeguard in case the Titans ever escaped, and the Last Titan uses it to command the five once they are awake. Each of the other five relics holds one sleeper's sleep. The sixth is the last and hardest to reach, the one every kingdom and the surviving twin want most, and the power of control it grants is what the plotting kingdom is after in Book 3.
- The Last Titan was not always a destroyer; ages in chains made it one. The lion becomes a Titan without becoming a destroyer.
- Never reveal that the Restoration has two leaders before the moment right before Book 1's final battle. The Dragon Clan's civil war happens earlier and does not count as the first war. Characters speak of one leader who can be in two places at once.
- The Dragon Clan war is the dead chief's brother against the chief's two sons. The younger son's killing that starts it was the Restoration's doing, to gain the dragons; the truth comes out in a conversation between the twins, overheard by the farmer right after the twins are revealed and before Book 1's final battle. In Book 2 he brings it to the Clan because the war is hurting his people and village, and the uncle and the elder son unite against the Restoration. Until then the uncle's faction is blamed, and the war has no winner in Book 1.
- Book 1's ending, in order: the twins are revealed right before the battle; the farmer overhears their conversation; the Restoration's first war breaks out; the battle at the seal; one twin dies as his lock gives way and the seal opens; the student and the farmer meet for the first time in the shaking catacombs; the Titan tears free of its chains and flies, and the book ends with the two of them watching it go.
- The Restoration opens the seal to capture the Titan and bind it to its cause, and loses control of it; it does not set out to free it.
- The catacomb seal has two locks that must be opened in two places at the same moment, which is why only twins could open it.
- Throughout Book 1, the Titan beneath Hearthmere is called **the Last Titan**. To ordinary people it is only a legend, which most think is myth; only Hearthmere's rulers and the knightly orders' inner circle know it is real, chained, and powering Hearthmere's magic.
- Both stories begin in the border region shared by Hearthmere and Saltreach: the school on Hearthmere's side, the farm and the Dragon Clan's lands on Saltreach's side.
- Both protagonists share one story and one timeline, told in strictly alternating chapters, and their paths converge at the end of Book 1. (From Book 2 other POVs slot between their chapters, while Maren's and Calder's keep strict alternation with each other.) Throughout, each protagonist's chapters reference events happening to the other (rumors, sightings, news, signs), until they meet.
- Calder stays near the Old Ruin while his family flees because the Restoration is hunting him; distance is what keeps Wren safe.
- Teo Varr's killer is **Rook**, a Restoration agent hired into Ulric's men. Rook dies at the Old Ruin before he can talk, so the truth waits for Book 2.
- Maren knows the Restoration has two leaders before she kills Cassian, from Calder's warning. Lisska can't speak to her, so the warning is physical: the cut chain, a strip of cloth with "TWO" burned into it, and Wren's ribbon.
- Maren gets into the catacombs because the dying Warden Hale gives her the long black key to the black door at the end of Greyhold's archive.
- Book 1 has no prologue; it opens on the river.
- Saltreach is a league of port-cities ruled by the Tide Council at Corsa, with no king and no knights. Everyone still calls it one of the six kingdoms. Its writ barely reaches the Marches, where farms pay their dues to the Dragon Clan.
- The Dragon Clan breeds and keeps many dragons; only a few are bonded to warriors. Most dragons in the Clan's flights, like the funeral flight in Chapter 2, are unbonded and kept under the Clan's control.
- Wyverns are not dragons: two legs and wings, not sentient like dragons, tamed without bonds by Saltreach pirates and sellswords. Unicorns and the phoenix are great beasts. Unicorns remember the old realm and almost never bond. A few phoenixes exist, revealed slowly because they are so rarely seen; no one bonds one, because when it burns its partner dies and only the phoenix returns. See `Bible/Bestiary.md`.
- A true bond forms only by the beast's choice. Forced "bonds" are **bindings**: coercion that imitates a bond, with no understanding, no mind-speech and no shared life. A binding can be where a bond happens, if someone offers themselves into it and the beast chooses them (Maren and Orrun).
- No new bonds with Titans form in Books 1–3, but a bond made before a beast grows into a Titan lasts through the change (Maren and Orrun; Calder and the three dragons).
- Each book is published complete as a single-volume ebook, never split. Only a simplified version goes to print. Once a book's draft is complete, a review pass may cut or adjust storylines.
- Every chapter opens with its abstract picture and closes with the updated map, in both the ebook and the print edition. The book opens on the bare map.
- Treat undeveloped names, mechanics, history, and plot turns as proposals until they are agreed.
