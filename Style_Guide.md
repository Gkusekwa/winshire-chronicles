# Winshire — Style Guide

*Working conventions for the prose. Items marked "proposal" are suggestions until agreed.*

## Point of view and structure *(established)*

- Two protagonists, **strictly alternating chapters**: odd chapters follow the student, even chapters the farmer (Book 1).
- Each protagonist's chapters **reference events happening to the other**: rumors, sightings, news, signs. The reader knows more than either protagonist until they meet.

### Books 2–3 *(established: as drafted)*

- **Book 2: three POVs** (S Maren, F Calder, R Idony). S and F keep strict alternation with each other; R chapters slot between them, never two in a row (`Book 2/Chapter_Plan.md`). Idony's chapters "think in measurements" (`Bible/Magic.md` §11).
- **Book 3: seven POVs** (S, F and R, plus G Rowan, C Arn, U Tobin and M Kit Merrin). S and F keep strict alternation with each other; the other five slot between them, never two in a row (`Book 3/Chapter_Plan.md`).
- **Magic on the page** follows `Bible/Magic.md` §10: "magic tips fights; steel and beasts win them."
- **Terms:** the Deep, the toll, dead ground, the six disciplines (charms, runes, drawing, shaping, tide-reading, binding); a *binding* is never a *bond*.
- **Kept-on-purpose near-names:** Orrin/Orrun, Idony Vael/the Vales/the city of Vael.

## Voice *(chosen: B + C together; see `Book 1/Notes/Voice_Samples.md`)*

- **Close third person, past tense.** Each chapter stays inside one protagonist's head and knows only what they know.
- **Two lenses, one voice.**
  - **C, the wide lens:** the world, the land, time, the old realm, oaths, songs and sayings. Longer sentences that take their time. A storyteller's "they say" is allowed.
  - **B, the close lens:** inside the scene. Bodies, danger, choices and talk. Short sentences, fragments, the right detail and no more.
- **When to use which:**
  - Open a chapter or a new place wide (C), then cut in close (B) when someone moves or speaks.
  - Pull wide again for things bigger than the character: dragons overhead, the river, a ruin, a Titan.
  - Action and dialogue stay B. Don't drift into lyricism during a fight.
  - C appears in dialogue only through older voices: oaths, songs, Ada's sayings, the mentor, the dragons' memories.
- **Description:** sky, land and anything ancient get C, with a few sentences, rhythm and a strange or exact comparison. People, beasts up close and violence get B: one or two hard details ("sand in her teeth"), never a full inventory.
- **Maren** leans B: counting, distances, short sentences under pressure, feelings held off the page.
- **Calder** is plainer. His C comes from folk sayings and his mother's songs rather than grandeur, his B from farm work. After the bond, three other minds crowd his chapters.
- **Mind-speech** (bond and dragon speech) goes in *italics*, with no quotation marks.

## Keeping it human *(working rules for every draft)*

**Show it on the page**
- Don't name an emotion when a body, an action or a choice can carry it. Never "a mix of fear and determination."
- Use specific nouns: "a grey cat the size of a calf," not "a magnificent beast."
- Similes come from the viewpoint character's own world: soldiering and the river for Maren; farms, weather and animals for Calder. Don't use any comparison they couldn't have thought of.
- Enter scenes late and leave early. End a scene on an action, an image or a line of dialogue, never a summary or a lesson.

**Rhythm**
- Vary sentence length on purpose. Fragments and run-ons are allowed when the mind works that way.
- Don't group things in threes by reflex (three adjectives, three clauses, three beats).
- Use few em dashes; prefer commas and full stops.
- Avoid balanced, symmetrical sentences ("not just X, but Y"; "It wasn't X. It was Y.").
- Use "Not X. Y." corrections sparingly, and never in clusters.
- No "the way you… / the way a…" similes. Use no comparison, or a plain "like" or "as".
- Thin out "twice", "say it twice", "didn't say anything", "for a long time" and "didn't know why" wherever they bunch up.

**Chapter exits**
- Vary how chapters end. No more than two chapters in a part end on lying awake, dusk at a window, or something "still warm".
- Don't end two chapters on the same line or image, and don't reuse a simile word for word.
- Never close on a stated design or lesson.
- "She didn't count them." is the last line of the trilogy (Book 3 ch 86) and appears nowhere else, nor anything close to it. Maren can still leave something uncounted; show it rather than say it.

**Dialogue**
- People talk past each other, interrupt, and leave things out. Nobody explains what both speakers already know.
- Use "said" almost always. Don't fill pauses with nods, soft smiles or sighs.
- Humor is allowed, even in grim chapters.
- Keep the voices distinct where they speak. Tobin is hurried and sharp; Liss warm and bustling; Cassian warm and persuasive, Corvin brief; Hob the miller dry and mercantile, prices first. Each dragon sounds different even in italics (`Bible/Characters.md`).

**Words and phrases to avoid**
- testament, tapestry, delve, palpable, intricate, symphony, ethereal, unspoken, visceral
- "the weight of…", "something shifted", "in that moment", "a dance of…"
- "let out a breath she didn't know she was holding", "eyes that held…", "a flicker of…"
- metaphorical "echoed" and "whispered" (wind whispering, history echoing)

**Process**
- Read each chapter aloud. Rewrite any sentence you wouldn't say.
- Every chapter gets a pass in Goodluck's own words. AI drafts are a starting point, not the finished voice.
- Amazon KDP asks whether a book's text, images or translations are AI-generated, so check its current content guidelines before publishing. If chapters start from AI drafts, keep notes on how much was rewritten.

## Length

- **No upper limit.** All three books are drafted: Book 1 in **79 chapters in six parts, about 311,000 words** (first draft, about 3,900 a chapter), Book 2 in 88 chapters and Book 3 in 86, 253 in all. Chapters run as long as their scenes need. Early chapters near 3,000 words are fine.
- Length has to come from people, place and consequence: every scene earns its room by changing something or showing someone we'll care about later. Cut anything that only repeats.

## Names and terms

- **Winshire** (never "WinShire"). Only ancient beasts, old songs, and a few scholars use the name; most people say "the Six Kingdoms."
- **Titans** for mountain-sized beasts; **the Last Titan** for the one beneath Hearthmere in Book 1.
- **The elder Titans** are known to people only as **gods**. Don't call them Titans on the page in Book 1.
- **The Marches** for the border region; **the Old Ruin** for the ruin on the border river.
- **The Restoration** (final) for the antagonist; before chapter 68, always **"the leader,"** one man.
- The six kingdom names and the place names as used in the drafts are final *(author, Oct 2026)*.

## Reveals to protect (Book 1)

- Don't reveal that the Restoration has two leaders before chapter 68.
- Don't reveal that other Titans exist; the Last Titan is only legend until chapter 45.
- Don't reveal the Restoration's part in the Dragon Clan killing until the farmer overhears it in chapter 68.
- Nothing about the Council of Immortals, ever, in Books 1–3.

## Manuscript files

- One file per chapter in each book's `Chapters/` folder (`Book 1/Chapters/`, `Book 2/Chapters/`, `Book 3/Chapters/`), named `Chapter NN - Title.md`.
- Each file begins with planning notes between `<!-- notes -->` markers, then a `---` line. **Write the prose below the line.** The notes don't go into the published book.

## Book design *(working)*

- **Every chapter** *(established)*: it **opens with its abstract picture** and **closes with the updated map**. The books are published as ebooks only, in one complete edition (Oct 2026).

- **Chapter openers:** each chapter opens with its abstract ink piece (`Book 1/Art/Chapter Openers/`).
- **Chapter endings:** each chapter closes with a map page (`Book 1/Art/Maps/Chapter endings/`). All of Winshire sits on top, showing only the names the story has reached so far; unexplored lands stay bare. A close-up of the Marches sits below it, with a list of what's new on the map. New places and events are ringed. Older events keep their mark without a label.
- **Front matter:** the book opens on a bare Winshire with no names (`Book 1/Art/Maps/Front matter/`). Readers fill it in by reading.
- **Books 2–3** still need their own chapter-opener art and chapter-end maps; neither is made yet. Each plan row names where its chapter-end map should centre (the **Ends at** column).
- **Keep the maps in step with the prose.** When a drafted chapter moves a place or event, or mentions a new one, update `Tools/atlas/marches_items.py` and rebuild. `Bible/Atlas.md` lists what appears in each chapter.
