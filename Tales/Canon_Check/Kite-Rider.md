# Canon check: Kite-Rider

**Checked:** Book 2 ch 3, 8, 28, 29, 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41 in full (25, 27, 31 and 32 for the Moot birds, the Titan's arrival and Brannagh). I also searched all three books and the Bible for stormkite, kite-rider, Pass Wardens, Runa, Tatter, Stell, Bettrys, Dorran, Jenet, Brannagh, Tilde, Grete, the lake gate, the toll-house and the pass-stone, and for days 116 and 166–178. Against the Bible I checked Bestiary (stormkites, tuskers, lantern owls), World.md (Glasswater's outflow, the Vael Pass, the Settled block) and Atlas.md, plus the map data in Tools/atlas (pass-stone, Vael, Glasswater, the Titan's day-173 route). The Plan entry (19) is respected.

**Fixed:**
1. Stell, the villagers: "a baby six days old" → "a baby five days old". The tale puts the birth on the night of the surge, which is night 172 (Book 2 ch 36: "The surge came up through the hill at dusk"). This scene is on the morning of day 177 (ch 37, "The lake rose in the middle of the morning" on the fifth day). That's five days, not six.
2. Dawn 178, the dragons leaving: "Something red went up off the shore behind them, and something green with it" → "Something green went up off the shore behind them, and something red after it". Book 2 ch 41: "Lisska went up first. Quick, green, into the grey light", with the yearling and Kharrak after.
3. The frost-map: "A circle, wide as a threshing-floor ... six long straight lines, each of them forty paces at least ... with a mark at the end of each one" → "A circle, the width of a cart ... five long lines, each of them forty paces at least ... with a mark at the end of each one, and a sixth that went a few paces down the river-line and stopped". Book 2 ch 41 says Calder "drew a circle ... round, the width of a cart". It also says the fifth line "went a little way south-east, and then turned east", so it isn't straight, and that "The sixth was short. He stopped after a few paces."
4. Notes block: added "Canon-checked against the books: 29 Oct 2026."

**Defensible:**
- **Warning Nessa.** Idony warns Nessa himself on the morning of day 172 (ch 36). The tale keeps to that: Runa warns Stell, as the Plan and Bestiary now say.
- **Idony's summonses.** He says "Three" and Tilde says "Four, as of tonight" on night 176. This agrees with ch 37 (night 175: "Quell's sent for me three times").
- **Day 172.** The binding, the lamps going out, the ice breaking, the three wingbeats, the Blue tower under the forefoot, the head into the hill, the stone in its jaws and the return to the ice all happen in the same order as ch 36. The fleet keel-up on the slips, the owl's cage lashed to the *Heron* and Mother Talley's chair all match ch 36. So does the surge at dusk.
- **Days 173–177.** These match ch 37: the Titan west at dawn 173; snow on the pass and Orrun at the lake gate on day 174; the dyers back down on day 176, with a Warden at the foot of Rope Street; the herd at noon on day 176 and Brannagh opening the gate himself; the Coil, the wave, the wall, midday south over the lip, the red and green dragons in the early afternoon, smoke on the hill, the lamps dying top-down at dusk.
- **The pyre and the dawn departure.** These match ch 40 and 41: the pyre on the Library steps as the only light in Vael, Kharrak lying by it and Orrun standing, then dawn 178 with the three going up the shore to the west road and the bent man on a stick.
- **Brannagh "didn't write it down".** This is about Brannagh, not the clerk, who in ch 28 writes something small beside Maren's name. It agrees with ch 37 ("without writing Orrun down").
- **"Six mornings".** Bettrys's "every morning for six days" is right if she crossed on day 171 (mornings 172–177).
- **Runa's age.** She's fourteen. The Bestiary says "a girl of fifteen or a slight old woman", which reads as typical. In Book 3 ch 49 and 56 the riders are "girls of fifteen", so Runa could be one of them a year later.
- **Horn calls.** Runa uses one note for *come down* and three for *trouble*. That's the pass-watch's own code. The one/two/long code in Book 3 ch 49 is a battle code agreed in the field.
- **Size of the lake.** Atlas.md gives Glasswater no width. The prose is on a league scale ("a league of it at least", ch 41; the lip heard "from a league off", ch 37), so a league across to Stell is fine.
- **The Titan's route on dawn 173.** The tale has it pass "north of the pass, half a league off". That paraphrases ch 37 ("west, low over the far shore ... along the snow-line under the High Crown"). On the map the line runs north-west, about one and a half leagues from the pass-stone. The book's own wording is the problem (see below).
- **The green glass.** Runa takes it for "the kind the College's tide-readers carried". That's her guess; in fact it's Idony's own make (ch 3).
- **Stell and Tern End.** *The Night the Glasses Broke* lands the *Heron* at Tern End. Ch 29 has her crossing "to the far shore villages", plural, so both tales can stand: Stell is one of her landings. The Bestiary's "the far landing" may want "a far landing".

**Book-side issues:**
- **Which shore is the far shore?** Book 2 ch 8 has "the snowfields of the High Crown above the far shore", and ch 37 has the Titan going "low over the far shore, and ... along the snow-line under the High Crown". But Atlas.md and ch 28 put Vael on the *north* shore with the High Crown to the north, and ch 32 has the Iron Fells to the south-east across the lake. That makes the far shore the south shore, under the Fells, not the High Crown. The map's day-173 Titan route (Tools/atlas t05) runs north-west and doesn't cross the far shore.
- The Bestiary line on Stell ("the far landing") could be softened to "a far landing" so it fits *The Night the Glasses Broke* (Tern End). That's for the author, not a fix.

**Verdict:** fixed (3 fixes, plus the checked line).
