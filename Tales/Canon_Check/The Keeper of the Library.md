# Canon check: The Keeper of the Library

**Checked:** Book 2 ch 3, 8, 28, 29, 31, 32, 34, 36, 37, 38, 39 and 40 in full (ch 39 line by line against the tale's last section, and ch 71 for the captain). I also searched all three books and the Bible for the Old Reader, the bird, the perch, the case, Merriman, Lusk, Corlis, Mayle, Dane, Quell and the Undercroft, and for days 128, 135, 138, 140, 152, 160 and 172–177. Against the Bible I checked Bestiary (the speaking raven), World.md (the beast that learned to speak; killed by the twin's hunters) and Atlas.md (the Long Library, half a league long). The Plan entry (13) and its Must not reveal are respected: nothing past ch 34, the ring's fate isn't touched, only *together* is given from the lock, the Unbound isn't mentioned, there's no Council of Immortals and no surviving Titan.

**Fixed:**
1. Night 176: the summer hum "the bird had felt through its feet on the oak for fifty days" → "fifty-six days". Book 2 ch 36 gives the count several times: "the hum, that had been under everything in Vael for fifty-six days"; the quicksilver "that had been climbing for fifty-six days".
2. The morning of the breaking: "Under the river hill on the border. Where Greyhold is." → add "The knights' school." The tale quotes Idony's line, and Book 2 ch 3 gives it as: "Under Hearthmere," said Idony. "Under the river hill on the border. Where Greyhold is. The knights' school."
3. Footnote on the thread: "He did not come for ten days." → "twelve days". The glasses were taken on the morning of day 2 (ch 3). The thread scene is the afternoon Dane meets him "two days later" after the day-12 Council (ch 8), so day 14, twelve days on.
4. Notes block: added "Canon-checked against the books: 29 Oct 2026."

**Defensible:**
- **Ch 39 in full.** The captain's lines, the net ("Old things. In a flood. Everything's full today. Even feathers."), the cage, "No", Idony stepping in front and the coin all match word for word, as do "Corvin", "Come with us ... every tide in Winshire", the drawer by the Undercroft stair and "Step aside, Reader ... one more in him". So do the blow thirty paces into the desk under the third window with the slates jumping, the three bolts from six paces, the bird sitting down on the case, and "Ferryman." / "Ask." / "Ask, ferryman. Ask everything."
- **Earlier scenes.** These also match: "Then the door is open" and the refused glasses (ch 3); the hour and the thread (ch 8); day 128, the walker gives back, "Not yet" (ch 29); the window on day 135 and its hour (ch 29); night 138, "please", "Nobody has asked / Nobody knew to / That was the point", the third clause and the appendix (ch 31); day 140, "Door-keeper", ten paces, "Not long enough", the foot and *together* (ch 32); day 152 and the fire on the steps (ch 34); night 160, the whitefish (ch 36).
- **Wystan Lusk.** The tale makes the old inky reader who kept writing when the lion came (ch 32, 40) the same man as the one who sat "every day for forty years" under the third window (ch 39). Ch 32 places the inky reader "at the end of the nearest table", at the door end, on day 140. I've read that as where he happened to sit that day. The tale doesn't say where he was.
- **"*On the steps. He remembers the fire.*"** This is Maren saying aloud what Orrun told her (ch 34 shows her hand flat on the desk). Ch 32 has her relay his words aloud in the same way.
- **The bird's age.** The tale leaves it unknown ("It remembered the carts ... Before that ... wind"). That fits both "older than this hall" (ch 31) and "not as old as that" (ch 32).
- **"Three hundred and four years" for the case.** Ch 34's "three hundred and six" is for the archivist's events, so the case came about two years after them.

**Book-side issues:** none new. The LOG's notes stand: the bird's age (ch 31 vs 32), and ch 65 implying the roll told Corvin where the ring is vs ch 34's "nobody knew".

**Verdict:** fixed (3 fixes, plus the checked line).
