# Canon check: The Older Stair

**Checked:** Book 1 chs 33, 58, 68, 74, 76, 77, 79 (74 and 76 in full for the chamber); Book 2 chs 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, 76, 77; Story.md, Atlas.md (Cross ten leagues south of Harrow's End; travel table). Grepped for Corvin, Cassian, Reede, Delling, Rook, Danny, Brask, the Fleece, Tuft and Fell, Millbrook's well, the noon bell, the older stair and Garran Vale, and the Book 2 days 0–20.

**Fixed:**
1. Prose, Gil on the stair: "He put Danny's own…" → "He put his knife in…". Book 1 ch 74: "Joss's knife into the next, low, under the coat"; Danny's own weapon isn't used.
2. Prose, Tull's count: "the floor had taken seven, and the man the farmer had put the brand into, screaming. That was eight." → "the floor had taken the other seven, the man the farmer had put the brand into among them, screaming. That was eight." Ten men in the chamber (ch 74). With Danny dead and two up the stair, the old wording made eleven.
3. Prose, the wood on day 0: "at the end of the morning, there were bells. They rang a long while and stopped." → "there should have been a bell. There wasn't. He listened for it a long while." Book 2 ch 2: Greyhold's noon bell doesn't ring on day 0, "the first day in three hundred years the bell itself is silent".
4. Prose, Reede on Millbrook's sweet well: "Since the morning it flew." → "Five days after it flew." Book 2 ch 6, day 6: "Brown since yesterday noon"; "by the sixth day after the flight it had turned".

**Defensible:**
- "Two of his men with him" (Book 1 ch 76) against "walked south… on his own" (Book 2 chs 12, 14, Brask and Fell): the tale treats the second as the story the yard built. Its notes say so.
- Where he comes up: under a slab with a drain over it, which fits Book 2 ch 76 (Garran Vale's drain under the grooms' yard), and it's never named.
- Tuft and Fell (Book 2 ch 12) sell fleeces at the Cross in the tale on day 14, yet on day 22 at Varrhold they know "there was two". The tale says "There was two" aloud on night 18, so they'd have to leave the Cross on about day 19 and cover about 24 road leagues (Cross to Harrow's End 10, then 14 to Varrhold) in three days. Tight for an ox-wain (7 a day). It holds if they lingered; nothing in either text says when they left.
- The bill's text matches Book 2 ch 12's prose exactly. Delling "two years", the Plough, his spectacles on a cord, his men owed four months, the green boar man who crossed "before they shut the crossings", the Fleece's three storeys and gallery and Reede's press in its yard all agree with Book 2 ch 14.
- Gil's dates (body up on day 3, the herald's horn on day 4, the dragons on the shingle) agree with Book 2 chs 2, 4 and 5.
- Nothing reveals the roll, the College, Kessmoor or Corvin's road after Book 2 ch 12.

**Book-side issues:** none.

**Verdict:** fixed (4 changes).
