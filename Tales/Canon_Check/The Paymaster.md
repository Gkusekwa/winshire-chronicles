# Canon check: The Paymaster

**Checked:** read in full Book 1 chs 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 (with Ford Water); notes and the relevant prose of Book 1 chs 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 24, 32, 50, 52, 54, 58, 60, 62, 66, 68, 72; Book 1 Rulings_41-79 (Ruling 35); Book 2 chs 7, 14, 17 (the ledger's lines, word for word) and 19 (Ulric and the horse line). Bible: Characters.md (Rook, the Captain, the coin man, Brask, Delling), World.md, Atlas.md, the sweep. Grepped the books for Rook, the steward, Gorm, Delling, Sedge, Brask, the Captain, the coin man, Hob, the Wheel, the Eelweir boy, the mare with the white sock, the ledger lines, Morrow, the moon on days 3-8, and "didn't count".

**Fixed:**
1. Night 3, the tally room: "He was a sour old man with ink on his fingers who had come up through the Clan's dues-school" → "He was Ulric's steward, a sour old man who had come up through the Clan's dues-school". The ink-fingered steward of Book 1 ch 6 and ch 10 is Gorm Askell, Arn's (ch 16). Book 2 ch 17: "Rook's own wage, which Ulric's steward paid him"; Book 2 ch 19 and ch 17: "*the uncle's steward, four silver and keep*".
2. Day 7, Harrow's End: "The Clan's riders had been on the green all afternoon" → "that morning". Book 1 ch 16: "They came in the morning, two lots of them, from the two directions, an hour apart".
3. Day 9, the Eelweir boy: "who had come down in the night with the news" → "at first light", and "He's sitting up in the stubble with his ribs tied" → "He's sitting up in the stubble". Book 1 ch 18-20: Calder goes under in the night and wakes and sits up at dawn; Ada binds his ribs later that morning, after Wren crosses the field.
4. Day 9, the field: Calder's coat "which lay beside him on the ground" → "which was round his shoulders". Book 1 ch 20: the coat hung on the byre door until "In the middle of the morning, Ada... came out again with a coat... and put it round his shoulders."
5. Brask: "without his crossbow" → "without his sword". Book 1 ch 54: "He had a sword on his belt that he hadn't drawn"; Calder "took the man's sword, and his knife, and his coat, and let him keep his boots".
6. Notes block: added "Canon-checked against the books: 29 Oct 2026." No Decided-here line changed.

**Defensible:**
- The ledger lines the tale quotes (the bucket, the wage, the horse, the miller's two silver, Upper Rush, the second meeting) match Book 2 chs 17 and 19 word for word. The mill line is written "in its place" but not shown, and Sedge isn't named, so Must not reveal holds.
- The horse: the tale has Rook turn the mare loose above Harrow's End at dusk on day 4. Book 2 ch 19, Ulric: "Returned. He brought her back. In four days, as he said. Lathered. I thought he'd ridden hard to see his mother." Ulric is telling what he believed, and a mare walking home fourteen leagues uphill could reach the lines about day 7. "Lathered" is a stretch for a loose horse. Left as is; the author may want "she came back" in the tale, or keep it as Ulric's mistake.
- Ch 68's account ("I went down before light... Took a horse... Lay up in the gorse... He was pleased to see me. Waved... held the reins out to me... He never drew... like a stone"; the plain grey armband on the alders, the axe in the shallows) all agrees.
- Day 4: Rook sees Hob's cart "Two leagues, three" off at dawn. Ch 14 has the cart leave at two and arrive an hour before noon, so at dawn it's about three or four leagues off. Close enough.
- The island night (ch 17), the mill seen from the raft (chs 17-19), the Captain's words (ch 21), the coin man and Hob (ch 24), Vey (ch 32), the Ewe Beck cart and the burning (chs 50, 52), night 105 and Brask (ch 54), the Ram's Head yard on night 124 (ch 58), the second ford (ch 60), Ada's paper (ch 62), the chest left with Delling (ch 72; Book 2 chs 7, 14) all agree in who, when and what's said.
- Morrow is "her" in the captain's report and in ch 60. The moon (a few nights past the full on night 3, none on night 8) fits the cycle.
- The twins stay "the man" and "the people he worked for"; "the Fellow" is named only as in Book 1.
- Ford Water (another tale) has Ulric leave before the feast ends, and this tale has him give Rook the mare "after the feast". Both are tales, and Book 2 ch 19 says only "in the yard. The night before the ford." Flagged for the prose pass, not changed.

**Book-side issues:**
- None found in the books for this tale. Book 2 ch 19's "He brought her back" is the only line that pulls against it (above).

**Verdict:** fixed (5 fixes).
